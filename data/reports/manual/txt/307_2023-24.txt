                                                        Our School
      Immaculate Heart of Mary is a government aided co-educational secondary school founded by the Franciscan
Missionary Sisters of Our Lady of Sorrows in 1981. The construction of the school campus was completed on 27th
August, 1981 and the official opening and blessing ceremony was held on 9th February, 1983 by The Most Rev.
Bishop John B. Wu, Rev. Sr. M. Francis OSF & Mr. Chan Yan Pang SEO. The school came into existence with 17
teachers and 6 classes of Form 1 and Form 4 respectively. With the support and guidance from the Franciscan
sisters, the school has optimized its class structure in face of changing needs of the society. The current student
population is totaled 766 with 24 classes in F.1 to F6.


                                        A Vision of a Catholic Religious School
       As a staff member of a Catholic school, we should carry out the following core values of Hong Kong Catholic
Education: Truth, Justice, Love, Life and family. We should also follow the Mission and Vision of the School
Sponsoring Body – Franciscan Missionary Sisters of Our Lady of Sorrows, and the Guidelines for Franciscan
Education – Go and Teach.
       At the beginning of educational practice, we as Franciscan, share the Church’s commitment to building the
world. We are aware that this social promotion, carried out with the Gospel spirit, is the expression and the sign of
the presence of the Kingdom of God in our society, a work that helps us deepen the educational and anthropological
vision developed by Franciscan Masters.
       In Franciscan spirituality, the concepts of brotherhood and minority constitute the centre or the fundamental
nucleus of interpersonal relationships.
       The fraternity is the vital and proper place of education. It is the place where various values meet and are
shared. The values of acceptance, dialogue, respect for diversity, equality, responsibility, familiarity, trust, happiness,
optimism, peace and forgiveness.
       Minority quantifies and directs relations with others. This value, being the basis for all relations, makes us
simple and obliging, and helps us to move closer to the other with an open heart and ready to discover his inner self.
A style or way of relating which is opposed to any intention of ownership and domination of persons or property and
which prevents any inferiority complex or contempt of itself.
       In the context of fraternity and minority it is important to educate on poverty, solidarity, service to each other,
serious work and building peace.


                                                School Mission Statement
       Immaculate Heart of Mary College is sponsored by the Franciscan Missionary Sisters of Our Lady of Sorrows.
Through the example of BLESSED VIRGIN MARY in the LIFE of JESUS, we are led to imitate HER
COMPASSIONATE LOVE and WILLING WORKS of SERVICE TO OTHERS. Empowered by the spirit of
Gospel, we are dedicated to providing a caring and supportive school environment so that learning is valued,
mutual respect is shared and all members can develop their potential to the fullest capacity. By means of our
concern for Catholic Faith, Personal Formation and Scholarly Development, we strive to prepare students to
become responsible citizens who contribute to society, are sensitive to the needs of others and meet the challenges
of today’s world.


                                                       Catholic Faith
      We provide an atmosphere whereby each student is encouraged to seek a relationship with God through generous personal
response to the Gospel and by acquiring an active understanding and experience of the Catholic tradition.




                                                            - P.2 -
Religious Education and Liturgical Activities
      Since we are a Catholic institute of education, Christian values are to be upheld and exemplified through religious
education. Moreover, students are encouraged to participate daily in prayer, to join the school community in various
liturgical activities, availing themselves of these opportunities to reflect their faith and lifestyle.
       We seek to provide all members of the college, working harmoniously under a common community spirit,
the experience of a genuine sense of belonging, where each one finds charity, support, gentleness, kindness and
tolerance.


                                                   Personal Formation
       We aim to develop each student’s value, self-esteem and career through personal guidance in which the
students realize their self-identity, are recognized as individuals, and experience a caring, supportive school
environment.


Holistic Development Approach
       Students are encouraged to reach for excellence in every dimension of their lives (spiritual, moral, cognitive,
emotional, physical, aesthetic and social) through the range of activities offered in the school so that they will grow
towards maturity as persons of character and education.
       Such approach seeks to reveal and develop each student’s character, abilities and leadership potential through
our concern for their religious, emotional, physical, academic and psychological needs.


Career Preparation
       By means of a balanced curriculum, we prepare our students for their own free, informed, conscientious
choices in study, work and life.      The essence of our career guidance programs is to assist students to qualify
themselves for further education and careers, and to pursue growth as an individual, capable of independence and
responsible life decisions.


                                                 Scholarly Development
       We seek to maximize the achievement opportunities of our students so that they can acquire self-motivation
and independent thinking, respect for knowledge and the search for truth.


Persevering Attitudes
       Students are encouraged to develop their intellectual skills through consistent hard work, regular study habits
and a fulfilling of their duties.


                                                   General Outcomes
       So that each student will live Catholic values, serve humanity and society, and grow towards maturity as a
person of character, of education and of charity, our college aims to develop:
1.     a college community that is genuinely interested in and supportive of all members, fostering individual growth
       and development.
2.     Catholic values, respect for the values of others, and the ability to critically evaluate the current social issues.
3.     an all-round development in moral, spiritual, cognitive, emotional, physical, social and aesthetic education.
4.     an awareness and challenge for students towards their responsibilities to love the less fortunate and the less
       privileged, who hold priority in our care and concern.
5.     the individual interests and abilities of students, for the betterment of their personal welfare and that of society.

                                                      Our Vision
       Being a professional educational organization which has inherited the education values of the Hong Kong
Province Franciscan Missionary Sisters of Our Lady of Sorrows, we adhere to our school mission to practice the
spirit of the Gospel – loving God and people.
       We envisage that all teaching and administrative staff in the Immaculate Heart of Mary family are dedicated
professionals who keep abreast of the trends in teaching. They are not only role models of students, but also caring
and committed teachers.
       We hope our students will develop simplicity and wholesomeness, while becoming intellectually competent
to learn actively and think independently. Being nurtured to be young leaders who can shoulder responsibility, they
also imitate St. Francis to be a channel of peace in communities.
       We envision that through parent-teacher collaboration and effective utilization of community resources,
students will develop their potential to full capacity. With a sense of social awareness and responsibility, they will be
compassionate in serving others and contributing to communities.


                                                   School Facilities
       The school is well-equipped with air-conditioned classrooms, laboratories and School Hall for learning
activities. Under the School Improvement Project, a new wing with a new staff room, 4 classrooms and a staff
common room was built in January 1998. A corner of solitude was founded in the same year to make room for
student activities. In preparation for the New Senior Secondary Curriculum, the Design & Technology Room was
renovated to make room for a second Computer Room for small group and self-access learning activities.

                                      Evaluations / Comments / Recommendations
Evaluations:
1. Developing and Nurturing a Consistent Reading Habit
- Lower form students show stronger engagement in developing a consistent reading habit, often bringing personal books
   to read. In contrast, higher form students exhibit less enthusiasm towards reading.
- Junior form students have successfully built a regular reading habit, particularly during designated reading periods,
   where they consistently bring and read their chosen books.
- Some students, especially boys, express reluctance to dedicate time to reading. This highlights the need for ongoing
   encouragement to foster a robust reading culture.
- Initiatives such as summarizing newspaper clippings have improved reflective reading skills, particularly among senior
   students, indicating positive progress in this area.

2. Development and Application of Study Skills, Time Management, and Organizational Skills
  - Many students effectively utilize mind maps and summaries to organize their learning materials, which aids in revision
    and comprehension.
  - The school has implemented teaching strategies for study skills and time management, including morning assembly talks
    and dedicated form periods. However, enhancing the effectiveness of these teachings could be beneficial, with a
    preference for interactive, face-to-face formats over online methods.
  - While some students actively engage with tools like GoodNotes for note-taking, others show less initiative. This
    variability suggests a need for tailored support to maximize student participation.
  - Many students are actively engaged in online assignments and utilize technology for learning, indicating a willingness
    to embrace digital resources for educational purposes.
3. Taking Initiative and Responsibility for Learning
  - High-achieving students demonstrate significant initiative, regularly seeking help from teachers and completing
    assignments on time.
  - Senior students set clear goals for their academic projects and engage in volunteering activities, indicating a responsible
    approach to their learning.
  - Junior form students exhibit enthusiasm for self-study and actively seek resources online, demonstrating an increasing

   initiative in their learning process.
 - Students across all levels actively reach out to teachers for assistance, showcasing their commitment to taking
   responsibility for their educational success.


4. Capacity for Original Thinking, Problem-Solving, and Idea Presentation
  - Students show the ability to reason through conclusions, though some may struggle with precise language. Training in
    presentation skills is evident, particularly in Physics and Putonghua, but further development is needed, especially in
    oral presentations.
  - Students participating in events such as the ABC Camp display strong problem-solving and initiative skills during
    practical challenges.
  - Students earning certificates in writing competitions demonstrate original thinking and effective communication of ideas.
    Group discussions and presentations reflect confidence in generating and presenting ideas.
  - Students effectively analyze current events and apply learned concepts, demonstrating their problem-solving capabilities
    through collaborative scientific projects and group presentations.

Recommendations for Further Enhancement:
 - Implement incentive programs to encourage reading among higher form students and foster a more engaging reading
   culture school-wide.
 - Provide regular workshops focused on study skills, time management, and organizational strategies to reinforce these
   essential skills.
 - Offer individualized support to students less engaged in study skills development, ensuring that all students have the
   tools they need to succeed.
 - Develop more opportunities for students to practice presentation skills in a variety of contexts, including oral
   presentations and group projects.
 - Continue to provide platforms for students to showcase their original thinking and problem-solving abilities, such as
   competitions, projects, and collaborative assignments.

                                       Evaluations / Comments / Recommendations
Evaluation:
1. Participation and Engagement in Learning Activities
  - Junior form students generally display higher enthusiasm in group activities compared to their senior counterparts, who
    engage more in instructional tasks like note-taking. Hands-on learning experiences are particularly effective across all
    grades, fostering excitement in students. Notably, Form 1 students have shown increased enthusiasm compared to previous
    years.
  - The use of iPads for learning activities indicates students' willingness to incorporate technology into their education. This
    active engagement is reflected in their participation in mathematics lessons and extra learning practices outside of
    scheduled classes.
  - Students exhibit curiosity by asking questions and engaging with subject matter, particularly in subjects like Integrated
    Science and Life and Society. Their involvement in self-learning programs and recognition for their efforts further
    illustrates their commitment to learning.

 2. Exhibiting Genuine Interest and Curiosity in Learning
 - A willingness to tackle bonus questions in subjects like Math and English reflects students' genuine interest in learning.
    Junior form students particularly thrive in interactive and project-based learning environments.
 - Students express curiosity about practical applications of their studies, demonstrating interest in real-world issues relevant
    to their lives, such as vaccinations in Biology and salary tax in Economics.
 - Recognition from teachers has been shown to significantly boost interest among students, especially those with lower self-
    esteem, highlighting the importance of encouragement in fostering curiosity.

 3. Goal Setting and Direction in Educational Journey
 - There is a noted lack of awareness among students regarding their educational journeys, particularly in relation to JUPAS
    choices and elective subjects. This indicates a need for enhanced guidance to clarify academic paths.
 - Some senior form students demonstrate engagement by setting specific goals for their subjects, reflecting a commitment
    to their academic progress. Junior form students are gradually becoming more aware of their educational choices and
    adjusting their goals accordingly.
 - An increased willingness among junior form students to ask questions post-transition to face-to-face learning suggests a
    growing engagement with their educational needs and goals.



                                                              - P.9 -
4. Willingness to Persist Through Challenges
- High-achieving students exhibit strong determination and persistence in overcoming academic challenges, demonstrating
   a commitment to their goals.
- Most students show a proactive attitude in completing difficult tasks and often engage in peer discussions to enhance their
   learning outcomes. This collaborative spirit fosters resilience in facing academic challenges.
- Students’ non-academic accomplishments, such as success in sports, reinforce the importance of persistence and serve as
   motivation for academic endeavors. The presence of role models, including mentors, further supports a positive learning
   environment.

Recommendations for Further Enhancement:
- Implement structured career counseling sessions to help students clarify their educational paths and future aspirations.
- Develop more robust recognition programs to celebrate student achievements, thereby fostering further interest and
  engagement in learning.
- Introduce more project-based and real-world applications in the curriculum to stimulate curiosity and engagement among
  students.
- Establish mentorship opportunities where older students guide younger peers, promoting a collaborative learning culture
  and building confidence.
- Offer workshops focused on resilience and coping strategies to equip students with skills to navigate academic challenges
  effectively.

- Students consistently exhibit qualities such as care and compassion in daily activities. Their treatment of peers, especially

                                      Evaluations / Comments / Recommendations
 Task 1.1 To equip students and teachers with the essential skills to build up positive attitudes and values
 Training workshops for staff development were held. The topics covered were implementation of HKU QTN Promoting
    Wellness curriculum, mental health and wellness and preventing suicides. Teachers then delivered the programme
    contents to students in FP lessons throughout the year. Also, our school became a senior member of the EDB WE
    Dynamic Positive Scheme after organizing a series of related activities at school. Training for teachers and parents would
    be provided next year.
 A large variety of training workshops, talks and activities were organized for both students and staff. First, two induction
    programmes and follow-up activity were held successfully for F.1 and F.4 where positive attitudes such as compassion,
    perseverance and respect to others, and team spirit were also built. At the same time, talks on peer relationship, positive
    living and time management respectively for F.2, F.3, F.5 and F.6 at the beginning of school year. Extra talks for F.3 on
    application of character strengths before subject selection and F.6 on introduction of guide dogs were also held in the
    second term in order to let students more understand themselves and enhance their positive attributes.
 A staff development programme on communication with parents was conducted by the education psychologist from
    HKU QTN Promoting Wellness Project while another workshop on positive education cum sharing with Baptist (Sha
    Tin Wai) Lui Ming Choi Primary School was arranged by The City University of Hong Kong Positive Education
    Laboratory through the EBD WE Positive Dynamic Scheme. Teachers found it resourceful and meaningful.
 Positive feedback from students was received as they appreciated teachers’ sharing on personal life experience during
    lessons.
 Based on school-based survey, 88% of students agreed that they were nurtured with positive values.
 Task 1.2 To enhance mental health awareness in school through wellness education curriculum
 HKU QTN Promoting Wellness “Out of Dark” Level 1 curriculum was carried out in F.1 Form Periods, Level 2
   curriculum was carried out in F.2 Form Periods and Level 3 curriculum was carried out in F.3, F.4 and F.5 Form Periods.
   The topics covered understanding emotions, emotional management, character strengths, information literacy,
   communication skills, empathy, conflict resolution, gratitude and mental health awareness.
 Evaluation results showed that 81.2% of students from school-based survey agreed that through FP and curriculum they
   could build up positive mindset and skills. Almost 90% of students were satisfied with the activities they participated in
   the first Mental Health Day and Mental Health Awareness Weeks. These programmes were subsidized by the one-off
   mental health grant and learning support grant.
 Task 1.3 To appreciate and recognize students’ good behaviour and accomplishment and give feedback on their
