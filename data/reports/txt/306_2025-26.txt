I.    Our School

      1. Vision and Mission
           Vision
           The core values of Catholic Education and the spirit of our founders, Don Bosco and St. Mary
           Mazzarello, are the guiding principles of our education mission. Imbued with a family spirit and
           directed by the three cornerstones of the Preventive System of Don Bosco— Reason, Religion and
           Loving-kindness, it is our vision to nurture our students into young women who will live life to the
           full.


           Mission
           We aim to nurture the whole-person development of our students holistically enhancing their spiritual,
           moral, intellectual, cultural and social aspects. This will enable our students to embrace life, with all
           its realities and challenges, with optimism, integrity and fortitude, as well as serving society with
           dedication. All this will empower them to live life to the full.

      MAJOR CONCERN 1: REFINING TEACHING AND LEARNING STRATEGIES TO
      ENHANCE STUDENT LEARNING OUTCOMES

      Target 1.1 To refine teaching strategies for enhancing student engagement in learning

      Achievements of Strategic Measures Taken are as follows:
      Various teaching strategies had been adopted by subject panels for enhancing student engagement in
      learning. Focus had been placed on the “opening, continuation, transition, and conclusion” of the teaching
      segments by the Chinese Panel when designing lessons. Examples/real life examples were given.
      Appropriate learning activities and school-based teaching materials were also designed and developed.

      Link questions/Wh-questions were used while pair work and group activities were conducted by the English
      Panel and subjects of PSHE. Regular speaking practices, presentations and oral tasks were assigned.

      Practical work was assigned and practical exam conducted for science in junior forms.

      Self-directed learning and assessment were adopted by BAFS and students were engaged in project-based
      learning by the Music and VA panels. Besides, concept maps, graphic organisers were used and problem-
      solving skills taught.

      On the other hand, a holistic review of students’ performance in HKDSE, TSA and internal assessment was
      conducted by all academic subjects concerned. The data gathered were fully utilized to inform changes in
      curriculum, pedagogy and assessment and enhance the efficacy of teaching and learning. Remedial
      measures suggested to address students’ weaknesses as mentioned in TSA reports.

      Reflection
      It was found that there was a great learner diversity and a lack of motivation and incentive to learn on the
      part of some students. Some students had a short attention span. Focus will continue to be placed on striving
      for the clarity of lessons.

      Some students relied too much on rote learning and the revision worksheets given while some students in
      the weaker classes continue to struggle because of limited vocabulary and grammar knowledge.

      It was reflected by subjects of the PSHE KLA that class and discipline problems have to be handled by
      teachers.

      Fine-tuning the science curriculum is deemed necessary as doing practical work is time consuming.

      Feedback and Follow-up
      A holistic review of students’ performance in HKDSE, TSA and internal tests/examinations should
      continue to be conducted annually. Teachers should be encouraged to use the data to inform teaching and
      learning strategies. Sharing on how different subjects use the data can be conducted.

      Assessments should be revised to better cater to learner diversity and ensure a fairer evaluation. More exam-
      typed practice could be assigned to help students develop their problem-solving skills. Complex tasks are
      to be broken into more manageable steps to help students enhance their sense of satisfaction and their
      motivation to learn. Tokens of appreciation are to be given by teachers to acknowledge students’ effort.

      More has to be done to improve the learning attitude of the students. Effective classroom management is
      essential to creating a good learning environment and encouraging student engagement.

      If possible, more past pupils could be recruited to teach students in after-school tutorial classes.

   Target 1.2 To develop learning skills for enhancing learning effectiveness

   Achievements of Strategic Measures Taken
   Students had been guided to use tools such as mind maps, concept maps, and graphic organizers while note-
   taking skills were used in listening activities. Problem-solving skills on the part of students were developed.
   These learning skills were integrated into the learning materials by subjects mostly in the junior forms.

   Reflection
   It was pointed out by the English Panel that note-taking remains difficult for some students because of their
   poor spelling. Students’ note-taking skills are to be built up through modelling and practices.

   On the other hand, it is not suitable to use graphic organisers to sum up all topics in science. It was observed
   by the Maths Panel that some students forget the learning skills learnt quickly.

   Feedback and Follow-up
   The effort to help students cultivate regular reading habits by requiring them to collect and record the
   exemplary words and sentences from newspapers in their notebooks will be continued by the Chinese Panel.

   Past test and examination papers of the past 3 years to be given to students by the Maths Panel so as to help
   them understand the structure, timing and question types of assessments.

   It was suggested by subjects of the PSHE KLA that more time be spent on managing the discipline of the
   students.

   It was proposed by the Science KLA that more support on using AI in education e.g. provision of training
   programmes and subscription of software should be given by the school.

   Self-directed learning, and peer learning are to be promoted and more ability-based projects to be assigned
   by various subjects.

   Target 1.3 To strengthen language skills for self-expression and self-confidence

   Achievements of Strategic Measures Taken
   Word-building materials based on lists of vocabulary items covered in S1-3 had been developed by the
   English Panel.

   The vocabulary lists provided for every topic by the Science Panel had proven to be highly useful. Besides,
   lesson notes and worksheets were given to emphasize the sentence structures and patterns by various
   science subjects.

   Written/oral reflection tasks based on the reading texts and/or audio recordings were assigned by various
   subjects. For example, one-minute presentations were conducted by students in S2 and S3 CES lessons so
   as to provide them with ample opportunities to articulate their ideas.

   Tailor-made vocabulary booklets were used during LAC lessons in S1-S2. Essential EMI terminology to
   ease their transition into secondary-level English-medium instruction was introduced to S1 students during
   the Summer Bridging Course. Scaffolding speaking and writing tasks were given. Students were guided to
   develop structured paragraphs through pre-writing work.

   Activities like the World Book Day Reading Carnival, Hong Kong Budget 2026-27 had been organised to
   increase students’ exposure to English in an authentic and engaging context.

   Reflection
   The alignment between LAC booklets and EMI subjects is effective.

   However, some students remain passive and rely heavily on teachers’ prompting before attempting oral
   responses. Some lack confidence in using English spontaneously in EMI lessons.

   More scaffolding is needed to support weaker students in producing fuller and more accurate responses.

   Feedback and Follow-up
   "Sentence Starters" and "Language Frames" are to be provided for weaker students. It was suggested by
   the English Panel that "Graphic Organizers", for example, Hamburger Model or PEEL structure, should be
   introduced to students by all EMI subjects. More short and simple speaking tasks, pair work and guided
   discussion to be given to build the confidence of students while more differentiated support is to be given
   so that weaker students can participate more actively in EMI lessons.

   Students will continue to be exposed to questions of different types and a variety of learning resources will
   be used to enrich their reading comprehension and answering skills by subjects of the Science KLA.

   A set of "Guided Prompting Questions" and a "Mood/Emotion Word Bank" is to be provided by the English
   Panel to help students articulate their experiences more deeply in future reflections.

   In addition, it was proposed by the PSHE KLA that a more immersive and language-rich English
   environment should be created across the school in collaboration with various subject panels and functional
   groups, such as S.A. and Moral and Civic Education Team. Students are to be encouraged to use English
   beyond the classroom in both formal and informal settings and an award mechanism to be devised, for
   example, Dojo points / House Points can be awarded to their use of English in class and outside classroom.

   Target 1.4 To enhance the meaningful use of IT and cultivate responsible digital users

   Achievements of Strategic Measures Taken
   e-Learning platforms (e.g., “篇篇流螢” and “每日小挑戰”), had been utilized by some students to
   gradually develop self-directed learning habits.

   Generative AI tools (i.e. Gemini) had been used to design learning and teaching materials by various subject
   panels. On the other hand, platforms such as Google Docs and Padlet had been utilized for "co-construction
   of knowledge” by the English Panel.

   IT tools such as Nearpod, Kahoot, AI app “Robo Coach”, and Google Forms had been used to enhance
   student engagement and motivation in learning by subjects of the PSHE KLA.

   The ethical and responsible use of IT/AI was covered in CES and related computer literacy topics. Students
   were reminded to use the device and technology correctly by teachers of other subjects.

   Reflection
   It was found by the English Panel that tools like Google Docs and Padlet were effective during the pre-
   writing stage.

   It was pointed out by teachers of the Maths Panel said that more insights into students’ thinking and
   misconceptions could be obtained through teachers’ manual marking of students’ work rather than relying
   solely on AI-assisted marking.
   Teachers of the PSHE KLA said that lesson design had to be refined so that the use of IT is linked more
   clearly to learning outcomes rather than being used only for novelty.

   It was reminded by teachers of the Science KLA that while AI tools are useful for brainstorming and
   generating ideas, all AI-generated contents must be thoroughly proofread and verified by subject teachers.

   Teachers of the Technology KLA mentioned that despite the BYOD policy, some students do not bring
   their iPads every day. It is not only time consuming for teachers to provide the iPads for them, but students
   also cannot login some apps without their google accounts.

   Some students have not yet fully understood the importance of academic integrity and the appropriate use
   of IT and AI tools. Plagiarism is commonly discovered in students work when they relied entirely on the
   AI to ‘get’ answer by screenshotting the questions without any proofreading.

   While IT tools have enhanced engagement, over-reliance on technology may distract students from the
   learning objectives and reduce opportunities for independent thinking and active language use.

   Cultivating proper citation habits is important and highly beneficial for students' future academic studies.
   Despite knowing that they should not abuse the use of technology, some students are still addicted to it and
   some of them misuse it and cause discipline issues.

   Feedback and Follow-up
   Over-reliance on AI for marking is not recommended and teachers should continue to exercise their
   professional judgement and expertise when assessing students’ work.

   Lesson planning should be strengthened so that each digital tool serves a clear pedagogical purpose.

   Teachers’ capacity of selecting suitable digital tools and designing more purposeful blended-learning tasks
   should continue to be developed.

   Digital citizenship, academic honesty, and responsible AI use should continue to be taught in formal lessons
   and via the talks by experts. Clear examples of proper citation formats should be provided to students rather
   than allowing them to simply state that their resources were "extracted from Google".

   It is essential to monitor students’ screen time and appropriate breaks or offline tasks should be included to
   maintain healthy learning habits. MDM screen and networking monitoring should be used to restrict the
   use of internet and/iPad during recess and lunch breaks to avoid their misuse, such as watching YouTube
   videos during their breaks. It was suggested that students should lock their iPads in their locker before the
   teacher requires them to take them out and use them in order to avoid distractions in the lessons.

   Target 1.5 To foster teachers’ ongoing professional development as lifelong learners

   Achievements of Strategic Measures Taken
   Teaching resources, such as marking insights, instructional videos, worksheet designs, and lesson plans
   were shared. Good practices and insights gained from attending professional development programmes
   were also shared at various meetings held.

   Collaboration existed amongst subject panels, for example, Maths, Science, Geography, Physics and
   Economics, in the development of teaching materials.

   Staff development programmes on digital education, artificial intelligence, national education as well as
   the Mandatory Reporting of Child Abuse Ordinance had been organised to cater to the needs of teachers.

   Most teachers have fulfilled the required staff development hours by the EDB.

   Reflection
   The AI workshop organised by the school was well-received and had built a strong foundation for the staff.

   Regular professional sharing and discussions were conducted among Mathematics teachers as well as with
   teachers from other subject panels concerned to exchange teaching experiences and ideas.

   Although sharing activities are in place, the depth and frequency of professional exchange could be further
   enhanced. It is deemed easier to have professional sharing on a subject rather than on a KLA basis

   As the professional development courses organised by the EDB have limited quotas, it is necessary for
   teachers to submit enrolment promptly. Some teachers reflected that they cannot attend PD courses because
   of clashes with lessons.

   The professional development courses offered by the EDB are relatively narrow in scope for some subjects
   such as T&L, PE and Music.

   More relevant and useful courses organized by private organizations or institutions are not recognized or
   counted as PD hours.

   Feedback and Follow-up
   Teachers will continue to be invited to share their experiences in different domains so as to promote
   professional exchanges within the subject panel and enhance teaching effectiveness.

   A collaborative professional learning culture is to be strengthened by creating more structured platforms
   for peer sharing and discussion.

   It was suggested that several triple free periods be arranged after lunchtime to facilitate the conducting of
   professional development activities and collaborative planning among Mathematics teachers.

   A more timely updating of teachers’ professional training hours should be provided so that teachers can
   make up for missed areas and deepen their learning in selected focus areas.

   MAJOR CONCERN 2: NURTURING POSITIVE VALUES AND MINDSETS FOR
   HOLISTIC STUDENT DEVELOPMENT

   Overview
   Throughout the 2025–2026 school year, the school worked hard to help students develop positive values
   and a strong mindset. This work follows the school’s Catholic identity and the teaching spirit of St. John
   Bosco and St. Mary Mazzarello. Many different groups in the school worked together. Together, their
   actions helped create a caring, resilient, and values-based school culture.

   Target 2.1: To Cultivate Respect, Appreciation and Love for Oneself to Promote Self-Worth
   Achievements
   Our school implemented many values- and mindset-related activities, and these were well received by
   students, who showed clear support and recognition for the learning.
   For example:
   The Virtuous Leadership Award Scheme (S.1–S.3) successfully recognized students who demonstrated
   exemplary character, providing positive reinforcement for embodying core values. Responsibility
   Workshops for S.1–S.2 enhanced students' sense of personal responsibility and ethical awareness. Character
   Strength Workshops for S.5 helped students explore their personal strengths, fostering greater self-
   appreciation and respect for others. The Pastoral Care Logbook guided junior form students in reflecting
   on their learning progress, helping to establish a positive classroom atmosphere. ERE lessons, including
   the S.1 "Knowing the Bible" seminar, promoted positive self-image and self-awareness.

   Reflections
   Some class teachers felt that too many students received awards, suggesting criteria should be more tailored.
   Many students lack confidence and mistakenly believe it comes only from academic results. Junior form
   students demonstrated weaker self-management skills and required additional guidance in developing self-
   awareness and reflection.

   Follow-up
   The award mechanism will be reviewed to ensure meaningful recognition. Similar character strength
   workshops should continue. More interactive and experiential activities should be arranged to help students
   discover their uniqueness and build confidence.

   Target 2.2: To Inculcate Inner Strength and the Power of Resilience to Embrace Challenges
   Achievements
   Stress Management Workshops for S.4–S.6 introduced students to relaxation skills and emotional
   management strategies, with positive feedback received. The Atonement of Demerits by Merits Scheme
   provided opportunities for students to rectify behavioural issues, with success rates of 61.2% for habitual
   lateness. The workshops and programme helped S.2–S.3 students build resilience in facing adversity are
   success. Positive Mindset Programmes, including a Nature Wellness Experiential Journey, enhanced
   mindfulness among student leaders. Preventive measures under the Student Mental Health Support Scheme
   provided professional support for students with emotional and psychological needs. Spiritual retreats for
   S.1–S.2 and S.4–S.6 integrated prayer and faith experiences, helping students build inner strength and
   perseverance.

   Reflections
   Students have made encouraging progress in developing coping skills, yet ongoing reinforcement and
   deeper internalisation are necessary for lasting impact. However, the low success rate (13.2%) of the
   Atonement Scheme in improving homework submission reveals that the current strategy does not
   adequately resolve students' academic struggles. As a way forward, it was suggested collaborating with
   external organizations to provide students with more practical, real-world skills training.

   Follow-up
   Continue organizing stress management and positive mindset programmes. Optimise the Atonement
   Scheme with a shortened and more targeted approach. Strengthen collaboration with NGOs and
   professionals to provide diverse support for mental health. Enhance the referral mechanism to ensure early
   identification and timely support.

   Target 2.3: To Nurture Bonding Within and Beyond the School Community to Build Relationship
   Achievements
   The Big Sisters Programme demonstrated caring relationships, with Big Sisters leading activities and
   supporting S.1 students. 「和諧校園–尊重友愛拒絕欺凌」programmes reinforced the school's zero-
   tolerance policy towards bullying. Inter-class and inter-house activities, including the Singing Contest and
   Sports Day, fostered collaboration and strengthened house spirit. Mass and school events fostered unity,
   mutual support, and strengthened teacher-student bonds.

   Reflections
   Junior form students frequently encountered peer conflicts and struggled to build positive relationships,
   often showing self-centred behaviour. The Big Sisters, however, were not fully equipped to address these
   challenges, as they bore dual roles as both Big Sisters and Prefects. Furthermore, some Big Sisters lacked
   the necessary communication skills to effectively support and guide the younger peers.

   Follow-up
   Separate the roles of "Big Sister" and "Prefect" to enable more targeted training. Strengthen anti-bullying
   initiatives and provide more interactive workshops on communication and conflict resolution for junior
   forms. Enhance communication with parents through regular updates and targeted outreach. Continue
   organizing activities to foster teamwork and positive peer relationships.

   Target 2.4: To Create Ample Chances for Exposure and Exploration to Enrich School Life
   Achievements
   Students benefited from a wide range of life-wide learning experiences, including local excursions and
   overseas study tours to Seoul, Shanghai and Suzhou, Sichuan, and the CSD Mainland Study Tour. The
   tours were particularly well-received, with participants reporting positive peer interactions throughout the
   activity. The Career and Life Planning Team contributed significantly through Career Live Career
   Exploration Games for S.4, Subject Selection Workshops for S.3, and a Mentorship Programme for S.3–
   S.5, which engaged 56 mentees and elicited positive feedback. Moreover, cross-curricular initiatives
   effectively wove values education into subject-based learning, while National Education activities
   enhanced students' understanding of national identity and civic duty.

   Reflections
   Some students failed to recognize the full value of these learning opportunities. The excessive number of
   concurrent tours has weakened student accountability and overstretched administrative capacity, frequently
   necessitating the re-enrollment of unprepared students. Consequently, highly motivated students tend to
   monopolize multiple trips, while passive students continue to show limited interest in participation.


OLC_School Report (2025-2026)                         9
   Follow-up
   Reduce the total number of tours offered, especially during the same period, to ensure resources are directed
   toward students who genuinely benefit. Limit student choices to streamline administrative processing.
   Strengthen follow-through with pre-goals and post-reflection. Provide more encouragement to help
   students become proactive in planning and organizing activities.

   Target 2.5: To Celebrate Accomplishments to Affirm and Share Joy
   Achievements
   Students' achievements were regularly celebrated through morning assemblies, the school webpage, the
   achievement board, and the Schools’ social media. Monthly prize presentations highlighted student
   successes.

   Reflections
   Public recognition boosts confidence and fosters a sense of belonging, motivating students to strive for
   excellence. The talent databank could be used more effectively to search for suitable applicants for external
   awards.

   Follow-up
   Continue providing platforms for students to showcase their achievements. Arrange more student sharing
   during morning assemblies. Strengthen the use of the talent databank to ensure comprehensive information
   about students' achievements is collected and utilized.

   Overall Conclusion
   The school has demonstrated a strong commitment to nurturing positive values and mindsets for holistic
   student development. Through collaborative efforts across multiple teams, the school has successfully
   cultivated self-worth, resilience, relationships, exposure, and celebration of accomplishments. While
   significant progress has been made, areas for improvement include refining awards criteria, strengthening
   resilience-building and self-management skills, improving peer relationships among junior students,
   optimizing study tour coordination, and deepening home-school collaboration. The school remains
   unwavering in its mission to provide a safe, caring, and stimulating environment where every student can
   flourish and grow into compassionate, responsible, and confident individuals.

III.      Student Performance
           1. Students’ Attitude and Behaviour


              As a school established by the Institute of the Daughters of Mary Help of Christians (FMA), we are
              deeply rooted in the Catholic faith and the enduring educational legacy of our Founders, St. John Bosco
              and St. Mary Mazzarello. Their vision continues to inspire our holistic educational philosophy,
              fostering a caring and supportive atmosphere in which students are nurtured to excel academically and
              grow into well-rounded individuals.


              Throughout the current school year, we have continued to draw upon our strong religious foundation
              and diverse school programmes to foster positive behaviour and embed fundamental values—
              including respect, empathy, and responsibility—in our students. Prayer rituals and other spiritual
              activities continue to resonate deeply with our learners, highlighting the lasting and meaningful impact
              of our faith-based practices on their moral growth and personal well-being.


              Acknowledging the difficulties arising from the wider social context—such as the far-reaching
              influence of social media, diverse and sometimes challenging family circumstances, and varying
              degrees of student self-assurance—we have redoubled our efforts to nurture positive thinking and
              emotional fortitude. Accordingly, we have prioritized the development of self-management and
              emotion-regulation skills across all year levels, whilst simultaneously addressing behavioural issues
              observed in our junior forms through focused interventions and dedicated mentoring support.


              It is heartening to observe that our students continue to exhibit genuine kindness and mutual concern
              for one another. While we acknowledge that occasional misunderstandings in communication among
              students do arise, and that there are times when teachers may not fully grasp every student's situation,
              we recognize that these are part of the normal dynamics of a vibrant school community. On the whole,
              the vast majority of our students demonstrate good conduct and a kind-hearted nature. Strong peer
              relationships and close teacher-student connections remain defining features of our school community.
              Collaborative learning experiences, together with a well-established system of recognition—
              encompassing awards, ongoing constructive feedback, and structured self-reflection—have
              consistently proven effective in reinforcing positive conduct and cultivating a strong sense of
              belonging among our learners.

    2. Students’ Participation and Achievement
       i) Academic Performance
          In HKDSE 2026, it was most encouraging to find that the percentage of students meeting the
          entrance requirements of JUPAS continued to be higher than that of day school candidates.

             The percentages of students attaining Level 4 or above continued to increase while the percentage
             of students fulfilling the minimum requirements of all the 4 core subjects (i.e. 332A), were higher
             than that of all school candidates.

             Besides, there was a significant increase in the percentages of Level 4 or above in subjects including,
             BAFS (accounting), BAFS (business management), Economics, English Language, Mathematics
             (compulsory part), and M1.

             In addition, there was a remarkable increase in the percentages of Level 3 or above in 9 subjects,
             namely, BAFS (accounting), BAFS (business management), Chemistry, Economics, English
             Language, Geography, Mathematics (compulsory part), M1 and Physics. For Chinese Language
             and English Language, some students got very impressive results of attaining Level 5** in reading
             and integrated skills and writing as well as speaking respectively.

             On the other hand, students’ performance in Category B subjects was very encouraging indeed. Of
             the students who took the examinations of Category B subjects in DSE 2026, 4 of them got
             Distinction II and 10 got Distinction I respectively.

             The school will continue to strive for a quality education for students and prepare them to get their
             best possible academic achievements.

       ii)    Non-Academic Performance
              In line with our goal of developing self-directed, confident, and resilient learners, the school
              strives to expose students to a wide range of opportunities, giving them room to explore their
              interests and experience real personal growth beyond the classroom. Over the past year, through
              active engagement across inter-school platforms, our students took genuine initiative in their
              development. They put great effort into academic enrichment activities such as speech, debating,
              science challenges, and creative writing, while also showing determination on the sports field in
              athletics, swimming, team games, and fencing.

              Providing students with platforms to showcase their work in music, dance, visual arts and design
              has helped them build lasting self-belief and perseverance. Equally meaningful was their
              experience in leadership programmes, environmental projects, and community service, where
              students stepped out of their comfort zones to care for the community. Their steady progress,
              personal growth, and positive values were important milestones in their learning journey,
              reflecting a process of nurturing and character formation.

              The school remains committed to nurturing every student, continuously creating ways for them
              to engage with new challenges, showcase their strengths, and grow as confident, resilient
              individuals.
