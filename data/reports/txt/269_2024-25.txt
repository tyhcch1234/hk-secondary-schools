1. Our School
   1.1 Type of School
       Shau Kei Wan Government Secondary School is a co-educational grammar school operated
       by the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Government.


   1.2 Historical Background
       Shau Kei Wan Government Secondary School was founded in 1961. As the school was still
       under construction, it operated in the shared premises of Clementi Middle School at Fortress
       Hill Road. It moved to its present premises at 42 Chai Wan Road, Hong Kong in the autumn
       of 1964 and was officially opened on 19 November 1964.


   1.3   School Facilities
         The school has a total of 29 classrooms, plus a Computer-Assisted Learning Room, a Multi-
         media Learning Centre, an English Language Room, 2 e-Learning Rooms, 4 laboratories (for
         Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Science) and 5 special rooms (for Visual Arts, Music, Design
         and Technology, Home Economics and Geography). The school also has a Library, a School
         Hall, a Wellness Hub, an Inspiration Hub, a Student Activity Centre, a Life Planning Room, a
         Multi-function Room and a Music Activity Room for various educational activities. For
         students’ health, well-being and safety, the school has a Guidance Room, 2 Social Worker’s
         Rooms and a sick room. Other facilities include 2 Staffrooms, a Staff Common Room, a
         General Office, a Conference Room and a printing room. In addition, the school has a garden,
         a courtyard, a tuck shop and a covered playground to enhance social interaction, as well as 2
         multi-purpose playgrounds and a mini football pitch to promote physical activities and sports
         participation.

 Overall remarks:
  The 2024/25 Annual School Plan for Major Concern 1 has been successful in cultivating an active
   and engaging learning environment. This was achieved through the adoption of diverse and
   targeted teaching strategies, collaborative lesson planning, and a variety of student-centred
   programmes.
  Although the new teaching strategies successfully increased student engagement and motivation,
   only a minority of teachers observed notable improvements in students’ academic results and a
   deep, sustained interest in learning. This suggests that further refinement and long-term efforts are
   necessary to translate engagement into stronger academic outcomes.
  Addressing the diverse learning needs of students remains a key challenge. Future strategies should
   emphasize differentiated instruction and personalized support to cater to varied abilities and
   learning styles. Strengthening students' study skills through focused activities and workshops will
   be crucial in enhancing their learning effectiveness and confidence.
  Considerable achievements were made in promoting STEAM education through interdisciplinary
   collaboration, hands-on activities, and participation in competitions and workshops. These
   initiatives have enriched students’ scientific knowledge, creativity, and problem-solving skills, while
   fostering enthusiasm and curiosity across various disciplines.
  Positive feedback from both students and teachers confirmed the value of STEAM-related
   programmes in broadening students’ academic and practical experiences. The school will continue
   to embed STEAM education into the curriculum, ensuring ongoing development of students’
   generic skills and preparing them for future academic and career challenges.
  Overall, the combined efforts in enhancing teaching effectiveness, promoting active learning, and
   integrating STEAM education have laid a strong foundation for continuous improvement. Going
   forward, sustained focus on optimizing teaching strategies, supporting diverse learners, and
   enriching cross-curricular learning will be essential to further elevate students’ academic excellence
   and lifelong learning capabilities.

Overall Remarks:
 Our school has made significant strides in promoting the healthy growth of students by facilitating
  the development of healthy lifestyle habits. Through various programs, we have encouraged
  physical activity, balanced nutrition, and mental well-being, creating an environment where
  students feel supported in their health journeys. We have also focused on cultivating positive
  attitudes and resilience, equipping students with the tools they need to navigate life’s challenges.
  Workshops and seminars on stress management and emotional intelligence have empowered
  students to face adversity with confidence and determination. Furthermore, we have fostered a
  culture of appreciation and understanding within the school, promoting values that celebrate
  diversity and individual strengths. Initiatives such as peer mentoring and appreciation events have
  reinforced the importance of recognizing and valuing each other’s contributions. These efforts have
  not only enhanced the overall school climate but have also encouraged students to build strong
  relationships and support one another.
 However, we acknowledge that there are still areas for improvement, particularly in strengthening
  social skills and peer support. By addressing these areas, we can continue to enhance our supportive
  environment and further promote the holistic development of our students.
 We need to prioritize health and well-being, particularly regarding breakfast habits and nutrition
  awareness. Organizing talks with nutritionists and sharing simple, healthy recipes with students and
  their families would be beneficial. We should also address students' sleep habits; workshops on time
  management and setting realistic expectations could help. Additionally, classroom discipline is a
  concern, some students reporting that peers often don’t listen to teachers, especially among younger
  students. Strengthening classroom management and encouraging student responsibility will be
  crucial for creating a better learning environment.
 For junior students, we should gradually introduce activities that help them understand the meaning
  of life. It’s concerning that only 60% have sought online information about universities or
  vocational training, so we need to help them build their strengths and confidence early on.
  Organising various activities and encouraging capable students to participate in external
  competitions can boost their leadership skills. Furthermore, running workshops for parents on
  youth development can improve communication and create a supportive home environment.
 To support senior students effectively, we should focus on social skills training through programs
  that promote friendship and peer support, making the school a more harmonious place. We will
  also incubate the ‘Let Students Shine’ scheme to help students develop their strengths and pursue
  excellence. Addressing these needs in our two-year plan will be essential for achieving our goals.

 The Major Concern 3 Team has organized reading activities and programmes aimed at achieving the
 school's development targets and meeting the needs of our students.
  This year, the team continued to collaborate with different departments and functional teams to
   enhance reading competence across subjects and through promotion talks.
  Reading periods were conducted successfully with regular theme-based reading materials tailored
   to cater to students’ learning and reading needs. To encourage students to develop a broader range
   of interests and abilities in reading, different teams made significant efforts in selecting appropriate
   reading materials, refining reading and viewing resources, and setting meaningful and interactive
   post-reading tasks throughout the year. This approach has successfully sustained students' reading
   habits and interests, encouraging them to explore a broader range of topics and enhancing their
   reading literacy over the long term.
  The APASO survey results indicated that reading has not been a top priority for students' learning.
   Yet, they are fully aware of its importance. In view of this, it is necessary to motivate them to fulfill
   their reading development needs through a wider range of activities in the long term.
  More interesting activities related to reading should be held to increase students’ motivation to read.
   It is expected that more students will be motivated to read extensively, supported by a richer reading
   environment and peer encouragement. Additionally, teachers should take a more active role in
   encouraging students to build reading competence through more practice and positive learning
   experiences.

7.2 Discipline Team
    The Discipline Team aims to develop students’ self-discipline, nurture positive values, attitudes,
    and a sense of belonging to the school.
    Believing that discipline fosters behavioral self-regulation, the Team endeavours to help
    students appreciate the importance of self-discipline and mutual respect, and to help establish
    a constructive and supportive learning environment within the school.


     The Discipline Team adopts a whole-school approach to achieve these objectives. To ensure
     that students adhere to the school’s standards of conduct, the Team has developed a
     comprehensive system of disciplinary measures and rewards, underpinned by clear procedures
     and expectations. A variety of programmes and activities are organized to instill discipline and
     promote positive behavior. The Team also collaborates closely with the Guidance Team to
     support students’ holistic development. To facilitate home-school partnership, it maintains
     regular communication with parents so that behavioral issues are identified and addressed
     promptly and effectively.


     The Discipline Team continues to work with subject teachers, class teachers, the Guidance
     Team, school social workers, and parents to administer and support school discipline. Through
     this collaborative effort, the Team seeks to cultivate positive values, foster a sense of citizenship
     and national identity, encourage maturity and respect, and equip students to conduct
     themselves appropriately in society.

7.5 National Security Education
    National Security Education aims to provide a safe and peaceful learning environment for
    students, ensuring they receive a quality education while fostering the concepts of national
    sovereignty and security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. As an integral part
    of the People's Republic of China, this initiative safeguards the high degree of autonomy under
    the "one country, two systems" principle as enshrined in the Basic Law.


     Adopting a whole-school approach, the Committee has integrated national security education
     into multiple domains of school governance and student development, encompassing such as
     Staff Development, School Administration, Learning and Teaching, Whole Person
     Development, Student Support (Guidance and Discipline), and Home-School Collaboration.
     The Committee has further mobilized alumni and community resources to enrich students’
     learning experiences both inside and outside the classroom, thereby broadening perspectives,
     fostering civic responsibility, and strengthening students’ community and national identities.


     Staff Development
     The Committee has strengthened teacher professionalism by ensuring that staff are well-
     informed of the Hong Kong National Security Education Framework. The Committee
     introduced its functions during Academic Affairs and Staff Meetings, where it outlined the
     perspectives of national security and demonstrated strategies for integrating relevant elements
     into classroom teaching and co-curricular activities.


     In addition, the Committee actively participated in EDB training programmes, which provided
     a platform to exchange best practices on cross-curricular and experiential learning approaches,
     thereby ensuring alignment with government strategies. Furthermore, the Committee
     organised experiential learning initiatives, most notably a visit to the National Security
     Exhibition Gallery for staff and students. This activity not only enhanced teachers’ awareness
     of national security but also reinforced their professional responsibility and cultivated a
     collective sense of mission in delivering national security education.


     School Administration
     The Committee regularly reviews the standard procedures manual for National Security at the
     School to ensure alignment with measures issued by the Bureau. This includes organizing
     activities to ensure that all events—whether by the School, service providers, or other
     institutions—strictly adhere to national security guidelines, thereby maintaining a peaceful and
     safe learning environment. At the same time, the monitoring mechanism for school events was
     strengthened by introducing an administrative review for all teaching and support materials,
     whether provided internally or by external service providers. Quotation and tendering
     documents now include compliance clauses regarding national security, ensuring that event
     coordinators remain vigilant throughout the planning and implementation process.

In addition, data collection tools were updated in accordance with EDB requirements and the
Assessment Program for Affective and Social Outcomes (APASO KPM17). Surveys on teacher
training, subject integration, and whole-person development were refined to reflect a broader
education framework encompassing national security education, value education, moral
education, and civic education. These refinements allow for more precise monitoring and
ensure that evaluations accurately reflect the effectiveness of the School’s initiatives.


Learning and Teaching and Whole-person Development
In alignment with the EDB requirements on national security education, the Committee has
established a systematic mechanism for the selection of learning materials to ensure full
compliance with stipulated standards. A centralized filing system has also been implemented
to archive all school-based national security-related resources for a period of four years, thereby
ensuring transparency, accountability, and ease of reference.


To promote a holistic and whole-school approach to national security education, the
Committee has actively monitored the progress of learning, teaching, and whole-person
development initiatives. At the conclusion of each term, a comprehensive survey is conducted
to review the latest progress across Key Learning Areas and functional groups in integrating
national security elements. With wide-ranging coverage in both academic and non-academic
domains, the effectiveness of national security education has been positively reflected in
students’ values, awareness, and overall development.


The Committee has further supported teachers by providing updates on the Hong Kong
National Security Education Curriculum Framework (2025) and issuing subject-specific
guidelines to facilitate the organic integration of national security education into curricula.
This ensures teachers are properly equipped to deliver aligned and consistent learning
experiences.


Through the collaboration of the Whole-Person Development teams, a variety of cultural and
thematic activities have been organised to deepen students’ understanding of traditional culture
and its evolution. Cross-disciplinary initiatives have been implemented to foster students’ core
values rooted in traditional Chinese morality and culture. In addition, the Committee has
worked in close partnership with the School’s Major Concerns Team to enhance the
comprehensiveness of national education delivery. Alumni have been invited to share
professional expertise and career experiences, linking their practical encounters with national
security to students’ learning. Notably, an alumnus represented the School in a sister-school
exchange programme with Guiyang No. 1 High School, exemplifying collaboration, mutual
learning, and youth exchange between Hong Kong and Mainland counterparts.

To further promote patriotism and the strengthening of core values, the Committee invited a
Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Deputy to the National People’s Congress to deliver
thematic talks on national confidence. Additionally, in collaboration with the Law Society of
Hong Kong, a Law-abiding School Seminar was held in which volunteer lawyers provided
students with essential legal concepts and knowledge, thereby reinforcing their awareness of
the rule of law and its importance to society.


Student Support
To safeguard student well-being and reduce potential risks to national security, the Committee
worked closely with the Guidance and Discipline Teams, as well as school social workers,
throughout the year. National security awareness was reinforced by incorporating relevant rules
into the student handbook and presenting them during the first discipline assembly. Monitoring
results show that no students were found to have violated national security-related regulations
during the reporting period, demonstrating the success of the preventative and supportive
measures in place.


Home-School Collaboration
The Committee has continued to prioritise transparent communication with parents in order
to foster trust, partnership, and parental engagement in advancing national security education.
Parents were regularly briefed on the latest national security policies and the School’s related
measures to strengthen their understanding and support. A notable highlight of the year was
Chinese Culture Week, during which parents were invited to participate actively in promoting
traditional Chinese culture, such as health preservation through tea culture, by operating game
stalls themed around core traditional values. Parents responded with strong approval and
expressed appreciation for the School’s dedication to cultivating a safe, supportive, and values-
driven learning environment for their children.


Through strengthened teacher professionalism, administrative compliance, student support,
and home-school collaboration, the Committee has promoted values of law-abidingness,
responsibility, and nationhood among students. The Committee remains firmly committed to
advancing national security education in a systematic, holistic, and sustainable manner.

9. Feedback on Future Planning
   9.1 Major Concern 1: To raise students’ learning capability in pursuit of academic excellence
       9.1.1 Most of the tasks outlined in the 2024/25 Annual School Plan for Major Concern 1 were
             accomplished satisfactorily. The implementation of specific teaching strategies proved
             effective in fostering a more engaging and active learning environment, as evidenced by
             positive feedback from both teachers and students.
       9.1.2 According to the Stakeholders’ Survey, student engagement increased with the adoption
             of specific teaching strategies. However, only 38.5% of teachers observed that students
             showed a deep interest in learning, and just 29.4% noted improvements in academic
             results. This suggests that teachers need more time and sustained efforts to further
             optimize teaching strategies, helping students of varying abilities turn their motivation
             into academic success. Considering the wide diversity of learning needs among students,
             teaching strategies should incorporate measures to cater to these needs. Our school also
             plans to organize activities aimed at strengthening students’ study skills to enhance their
             learning effectiveness and boost their confidence.
       9.1.3 Our school provided a broad array of opportunities for developing students’
             competencies in STEAM. Students generally exhibited a strong interest and active
             participation in these activities. Moving forward, we will further integrate STEAM
             education into the school curriculum next year, aiming to enhance the overall learning
             experience for students and strengthen their generic skills, thereby fostering a more
             comprehensive educational environment.
       9.1.4 To better support teachers’ efforts, targeted professional development programmes
             focused on promoting learning effectiveness and catering to learning diversity are
             promoted. In 2025/26, AI technology will continue to be integrated into the computer
             curriculum and will be applied across other subjects to enhance students’ self-directed
             learning. Through professional development programmes and strategies, teachers will be
             empowered to explore and implement innovative approaches.


   9.2 Major Concern 2: To nurture students’ positive values and attitudes for the development
       of the sense of citizenship and national identity
       9.2.1 Our school has made significant strides in promoting the healthy growth of students by
             facilitating the development of healthy lifestyle habits. However, there is a need to further
             enhance the health and well-being of senior students.
       9.2.2 In 2025/26, we are dedicated to helping students build resilience by identifying their
             strengths and maximizing their potential, resulting in notable improvements in
             engagement and overall well-being. We would also deepen junior form students'
             understanding of life’s meaning and strengthen their resilience further through targeted
             programmes and activities.
       9.2.3 While we have made progress in cultivating students’ appreciation for others and
             fostering gratitude, we should focus on fostering a positive mindset among students and
             begin designing a school-based reward programme that nurtures their strengths.


                                                   60
9.3 Major Concern 3: To cultivate a broader spectrum of interests and skills towards reading
    and navigate the world with reading competence
    9.3.1 Most of the tasks in the School Development Plan for Major Concern 3 were completed.
          Positive feedback and comments were given by both teachers and students.
    9.3.2 Efforts have been made to encourage students to develop a wider range of interests and
          skills in reading. To sustain this positive habit, the Reading Fun Pass will be modified to
          include spaces for students to express their thoughts after reading. Reading teachers will
          regularly provide feedback focused on students’ reading experiences, aiming to
          strengthen their reading competence and train them to become lifelong readers.
    9.3.3 In the Stakeholders’ Survey, the findings indicated that students’ motivation and
          engagement in reading can be further developed. To enhance the reading atmosphere,
          both teachers and students should take on more influential and active roles. In addition
          to existing measures, displaying exemplary student work and forming a reading circle to
          facilitate peer book sharing in the newly decorated reading corner are recommended.
