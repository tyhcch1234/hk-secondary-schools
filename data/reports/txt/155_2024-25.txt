 II. OUR SCHOOL
Brief Introduction of the School

Canossa College, the former Canossian Convent Secondary School, was founded by the Canossian Daughters of Charity in 1959. The Canossian sisters left Italy, their
homeland, to begin their missionary work in Hong Kong in 1860. In 1891, on Mr. David Sasson's generous donation of a piece of land of 3,000 sq.ft in Shau Kei Wan,
the Canossian Sisters' convent and a Chinese primary school were built. In 1932, a new convent and a primary school were erected but were occupied by the Japanese and
British soldiers during World War II. In 1951, the Canossian Chinese Primary School was re-opened and the Canossian Convent Secondary School was run in the new four-
storey premises adjacent to the primary section in 1959.

To make way for the development of the Eastern district, both the secondary and primary schools were relocated to the present premises in Quarry Bay in 1984 and renamed
Canossa College and the primary, Canossa School (Hong Kong). Canossa College began to offer an education in the medium of English.

The Incorporated Management Committee (IMC) of Canossa College was set up on 31 August 2013 to replace the School Management Committee.

School Premises and Facilities
The school premises not only won the 1984 Highest Honours Award in Architectural Design for its simplicity, harmonious colour scheme, brightness and spaciousness, but
also provide an ideal learning environment for students. The School Improvement Programme was completed in 2004 with a new wing erected from the parking area by
the main entrance, providing more facilities for students’ learning.

There are now 25 classrooms and 20 special rooms (2 visual arts rooms, a computer room, a STEM room, a multi-media learning centre, 4 laboratories, 5 multi-purpose
rooms, a needlework room, a cookery room, a geography room, a music room, an English Learning Centre and a student activity center). In addition, the school hall, library,
tuck shop, backyard, two multi-purpose ball courts, two covered playgrounds (with air-conditioning system) and the chapel provide ample space for various student activities.

 V. MAJOR CONCERNS
   (ACHIEVEMENTS AND REFLECTION)
Major Concern 1: To foster a healthy lifestyle among students
Achievements

1.1 Students physical health has improved.

      In response to the evaluation of our previous 3-year cycle, the school placed heavy emphasis on promoting students’ physical health in 2024/25. The Physical
       Education department spared no effort in fostering an active sports culture on the campus by organizing various activities and participating in the School Physical
       Fitness Award Scheme. According to APASO (KPM25 Physical Fitness Performance), the percentages of students who achieved Gold level across all forms were
       higher than the Hong Kong school average in 2024/25.

      To encourage students to exercise more often, the Physical Education department offered sports equipment rental service during recess and lunchtime so that students
       could play different sports with their peers in their spare time. Other sports activities, such as badminton and yoga classes as well as inter-house dodgeball
       competitions, were also popular among students. More than 90% of teachers and students reflected in the School Review Survey that they agreed the school had
       provided a wide range of physical activities for students during recess and lunchtime and student participation was keen.

      On the other hand, the school participated in two vaccination schemes, Seasonal Influenza Vaccination School Outreach Programme and Human Papillomavirus
       (HPV) Vaccination Catch-up Programme, to further protect students’ physical health and prevent diseases. Over 500 students were vaccinated against influenza, and
       138 against HPV.

1.2 Students have learnt the importance of mental well-being and stress management skills.

      Apart from students’ physical fitness, the school takes to heart the mental health of students. Different committees spent huge effort equipping students with proper
       channels, skills and strategies to handle stress and emotions. As reflected in APASO, the Q-scores of KPM17 Affective Development (“Positive Affect”, “No Negative
       Affect”, “No Anxiety, Depressive Symptoms”, “Satisfaction (School)”, “Meaning in Life” and “School Atmosphere (Belongingness)”) showed improvement when
       compared to 2023/24. It proved the effectiveness of the strategies currently adopted to strengthen students’ mental health.

      In 2024/25, different committees, namely the Health Education Committee and the Counselling & Guidance Committee, organized various activities for students to
       enhance their awareness of 4Rs (Rest, Relaxation, Resilience and Relationships) in handling emotions / stress. For example, there had been handicraft workshops,
       ranging from making calm bottles to balloon twisting, to allow students to relax while mingling with friends. There were also workshops on mindful eating and stress
       coping for students to explore means to reduce stress in daily life. SWITCH Dance Battle and Lunch Karaoke were well-received among students. According to the
       School Review Survey, 90% of students agreed that they had learnt different skills to navigate their emotions from talks and workshops.




                                                                                     11
      The school worked closely with the Parent-Teacher Association in the Bad Day Campaign, aiming to empower students to face challenges in life with a positive and
       proactive attitude. Parents attended training workshops to understand teenagers’ emotional struggles and acquire basic counselling skills to help their children manage
       stress better. Students were also given opportunities to relax and express emotions in the Bad Day Café and Bad Day Busking experiences.

      Students were also provided with proper channels to express their emotions. Each class was assigned two class teachers who were required to meet their students
       regularly to establish strong teacher-student rapport. The school social workers also conducted regular class visits and many DIY workshops to build relationships
       with students. According to the School Review Survey, about 85% of students agreed that they felt supported and safe to express emotions in school.

1.3 Students enhanced social interactions and interpersonal skills.

      The school organized various house-based, level-based and class-based activities to provide students with opportunities to meet and interact with friends. Examples
       were Athletics Meet, Swimming Gala, School Picnic, Experiential Learning Days, Inter-house Dodgeball Competition, S1 Adventure Training Camp in Lei Yuen
       Mun Park, S2 Inter-class Cheerleading Competition, S3 English Debate Competition and different extra-curricular activities. More than 85% of students stated in the
       School Review Survey that there had been plenty of school activities that allowed them to meet new friends and they enjoyed good relationships with schoolmates
       and teachers.

      The school offered extra support to students with weak social skills. Students with ASD were invited to join a programme from which they acquired social skills,
       such as how to start and maintain conversations as well as how to interpret others’ reactions. Instructors, teachers and parents observed significant progress in the
       participants.

      Career and life planning education in junior forms was strengthened. Activities and workshops that encouraged self-understanding and self-management were
       organized. S3 students attended themed talks on subject selection. Students in general responded positively to the abovementioned and had gained a better
       understanding of their interests, strengths and weaknesses.

1.4 Students have been equipped with proper values and attitudes.

      New teaching materials related to healthy lifestyles were added to the curriculum of Life Education. Different forms were assigned up to three topics. Class teachers
       were given guidelines to dedicate at least one Life Education lesson to a topic of healthy lifestyles.

      The Religious Affairs Committee organized prayer assemblies and cell group meetings to support students’ spiritual growth. There were also out-of-campus activities,
       like Caritas Bazaar and Foundress’ Day Celebration at the Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception, to shape students into keen learners with a spiritual mind and
       great empathy for others.

      Adopting the whole-school approach, all classes were responsible for making speeches and leading prayers in the morning assemblies under the guidance of teacher
       advisors. More than 85% of students agreed that the prayers they said during the morning assemblies helped them understand how to lead a positive life.

      Different committees, namely the Discipline Committee, the Counselling & Guidance Committee and the Values Education Committee, organized different activities
       and schemes to instill positive values and attitudes into students. Their work would be detailed in the next section (the achievements of major concern 2).

Reflection and follow-up

 According to APASO and School Review Survey, the school succeeded in promoting students’ health awareness and fostering a healthy lifestyle among students. Since
 there is a more active sports culture among students now, the school would consider promoting other aspects of a healthy life such as healthy habits like having a balanced
 diet and a good sleep routine.

 While different committees worked hard on achieving the common goals, most of the students were benefited throughout the year. More coordination among different
 committees would be found in allocating resources and utilizing manpower in the coming year.

Major Concern 2: To nurture students’ positive values and sense of national identity
Achievements

2.1 Essential values and attitudes were instilled into students through class teacher periods, subject lessons and other activities.

       The school fostered students' positive values and attitudes through learning and teaching as well as other learning experiences. Positive values education had been
        organically integrated into the formal and informal curricula, life-wide learning activities as well as services in school or in the community. Aligning with the
        educational theme of 2024/25, the value of gratitude towards God, our country, community, family and self was highlighted among all values. More than 95% of
        students agreed that they had taken actions to help, show gratitude and appreciation to different people. Reciprocally, they had received appreciation from teachers
        and schoolmates, which helped boost their confidence.

       Apart from the value of gratitude, students were also taught other positive values in their lessons. Subject panels revised their schemes of work and teachers elaborated
        on different values to students whenever they saw suitable. 21 positive values (including 12 positive values advocated by the EDB, Catholic core values and Canossian
        values) were repeatedly mentioned in formal lessons of different subjects, accounting for 1553 lessons in the first term and 1361 in the second term.

       The school implemented values education in a whole-school approach. Different committees and departments worked tirelessly to educate our students to be young
        people with good morals. Activities and programmes, such as essay contests, cultural drama, interactive reading workshops, inter-class ICAC quizzes, inter-class
        cleanliness and energy-saving competition, talks and workshops on the topic of media and information literacy, sex education workshops, Discipline Prefect Board
        and Foster Prefect Scheme, were held for different forms throughout the year to strengthen students’ positive values. Overall speaking, students responded positively
        to all the activities. More than 70% of students admitted that these activities had broadened their horizon and improved their awareness of individual strengths and
        weaknesses. The values that students believed to be the most practicable in life included respecting others (66%), sense of responsibility (61%), and perseverance
        (52%). Additionally, according to APASO, our Q-scores in the KPM17 subscales “Honesty” and “Morality” remained above 100 in the year 2024/25. Such data
        indicated the success of values education in the school.

       To further enhance students’ service mindset, numerous opportunities were provided for students to serve their families, school and society with humility and
        compassion. On one hand, all students were members of Community Youth Club. They participated in visits to elderly centers, beach clean-ups and flag selling for
        non-governmental organizations. On the other hand, students were encouraged to take up at least one service role in their own class, being a subject monitress or a
        class committee member. Students showed strong initiative in serving schoolmates by taking up the roles of student leaders, such as Student Council, House
        Committees and Discipline Prefect Board. Talented students were recruited into a team for public speaking and leadership training. They also served as masters of
        ceremonies and ambassadors in different school events.

       The school implemented a “Service Project” to encourage students to utilize their talents and skills to serve in school and in the community. Students who completed
        a certain amount of service hours could nominate themselves for the Outstanding Service Award. In the Outstanding Service Award interviews, students reflected on
        their community service experiences. Both the interviewees and interviewers were amazed at their achievements and how the experiences had inspired them to
        become better people. In the school year 2024/25, 10 students received a certificate for the Service Project while 6 students were awarded the Outstanding Service
        Award. The project could motivate students to make meaningful contributions and enhance their sense of belonging to the school and society.




                                                                                        14
      The S.H.I.N.E. Character Formation Scheme was launched in 2024/25 to reinforce positive values and commendable behaviors among students. Students were
       encouraged to strive for self-improvement continuously by completing simple tasks related to different values. Parents were also invited to sign in the record to get
       involved in their children's learning. More than 65% of students actively participated in S.H.I.N.E.

      Apart from the activities listed above, the Discipline Committee was very dedicated to upholding the school rules as well as guiding students to make sober
       judgements. There was a very comprehensive policy regarding how to handle students’ misbehaviors and the team worked closely with class teachers and the
       Counseling & Guidance Committee for the best interest of our students.

      To strengthen positive values among students through the acquisition of life planning skills, different workshops were held for different forms by the Career Guidance
       Committee. Students were equipped to make informed career decisions that resonate with their interests, skills and aspirations. Students benefited from the sharing
       of the guest speakers while parents were provided with essential information to facilitate their communication with their daughters regarding their life planning,
       which might help students develop positive values and attitudes towards life. Overall speaking, most students who took part in these programmes agreed that the
       activities effectively enhanced their understanding of potential career paths. Participants were positive about the activities and reportedly said that they felt more in
       control of their future. More than 80% of respondents indicated in the evaluation that the workshops were useful for them.

2.2 Students’ sense of national identity has been strengthened and they show respect for our country.

      According to APASO, our Q-scores in the KPM17 subscales “National Identity (Responsibility, Obligations)”, “National Identity (Proud, Love)”, “National Identity
       (National Flag, anthem)” and “National Identity (Achievements)” remained very high as compared to Hong Kong school average. This proved the effectiveness of
       national education in our school.

      Led by the Principal, our school strategically implemented national education (including national security education) in a whole-school approach. The National
       Security Education Committee was set up to oversee the planning and implementation of national security education in the school. Different departments and
       committees integrated concepts of national education into schemes of work and year plans, all of which were reviewed by the National Security Education Committee
       to formulate the school-based curriculum framework for National Security Education to ensure its holistic implementation in the school. In addition, teachers attended
       multiple workshops and sharing sessions to exchange ideas and enhance their capabilities to conduct national education in an efficient manner. In 2024/25, there
       were 1882 lessons covering the major fields of national security education specified by the EDB.

      The school adopted a multi-pronged and coordinated approach to nurture students’ sense of national identity. Students were recruited to be flag guards to host the
       weekly flag raising ceremony. All teachers and students were invited to give speeches under the national flag. Students were recruited to take part in the Constitution
       and Basic Law Student Ambassador Training Scheme. Meanwhile, different challenge tournaments relevant to important historical events or Constitution and Basic
       Law as well as workshops on traditional handicrafts and intangible Chinese cultural heritage were held in 2024/25. Such activities successfully enhanced schoolmates
       and staff’s understanding of our country’s history, culture, heritage and recent development. According to the School Review Survey, students generally agreed that
       interactive activities were more effective in deepening their understanding of national security education.

      Most notably, the National Security Education Committee and the Integrated Science Department jointly organized a National Outer-Space Safety Education
       Exhibition. Featuring display boards and VR experiences, the event helped students gain a deeper understanding of China’s achievements in aerospace technology
       development, as well as the challenges facing national space security. Designed to strengthen their sense of national identity and responsibility, the activity yielded
       notable outcomes: over 90% of students admitted that the activities could enhance their understanding of the importance of safeguarding national outer-space security.
                                                                                       15
      To immerse students in authentic cultural experiences and facilitate meaningful exchanges with mainland peers, students were encouraged to join study tours to gain
       first-hand experience and be inspired by the history, culture and development of China. Six study tours, including F.5 CSD Study Tour and F.3 Historical and Cultural
       Study Tour to Jiangmen, took place in different cities in the mainland and more than 300 students participated in different tours in 2024/25. These study tours achieved
       significant results in enhancing students' sense of national identity and shaping their positive values. Students were marveled at China’s achievements in different
       areas and gained pride in their national identity. According to the School Review Survey, more than 95% of students developed heartfelt gratitude for the country
       and understood their role in upholding national security.

Reflection and follow-up

      Values Education and National Security Education in the school are believed to be successful. Students’ sense of national identity has been strengthened and they
       possess confidence and a positive life outlook. With essential values instilled in them, students are self-disciplined, rule-abiding, hard-working, respectful, and
       courteous to others, as reflected in APASO. Students show enthusiasm in school activities and service opportunities. They acknowledge their civic obligation and
       national identity.

      Student participation in community service has declined compared to previous years, which points to the need to organize more community service activities to help
       students understand our society and make contribution to the community. In 2025/26, service learning will be strengthened, enhancing students’ social awareness,
       empowering them to contribute to the community and allowing them to practise benevolence via social service.

      According to the School Review Survey, only 66.8% of students actively participated in the S.H.I.N.E Character Formation Scheme, students’ initiative in this
       rewards program should be further strengthened. To encourage student participation, the scheme will be revised next year to attain its goals of promoting positive
       values, continuous self-improvement and a culture of appreciation.

Major Concern 3: To nurture active learners through various teaching strategies
Achievements

3.1 Interactive learning happened inside and outside classrooms.

      To boost students’ learning motivation, teachers adopted cooperative learning strategies, including Carousal, Think-pair-share and Peer Editing, in their lessons to
       encourage active participation and collaboration among students. For example, students worked in groups in Information and Communication Technology lessons to
       explore solutions in algorithms, AI, and cybersecurity while students tackled higher-order thinking questions in pairs in BAFS lessons. It was observed that students
       demonstrated a higher level of engagement and understanding, which contributed to enhanced critical thinking skills and more satisfactory lesson performance. In
       fact, according to the School Review Survey, over 90% of students expressed appreciation for the collaborative tasks woven into their daily studies. More than 84%
       recognized how teamwork allowed them to draw upon each other’s strengths, creating a more inclusive and effective learning process.

      Teachers also utilized different online resources and platforms to facilitate students’ self-learning. For example, F.2 students participated in the EDB’s Online Science
       Learning Award Scheme, which helped ignite students’ curiosity and enthusiasm for scientific exploration. History and Chinese History teachers leveraged e-
       platforms for collaborative idea-sharing and answer compilation, aiding revision and improving their understanding of subject knowledge more effectively. The
       integration of diverse digital teaching tools further enriched students’ learning experience, ensuring students could access and absorb lesson content with greater ease.
       The wealth of available online resources also provided invaluable support, enabling learners to explore concepts at their own pace.

      Learning was extended beyond classroom, and students were given the opportunities to participate in numerous engaging out-of-classroom activities in 2024/25.
       Different departments encouraged students to take part in external competitions, including the HSBC/JA Company Programme, 2025 Hong Kong Financial
       Challenge, and HKICPA Accounting in Action Quiz Competition, ICAS, the Maths Contest for Junior Elites, and Mathematical Olympiad contests. Such experiences
       allowed students to apply their subject knowledge to practical use, enhancing their financial literacy and business acumen as well as mathematical reasoning and
       problem-solving ability. Other subjects also organized visits and field trips to various locations where students could learn about the history, geography and
       biodiversity of Hong Kong. According to the School Review Survey, more than 90% students expressed not only enjoyment but also a meaningful shift in their
       learning approach, becoming more active and engaged participants in their education.

3.2 Students learned through cross-disciplinary collaboration and STEAM activities.

      According to the School Review Survey, over 80% of teachers agreed that synergy in learning and teaching was achieved through cross-disciplinary collaboration.
       For example, the ICT team successfully enhanced students' financial literacy by teaching spreadsheet applications for accounting reports in BAFS. Students
       demonstrated strong analytical skills in verifying financial data accuracy through technological tools, showing significant improvement in their accounting
       competencies. The Chinese-History collaboration for the Quarry Bay research project produced outstanding outcomes, with F.2 students creating professional-quality
       tourist guides and promotional materials. Their work displayed advanced research skills and sophisticated language use, particularly in crafting persuasive content
       for authentic audiences. Different junior subjects also collaborated with the English department to enhance students’ language awareness.

      Several STEAM activities were held in 2024/25, with the most popular one being a forensic science STEAM talk organized by the Chemistry department. The session
       successfully connected theoretical knowledge with practical applications, with many students producing thoughtful reflections on potential career paths.

                                                                                       17
3.3 Learner diversity was catered.

      To cater for learner diversity, teachers adopted diversified strategies to enhance students’ confidence and motivation in learning. Enrichment activities were held to
       stretch students’ potential while remedial classes were conducted to offer additional support to less academically-inclined learners. Simultaneously, subject teachers
       strengthened scaffolding in class, designed tiered assignments to facilitate learning and evaluate students’ learning outcomes with various kinds of assessments.
       According to the School Review Survey, over 90% of students reported that there were various classroom activities in different lessons and they were asked to
       complete different types of assignments that allowed them to showcase creativity. Same percentage of students agreed that the level of difficulty of school assignments
       was suitable, and teachers helped them learn by assigning challenging yet manageable tasks. In the Stakeholders’ Survey, the scores of both parents’ and students’
       view on teaching slightly rose when compared to 2023/24.

      Blended learning was widely implemented in different subjects to enable students to learn at their own pace. Over 90% of students agreed that blended learning
       helped them learn better as they could revisit the material at their own pace, allowing for consistent review and deeper comprehension.

3.4 Students’ reading habits were improved.

      According to statistic provided by the Library, during the period from September 2024 to May 2025, about 40% of our students read 2-3 books monthly. Students
       also showed strong engagement with HyRead, an eReading platform, reading an impressive total of 143,200 chapters in 2024/25. The high participation rates and
       positive feedback indicated successful cultivation of both academic skills and lifelong learning habits. According to APASO, the Q-scores for KPM17 subscales
       “Reading (Non-assigned materials)” and “Reading (Time for leisure reading)” remained higher than Hong Kong school average in 2024/25, indicating that our
       students possessed good reading habits.

      To promote reading across curriculum, different subject departments contributed reading materials for the morning reading scheme. Students were encouraged to
       read fun pieces related to different topics to broaden their horizon.

      All students were required to give a book sharing presentation once a term to recommend interesting books to their classmates.

      Different subject departments collaborated with the Library and organized themed book fairs with optional follow-up activities throughout the year. For example,
       there was an ICT book fair on the topic of cybersecurity. The accompanying online quiz and book voting activity achieved high participation rates, with learners
       actively expanding their technological knowledge while developing critical digital literacy skills.




                                                                                      18
Reflection and follow-up

      A recent survey revealed that 78% of students borrowed books from the school library during the academic year. Many also attended book fairs hosted by the library,
       where they discovered engaging and educational content. Yet despite the efforts by the library and subject panels to promote reading, half of the respondents admitted
       to spending less than two hours per week on leisure reading. This highlighted a concerning trend: even with teachers actively encouraging reading, students are
       dedicating less time to it.

      The recent school survey revealed that while many students participated in the book fair, they did not perceive their involvement as significant. To address this issue,
       subject teachers would be encouraged to schedule visits with the Library when book fairs, of which the theme is relevant to the subject, are being held. Incorporating
       interactive activities, such as author talks and workshops, could further enhance their experience and recognition of their involvement. Finally, strengthening the
       feedback mechanism during the event would allow teachers to better understand student perceptions and make necessary adjustments. By taking these steps, it is
       hoped that students' awareness of their participation would be enhanced and a greater appreciation for reading and literature would be fostered within our school
       community.

VIII. FUTURE PLANNING 2025-2026
Our school believes that health comes first. We are delighted to see our students become more health-conscious, both physically and mentally, throughout
the 2024/25 school year. In the coming year, we will continue to explore ways to encourage students to maintain healthy habits, strengthening their body
and mind for their busy school life. Current practices deemed effective will continue, while several new measures will be introduced. A refined timetable
will be implemented to allow for a longer recess, providing students with more time for rest. We will also engage parents to help students form good
daily habits through the S.H.I.N.E Formation Scheme.


Character formation remains a cornerstone of our work. Following the example of our Foundress, we nurture our students to become humble, respectful,
kind, and loving young women. They learn to embody a diverse range of positive values and live them out in their daily lives. It is also evident that
students take great pride in being Chinese nationals. The school remains steadfast in its commitment to values education, shaping students into grateful,
responsible, and loyal servant-leaders. In 2025/26, we will strengthen both values education and national security education by developing school-based
programmes.


The pursuit of knowledge is essential. Teachers have successfully improved students’ learning motivation and self-learning skills through various
cooperative learning strategies and blended learning. Cross-disciplinary projects and out-of-classroom activities have effectively aroused students’
interest and enhanced their skills and knowledge. In 2025/26, extensive and cross-curricular reading will be widely promoted through various schemes
and programmes to foster a vibrant reading culture among students.
