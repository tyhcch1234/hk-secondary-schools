Our School
The school is one of the 32 Sheng Kung Hui secondary schools in Hong Kong and is named after Bishop
Mok Sau Tseng. Founded in 1975, it has a history of 50 years and is a co-educational secondary school.


Mission
Our mission is to provide the best opportunities for the formation of Christian and humanistic values, to
foster constructive attitudes and develop a range of skills in order that our students can develop their
potential to participate fully in society and become a responsible citizen of the world.

We would endeavour to provide a caring and loving environment where everyone is regarded with mutual
trust, respect and love to make the years we spend in MST a most nurturing and happy experience.

Evaluation of Annual School Plan 2025 – 26
Major concern 1: To nurture students with in-depth learning skills for life-long learning in the era of AI
Intended Outcome (1): To strengthen students’ foundation of learning habits and skills so that they can
                      establish diverse and individualized learning methods in the era of AI
Achievements:
﹣    AI Tools Widely Introduced & Used Across Departments: Various departments successfully introduced AI
     tools to students. For example, Maths (Deepseek, MathGPTPro), English (Penso, Wiseman OWL), with
     significant student engagement. Many students applied AI and design thinking in practical projects
﹣    Evidence of Improved Teacher Practices in Fostering Learning Skills: Across departments, teachers are
     actively cultivating learning habits. English implemented Lift-off classes and small-group tutorials, Maths
     conducted student demonstration sessions, and many departments guided students to create error books and pre-
     lesson materials.

Reflection:
﹣    Significant Implementation Challenges Due to Time Constraints: Several departments reported that despite
     planning, they were unable to implement new strategies due to curriculum time constraints and other school
     events, highlighting a need for more strategic planning.
﹣    More guidance and deeper application of students’ AI Usage: While engagement is high, the depth of AI use
     is a concern. Some teachers reported that only a small proportion of students used AI for self-practice, and
     History teachers noted AI-examples are not always syllabus-relevant, indicating students need more guidance
     on critical and deeper application.

Feedback and Follow-up:
﹣    Strengthen Guidance on AI Literacy & Ethical Use: Departments will focus on developing structured
     activities that explicitly teach students to critically evaluate AI outputs, consider ethical use (copyright,
     integrity), and move beyond basic use to deeper analytical application.
﹣    Adjust for Time Constraints & Provide More Structured Support: To address curriculum time limits, some
     departments plan to refine project designs with clearer guidelines and rubrics, like the error book and design-
     thinking templates, to ensure strategies are feasible and effectively integrated.
﹣    Strengthen Teacher Training: The IT and Staff Professional Development Committees recommend more
     teacher training on utilizing AI effectively to bridge the gap between awareness and classroom mastery.



Intended Outcome (2): To encourage students to apply the 5Cs (critical thinking, creativity,
                      communication, collaboration and current issues) in their learning
Achievements:
﹣    Extensive Integration of 5Cs into Curricula and Activities: Many departments successfully embedded 5Cs
     elements into lessons and projects. Examples include the S2 PSHE cross-curricular project, S3 Joint Science
     STEAM project, game booths and activities on English Enhancement Day, group discussions, presentations, and
     external competitions.
﹣    Positive Student Outcomes: Surveys and teacher observations indicate that students demonstrated enhanced
     creativity, collaboration, and critical thinking in projects. The S2 PSHE project survey showed 72.4%
     agreement that 5Cs were improved
﹣    Notable Achievements in external competitions: Student success in external competitions confirms students
     effectively applied their 5C competencies in authentic contexts.



                                                                                                                     P.7
Reflection:
﹣    Student Engagement in Collaboration and Current Issues is Inconsistent: While many group projects were
     successful, some teacher reports indicate that a significant number of students remain passive or lack awareness
     of current issues.
﹣    Imbalance development of 5Cs: Students performed better in critical thinking and communication, but
     collaboration and application of current issues were weaker.

Feedback and Follow-up:
﹣    Strengthen Current Issues: The school will put greater emphasis on developing students’ skills in applying
     current issues to their learning across all subjects in the next academic year.
﹣    Refine Assessment: Several departments will continue to refine rubrics and project structures to better evaluate
     specific 5C components and ensure balanced development.



Intended Outcome (3): To enhance students’ research methods and design thinking skills in STEAM
                      education
Achievements:
﹣    Integration of Design Thinking into Curricular Projects: Departments successfully embedded the five-step
     design thinking cycle into projects, such as the S3 Joint Science STEAM project (Physics, Chemistry) and S1
     AI teachable machine (STEAM Team), helping students structure their problem-solving process.
﹣    Hands-on Application with STEAM Equipment: Students gained practical experience using STEAM tools
     like 3D printers, Micro:bit, food printers, and drones to prototype and test their designs, which enhanced their
     design thinking and practical research skills.
﹣    Strong Participation and Success in External STEAM Competitions: Many students participated and
     achieved recognition in competitions (e.g., Talents Cup, STEM Racing, AI Generation Creation, HK Physics
     Olympiad), demonstrating their ability to apply research methods and iterative design in real-world challenges.

Reflection:
﹣    Varied Depth in Research Skills Development: The depth of research methods (e.g., documentation, source
     evaluation, iterative refinement) was uneven. While students could complete the practical steps, they often
     struggled to clearly articulate their design choices and apply fair testing principles, showing a gap between
     doing and understanding.

Feedback and Follow-up:
﹣    Continue Successful Practices: Most STEAM-related departments will maintain their STEAM projects and
     design thinking integration as routine work, with ongoing refinements to rubrics and student guidance.
     Departments will create more explicit guidelines, note-taking templates, and rubrics (e.g., for S3 STEAM
     projects) to help students systematically document their design thinking process and explain their decisions. To
     ensure consistency, departments will share best practices and refine STEAM projects collaboratively, with a
     focus on teaching research ethics, fair testing, and iterative prototyping.
﹣    Continue and Carefully Select External Opportunities: While competitions and workshops are effective,
     with an expected reduction in financial resources next year, we need to prioritize high-quality events to
     maximize student learning while maintaining broad participation.

Major Concern 2: To cultivate a supportive and growth-focused learning environment that nurtures
                 students' well-being
Intended Outcome (1): To nurture students' Christian value of patience (忍耐)

Achievements:

﹣    Multi‑channel delivery with positive feedback: The Religious and Evangelistic Affairs Committee held 19
     morning broadcasts, eight on patience; Christian fellowships and the Boys’ Brigade explored the theme. The RE
     department incorporated patience-related biblical figures and topics across all levels, over 80% of students
     agreed RE lessons deepened their understanding of patience. Visual Arts used Chinese calligraphy on patience
     quotes to cultivate perseverance through repeated practice. The Moral, Civic and National Education Committee
     co-organised the “Youth in Motion” scheme with PE, using 10 fitness sessions to build perseverance; the
     Discipline Committee delivered morning broadcasts on mutual respect and tolerance.
Reflection:

﹣    Internalisation requires time to mature: Minutes of form meetings and various committees reported that most
     students and teachers found the dedicated lessons, morning broadcasts, and fellowship sessions on “patience”
     relevant and impactful, indicating that students have acquired a better understanding of the value, but deeper
     understanding and personal application take time to develop. Teachers reported that some students lacked
     reverence during assemblies, failing to maintain silence before the events, which reflects their failure to apply
     patience in real-life scenarios.
Feedback and Follow-up:
﹣    From understanding to practice: More emphasis should be placed on behavioural examples that demonstrate
     how the value is applied and, more importantly, how it is incorporated into daily life.


Intended Outcome (2): To empower students to embrace and apply a growth mindset, fostering
                      resilience and continuous improvement in learning

Achievements:
﹣    Succcessful programmes in cultivalting the concept: The "Week of Resilience" featured a guest speaker
     sharing adversity experiences. Student observations confirmed most participants gained greater awareness of
     applying a growth mindset to overcome challenges. Over 90% of peer mentors in the MST Mentor programme
     agreed it enhanced their confidence and communication; both mentors and juniors learned from setbacks and
     improved performance in subsequent tasks
﹣    Growth mindset embedded in student records: The I-File record was revised with growth mindset concepts;
     two students shared video testimonials on applying these principles, and most successfully set goals and
     conducted self-reflection.
Reflection:
﹣    Students struggle with self-reflection and feedback: Teacher survey data showed a slight decline in providing
     timely, specific feedback, while students' ability to reflect on their learning remained static, indicating
     continuous improvement skills need stronger scaffolding.
﹣    One-off activities fail to create lasting habits: While students acknowledged learning from failure, translating
     this understanding into consistent academic or behavioural resilience remains difficult, as single events often
     lack long-term follow-up. Observational feedback reveals many students still lack sustained resilience,
     suggesting that simply introducing growth mindset concepts is insufficient without consistent, experiential
     reinforcement throughout the year.

                                                                                                                P.9
Feedback and Follow-up:
﹣    Enhance feedback and error-analysis guidance: Teachers should provide more concrete examples of diverse
     learning strategies and strengthen error-analysis practices, helping students turn feedback into actionable steps
     for academic improvement.
﹣    Continue refining the I-File and explore new angles: MCNE will keep embedding growth mindset prompts in
     the I-File and plans to shift resilience themes (e.g., “self-appreciation”) to broaden students’ understanding of
     overcoming adversity.

﹣    Sustain successful programmes with experiential follow-ups: The S1 adaptation programme and Week of
     Resilience should continue, supplemented by more experiential workshops and regular sharing sessions to
     bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and daily application



Intended Outcome 3:        To enhance student wellbeing by implementing sustainable care practices that
                           support their physical, mental and social development
Achievements:
﹣    Reduced academic stress via workload adjustments: Assignment and test schedules in junior forms and SBA
     deadlines in senior forms were reviewed and adjusted. KPM 17 data showed significant improvement, with
     anxiety Q-values rising from 86 to 95 and negative emotions from 88 to 104
﹣    Established multi-layered mental health support: The “JC LevelMind” project formed a student team and
     mental health ambassadors, conducted 3 school-wide mental health activities, and engaged the whole school in
     designing a rest room to promote relaxation.
﹣    Strengthened peer support and class cohesion: Across all forms, class-based bonding activities (e.g., Kahoot,
     sports, board games) effectively fostered mutual support, unity, and a positive social environment (all Form
     Committee minutes).

Reflection:
﹣    After-school attendance remains a barrier: Hiking events had 16 sign-ups but only 8 attended; social skills
     training saw poor completion rates as most students could not fully attend 5 sessions due to conflicting after-
     school commitments.

﹣    Physical rest room implementation is still pending: Although the“JC LevelMind” rest room design went
     through extensive co-creation workshops and a school-wide vote, the physical space has not yet become an
     operational, daily resource for students
Feedback and Follow-up:
﹣    Reschedule support programmes to boost participation: To address low attendance, Guidance Team will
     explore alternative timings (e.g., during school hours or lunch) for social skills groups and hiking activities to
     better accommodate students’ busy schedules
﹣    Finalize and operationalize wellbeing facilities: The Guidance Committee will continue refining the student-
     designed relaxation room and ensure ongoing training for Wellbeing Ambassadors to sustain mental health
     promotion.
﹣    Maintain academic load monitoring: The Academic Committee will continue using KPM data and review
     test/assignment schedules proactively to prevent undue stress across all year levels.

Whole Person Development
Our school emphasizes on whole person development of each students. Training programmes and
activities are organized to assist positive development in the following aspects:


Religious and Evangelical Affairs

       The Religious and Evangelical Affairs Committee strives to nurture students’ positive outlook on
life, deepen their understanding of Christianity, and encourage them to live out their faith in daily
practice.

•    Throughout the year, religious morning broadcasts and praise and prayer sessions were conducted
     during assemblies by Christian teachers, with the generous support of Revd. Tony Au Yeung Siu Kei
     and Mr Oliver Chin Kan Yuen from HKSKH the Church of Our Saviour. These activities broadened
     students’ spiritual horizons and strengthened their connection with the Christian faith.
•    At the school’s major services, the school community was uplifted by inspiring messages delivered
     by guest speakers — Revd. Cheung Tsz Yin at the Christmas Service and Revd. Ip Tsz Leung at the
     Easter Service.
•    A highlight of the year was Gospel Week (January 2026), themed “Cherished Peace” (珍平安). The
     week began with an alumna’s heartfelt testimony during a morning broadcast. Christian hymns and
     praise songs were shared through song dedication sessions, while Christian Fellowship and BB260
     organized a foyer game booth to raise awareness of students’ intrapersonal and spiritual well-being.
     Lunchtime hymn sharing by teachers and students created a warm atmosphere of worship, and the
     whole-school assembly was uplifted by the presence of Amazing Grace Worship (基恩敬拜).
•    In addition to Gospel Week, Christian Fellowship gatherings provided regular opportunities for
     students to deepen their understanding of the Christian faith.
•    Boys’ Brigade activities were held to foster discipline, responsibility, and Christian values among
     participants, further enriching their personal growth.



Moral, Civic & National Education

    The Moral, Civic & National Education Committee aims at promoting values education, civic
education and national education.

   Values Education is an essential element of whole-person education which aims at fostering students'
    positive values and attitudes through the learning and teaching of various Key Learning Areas or
    subjects and the provision of relevant learning experiences. The Committee upholds the belief that
    "Everyone should have empathy". Cultivating care and empathy involves understanding and meeting
    the needs of others with concern and love. By promoting care and empathy in students, they can
    develop a friendly and caring attitude towards family, teachers, and friends. This can extend to caring
    for the welfare of vulnerable communities, even to loving and protecting all living things, and
    practicing "treating others with kindness and loving all creatures". This can help prevent and
    eliminate bullying and animal cruelty incidents, and build a caring campus and harmonious society.

    An "MST Voluntary Service Team" programme was conducted for all S.4 and S.5 classes to serve
    different groups of needy people. Students gained valuable experience and developed positive values
    related to caring for others and dedication to the community.
   The "Active Talent" programme was co-organized with the PE Department again this academic year.
    This programme used diversified physical activities as an intervention point, aiming to enhance the
    self-image of teenagers, boost their confidence, and encourage social connections. A Wing Chun
    instructor was invited to conduct a Wing Chun lesson for students, teaching students a basic form of
    Wing Chun. Besides, a Chinese Tea Art Workshop was organized, teaching students basic tea
    knowledge and etiquette. Students later set up a booth at the school’s Cultural Fair to share this
    knowledge with peers. Finally, the programme concluded with a two-day adventure-based training
    camp, where participants engaged in coach-led activities to cultivate perseverance, teamwork, and
    broader perspectives.
   In response to the need to cultivate students' positive values and capacity conducive to their whole
    person development, one form period on having a positive attitude was organized for all forms to
    empower them to overcome challenges and failures with courage and perseverance. Moreover, one
    form period was organized for S.1 to S.3 students to alert students to the unhealthy elements in our

    study tour experiences and volunteer service reflections, promoting a deeper connection with our
    motherland. This initiative aims to inspire students to actively participate in diverse activities and
    cultivate a sense of responsibility to contribute to society and our country in the future. Moreover,
    our students continued to participate in the "Constitution and Basic Law Student Ambassadors
    Training Scheme". Students were relatively keen to participate in activities related to China's

Counselling and Guidance

    The Counselling and Guidance Committee aims to promote students’ personal growth and holistic
development.

JC LevelMind@School Programme

•   The school joined the JC LevelMind@School Programme to enhance students’ mental well-being
    across campus. Through this initiative, we established a Wellness Club and provided Mental
    Wellness Self-Management Training for both students and teachers. These efforts helped fostered
    a caring and supportive school environment, encouraging students to develop self-awareness and
    effective stress management skills.

Peer Mentoring Scheme
