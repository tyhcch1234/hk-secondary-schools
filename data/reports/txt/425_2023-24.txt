  1.2 School Type

           Our school is a government subsidized whole-day EMI co-educational secondary school,
       established by the Yuen Long Merchants Association in September 1978. There were eight S.1
       classes in the first year, and gradually increased to 30 classes in September 1984, with six classes in
       S.1, S.2 and S.3, four classes in S.4 and S.5, and two classes in S.6 and S.7. From September 1998
                                                                                                            3
     to July 2004, the school ran 29 classes from S.1 to S.7, with five classes in each level from S.1 to
     S.5, and two classes in S.6 and S.7. From September 2004 to July 2009, the total number of classes
     ran up to 30 again as the Education Bureau allocated one more class to our school as a one-off plan
     to cater for the excessive number of S.1 students in Yuen Long. In 2009-2010 and 2010-2011, there
     were altogether 29 classes from S.1 to S.7, with five classes in each level from S.1 to S.5 and two
     classes each in S.6 and S.7. In 2011-2012, under the new senior secondary curriculum, there were
     30 classes from S.1 to S.6, with five classes in each level from S.1 to S.5 and 2 classes in S.7.
     Starting from 2012-2013, there have been 30 classes from S.1 to S.6, with five classes in each level.

3. Our Learning and Teaching
  3.1    Learning and teaching

         I. Self-regulated Skills & Study Skills
             The implementation of self-regulated learning varies across different forms. Students have
        been equipped with general study skills such as note-taking, self-reflection, and time management.
        S.1 students are encouraged to jot down notes and organize their learning content using appropriate
        graphic organizers. S.3 students are taught how to plan their revision schedule before assessments.
        S.4 students learn how to bridge the learning gaps between junior and senior forms.


             Additionally, each department has developed subject-specific study skills that are integrated
        into their respective curricula. High-order thinking skills and presentation skills are developed
        through project-based learning activities. Different KLAs provide sufficient opportunities for
        students to practice study skills, particularly note-taking, self-reflection, and revision skills, across
        different subjects to enhance their quality. Spiral and vertical planning should be considered for both
        study skills and high-order thinking skills to ensure a comprehensive and cohesive approach.

         II. Cater for Learning Diversity

               The Gifted Education Team has established a comprehensive data bank to enhance students'
         experiences through participation in competitions, external courses, and awards schemes. The data
         bank now covers a broader range of aspects related to gifted education. The committee has
         successfully nominated several students to participate in prestigious gifted programs organized by
         HKAGE and various universities. Notable achievements include 12 students being accepted as
         new members of HKAGE, the HKU Academy for the Talented English Programme, and the
         CUHK Summer Programme for the Gifted and Talented.


               In S.1-S.2 examination papers, optional bonus questions (up to 5% of the full mark) were
         introduced to meet the needs of the top 10% of students, who appreciated the additional challenge.
         More demanding tasks have also been integrated into lessons.


               To address student diversity, the school flexibly allocates various school grants and resources.
         Consolidation classes and learning schemes were arranged for various subjects are arranged, which
         bolsters the learning habits and organizational skills of academically challenged students.

III. Assessment & Professional Development
      Assessment serves various purposes within our school. With a strong focus on assessment for
learning, teachers utilize formative assessment to gauge students' knowledge and skills. Assessment as
learning is also employed, which encourages students to assess their own learning, reflect on their
progress, and make necessary adjustments. To promote Assessment as Learning, senior form students
are issued report cards with predicted grades and challenge grades. This practice is well-received by
both teachers and students, as it provides teachers with stronger evidence to prepare senior form students
for the HKDSE and guides students in their academic progress. It also exemplifies the use of big data to
enhance learning and teaching. The correlation between predicted grades and actual DSE results will be
further studied in the upcoming school year.


      Formative assessments play a crucial role in informing teachers about students' understanding of
the material covered in class. The variation in teaching progress is discussed during form meetings,
providing opportunities for teachers to collaborate and share effective strategies. Professional sharing
sessions in staff meetings and department meetings encourage teachers to discuss not only teaching
progress but also refine their pedagogies based on their collective insights. Post-lesson peer-observation
forms are utilized to facilitate constructive discussions among teachers, helping them identify strengths
and areas for improvement in their teaching practices.


      Recognizing that teachers' professional knowledge is invaluable, YLMASS prioritizes staff
development by offering a wide range of training opportunities, including internal and external courses,
lesson observations, and book inspections. Supporting measures are in place to encourage engagement
in professional exchange and development. For instance, external support from organizations like EDB
and QSIP is provided to promote professional development in different subjects.

IV.    e-Learning
      The emergence of information technology has brought about a significant shift in teaching and
learning methods. YLMASS is committed to harnessing the advantages of e-learning for the benefit of
its students. Students have been well-prepared for blended learning, and with the strong support of the
ITED Committee, teachers have developed competence in this area. All teachers have received
comprehensive training in using Microsoft Teams to conduct various learning and teaching activities.
They are proficient in blended mode of teaching.


      E-learning has been further enhanced through the implementation of the Bring Your Own Device
(BYOD) Pilot Programme, which began in 2021. Students have been able to utilize tablets or notebooks
as daily learning tools both inside and outside the classrooms. The integration of such technology into
the curriculum has created an interactive learning and teaching environment for teachers and students.
In addition to accessing notes and webpages, using educational apps, students can also engage in
activities such as creating mind maps, participating in instant feedback polls, and attempting short
questions and quizzes, making their learning more engaging and productive.

V. STEAM Education

       Apart from developing skills and consolidating knowledge in each individual subject, STEAM
education aims at developing students’ ability to work across disciplines through authentic situations.
STEM learning is experienced in various ways, inspiring students to acquire different skills and
capabilities: (1) problem solving and risk assessment; (2) interpreting and analyzing data and
information; (3) collaboration and working across different fields and disciplines; (4) research and
critical enquiry – to develop and test ideas; (5) creativity and innovation – to develop new products,
approaches and skills. The curriculum mapping among Services Learning & Values Education,
Computer Literacy, Home Economics and Design and Technology subjects has been completed in this
school year. This curriculum enables students to become familiar with the way that STEM skills and
knowledge are used in authentic situation and learn how to tackle real-life problems.


VI.     Reading across curriculum

       During the Morning Reading Period on Tuesdays and Fridays, an excerpt reading initiative has
been introduced to cultivate students' reading habits. This initiative offers a diverse selection of engaging
reading materials on various subjects, themes, and topics. Curated by different subjects and functional
groups, these materials enrich students' knowledge with accompanying guiding questions. To facilitate
morning book sharing sessions, teachers and students collaboratively prepare content that is later shared
through Teams podcasts. In the coming school year, an increased number of students will be encouraged
to share their book reviews, fostering a vibrant reading culture.


       The school library plays a pivotal role in acquiring and promoting new books regularly, with a
focus on value education and inspirational content. These books aim to instill positive attributes such as
diligence and perseverance in students.


       To spark students' interest in reading, they are motivated to explore materials spanning different
subjects, themes, and topics. An enriching book fair was organized this academic year, allowing students
to purchase books of their choice. The visit to the book fair was met with enthusiasm by the students,
who provided positive feedback. Looking ahead, plans are underway to strengthen collaboration among
various subjects to organize cross-curricular reading projects in the upcoming year.


VII.     Language across curriculum

       To support S.1 students in acclimating to an English-speaking environment, improving their
English language proficiency, and nurturing their interest in English-related subjects, a bridging program
was introduced as part of the summer bridging course. This program included lessons and activities
designed to familiarize students with an English Medium of Instruction (EMI) environment. The students
thoroughly enjoyed these activities and found them engaging.

  organized during lunch breaks, recess, or after school.
