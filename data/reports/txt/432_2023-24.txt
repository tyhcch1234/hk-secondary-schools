School Vision & Mission

To
        provide a holistic education;
        nurture people of excellence; and
        promulgate the Truth and lead students to an abundant life.




Our School

CNEC Christian College is a subsidised EMI school. Being a Christian school founded in 1973, it does
not only uphold the spirit of serving the community, but also practises its belief that young people
are to be educated and developed morally, cognitively, physically, socially, aesthetically and
spiritually if an all-round education is to be achieved. Consequently, CNEC Christian College
provides students with an environment whereby their academic performance can be improved,
their moral character can be edified and their interpersonal skills can be enhanced. Then, in
doing so, the school hopes that students will be inspired to know the Truth and lead a life of
excellence.


School Campus

Our school, with a floor area 100,000 square feet, comprises three teaching blocks with lift tower
installed, a hall, two covered playgrounds, two basketball courts, four volleyball courts, two
gardens and a five-lane running track.

Major Concerns 2024-2025 (Strategies, Success Criteria, Actual Progress & Follow-up Suggestions)
Major Concern 1: Nurturing students to be self-directed learners and catering for their diverse learning needs
Achievements

Area 1: Improve students’ self-directed learning skills through reading and note-taking
 One reading period was implemented into the school timetable for F.1 and F.2 students.
 Reading / activities Across the Curriculum (RaC / AaC) were arranged in all subjects.
 Promoting reading through DEAR periods, reading across the curriculum in all subjects, library activities and reading periods.
 Book sharing by teachers and students have been done throughout the year during the DEAR periods.
 Improve students’ self-directed learning skills and train F1 and F2 students for their note-taking skills by our teachers. Two workshops are arranged in the first term of the
   academic year:
   - F.1 workshop: 26/9/2023
   - F.2 workshop: 3/10/2023
 Encourage students to design their personalized learning method, provide tools and materials for students to be self-directed learners.
Area 2: Cater for the diverse learning needs of students
 In-depth group discussion and provoking questions were incorporated in the lessons. Various means of tasks and assessment to cater for diverse students’ learning needs
   are adopted.
 Formal lesson observations and peer observations with the focus of catering for the diversified learning were arranged in a large scale.
 Peer learning groups among students from the same class or from the same house were established.
 Various learning support programmes for gifted students and low achievers were provided.
 Effective pedagogies or good practices at department level were shared.

Reflection

 All subjects have designed subject-based reading and cross-curriculum reading activities.
 Workshops on note-taking skills for F.1 and F.2 students have been conducted (26/9/2023 and 3/10/2023) successfully. In the evaluation survey, 87.9% of students applied
  the note-taking skills they learned in the workshop. 87.7% of students felt more confident in the ability to take effective notes after attending the workshop.
 The borrowing rate of books in School Library is 18.83 item /day in the school year 2022-23. The rate was 18.66 item / day in the school year 2023-24. E-Book Reading Rate
  is sharply increased from 4679 times in 2022-23 to 29233 times in 2023-24.
 In the Stakeholders’ Survey, 47.2% of the students showed that “I often read materials such as leisure reading materials and newspapers outside class.” and 38.9% of the
  parents showed that “My child likes reading.”.

Feedback and Follow-up

Our school goes through a planning, implementation, and evaluation (PIE) process every year. By evaluating the 2023-24 annual plan, which is the second year of the
Three-year Development Plan (2022-25), follow-up actions are taken in the coming academic year.
 To improve students’ self-directed learning skills through reading and note-taking.
   - Workshops on note-taking skills for F.1 and F.2 students would be conducted by Chinese, English, Science and PSHE teachers.
   - Book sharing of teachers and students during the DEAR periods.
   - Reading period would be implemented into the school timetable for F.2 students.
   - More reading activities and reading promotion from library and academic departments.

Reflection

 The element “Benevolence” was optimised and “Unity”, “Filial Piety” were newly added that the planning of the curriculum must consider all the twelve priority values and
  attitudes. They will be clearly stated in the teaching progress of all subjects.
 Understanding the importance to instil positive values in students and promote mental health, lots of activities or programmes were implemented to promote positive
  values education and mental health that helping students live a purposeful life.
 Through various learning activities related to national education, students have a strong sense of national identity which is the same as last school year.
 With the pandemic having ended, normal school life is gradually allowing students to return to their regular social lives, providing more opportunities for discussions with
  teachers and support from peers. Students’ recognition of benevolence is uplifted as the Q-score of the subscale “School Atmosphere (Not Lonely)” is higher than that of
  last school year.
 Students have a strong sense of morality and are honest. However, they are weak to abide by the law and regulations. Students in general recognize the schoolmates
  should behave well. The findings are similar to the last school year. More efforts should be made to educate students about good discipline.
 Students possess a significant ability to persist in the face of challenges or difficulties in learning.
 Students should be aware of doing more physical exercise, sleep more and have less time played on electronic devices. Meanwhile, they are not addicted to electronic
  devices.
 Students are fear to face failure and may be with anxiety, depressive symptoms.
 Students find meaningful in life and with no negative affect in general.
 Students have normal social behaviours with friends.
 Students’ emotional stability and relationship with same sex improve a lot. However, their self-concept concerning the physical appearance and relationship with opposite
  sex lower, especially the latter. Students need to enhance their self-confidence.

Feedback and Follow-up

 More planning work involving the teaching of the self-discipline of students should be done. The school’s disciplinary management should be effective and appropriate
  reward and punishment measures should be implemented well such that students develop habits of respect. Teachers should strictly follow school regulations to give
  students appropriate rewards and punishments. This is one of the ways to guide students in establishing the correct set of values.
 According to the EDB Circular Memo No. 105/2024, the emphasis on “courtesy” among students should be encouraged to be further strengthened through
  school-based learning activities.
 Continuous efforts should be made to assist students in having healthy lifestyles.

Social Services
  Years                            Details of the participation of Social Services
2022/2023  Three flag-selling activities were organised for all F.3 students throughout the year.
               Six F.3-F.5 students actively participated in a yearly pilot programme named ‘Voices of the
                Children Phase’ organised by Plan International Hong Kong. With the support of our students,
                children in the Shek Lei community were helped voice their views and put forward their
                innovative solutions to issues about transportation and play space.
               Collaborated with Adventure Ship Trainers and The Hong Kong Society of the Blind, Counselling
                Action Group arranged the ’Adventure Ship Buddies Program’ which aimed at providing an
                opportunity to our students and people with special needs to have a positive interaction. The
                programme included a volunteer-service training workshop, an inclusion orientation program
                between our students and the members of The Hong Kong Society of the Blind, an adventure
                ship training day and, the evaluation and sharing session.
               Members of Girl Guides participated in a raffle selling day, two flag hoisting ceremony on 4 May
                and 1 July 2023, and social inclusion with the elderly, through which they learnt to serve the
                community with a sense of responsibility and gratitude.
2023/2024  Counselling Action Group launched Adventure Ship Buddies Programme which provides an
                opportunity for positive interaction between our students and people with special needs via
                the volunteer-service training workshop, inclusion orientation programme between our
                students and the members of The Hong Kong Society of the Blind, adventure ship training day
                and the evaluation and sharing session.
               Seventeen students participated in a volunteer service titled ‘跨代共融-和諧粉彩義工活動’.
                This initiative aimed to foster creativity and connection between generations. At the
                Neighbourhood Elderly Centre, the students engaged the elderly in the soothing and expressive
                art of Pastel Nagomi. The vibrant colours and gentle techniques encouraged participants to
                share stories, fostering a warm and joyful atmosphere. Many elderly participants expressed
                delight in creating art, while students learnt valuable lessons in patience and empathy.
               Sixteen students joined the volunteer service ‘躍動成長路-義工服務日’ at the Heep Hong
                Society Jockey Club STAR Resource Centre. This event was designed to support families with
                children who have ADHD by providing a series of interactive game booths. Participating
                students not only were entertained but also promoted teamwork and social skills among
                participants. The smiles and laughter of the children filled the room, and parents expressed
                gratitude for the opportunity to connect with others facing similar challenges. This experience
                highlighted the importance of community support and understanding.
               Members of Girl Guides participated in three raffle selling days on 3 February, 17 February, 18
                February 2024 respectively; cross-sectional gathering-‘跨代同樂 中式步操初探與美食交織之
                旅’ on 2 March 2024, and the Color Party of National Security Education Day Carnival 2024 on
                14 April 2024. Through these activities, students learnt to serve the community with a sense of
                responsibility and gratitude.
