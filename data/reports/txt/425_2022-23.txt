1. Our School
  1.1 A Brief Introduction

            A ceremony laying the foundation stone of our school building was held on 21st January 1975.
       Classes started in September 1978 when we temporarily used classrooms in the Yuen Long Chamber
       of Commerce No. 2 Primary School, until 1st April 1979 when we moved into the present school
       building. The opening ceremony of the school building was held on 23rd October 1979.

            The School Management Board was established before September 1978. Mr. TANG Tung-
       kwong served as our Founding School Supervisor from September 1978, until the second School
       Supervisor, Mr. CHOI Chong-yip took over in January 1994. In March 1997, Mr. CHENG became the
       third School Supervisor, followed by Mr. TANG Kwok-sun in April 2004 as the fourth School Supervisor.
       From April 2010, Mr. TAI Yiu-wah served as the fifth School Supervisor, and Mr. CHAN Kin-yip
       became the sixth School Supervisor. The present School Supervisor is Mr. WONG Yuen-tai. The
       founding School Principal was Mr. TIU Pui-kwong, who took office from 1978 and Ms NG Lai-wah
       served as the second School Principal from 2002. Ms. IP Wai-ching took over upon her retirement on 1st
       September 2015. The present School Principal is Mr. YAU Chi-leung, who took over on 1st September
       2018.

           The school established the Incorporated Management Committee on 1st April 2011. Led by the
       School Supervisor, the IMC consisted of stakeholders of school managers, including parent, teacher
       and alumni managers.



  1.2 School Type

            Our school is a government subsidized whole-day EMI co-educational secondary school,
       established by the Yuen Long Merchants Association in September 1978. There were eight S.1
       classes in the first year, and gradually increased to 30 classes in September 1984, with six classes in
       S.1, S.2 and S.3, four classes in S.4 and S.5, and two classes in S.6 and S.7. From September 1998 to
       July 2004, the school ran 29 classes from S.1 to S.7, with five classes in each level from S.1 to S.5, and
       two classes in S.6 and S.7. From September 2004 to July 2009, the total number of classes ran up to 30
       again as the Education Bureau allocated one more class to our school as a one-off plan to cater for the
       excessive number of S.1 students in Yuen Long. In 2009-2010 and 2010-2011, there were altogether 29
       classes from S.1 to S.7, with five classes in each level from S.1 to S.5 and two classes each in S.6 and
       S.7. In 2011-2012, under the new senior secondary curriculum, there were 30 classes from S.1 to S.6,
       with five classes in each level from S.1 to S.5 and 2 classes in S.7. Starting from 2012-2013, there have
       been 30 classes from S.1 to S.6, with five classes in each level.

3. Our Learning and Teaching
  3.1    Learning and teaching

         I. Self-regulated Skills & Study Skills
              The implementation of self-regulated learning varies across different forms. Students have
         been equipped with general study skills such as note-taking, self-reflection, and time
         management. S.1 students are encouraged to jot down notes and organize their learning content
         using appropriate graphic organizers. S.3 students are taught how to plan their revision schedule
         before assessments. S.4 students learn how to bridge the learning gaps between junior and senior
         forms.

               Additionally, each department has developed subject-specific study skills that are integrated
         into their respective curricula. However, there is a need to further develop high-order thinking
         skills and presentation skills through project-based learning activities. It is recommended to
                                                                                                                 9
provide more opportunities for students to practice study skills, particularly note-taking, self-
reflection, and revision skills, across different subjects to enhance their quality. Spiral and
vertical planning should be considered for both study skills and high-order thinking skills to
ensure a comprehensive and cohesive approach.

II. Cater for Learning Diversity

     A comprehensive data bank has been established by the Gifted Education Team, enriching
students' experiences through participation in competitions, external courses, and awards schemes.
The data bank now includes a wider range of aspects related to gifted education. The committee
has successfully nominated several students for participation in prestigious gifted programmes
organized by HKAGE and various universities. Notable achievements include 12 students being
accepted as new member of HKAGE, HKU Academy for the Talented English Programme and
CUHK Summer Programme for the Gifted and Talented.


     In S.1-S.2 examination papers, optional bonus questions (max 5% of full mark) were added
to cater to the needs of the top 10% students, who appreciated the additional challenge. More
challenging tasks were also incorporated into lessons. The shooting stars programme will be
continued in the upcoming school year, targeting the top 10 students in senior forms.


     To cater to student diversity, the committee flexibly deploys the Learning Support Grant (LSG)
and other resources. Consolidation classes for various subjects are organized, and the committee
collaborates with the Learning and Teaching (L&T) Committee to implement the Learning Scheme,
which strengthens the learning habits and organizational skills of students with special educational
needs (SEN) and academically challenged students during holidays. In collaboration with a Speech
Therapist, the committee nominated S.1 and S.3 students to serve as Student Ambassadors for the
English Oral Communication Workshop for YLMAPS. These ambassadors actively participated
in the workshop, providing constructive feedback to P6 students, and their performances received
acclaim. The majority of participants agreed that the workshop enhanced their communication
skills.


     To support low achievers, a well-developed Case Management System is in place.
Consolidation classes are conducted after school, where teachers tailor materials to suit the
proficiency of individual students. These classes not only help students improve their learning
habits but also boost their confidence and aspirations. The Catering for Learner Diversity
Committee (SEN) collaborates with professionals such as Educational Psychologists, Clinical
Psychologists, Speech Therapists, and Counselors to provide learning support through case
conferences, student consultations, parent consultations, and Individual Education Plans (IEP).

III. Assessment & Professional Development
      Assessment serves various purposes within our school. With a strong focus on assessment for
learning, teachers utilize formative assessment to gauge students' knowledge and skills. Assessment as
learning is also employed, which encourages students to assess their own learning, reflect on their
progress, and make necessary adjustments. To promote Assessment as Learning, senior form students
are issued report cards with predicted grades and challenge grades. This practice is well-received by
both teachers and students, as it provides teachers with stronger evidence to prepare senior form
students for the HKDSE and guides students in their academic progress. It also exemplifies the use of
big data to enhance learning and teaching. The correlation between predicted grades and actual DSE
results will be further studied in the upcoming school year.


      Formative assessments play a crucial role in informing teachers about students' understanding of
the material covered in class. The variation in teaching progress is discussed during form meetings,
providing opportunities for teachers to collaborate and share effective strategies. Professional sharing
sessions in staff meetings and department meetings encourage teachers to discuss not only teaching
progress but also refine their pedagogies based on their collective insights. Post-lesson peer-
observation forms are utilized to facilitate constructive discussions among teachers, helping them
identify strengths and areas for improvement in their teaching practices.


      Recognizing that teachers' professional knowledge is invaluable, YLMASS prioritizes staff
development by offering a wide range of training opportunities, including internal and external
courses, lesson observations, and book inspections. Supporting measures are in place to encourage
engagement in professional exchange and development. For instance, a common block timetable is
arranged to facilitate professional sharing within subject panels. External support from organizations
like EDB and QSIP is provided to promote professional development in different subjects.

IV.    e-Learning
      The emergence of information technology has brought about a significant shift in teaching and
learning methods. YLMASS is committed to harnessing the advantages of e-learning for the benefit of
its students. Students have been well-prepared for blended learning, and with the strong support of the
ITED Committee, teachers have developed competence in this area. All teachers have received
comprehensive training in using Microsoft Teams to conduct various learning and teaching activities.
They are proficient in blended mode of teaching.


      E-learning has been further enhanced through the implementation of the Bring Your Own Device
(BYOD) Pilot Programme, which began in 2021. The scheme was fully implemented for Form 1 and
Form 2 students last year. Students have been able to utilize tablets or notebooks as daily learning
tools both inside and outside the classrooms. The integration of such technology into the curriculum
has created an interactive learning and teaching environment for teachers and students. In addition to
accessing notes and webpages, using educational apps, students can also engage in activities such as
creating mind maps, participating in instant feedback polls, and attempting short questions and
quizzes, making their learning more engaging and productive. Consequently, an increasing number of
                                                                                                         11
students at YLMASS are benefiting greatly from e-learning. Therefore, the BYOD programme will be
extended to S.3, aiming to further enhance interactions between students and teachers through e-
learning and foster the development of self-regulated learning skills.


V. STEAM Education

      Apart from developing skills and consolidating knowledge in each individual subject, STEAM
education aims at developing students’ ability to work across disciplines through authentic situations.
STEM learning is experienced in various ways, inspiring students to acquire different skills and
capabilities: (1) problem solving and risk assessment; (2) interpreting and analyzing data and
information; (3) collaboration and working across different fields and disciplines; (4) research and
critical enquiry – to develop and test ideas; (5) creativity and innovation – to develop new products,
approaches and skills. The curriculum mapping among Services Learning & Civic Education, Computer
Literacy, Home Economics and Design and Technology subjects has been completed in this school year.
This curriculum enables students to become familiar with the way that STEM skills and knowledge are
used in authentic situation and learn how to tackle real-life problems.


VI.    Reading across curriculum

      During the Morning Reading Period on Fridays, an excerpt reading initiative is implemented to
foster reading habits among students. This initiative provides students with a diverse selection of
appropriate and engaging reading materials on various subjects, themes, and topics. A total of 24 sets
of reading materials were curated by different subjects and functional groups. These materials cover a
wide range of topics, contributing to students' knowledge enrichment, accompanied by guiding
questions. To facilitate the morning book sharing sessions, twenty teachers and two students prepared
content that was later podcasted through Teams. In the upcoming school year, more students will be
encouraged to share their book reviews, further promoting a culture of reading.


      The school library takes an active role in regularly acquiring and promoting new books, with a
focus on value education and inspirational content. These books aim to instill positive attributes such
as diligence and perseverance in students. As part of a reading activity titled "一起悅讀的日子"
organized by the Hong Kong Federation of Education Workers, approximately 14 students,
accompanied by teachers from the Reading Team, visited the Hong Kong Book Fair. The students
utilized subsidies to purchase books of their choice. The visit to the book fair was highly enjoyed by
the students, and their feedback was positive.


      To nurture students' interest in reading, they are encouraged to explore materials across different
subjects, themes, and topics. In late May, a book fair was held, further enriching the reading
environment. Looking ahead, there are plans to enhance collaboration among different subjects in
organizing cross-curricular reading projects in the upcoming year.

       VII.     Language across curriculum

              To assist S.1 students in adapting to an English-speaking environment, enhancing their English
       language skills, and fostering their interest in English-related subjects, a bridging program was
       introduced as part of the summer bridging course. As part of this program, a native English-speaking
       teacher (NET) provided instruction on classroom English, while a Language Across the Curriculum
       (LAC) teacher organized various checkpoints around the campus, creating opportunities for students to
       practice English. The students thoroughly enjoyed these activities and found them engaging.

              To further promote collaboration across the curriculum in junior forms, a specific focus was placed
       on sentence patterns, vocabulary, and grammar within English readers during the previous year. To
       strengthen cooperation among different subjects, it is recommended to conduct a curriculum mapping

       English, it is suggested that a variety of LAC activities be organized during lunch breaks, recess, or after

4.4    Moral & Civic Education
       To cultivate the seven priority values and attitudes among students, a wide variety of cocurricular
       lessons and activities were held to align with the developing school-based MCNE curriculum.
       4.4.1 Flag-raising ceremonies
        The flag-raising ceremonies and the playing and singing of the National Anthem were
            arranged regularly to promote national education and enhance a sense of national identity
            among students and staff.
        All students were taught the appropriate etiquette at the flag-raising ceremonies.
                                                                                                                16
          The learning and teaching materials highlighted the core value of a more robust national
           identity.
          All classes had a personal growth period about the history of the National Constitution.
      4.4.3 National Security Education Week in April 2022
       We delivered a class teacher lesson about national security education to the whole school. We
           also incorporated the National Security Education in the Curriculum of Personal Growth
           Periods. Lessons of cyber security and background of National Security Law were
           incorporated in the curriculum.
      4.4.4 S.1 Inter-class Potted Plants Competition cum Chinese New Year Blessings Design
      Competition
       We organized the competition with PTA involving students, parents, and teachers to let them
           experience Chinese cultural activities, promoting a better sense of national identity and life
           value education.
          The potted plants were healthily planted and grown with care by our students. We displayed
           all the potted plants outside the S.1 classrooms for voting for The Award of the Most Beautiful
           Potted Plants. All MCNE committees and PTA took part in the activities. Our teachers and
           parents bought the beautiful plants. The proceeds were donated to the charities.
       The learning and teaching materials highlighted the core values of care for others and national
           identity.
       All S.1 classes had a personal growth period about life education and the importance of
           national identity cultivation.
      4.4.5 Collaboration with departments
       With the requirement to incorporate the national security element into the curriculum of
           fourteen subjects, there was more collaboration between MCNE and the subjects concerned
           for the MCNE-related activities. We joined the 2023 Territory-wide Basic Law Competition
           organized by EDB, with all classes participating in the activity. We also collaborated with the
           Department of Values Education and Information & Communication Technology to provide
           a platform for our students to join the activity.
