      1. School Vision & Mission
          Our mission is to educate students to be sensible, aspiring, law-abiding and disciplined young people, by cultivating their
          moral sense and integrity and bringing forth their potentials so that they will excel in learning and conduct and become
          competent and virtuous individuals who can contribute to the well-being of society and humankind.


      2. School Motto
          Our school motto is "Reverence, Humility, Perseverance and Earnestness".


      3. Core Values of Education
          On the basis of our school motto, we educate our students in the following principles:
          (1) Devotion to knowledge and truth, with love and respect for self and others;
          (2) Humility as self-discipline, and honesty as guiding principle;
          (3) Advancement with the times, with self-enhancement as the means;
          (4) Prudence in thought and readiness in deed, with care and concern for society.

b.        Based on the reflection against the seven learning goalsII, conduct school self-evaluation to review how good the school
          is in fostering whole-person development and lifelong learning of students.

     Promotion of Reading: Strengths and Challenges
     ◆ Reading remains a cornerstone of academic development, and the APASO data indicates that students at our school demonstrate strong reading strategies, with both junior and
        senior forms scoring highly (P-value: 85.7). This suggests that structured reading instruction, such as guided reading sessions and comprehension strategies taught in English
        and Chinese classes, has been effective. However, a deeper analysis reveals disparities in reading engagement. While junior students show a relatively healthy interest in leisure
        reading (P-value: 72.6), senior students exhibit a significant decline (P-value: 21.2).


     Self-Regulated Learning: Progress and Areas for Growth
     ◆ Self-regulated learning (SRL) skills—such as goal-setting, time management, and metacognitive reflection—are critical for academic success and personal development. The
        APASO data highlights that students perform well in active learning (P-value: 85.7) and self-planning (P-value: 85.7), indicating that pedagogical approaches emphasizing
        student autonomy, such as project-based learning and flipped classrooms, are yielding positive results.


     Language Skills: A Mixed Picture
     ◆ Language proficiency is another critical area of focus, and the APASO results present a mixed picture. English performance is strong (P-value: 78.8 for junior forms), likely due
        to the school’s emphasis on bilingual education and immersive language activities such as debates and drama performances.


     Information Literacy: Balancing Technology Use
     ◆ In today’s digital age, information literacy is essential for navigating the vast amounts of online content. The APASO data shows that students are highly proficient in using
        technology for learning (P-value: 85.7), reflecting the successful integration of IT tools into the curriculum.


     Healthy Lifestyle:
     ◆ With a main focus on positive education, many efforts have been made by different units in the school. Signature activities have taken place, such as Gratitude Festival (感謝祭)
        from the Counselling Unit. More students are using their character strengths to enhance their self-understanding. Compared to last year's APASO results, many mental health
        indicators have improved this year, indicating that our efforts in positive education are starting to be effective.

 Careers and Life Planning:
 ◆ The paradigm shift has been generally successful, extending the scope of career guidance from further studies counseling to include careers and life planning in these few years.
    Our school has joined the CLAP@JC project and has been a network school since the 2023-24 academic year. We have used the Hong Kong Benchmarks for Career & Life
    Development (HKBM) to identify weaknesses, putting significant effort into improving benchmarks 2 (Professional Competencies and Leadership) and 8 (Meaningful Encounters
    with the Workplace).
 ◆ According to the latest evaluations, four out of ten benchmarks received full marks, showing great improvement over the past two academic years. This progress has been highly
    recognized by the CLAP, and two public sharing sessions were conducted by the Careers and Life Planning Unit.
 ◆ APASO shows significant improvement in the junior form, with three items ranking at the top of the Hong Kong norm for junior students, including work experience, career
    guidance, and experiences from university tours and talks. Meanwhile, four items rank at the top of the Hong Kong norm for senior students, which include work experience,
    career guidance, university tours and talks, and receiving career information, with a P-value of 85.7.


 National Identity, Positive Values, and Attitudes:

c. How Can My School Be Better

 Nurture students to be self-directed learners
 ◆ As evidenced in our recent school review, cultivating self-directed learning, higher-order thinking, collaborative learning, and strong lesson preparation remains essential for
   student growth. Over the past years, we have introduced various strategies to promote independent study skills, with teachers gradually incorporating more student-centered
   approaches. Some educators have successfully implemented flipped classrooms and inquiry-based projects, demonstrating the potential for deeper implementation. Moving
   forward, we will refine our focus by reducing fragmented activities and embedding these core competencies systematically across all subjects. This includes structured approaches
   to reading across the curriculum, thoughtful lesson design, and regular reflective practices to strengthen metacognitive awareness.

 ◆ Some students face challenges in becoming self-directed learners due to over-reliance on teacher guidance or fixed mindsets about their abilities. While external factors like
   home environment may influence this, we can foster growth by explicitly teaching learning strategies, critical questioning techniques, and collaborative discussion skills. Students
   need to understand that true learning involves curiosity, analysis, and the ability to learn with and from peers—not just memorization. By integrating pre-lesson preparation and
   post-lesson reflection into routines, we help them take ownership of their progress.
 ◆ When students develop these competencies, they become more analytical, adaptable, and engaged in their learning. Self-directed inquiry encourages deeper understanding, while
   higher-order questioning and collaborative reading discussions strengthen critical thinking. Structured preparation ensures students arrive ready to contribute, and reflection
   consolidates their learning. This creates a powerful cycle: effective preparation enables active participation, collaboration deepens understanding, and reflection reinforces long-
   term retention. Over time, students grow into independent thinkers who approach challenges with confidence and resilience.

 Develop a growth mindset in students so they have the confidence and courage to face challenges.
 ◆ As shown in the analysis of Part B above, emotional well-being is still an important issue for our school to address. In the last three-year plan, our school used positive education
   as a theme and organized many activities based on the PERMA Model. Teachers have a basic understanding of positive education. Some have even received training and gained
   insights on how to put it into practice, and the school should give them the chance to share this knowledge with other teachers. In the next three-year plan, we may slightly reduce
   the number of activities and lessen ineffective academic burdens to create more space for students. Additionally, we should integrate more elements of positive education into
   school policies and classroom teaching to improve depth and effectiveness. We also expect more collaboration between units.
 ◆ Some students face different growth challenges that can cause emotional difficulties. Some of these issues, such as family background, are not things our school can solve on its
   own. To tackle these problems at their core, we should focus on the education of mindset and values. We need to teach students to view challenges as chances for growth and to
   realize that they can change their situation through effort. This is what a growth mindset is all about.

 ◆ With a growth mindset, students become more resilient to challenges, seeing setbacks as opportunities to grow rather than failures. This also helps them build positive relationships
   with others, encouraging them to be open to feedback and personal growth. This approach creates a cycle: a growth mindset encourages effort, effort leads to improvement, and
   improvement boosts self-confidence and emotional well-being.
