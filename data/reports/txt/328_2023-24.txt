1. Our School

School Background
Shun Lee Catholic Secondary School is an aided co-educational secondary school founded in 1982 by
the Hong Kong Catholic Diocese, which has a mission for her schools to promote the formation of the
whole person. The school motto SIC DEO PLACET “悅樂主心” reflects our commitment to realize this
mission of the sponsoring body.


School Motto
SIC DEO PLACET
The Biblical Origin:
“So whether we are at home or away, we make it our aim to please Him” (2 Corinthians 5:9)
Message: May we live according to the will of God


School Mission
The School is dedicated to providing a positive learning environment in which students understand the
truth of the Gospel and experience the love of Christ. It is where students learn to integrate Christian
faith with Chinese culture and life for holistic personal development.

We believe that students are unique individuals possessing distinctive gifts and talents. We thus commit
ourselves to helping students develop their potential to the fullest and become independent learners
capable of mastering self-learning skills, with emphasis on fostering positive attitudes towards life.

We devote ourselves to educating students to become responsible citizens who are eager and competent
to make contributions to society, and to encourage them to carry on the best of Chinese culture.


Core Values of School
As a Catholic school, we shall uphold and pass on the core values of TRUTH, JUSTICE, LOVE, LIFE
and FAMILY to our students to prepare them properly for their life and future responsibility. The
school also works in accordance with the school mission and dedicates itself to cultivating the core
values of “Love”, “Mutual respect” and “Responsibility” in students.

3. Strengthening assessment literacy
To strengthen the assessment literacy of different subjects, assessment literacy reports have been
collected from the DSE core and elective subjects as well as some selected junior secondary subjects
(i.e. S1-2 Science and S1-3 Life and Society) in the first term.
Teachers have demonstrated heightened awareness of analyzing students’ strengths and weaknesses in
learning based on their assignment, test and exam performance. Curriculum design and teaching
strategies have been adjusted to tackle the students’ learning difficulties identified and improve their
learning performance.
Sharing has been conducted in both the collaborative lesson preparation in the subject and the subject
heads’ meetings to promote the professional exchange among teachers on assessment literacy and
curriculum implementation.

 Reflection

 ⬥ Based on the evaluation findings, analyse the facilitating and hindering factors, and consolidate
    the experience and reflection derived from the process of planning, implementation and
    evaluation.
 Following the resumption of face-to-face classes in the post-pandemic era, more e-Learning strategies
 in 4Ps have been implemented by different subjects over the past academic year. The regular practice
 of 4Ps in classroom teaching and learning has effectively enhanced students’ active participation in
 the lessons. Students’ learning habits and performance have been improved.
 To develop students’ self-learning capacity, about 5% of the curriculum content of different subjects
 have been provided as self-access learning materials on Google Classroom for the students. Mini
 projects have also been implemented in various subjects at different levels. The research works could
 effectively enhance students’ collaboration with peers, self-confidence and self-regulated learning
 skills.
 The results of the school-based teaching and learning survey and the Stakeholders’ Survey
 demonstrated that students have developed a good understanding of the learning strategies and they
 acknowledged teachers’ efforts in providing them with learning strategies, feedback and learning
 opportunities inside and outside classroom to enhance their learning effectiveness.
 In the next academic year, the existing practice of implementing e-Learning strategies to strengthen
 students’ engagement in learning as well as the practice of mini research projects to foster students’
 self-regulated learning skills will be maintained. Furthermore, the use of assessment data to identify
 students’ learning difficulties and to inform curriculum planning and implementation will be
 strengthened further through the collection of assessment literacy reports from subjects and the
 sharing among teachers in collaborative lesson preparation and subject heads’ meeting.


 Feedback and Follow-up

  With reference to the above “Achievements” and “Reflection” of the Major Concern, suggest
   follow-up measures to further help students achieve the seven learning goals1, so as to inform the
   planning of the next Annual School Plan / School Development Plan.


The seven learning goals of primary education are national identity, positive values and attitudes,
knowledge of key learning areas, language skills, generic skills, reading and information literacy, and
healthy lifestyle. The seven learning goals of secondary education are national and global identity,
breadth of knowledge, language proficiency, generic skills, information literacy, life planning, and
healthy lifestyle.

  2.2 Major Concern 2: Strengthening Catholic Education Core Values

Achievements

Teachers and Students share the values of Life, Family, Justice, Love and Truth
   ➢ Using the whole school approach to promote the five core values of Catholic Education,
     programmes held this year successfully continued to raise students’ awareness of the focus core
     value “Truth”. Whole school mass programmes, such as School Commencement Mass, Christmas
     Prayer Services, Activity of Lent and Morning Prayer were held with the meaning of “Truth”.
     According to the survey, students generally believed that they could reflect on issues related to
     “Truth” in these activities (Average 5.7 out of 7).
   ➢ Career Guidance Team invited a barrister to conduct a career talk for all S3 students. 79.6% of S3
     students agreed and strongly agreed that the talk helped them understand how legal professions
     saw the value “Truth” in their career.
   ➢ A series of activities were organized to nurture students to become an informed and responsible
     citizen with a sense of national and global identity, appreciation of positive values and attitudes as
     well as Chinese culture, and respect for pluralism in society. And the P-value of National Identity
     aspects in APASO keep at highest 85.7.


Foster students’ positive attitude
   ➢ Family Meeting “Be a better Shunleer” was conducted in January. 75% of students agreed that the
     Family meeting can build up “Supportive and Family-based school culture”.
   ➢ Assembly talks related to mental health and stress management were conducted for different
     levels of students to develop students’ positive thinking. 86% students showed their understanding
     of the importance of mental health and stress management based on statistical results.
   ➢ Some students show the ability to feel, express and manage a range of positive and negative
     emotions by attending the workshops and group activities organized by social workers, guidance
     team and student support team. The workshops and group activities heightened their awareness of
     mental health and students learned how to manage their emotions through different ways such as
     mindfulness and coped with stress when facing difficulties.
   ➢ “Core values” was one of the key components when teachers trained prefects. After the training
     camp the results of the survey of prefects agreed that it was beneficial. 86% agreed it improved
     teamwork, 82% agreed it enhanced problem-solving skills, and 77% agreed it boosted
     communication abilities. These training activities could help students improve their ability to cope
     with the difficulties and face adversity. Prefects performed in line with core values-
     “Responsibility, Perseverance and Commitment”. They perform satisfactorily in handling daily
     works.
   ➢ With the collaboration of the Parent-Teacher Association, some workshops were held for parents.
     Most parents agreed that the workshop was useful and practical to release stress when dealing
     with the family affair or the communication problem with their children.

Reflection
➢   The school-based value education curriculum will be refined to better cater to the needs of students
    at different developmental stages.
➢   School Commencement Mass and Christmas Prayer Services will be continued next year. Keep on
    using morning prayers as the medium to help students understand and reflect the core values of our
    school.
➢   Students showed awareness of mental health by sharing their experience and difficulties with Social
    Workers, Class teachers and Guidance teachers. Some students displayed curiosity in symptoms of
    depression, Anxiety and early psychosis delivered by the Psychiatrist and Chinese Medicine
    Practitioner. Some students showed interest in knowing the ways to improve emotional, sleep
    problems and the ways to release the stress and anxiety when facing difficulties.
➢   Some students were confident in applying positive thinking when facing difficulties. However,
    according to the statistical result from APASO, some students have negative emotions such as
    anxiety and depressive symptoms. The P-value of “Affect, Satisfaction in school” aspects in APASO
    were kept at lowest 15.9,
➢   Statistics revealed that special attention should be given to students’ mental health and social health.
    Training on stress management should be arranged along with close observation.
➢   Through participating in various CUHK-QSIP workshops and courses, our teachers have enhanced
    their knowledge of evaluation and understand the importance of data analysis.

Feedback and follow-up
➢ For the upcoming year, the proposed theme is 'Thanksgiving”. The new theme will encourage
  students to express appreciation for those around them and also further develop a sense of empathy
  through interactions with others.
➢ To echo with the implementation of positive values education, a “Thanksgiving” journal will be
  designed for students to write down daily life reflections.
➢ We are aware of the importance of evaluating student performance in values and we have adopted the
  performance indicators established in Values Education Whole Curriculum Framework for Catholic
  Schools. We will continue to refine the measurement of students’ performance in values.
➢ Mass activities related to mental health will be further modified to meet the needs of students in the
  next academic year. Mental health programmes, workshops and activities will be continually held by
  social workers, guidance team and student support team to reduce the negative emotion such as
  anxiety and depressive symptoms of some students.
➢ In the coming year, the Prefect Team aims to build unity among the prefects, striving to foster a
  family-like culture that promotes positive peer relationships and a sense of belonging to the school.
➢ Various activities promoting students' healthy lifestyles will be promoted in the upcoming year with
  the aim of fostering a holistic and positive outlook of life.

Reflection

1. Enhancing the current ECA system
  ➢ Teachers may think that the school has provided more activities to the students (refer to
    Stakeholders’ Survey – teacher). However, referring to the Stakeholders’ Survey of students and
    parents and APASO III about physical training, the school may not provide sufficient

    especially those who have to take charge of the sports team.

Feedback and Follow-up

1. Enhancing the current ECA system
➢ In order to suit students’ interests, the number of clubs will be slightly increased upon teachers’
  duty arrangement. As more students are devoted to the clubs’ activities, their collaboration skills
  will be increased. Also, the increase in the number of clubs will increase the breadth of knowledge
  of students.
➢ As many newly joined teachers in the coming year have to take charge of the sports team, more
  guidelines and briefings will be made by the PE teachers.


2. Developing Students’ Competence
➢ The number of activity days will be reduced to two. To fulfil the requirement of membership,
  students have to join the club’s activities at least three times. Also, the committee members have to
  hold at least four activities. However, clubs are encouraged to hold more activities after school or
  during lunchtime. Besides, these activities may invite non-members to join.
➢ As the pandemic is gone, more resources from external organizations and external programmes
  and competitions will be explored. Through these activities, students’ collaboration skills,
  communication skills and problem-solving skills will be enhanced.
➢ The clubs and students’ organizations are encouraged to hold co-/cross-curricular activities.
  Through these activities, students’ collaboration skills, communication skills and problem-solving
  skills will be enhanced.

4.3 祈禱氣氛
內容：      早禱、中六畢業祈禱聚會、煉靈月祈禱會、誦唸玫瑰經
對象及人數： 全校師生

簡述活動內容及成效：
誦唸玫瑰經的活動安排在十月玫瑰月。天主教同學會幹事及牧民助理會帶領同學在
小息期間於小聖堂念誦一端玫瑰經，並由牧民助理講解其訊息及默想。由於同學較
爲繁忙，參與的同學不多；但有賴宗教老師宣傳及同學邀請，依然有少量中二至中
五同學固定的出席活動。

4.4 宗教環境和標記
內容：      展板、張貼海報或標記、宗教室佈置、聖堂、聖像
         其他： 聖誕節佈置（馬槽、聖誕節）
對象及人數： 全校師生、中五學生
簡述活動內容及成效：
牧民助理定期更換展板主題，以提升同學對公教的認識，配合一些課堂的活動，讓
同學關注公教的知識。另外，張貼於壁報板及宗教室外的海報亦讓同學知悉的最近
天主教的活動，提供渠道認識天主教。

4.5 信仰培育
內容：      彌撒、Shine Movie、宗教體驗活動、心靈教育活動

對象及人數： 全校師生
       其他：天主教同學會學生
簡述活動內容及成效：
天主教同學會
內容：舉行不同活動，如學會活動日、參觀明愛天糧等
成效：天主教同學會的活動讓同學加深如何帶領活動的技巧、檢視自己内心，以及
培養自己與群體之間的友誼。透過參觀機構，讓學生瞭解天主教團體對社會所作出
的貢獻。

4.6 福傳工作
內容：       Alpha 啓發青年、明愛賣物會
對象及人數： 全校師生
          其他：天主教同學會學生
簡述活動內容及成效：
Alpha 啓發青年影片播放及分享活動在午膳期間進行，邀請了中三級感興趣的同學
以及天主教同學會幹事協辦。同學共進午膳，並一同觀看影片加深信仰認識，並進
行討論分享自己經驗、看法和感受。同學反映觀看影片后理清對信仰的誤解，加深
與身邊人的關係。然而部分同學認爲單純觀看影片的活動沉悶，而影片會單向灌輸
對信仰的看法。
明愛賣物會邀請了天主教同學會的同學和幹事到社區進行義賣、攤位遊戲及點唱，
透過愛德活動散播天主的關懷和愛。同學們普遍投入是次活動，熱烈的兜售商品；
然而部分同學較繁忙，未必能安排更多的時間為賣物會作籌備。

Summary of 2023-24:

The total expenses of DLG has been increased due to the resumption of serveal study tours. More S4 students
particiapte in the Credit bearing courses of tertiary insitutions in different areas.

Suggestions for 2024-25:

Given that the gifted education programmes offered by tertiary institutions and professional bodies are costly
(i.e. $1000-$5000) to most grassroot families in the school:

➢ For CSSA and Full-Granted students, about 50% of the programme fee could be subsidized by DLG while
  another 50% by School-based After-school Learning Support Grant (SALSG, recurrent) and/or Students
  Activities Support Grant (SASG, clawed back every year).
➢ For Half-Granted and other NON-CSSA/Full/Half-Granted students, about 50% of the programme fee could
  be subsidized by DLG while another 50% could be self-financed by themselves. In case the students express
  that their families cannot afford the programme fee, they could be given some subsidies through SALSG
  and/or SASG (i.e. by signing a circular to apply for “Special Half”).
➢ The DLG subsidy for each student in a year shall be capped at $12000 and/or 3 programmes only.
