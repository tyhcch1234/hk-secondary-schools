CHAPTER 1: Portrait of Heep Yunn School


School Background

By the Grace of God, Heep Yunn School is an Anglican school established in 1936, when two former Church Missionary Society institutions, namely
Fairlea School (1886) and Victoria Home and Orphanage (1887), were joined together. The name “Heep Yunn” means “united in grace” in Chinese.
Over the years, Heep Yunn has gradually grown from a Vernacular school into an English-medium school providing a high quality, all-round
education for girls in Hong Kong. In 2012, the school switched its status from a government grant-in-aid school to a direct subsidy scheme school.


The founders of Heep Yunn were committed to bringing education to young women and orphans in answer to the call of duty at a time of economic
instability and threat of war in 1930’s Hong Kong. They believed in empowering students to bring enlightenment to our nation and support to our
people. To this day, the school abides by its dedication to serve our fellow men in the love of Christ.



School Vision and Mission


It is the mission of the school to provide our students with a Christian education that develops the whole person in the following areas: moral,
intellectual, physical, social, aesthetic and spiritual; to instil in our girls a spirit of public service, and to build a firm foundation in both Chinese and
English.

Campus and Facilities

The school is located on an 11,000 sq. m. campus consisting of six building blocks: the Lau Cheung Suet Ming Building (1936), the Chapel of St.
Clare of Assisi (1957), the Chau Hau Leung & Li Shiu Ching Building (1964), the Library (1966), the Jubilee Building (1986) and the Mr. & Mrs. Au
Sue Hung Building (2005). Outdoor facilities include a 25-meters swimming pool, 4 multi-purpose games courts, a Sorority Garden, a parking area
and a lawn. Other facilities include the School Hall, the School Canteen, Student Development Centre, 30 special rooms including the Black Box
Theatre, the Innovation Laboratory, the Visual Arts Room, the Music Room, the Technology & Living Room, and Lecture Theatre, 30 classrooms,
and a 3-storey high sports centre.

Progress and Achievements
Curriculum Enrichment with Global Elements across Academic Departments
     To systematically expand students' academic horizons, the School has integrated international dimensions and global perspectives across
multiple Key Learning Areas (KLAs). This curriculum enrichment is supported by strong professional engagement, as evidenced by the year-end
teacher survey. An overwhelming 84.06% of respondents reported that they had successfully incorporated global elements into their schemes of
work and daily lessons, actively encouraging students to analyze academic topics through a global lens. This pedagogical shift was further reinforced
by 88.41% of teachers who actively utilized international case studies, diverse texts, and global datasets to enrich their classroom instruction.


      Across selected departments, this strategic mandate has translated into exemplary classroom practices and student achievements. In the English
Department, all Form 4 students completed a structured 12-lesson module in Term 1 entitled "Global Perspectives: Skills for Effective Communication".
This module covered contemporary international themes, such as emerging trends in global workplaces and pathways to climate awareness, utilizing
active learning methodologies like case studies, collaborative discussions, and professional CV writing. The outcomes were exceptional, resulting in
a 100% pass rate and 74.7% of students attaining a distinction-level score of 70 marks or above, which demonstrates a high level of global
communication proficiency.


     In other disciplines, curriculum enrichment was equally impactful. The Mathematics Department successfully introduced historical and
cultural contextualization into core mathematical concepts; before teaching Pythagoras' Theorem, teachers shared historical narratives regarding
Pythagoras' life and mathematical discoveries in ancient civilizations, fostering a deeper appreciation for the global history of science. In the
Economics Department, students' textbook learning was broadened through customized e-learning modules and interactive "Travel Econ Journey"
segments, exposing them to real-world global economic dynamics and contemporary financial trends beyond standard classroom boundaries. The

Progress and Achievements
Two-tier Approach
       In our inaugural year of systematically integrating artificial intelligence into education, the school adopted a two-tier approach to effectively
cultivate AI literacy among our key stakeholders. Tier 1 focused on establishing baseline AI competencies across the entire teaching staff. To facilitate
this, the school secured a comprehensive subscription to the AI platform “Goodclass.ai”, granting all teachers access to user-friendly toolboxes and
multiple instructional chatbots. This foundational infrastructure was reinforced by professional development workshops delivered by the Solomon
Learning Group, which successfully ensured that staff attained technical mastery and instructional confidence in navigating these digital tools.


     Simultaneously, Tier 2 utilized designated Education Bureau (EDB) grants to launch targeted pilot programs across selected departments,
namely Chinese, English, Mathematics, Junior Science, Information and Communication Technology (ICT), and Science and Technology. These
specific panels pioneered the practical integration of AI into school-based learning and teaching workflows.


     The pilot departments reported encouraging milestones and valuable pedagogical reflections at the conclusion of the academic year. Here are
some highlights: The Chinese Department successfully implemented the "Mubi" (巫筆) AI platform to support descriptive and expressive writing
instruction among Form 4 students, achieving commendable learning outcomes. To address minor technical limitations observed during the pilot,
the department plans to deploy targeted instructional strategies next year, including proactive pre-screening of content by teachers, structured
classroom guidance, and standardized student rewriting procedures to maximize the tool's educational value. Meanwhile, the Junior Science
Department focused on building professional capacity and developing high-quality curriculum resources. Panel teachers actively enrolled in
specialized short courses organized by The University of Hong Kong (HKU) covering various facets of AI-assisted science education. In
collaboration with the Centre for Learning Sciences and Technologies at The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK), they successfully co-
developed and integrated custom AI-assisted learning resources into science lessons. In the Science and Technology Department, administrative
efficiency and peer collaboration were highly prominent. The panel head and department members integrated generative AI tools into their daily
lesson preparation routines, reporting a noticeable reduction in administrative preparation time. The department systematically deployed over two
distinct AI applications, and panel members regularly held internal sharing sessions each term to exchange prompt engineering exemplars and digital
workflow records.

Teacher Proficiency and Efficacy
      The overall adoption rate and self-efficacy levels among the teaching staff are highly positive. An overwhelming 92.75% of teachers agreed that
the school provided appropriate resources—such as licensed AI platforms, targeted professional development, and the School-based Artificial
Intelligence Assessment Scale (AIAS) guidelines—to support their pedagogical transition. Crucially, 91.30% of staff members successfully
incorporated AI into their teaching or professional administrative tasks, which include lesson planning, feedback generation, and assessment design.
The pedagogical utility of these tools was further confirmed by 81.16% of teachers who agreed that AI assisted them in effectively differentiating
instruction and supporting diverse learners. Furthermore, our educators demonstrated strong critical engagement with the technology, with 85.51%
of respondents actively evaluating AI outputs for accuracy, bias, and suitability for student learning. Ultimately, this comprehensive support system
culminated in 88.41% of teachers reporting a significant increase in their professional confidence when utilizing AI for teaching and learning.


Student Literacy and Engagement
     Our student body demonstrated a parallel enhancement in AI literacy and ethical awareness. According to subject assessments in Senior Form
ICT (F.4 to F.6), the vast majority of students successfully demonstrated an understanding of AI concepts and information literacy. In the junior
secondary forms (F.1 to F.3), a strong majority of students reflected that their AI literacy had either enhanced or enhanced greatly, representing 90%
of F.1, 77% of F.2, and approximately 80% of F.3 students.


     These outcomes are further supported by the year-end student survey, which measured responses on a 5-point Likert Scale. Students expressed
a high level of critical awareness, scoring 4.35 on their understanding of the importance of verifying AI outputs for accuracy, bias, and relevance,
and 4.32 regarding their awareness of ethical boundaries and plagiarism. Students also recognized the practical benefits of technology, scoring 4.26
on how AI helped them learn better or work more efficiently. General knowledge and technical confidence were also rated highly, with a score of
4.20 indicating that they had successfully learned how AI works, 4.19 representing confidence in using AI to support self-directed studies, and 4.16
demonstrating proficiency in writing effective prompts to obtain high-quality results.

Challenges Identified
     Despite these significant achievements, the implementation process highlighted several critical challenges that must be addressed to ensure
sustainable progress. For our teachers, the primary concern was the inconsistent accuracy of AI outputs and the occurrence of "hallucinations,"

which was flagged by 53.62% of the staff. Additionally, 37.68% of teachers reported a lack of adequate time to learn and experiment with new tools,
with an equal percentage expressing uncertainty regarding academic integrity, plagiarism, and ethical boundaries. Concerns over data privacy and
information security were cited by 34.78% of teachers, while 27.54% noted a personal deficit in high-quality prompting skills, and 26.09% struggled
with a lack of access to premium or paid AI models.


     Students faced similar hurdles, with an overwhelming 74.1% expressing difficulty in trusting AI outputs due to the prevalence of "hallucinated"

Reflection
     While Artificial Intelligence offers significant educational benefits, we recognize that it is not without its faults and limitations. It is imperative
that we continue to empower our teachers, equipping them to guide students in utilizing these tools with wisdom, critical thinking, and purpose. To
ensure a cohesive approach and to effectively address the reported pain points of both staff and students, the establishment of a dedicated committee
for centralized digital education planning is highly recommended.


     Given the rapid evolution of technology, cultivating a culture of continuous learning and critical reflection is essential for future-proofing both
our educators and learners. Amidst growing public discourse regarding the potential societal impacts of AI, our role as educators is more crucial
than ever. We must actively nurture core values and virtues, ensuring students possess the moral compass to utilize AI responsibly and ethically. By
prioritizing character development alongside technological competence, we equip our students to leverage AI as a powerful tool for empowerment,
ensuring human ingenuity remains at the forefront of their development.


     We are highly appreciative of the Education Bureau (EDB) grants, which have provided the critical initial resources for AI platform
subscriptions and foundational professional development. However, as these specific funding streams conclude, securing long-term sustainability
becomes a primary institutional focus. It is vital for the school management to identify alternative resources and strategic partnerships. This proactive
planning will ensure we maintain our current developmental momentum and continue building a sustainable AI infrastructure that supports long-
term educational excellence.

Future Planning
     Under this major concern, the school has successfully cultivated robust “digital literacy” among key stakeholders, strictly aligning with
the “Seven Learning Goals” of the Education Bureau (EDB). To sustain and build upon this momentum, we will establish the Digital Education
Committee in the upcoming academic year to formulate and launch our First Five-Year Plan on Digital Education. In direct alignment with the
EDB's Blueprint for Digital Education Development, this strategic roadmap will coordinate holistic planning across four levels—school, system, teacher,
and student—to systematically address the EDB's four key performance indicators.


      The committee's immediate priority will be resolving identified stakeholder pain points. To support teachers in learning and teaching, our
strategic plan will prioritize formulating clear, school-wide guidelines to eliminate ethical ambiguities and align classroom expectations. We will also
develop and deploy a dedicated school-based prompt library for teachers, focusing on sophisticated prompt engineering and rigorous output
verification to mitigate accuracy and hallucination concerns. Simultaneously, the school will explore sustainable avenues to secure institutional access
to premium AI models, thereby resolving resource disparities and fostering a responsible and ethical AI-assisted learning environment across the
curriculum.

Professional Development: Empowering Teachers as Facilitators of Well-being
     To ensure the effective delivery of positive education, the school established a hearty training pipeline for its teaching staff. The capacity-
building process began with targeted, specialized training where selected teachers attended the intensive Discovering Positive Education training hosted
by the Bei Shan Tang Foundation on 6–7 November 2025. This specialized programme provided key staff with the pedagogical frameworks

a targeted workshop, "PERMA in Schools: An Introduction and Practical Applications," facilitated by St. James' Settlement, alongside a specialized mental

strongly agreed that the St. James' Settlement workshop successfully built their capacity across multiple dimensions. Teachers reported that the
workshop successfully deepened their understanding of positive psychology, yielding a high mean score of 8.09, and enabled them to experience the
power and application of the PERMA model, which scored 7.89. Furthermore, they felt equipped with concrete, practical strategies for applying
PERMA principles in a school setting, rated at 7.61, and appreciated the introduction to character strengths, which promoted self and peer
appreciation, scoring 7.74. Crucially, the training successfully equipped staff to implement the School Development Plan's well-being objectives,
scoring 7.54, while strengthening overall team collaboration and mutual understanding within the school team, which scored 7.64. This resulted in
an overall enjoyment and satisfaction score of 8.01. Ultimately, 84.06% of teachers agreed in the year-end evaluation that the year's professional
development programs had successfully enhanced their professional capacity to promote student well-being and engagement.


Translation into Daily Classroom Practices
     This professional training successfully translated into direct, positive changes in daily classroom dynamics. According to the year-end teacher
survey, educators actively adopted strengths-based and engagement-driven teaching methodologies. Specifically, 88.41% of teachers actively
employed classroom strategies to cultivate positive emotions, such as cultivating a growth mindset, practicing gratitude, and spotting character
strengths.

      In terms of lesson design, 85.51% of teachers successfully designed lessons that promoted active engagement and flow, utilizing hands-on
tasks, student choice, and calibrating lessons to provide an appropriate level of challenge. The same percentage of educators (85.51%) successfully

Targeted Student Well-being and Mental Health Programmes
     Through collaboration between the Counselling Committee, external NGOs, and student leaders, a multi-tiered support system of wellness
programs was executed with exceptional results.


(i) School-Based Family and Life Education Curriculum
      Fostering social-emotional competence was prioritized in the junior forms through a dedicated partnership with the Integrated Community
Centre for Mental Wellness (ICCMW). During the First term, ICCMW conducted an intensive 4-hour social-emotional competence workshop
for Forms 1 and 2, which yielded outstanding student feedback. An overwhelming 96.0% of students reported that the workshop gave them a
clearer appreciation of positive emotions, while 91.9% of students indicated that the training helped them engage more actively with their
surroundings. Additionally, 96.4% of students affirmed that the instructional materials provided by the ICCMW were highly effective in enhancing
their emotional management and overall social-emotional competence.


(ii) The JC LevelMind @ School Project
      Led by the Counselling Committee, the School's participation in the JC LevelMind @ School Project exceeded all success criteria. The project
successfully executed three core components, starting with the establishment of the Student Wellness Club to drive peer-led support. This was
followed by the organization of the "Wellness+" Programme, which featured campus-wide activities including latte art workshops, Bubble Football,
and a graduation photo booth specifically designed for Form 6 students. Finally, the physical infrastructure was upgraded through the renovation of
the LevelMind Hub, establishing a physical campus sanctuary for decompression. Over 95% of student participants agreed that these activities
successfully cultivated positive emotions and encouraged active engagement. Students demonstrated improved self-care skills, stronger peer support
networks, and reported a noticeable reduction in academic and exam-related stress. Social workers and teachers observed remarkably high student
participation rates and a significantly warmer, more caring campus atmosphere.


(iii) Structured Well-being Interventions and Specialised Support
      Structured class periods dedicated to relaxation activities proved highly successful across the student body. Well above 70% of students agreed
that these sessions cultivated positive emotions, encouraged engagement, and benefited their mental health. Popular activities—including communal
breakfast sharing, music, and light interactive games—fostered a relaxed classroom atmosphere and enhanced student-teacher connections.

Reflection
     Overall, the school’s third major concern has been attained to a large extent. To ensure these achievements are sustained, the school must focus
on consolidating our whole-school wellness culture. A key lesson from this cycle is that prioritizing teacher training and staff well-being prior to
student rollout is essential to creating a unified, campus-wide language around the PERMA model.


     On the student level, the positive education programs, active classroom learning elements, and appropriately calibrated lesson challenge levels
were highly appreciated. Future curriculum planning and student activity design will continue to build on these successful, student-centered lesson
structures to deepen cognitive flow and classroom engagement.


     Ultimately, for positive education to take deep root across our school, a collective paradigm shift in mindset is required. The School remains
committed to cultivating a growth mindset among all key stakeholders. This ongoing cultural evolution will allow PERMA principles to benefit the
wider community, successfully translating positive education from a set of administrative objectives into a lived daily classroom pedagogy.
