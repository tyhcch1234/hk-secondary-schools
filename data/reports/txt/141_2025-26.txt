                                        Our School

1.1 School Mission
    It is our belief that our students are created in God’s image and they are unique, respectable
    and valuable. Therefore, it is our mission to educate our students to have respect for life as
    well as develop their abilities and potential given by God. We believe that education is a
    means to practice the Christian faith. Based on the love of Christ, we are dedicated to educate
    our students with love and care.

1.2 Mission Statement
    Our main objective is to promote self-esteem, passion for life, concern for others, social
    responsibility and lifelong learning among students with an emphasis on virtue, wisdom,
    physical development, social ability, aesthetic appreciation and spirituality. To achieve the
    objective, our school strives to reinforce the foundations of “knowledge”, “character and
    value” as well as “skill and quality” for our students. The following mission statement, in
    line with our belief and aspiration, serve as a blueprint for the long-term development of
    our school. “Based on the teaching of the Bible and through the practice of love, we are
    committed to create a learning environment filled with creativity, initiative, care and trust.
    We aim to help students build harmonious relationship with others, and to develop their own
    unique potential in the areas of intellect, character and temperament. We aspire to cultivate
    young people with a sense of responsibility, rationality and affection, who pursue excellence
    in life with vision and passion.”

1.3 Introduction
    ECF Saint Too Canaan College, a Christian co-educational secondary school joining DSS,
    commenced operation in September 2003, with the aim of providing quality Christian
    education for students. With a millennium school campus in Kwun Tong, our school
    provides a good learning environment with comprehensive facilities for our students, which
    include 33 classrooms, 4 laboratories, 1 STEM room, 1 computer room, 7 special rooms,
    library, English Corner, health center, 1 basketball court and 1 football court. Moreover, our
    school has set up a Campus TV Studio in order to provide a platform for students to develop
    their oral training and creativity.

   During the summer, The renovation of the library Joy@EDEN was completed and has
   started its service in November. It has been more convenient for students to borrow learning
   resources and to promote learning atmosphere. Moreover, major renovations, including the
   covered playground, have started in summer. It is expected to provide students with a better
   learning environment.

(2) Achievements and Reflection on Major Concerns; Feedback and
    Follow-up
    Priority Task 1: The major concerns were discussed in the School Administration Committee
    meeting. The following two major concerns were adopted for 2024-2027, School 3-year
    Development Plan:

    Major Concerns
    1. To foster the development of positive values and promote the holistic well-being of students.
    2. To empower students’ uniqueness.

    Major concern 1: To foster the development of positive values and promote
    the holistic well-being of students.
    1.1 To empower students to cultivate a growth mindset.
    1.2 To inculcate a constructive learning attitude among students.
    1.3 To strengthen the integration of Christian principles into the educational experience.
    1.4 To bolster students' sense of national identity and civic responsibility.
    1.5 To nurture students with STCC's core values (Respect, Responsibility, Resilience).

 Achievements
 Achieved.

 In respect of students’ well-being, after the first attempt in 24/25, the measure of setting the
 first two weeks of September as Transition Week received good response. The measure
 continued this year. Students only attended half-day classes in the first two weeks and they
 adapted more easily to the new school year, which enhanced their well-being. APASO scores
 for positive emotions and absence of anxiety reached 116.

 Additionally, Soul Healthy Day was especially launched in the start of school term to brighten
 up students’ school life and to draw students’ attention to their own well-being. Other examples
 were Soul Place, 105 Degree group were organized.

 According to SD report, Home-school Co-operation Committee organized workshops,
 including the Youth Mental Health First Aid Certification Course, with over 85% of parent
 respondents agreeing the sessions improved family parenting.

 In response to Target 1.1 (growth mindset),
 As reflected from the survey for teachers, more than 90% of teachers agreed that they had
 instilled a growth mindset in students through their lessons and curriculum.

 The school organized the OLE Day and Life-wide learning programme in Jan and April of this
 year respectively. For junior students, a new trial this year was a project learning about
 redevelopment of Choi Hung Estate. S2 students visited different places in their neighborhood
 Choi Hung Estate, interviewed the residents and designed different outcomes such as booklets,
 videos, artworks. It worked as a starting point for students to grow and initiate their co-
 operation with classmates and build up self-study habits. OLE Day with different activities was
 organized in recent years to enhance learning exposure.

In response to Target 1.2 (constructive learning attitude), according to the survey for
teachers, more than 80% of teachers agreed that effective learning strategies across the
curriculum have been implemented.

According to students’ stakeholder survey, the rating has improved in all questions in the aspect
“My views on student learning”. For example, they rated themselves 3.5 in “taking an active
role in learning” (Qt 7), 3.6 in “completing learning tasks seriously” (Qt 10), 3.5 in “trying my
best to overcome learning difficulties (Qt 11).

In response to Target 1.3 (Christian principles),
According to the SD survey, Spiritual Nurturing Committee deepened students' faith through
the Christian Ministry Team (CMT), Gospel Week, cell groups, and Christian fellowship. Over
60% of student leaders reported increased satisfaction with their service and spiritual growth.

This year, before Revision Test week and examinations, CMT team continued their practice to
sing songs and deliver small gifts for encouragement.

In response to Target 1.4 (national identity), the Focus Inspection was completed in Apr. The
team provided positive feedback to us. Their report wrote : (1) National security-themed sharing
during morning assemblies and "Speeches under the Flag" were delivered. (2) The school has
suitably integrated national education elements into curriculum planning and classroom
learning to foster students' sense of belonging to the country. For example, low-altitude
economy in the STEAM curriculum, to practise sports like Tai Chi and shuttlecock in PE,
cheongsam design in VA, etc.

Regarding non-Chinese speaking students, the school has developed a well-structured school-
based Chinese Cultural Studies programme at the junior secondary level. The programme has
comprised an adapted Chinese History curriculum developed with reference to the framework
provided by the Education Bureau, together with purposefully selected cultural learning
elements and learning activities such as museum visits and mooncake-making workshops.

Beyond the classroom, activities such as "Salute to Workers" series were organised in
connection with the approaching Labour Day offered a clear example of coordinated learning
across school activities: highlighting the value of labour and respect for workers' contributions.

Yet, the Focus Inspection gave us the suggestions that opportunities for students to express their
feelings towards our country and reflect on their personal role in safeguarding national security
though lessons and assignment designs are relatively limited. The school could further
strengthen lessons and task design by incorporating more structured questioning, discussion
and reflection to help deepen students' affective engagement for our country.

In response to Target 1.5 (STCC's core values), Moral and Civic Education Committee
(MCEC) maintained weekly flag-raising ceremonies and trained student flag-bearers. APASO
Q-values for National Identity reached strong levels: Sense of Belonging (112), Responsibility
& Obligation (116), and Pride & Care (116).

Character & Nurturing Committee (CNC) promoted ethical slogans, assemblies, and the Yellow
Paper reward scheme, leading to an increase in conduct credits and a reduction in major
disciplinary cases

 Reflection
 With a more well-established class structure set, teachers are more capable of handling and
 teaching different levels of students. Teachers are committed to inculcating a constructive
 learning attitude among students. Though there is challenge of learning diversity has become
 more complicated due to their differences in learning capacity, languages and cultures.

 The relatively high turnover rate of teachers in Hong Kong has posed a threat to the curriculum
 development. It takes more time for the less experienced teachers to adopt effective classroom
 management skills, which affects inculcating a constructive learning attitude among students.

 Feedback and Follow-up

 In terms of national security, directly address the focused inspection team’s evaluation by
 shifting values education from extra-curricular activities into daily classroom instruction. KLA
 panels should integrate explicit growth-mindset prompts, Biblical principles, and reflection
 tasks into routine lesson plans. Subject heads can monitor teaching effectiveness through lesson
 observations and homework inspections, ensuring students actively internalize resilience, self-
 discipline, and constructive learning attitudes during regular academic studies.

 In terms of well-being, the "LevelMind Hub" will be established on campus in collaboration
 with the Jockey Club LevelMind@JC Program next year. Cross-committee coordination will
 be strengthened to address academic stress and emotional well-being.

     2.1 To build self-efficacy in students.

         and assessments.
     2.3 To unlock students’ reading potentials.
     2.4 To create a wide variety of opportunities for students to showcase their unique talents
         and strengths.

 Achievements
 Achieved.

 In response to Target 2.1 (self-efficacy), there were ample opportunities to showcase students’
 good work. We displayed their good coursework on the second Parents’ Day.

 In response to Target 2.2 (evidence -based approach in instructional planning), with
 reference to the L&T year-end survey, 98% of teachers agreed that they had made changes to
 the curriculum and adopted different and more effective teaching strategies in their lessons.

 In response to Target 2.3 (reading potentials), reading activities were integrated into the
 Chinese and English curriculum inside the classroom. Good book recommendations were run
 in Chinese lessons while in English lessons.

 Nevertheless, APASO’s finding is relatively not high in reading. (85-87).

In response to Target 2.4 (showcase talents),
According to student’s stakeholder survey, students generally agreed that “through the
opportunities that the school provides, they can develop their interests and life skills (score
3.6). Over 60 extra-curricular activities have been set up.

Performances such as mini English musical, Chinese drama, and choir singing were given in
the L&T year-end school assembly. Moreover, great events such as Canaan Show, Canaan Fest,
Singing Contest were organized. According to the L&T year-end survey, more than 80% of
teachers agreed there were ample opportunities.

Reflection
All KLA panels are aware of the importance of assessment literacy and the alignment of
teaching and learning. Most of them actively participate in different kinds of teacher training
and seek for the strategies to improve the students’ academic performance.

Joy@Eden was re-allocated to lower level to facilitate students to use the library and borrow
books. It nurtures the reading habit.

Feedback and Follow-up

Capitalize on Joy@Eden Resource Learning Centre by expanding alumni tutoring and peer-led
study groups into a year-round program, rather than restricting heavy usage to S6 study leave.
Establishing structured academic clinics in core areas like Chinese and Mathematics will build
self-efficacy and intrinsic motivation across junior and senior forms.

Suggestions were heard such as reading real books (hardcopy), reading books by topics (instead
of whole book), and to request students to read and achieve missions to arouse students’ reading
motivation.

Enhance vertical curriculum mapping for values education across all year levels.

(3) Student Performance
3.1 Students’ academic performance
   Cultivating Self-Driven Learners and Academic Excellence
   Our educational philosophy centers on cultivating intrinsically motivated, lifelong learners.
   Throughout the junior forms, we focus on inspiring intellectual curiosity and broadening
   horizons through enriched learning experiences both within and beyond the classroom. As
   students advance to senior forms, we prepare them to navigate real-world challenges and
   competitive environments with confidence. Our dedicated teaching faculty works tirelessly to
   guide and equip students for the Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education (HKDSE),
   ensuring they achieve academic standards of high distinction.

   Distinguished HKDSE Results and Tertiary Articulation
   Thanks to continuous pedagogical enhancements, our students have achieved marked
   improvements across a broad spectrum of HKDSE subjects. This year, we achieved a 100%
   tertiary education articulation rate among our graduating cohort, with top individual
   performance reaching 31 marks. A commendable portion of graduates secured University
   Grants Committee (UGC)-funded places at Hong Kong's top three universities ("the Big
   Three"), while others successfully matriculated into prestigious local post-secondary programs
   or pursued higher education overseas.

   Innovative Pedagogy and Continuous Improvement
   Our dynamic teaching team actively adapts innovative instructional strategies to cater to
   evolving educational needs. Moving forward, the school remains steadfast in exploring
   progressive strategies and strengthening institutional policies to continuously elevate teaching
   effectiveness and student achievement.

3.2 Students’ personal growth
   Our students have satisfactory performance in physical education and arts development. They
   participated in various activities and competitions to widen their exposure. Moreover, their
   efforts to and perseverance in practicing paid off in the end and many students won different
   awards in the competitions.

   There are over 60 extra-curricular activities in our school and students have full chance to
   explore their interests and develop their potential. AIMS programmes were developed recently
   to facilitate students devising their activities and career plans to achieve whole-person
   development. 82.2% of students reported successfully developing their talents or skills through
   ECA participation, which reflects an increase from 74% in the previous year. This positive
   growth strongly supports the school's Cultural Security by fostering a vibrant, talent-rich
   campus culture where 75.5% of students easily fulfilled the AIMS program requirements.

   In terms of the core values of Responsibility and Diligence, 83.2% of students reported
   maintaining a good attitude, which includes active participation, punctuality, and being
   responsible. This represents a positive increase from 79% last year.
