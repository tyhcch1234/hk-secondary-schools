(1) Our School

1.1 School Mission

Based on the teaching of the Bible, we are committed to imparting knowledge and cultivating good
behaviours among students. We aspire to instill proper values in students by educating them about
the truth in the Bible and building their belief in God as the Master of their lives. We endeavour to
empower our students to be well-rounded, righteous and responsible citizens with godly character.



1.2 School Motto

Jesus Christ is the Master of New Life



1.3 School History

Founded in 1969 on Hong Kong Island, the original name of the school was New Life School.
Thanks to the donation of the Lui Ming Choi Foundation, the school was relocated to its current
campus in 1985 and was renamed as NLSI Lui Kwok Pat Fong College.



1.4 School Management

Our school Incorporated Management Committee was founded in 2008.

Achievements:
1. The ESR Team commended the school for offering a broad curriculum that continuously
   fosters student self-directed learning. In the junior forms, curriculum content broadly
   encompasses essential learning elements across different Key Learning Areas (KLAs).
   Furthermore, in alignment with EDB initiatives, the school joined the Other Languages Pilot
   Scheme to support students in acquiring Japanese and Korean, thereby enriching our
   multilingual learning environment.


2. The school is committed to enhancing students’ learning motivation by designing effective
   lessons that emphasize relevance, competence, relatedness, and autonomy. Subject teachers
   have conducted lesson studies, during which they identified students’ learning challenges and
   implemented appropriate strategies through team discussions. The first focus of this year is the
   connection between students’ pre-lesson preparation and their lessons, while the second focus
   is the use of questioning techniques to enhance learning and teaching effectiveness.


3. Teachers' encouragement and morning assembly sharing serve as key drivers in motivating
   students to improve, set goals, and persevere. Stakeholder survey results reflect an upward trend
   in student performance, specifically regarding willingness to face challenges (S11) and
   goal-setting capabilities (S13). The school remains committed to fostering an encouraging
   environment that continually empowers our students.


4. Surveys show that teachers have strengthened their guidance on student learning strategies, with
   a particular focus on pre-class preparation and the use of online resources. Student rating
   averages for teachers regarding the learning strategies taught (S01) reached a four-year high. In
   addition, the data also highlights the need for the school to continuously promote professional
   development in teaching strategies and learning methodologies, ensuring ongoing progress in
   learning and teaching at school.


5. Teachers provided opportunities to explore diverse topics and regularly arranged varied learning
   activities—a positive trend echoed by survey results regarding the exploration of a variety of
   topics (S04) and engagement in different learning activities (S05). Engaging students in
   acquiring knowledge both within and beyond the classroom has proven to be a vital strategy,
   significantly boosting their motivation and overall enjoyment of learning (S09).


6. Regarding students’ learning improvement (S02, S15), survey results show that teachers placed
   greater emphasis on providing constructive feedback to help students reflect on and improve
   their learning—a positive trend recognized by both teachers and students. However,
   observations by some teachers indicate that while progress is being made, continued efforts are
   needed to help all students fully achieve target learning outcomes.



                                                                           2025-2026 School Report P. 6
7. Stakeholder survey results regarding student learning initiative reflect that parents and teachers
   both observed students demonstrating greater initiative compared to previous years. We express
   our heartfelt gratitude to parents for partnering with the school and continuously supporting and
   encouraging their children's active learning at home.


8. The school applies the whole-school approach in school plan implementation. More hands-on
   learning tasks and problem-based learning activities were organized this year, challenging
   students to collaboratively address real-world issues. For example:
       - Students engaged in hands-on STEAM projects that applied core scientific principles to
       real-world engineering challenges. S1 students designed and tested thermal containers using
       insulating and reflective materials to minimize heat transfer. S2 students constructed model
       greenhouses, engineering controlled environments to optimize plant growth by monitoring
       temperature, light, and humidity. Building on these skills, S3 students applied design
       thinking and collaborative problem-solving to develop practical solutions for global
       environmental and social challenges aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals
       (SDGs).


       - In collaboration with the Discipline Committee, the Business, Accounting and Financial
       Studies Department (BAFS Department) successfully organized the ‘Campus Sales’,
       empowering students to apply marketing strategies, solve problems, and design creative
       products in teams. The event highlighted their leadership, communication, and creative
       talents, with all participants agreeing that it consolidated their knowledge. Emphasizing core
       values like commitment through dedicated preparation and responsibility in managing
       finances and inventory will further enrich this practical sales experience.


       - Citizenship, Economics and Society Department focuses on studying problems related to
       the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). S1 adopted a new project format focusing on
       first-hand waste data collection and SDG mitigation strategies, though students need support
       in analyzing issues from multiple perspectives. S2 students demonstrated commendable
       mastery of economic globalization concepts—with high-ability groups independently
       researching non-listed corporations—despite tight May scheduling. Moving forward, the
       department will focus on refining presentation skills and optimizing project timelines.


       - The Home Economics Department continued project-based learning, NGO collaborations,
       and upcycling workshops to broaden students' social awareness. S2 projects on plant-based
       diets and eco-friendly clothing fostered strong student engagement by applying research to
       daily life. Additionally, a group of S1 and S2 students visited The Mills for a "Denim
       Deconstructed" workshop, crafting keychains from deadstock to gain practical insight into
       textile recycling and waste reduction beyond the classroom.

9. To enrich students’ learning experiences, various subjects—including English Language,
   Chinese Language, Mathematics, Chinese History, Science, Geography, Biology, Physics, CES,
   Life Education and Religious Education—collaborated with the school library to organize a
   wide range of activities. These included Reading Club activities, study trips, VR reading
   experiences, and more, providing students with diverse and engaging opportunities to deepen
   their knowledge across disciplines.



Reflection:
1. The school has consistently stressed pre-lesson preparation to cultivate students' active
   learning habits. Building on this foundation, the ESR Team recommended explicitly
   connecting pre-lesson tasks with lessons to maximize learning effectiveness. Furthermore, to
   better address learner diversity, the school is prioritizing refined questioning techniques to
   keep students actively engaged during lessons.


2. The upward trends in willingness to face challenges (S11) and goal-setting capabilities (S13)
   demonstrate that moral and affective education directly supports academic perseverance.
   Moving forward, maintaining this positive atmosphere will require sustaining visible
   recognition of student growth, encouraging a growth mindset across all subject areas.


3. Equipping students with suitable learning tools transforms them from passive receivers into
   active learners. To sustain this momentum, the school must continuously invest in teacher
   professional development, ensuring that teaching methodologies stay aligned with evolving
   educational technologies and self-directed learning frameworks.


4. The positive survey feedback on topic exploration (S04) and varied learning activities (S05)
   highlights the success of experiential and project-based approaches. Connecting academic
   concepts to real-world applications not only maintains high motivation but also helps students
   develop holistic problem-solving skills.


5. Assessment for Learning through teachers’ constructive feedback (S02, S15) is recognized by
   both teachers and students. However, narrowing the gap between teacher feedback and student
   improvement requires pairing feedback with individualized learner support. To effectively
   address learner diversity, further instructional scaffolding and targeted interventions will be
   essential to ensure no student is left behind.


6. The implementation of a whole-school approach to hands-on and problem-based learning has
   successfully broadened students' social awareness, deepened their understanding of the SDGs,
   and strengthened their real-world problem-solving skills. Moving forward, the school will
   build on this success by organizing a learning fair to celebrate student achievements under the

                                                                          2025-2026 School Report P. 8
   current three-year school plan. In addition, subject departments will continue to strengthen
   scaffolding for multi-perspective thinking and integrate core values—such as responsibility
   and commitment—alongside technical and presentation skills.


Feedback and Follow-up:
1. To help departments focus on student learning performance during planning and evaluation, the
   success criteria have been revised.

2. To further enhance teaching effectiveness and student engagement, lesson observations will
   specifically focus on how teachers connect pre-lesson tasks to lessons and utilize questioning
   techniques.

3. To celebrate student achievements in SDGs learning and the 12 Priority Values and Attitudes,
   the school will organize a learning fair to highlight the students’ learning outcomes in the
   coming year.

4. Some suggestions for the four targets in Major Concern 1 will be continued:
   -   To design more challenging learning tasks which involve higher-order thinking skills
       (HOTS) /competitive activities (e.g. subject-based inter-class competitions) to enhance
       students’ learning motivation
   -   To help students relate what they learn to daily life to enhance students’ capacity of
       integrating knowledge and skills across curriculum
   -   To expose students to the real world and consolidate the existing knowledge base (e.g.
       broadcasting news within the school and organizing more activities beyond the classroom.)

Achievements:
1.    The Student Christian Fellowship gatherings, worship services and prayer meetings attracted
      steady participation, nurturing faith, moral character, and a sense of belonging. Students
      reflected on perseverance and compassion through hymn sharing and testimonies.
2.    Evangelical events and Religious Week activities were well received, encouraging students
      to connect faith with daily life. Assemblies and lunchtime sharing broadened perspectives
      and strengthened empathy.
3.    Discipline training camps and prefect leadership programmes successfully cultivated
      perseverance, teamwork, and responsibility. Adventure-based activities and civic awareness
      programmes gave students confidence in leadership roles.
4.    Wellness initiatives such as Failure Experience Day, Relaxation Days and the Mental Health
      Corner provided opportunities for stress relief and resilience training. Survey feedback
      confirmed positive impacts on emotional regulation and mental well-being.
5.    Career and life planning programmes, including Applied Learning courses, university gifted
      schemes, and counselling services, guided students in exploring tertiary education and
      career options. Overseas exchange tours broadened cultural awareness and global
      perspectives.
6.    Sports competitions, dodgebee and dodgeball events, and Picnic Day fostered unity,
      belonging, and healthy lifestyles. These activities ranked highly in the School Year-end
      Wellness Activity Survey.
7.    Leadership training for club leaders and nominations to external programmes enhanced
      skills in teamwork, goal setting, and communication. Student leaders applied these skills in
      their clubs and community service projects.
8.    Prize presentation ceremonies celebrated student achievements inside and outside school,
      boosting confidence and reinforcing a culture of appreciation.
9.    Life Education workshops, including growth mindset, harmonious school culture, and
      sustainability programmes, promoted respect, resilience and environmental responsibility.
      Students reported greater awareness of bullying prevention, environmental care, and positive
      values.
10.   National identity programmes, service-learning, sister school exchanges, and SDG-related
      learning activities broadened horizons, strengthened civic awareness, and deepened cultural
      understanding.


Reflection:
1.     Maintaining a balanced focus on spiritual, physical, mental, and social wellness activities is
       essential for supporting students’ holistic development.
2.     The Student Christian Fellowship nurtured belonging and faith, though senior form
       participation was lower. Offering more age-specific topics and interactive formats could
       help sustain engagement.
3.     Evangelical events and Religious Week activities were well received, but assemblies could
       be more closely connected to daily life and include greater interactivity.
4.     Discipline training camps, prefect leadership programmes, and leadership workshops built
       perseverance, teamwork, responsibility, and team spirit. Ongoing reinforcement of these
       skills will help students continue to thrive in their roles.
5.     Wellness initiatives were appreciated, however, some planned counselling groups could not
       be held due to resource constraints, highlighting the need for better resource allocation and

       scheduling.
6.     Career and life planning programmes provided valuable exposure, but some students tended
       to overestimate their academic potential, underscoring the need for earlier and more realistic
       guidance.
7.     Prize Presentation Ceremonies successfully recognized students’ achievements, fostering
       confidence and a strong sense of accomplishment. Continuing to highlight both academic
       and non‑academic accomplishments will further strengthen student motivation.
8.     Picnic Day was the most popular activity of the year, receiving highly positive feedback
       from both students and teachers. Continuing this tradition will further strengthen friendships
       and students’ sense of belonging.
9.     Leadership training for club leaders enhanced teamwork and communication; expanding
       opportunities for junior forms would strengthen succession planning.
10.    Life Education workshops and national identity programmes were effective, but diversifying
       activities and collaborating with NGOs could broaden impact and deepen student
       engagement.


Feedback and Follow-up:
1.    It is recommended to promote the 12 Priority Values and Attitudes through a thematic
      approach, with particular emphasis on responsibility and integrity.
2.    Strengthening students’ generic skills, including communication, critical thinking, and
      collaboration, is encouraged to support holistic development.
3.    Continuing Principal–Parent Tea Gatherings and Principal–Student Dialogues is advised to
      enhance transparency in school policy and provide constructive feedback channels.
4.    Incorporating personal testimonies and scriptural insights into the “Life Freestyle
      Broadcast” is recommended to nurture spiritual growth and foster deeper connections.
5.    Embedding Christian values into career and life planning activities is suggested to support
      personal development rooted in faith.
6.    Promoting anti-drug education is advisable to help students identify risks, thereby
      encouraging healthier choices and a drug‑free lifestyle.
7.    Highlighting the importance of sufficient, high‑quality sleep is recommended to sustain
      mental health, academic performance and overall well‑being.
8.    Enhancing discipline training for S1 students is encouraged to cultivate responsibility and
      self‑management skills.
9.    Ensuring greater consistency in discipline enforcement among teachers is recommended to
      create a fair and structured learning environment.
10.   Leveraging the 4Rs Carnival (Rest, Relationship, Relaxation, Resilience) with diverse
      activities is suggested to foster positive thinking and strengthen unity among students.
11.   Intensifying recognition of student leaders is advised to boost engagement and strengthen
      involvement in school activities.

      collective sense of belonging.
13.   Continuing the organization of Picnic Day is suggested to foster friendships and enhance
      students’ sense of belonging.
14.   Providing national education from multiple perspectives is recommended to ensure a
      well‑rounded understanding of identity and responsibility.
15.   Collaborating with AYP is encouraged to enhance students’ resilience and perseverance.

Results in 2026 HKDSE
A total of 105 candidates have sat for the exams.


All subjects (18 subjects) outperformed the territory averages in Level 2 or above (Level 2 to 5**)
attainment.


Furthermore, our students surpassed the territory average in Level 3 or above (Level 3 to 5**)
attainment across 17 subjects, with the overall percentage of students achieving Level 4 or above
(Level 4 to 5**) exceeding the territory average.


Among core subjects, in addition to a 100% attainment rate in Citizenship and Social
Development, pass rates (Level 2 to 5**) for both Chinese Language and English Language
stood at approximately 99%. Over 80% of our students achieved Level 3 or above (Level 3 to
5**) in English Language, significantly outperforming the territory average (54.6%). The overall
pass rate across core subjects approached 99%, well above the territory average (71.3%).
Through the continuous dedication of our teachers and students, the school has achieved impressive
academic performance.


Our students achieved a higher percentage of Level 5 or above (Level 5 to Level 5**) compared
with all candidates in 8 subjects. In addition, students secured top-tier Level 5* and Level 5**
grades across a wide range of subjects, including Chinese Language, English Language,
Mathematics (Compulsory Part, M1, and M2), Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Business, Accounting
and Financial Studies (BAFS), Economics, and Visual Arts. These impressive achievements are a
testament to our students' dedication.


Additionally, the percentage of our students who obtained a total of 19–35 grade points in the best
five subjects far exceeds the territory average.


This year, over 95% of our graduates are pursuing further studies at universities and other higher
education institutions. Through diversified academic pathways, a substantial number of students have
embarked on professional disciplines dedicated to medical and social care, as well as the fields of law,
engineering, technology, artificial intelligence (AI), and innovation.
