1. Our School

     Vision, Mission Statement and Core Value on Education of Our School Sponsoring Body

     Vision

     Together we nurture the fullness of life; Hand in hand we witness the love of Christ.

     Mission Statement

     With the love of Christ, compassion for humanity and a progressive attitude, we strive to deliver a
     quality education, to develop students’ potential to the fullest, to share with them the Gospel, and

     With the aim of spreading the word of God and serving the community, we provide a holistic
     education to all with loving care, a progressive attitude and total commitment.


     Information about the School

     School Profile

     Heep Woh College is a co-educational aided school. The origin of Heep Woh College can be traced
     back to 1911 when Mrs. Bigelow, from the United States, founded the school in Xiguan, Guangzhou,
     and dedicated herself to early childhood education. In 1921, the private Heep Woh Girls’ Normal
     School was established. The motto was “When you know the truth, the truth will set you free.”
     Principal Liu Fung Ling became the first Chinese principal of Heep Woh. In 1947, Principal Liu
     and Mrs. Schaefer established the Hong Kong Heep Woh School (kindergarten and primary school).
     Heep Woh College commenced operation in 1970 with a view to providing secondary school
     education to teenagers.

Community of Practice (CoP)
In the 2025/26 academic year, the school launched a newly designed Community of Practice (CoP)
program. Introduced as a significantly more comprehensive initiative compared to previous
iterations, the program was developed to foster a vibrant environment where teachers could co-learn,
collaborate, experiment, and share best practices in specific teaching and learning areas.
The initiative was grounded in the concept that a CoP brings together individuals who share a passion
and strive to improve through regular interaction. By participating in this upgraded program, teachers
were provided with expanded access to professional development opportunities, a wealth of
educational resources, and a supportive network aimed at enhancing their instructional strategies.

Topics:
1. AI in Learning and Teaching
2. Self-regulated Learning
3. Thinking Routine (e.g. Making Thinking Visible)
4. Data Mindset (Think and work with Data)
5. Assessment for Learning
6. Miniature Interdisciplinary Learning Project (Project Learning)
7. Enhancing Class Spirit: The Role of the Class Teacher
8. Nurturing Student Leaders

4. Achievements and Reflection on Major Concerns; Feedback and Follow-up
   4.1. Major Concern 1:  Developing students’ healthy lifestyles
 Achievements

    1. To develop students’ skills and attitudes to make healthy choices, including physical
       health, and mental and social well-being, with the aim that students can thrive in all
       areas of their lives

        1.1. Mental Health Initiatives & School Connectedness:
        The school successfully implemented the "4Rs Mental Health Charter," achieving a 70%
        whole-school participation rate through initiatives like "Growth Wednesday" and animal-
        assisted therapy. The Mental Health Corner (靜靜地) decompression space was highly
        utilized, recording 1,283 visits by 108 unique students over 126 days.

        Evidence & Data Triangulation:
             ●    APASO Metrics: The Q-score for Absence of Negative Affect rose significantly from
                  92 to 106 (above the territory norm of 100), with Absence of Anxiety and Depressive
                  Symptoms demonstrating steady progress across all levels. Senior Secondary (SS)
                  students exhibited commendable, statistically significant improvements over the
                  year.
             ●    School Climate: Satisfaction with School (Q=113), Sense of Belonging (Q=113), and
                  Satisfaction with Family and Friends (Q=112) ranked well above territory averages,
                  reinforced by high parental involvement in school learning.
             ●    School-based Survey: 71.4% of students expressed satisfaction with the overall
                  school atmosphere, 65.8% affirmed that the school fosters a harmonious and caring
                  learning environment, and 76.3% reported maintaining positive, trusting
                  relationships with teachers. Furthermore, 68.3% affirmed receiving adequate
                  pastoral support to feel cared for.

        1.2. Physical Health, Social Well-being & Healthy IT Habits:
        Preventive education was delivered through LEAP anti-smoking talks for S1 students, anti-
        drug interactive booths, and targeted guidance on healthy e-device usage. Experiential
        learning programs, such as the "Pause Café" barista scheme and structured board game
        groups, enabled 91% of participants to proactively initiate communication and 81% to
        enhance emotional regulation.

        Evidence & Data Triangulation:
             ●    Healthy IT Habits: APASO Absence of Addictive IT Behaviour achieved a territory-
                  leading Q-score of 116, demonstrating the success of early preventive guidance.
             ●    School-based Survey: Majority of students agreed that the school provided
                  appropriate guidance and support in maintaining healthy living habits.

Feedback and Follow-up
   1. Streamlining Administration and Support
   ● Centralized Scheduling: Enforce a rule requiring activity applications to be submitted 2 to 3
      weeks in advance via a master Google Calendar to prevent scheduling clashes.
   ● Role Clarity and 3-Tier Mental Health Framework: Clearly define the responsibilities of
      committees, social workers, and teachers to avoid duplication. Deploy a 3-tier mental health
      system: Tier 1 (Schoolwide promotion), Tier 2 (Preventive at-risk groups), and Tier 3
      (Targeted in-depth counselling).

    2. Enhancing Holistic Development Tracking
    ● Personal e-Profile (PGP) Expansion: Utilise the 24/7 online PGP system for students to
       build self-directed portfolios, track service and leadership, and integrate fitness tracking data.
       The PGP will be systematically reviewed during class teacher and CLP sessions to facilitate
       coordinated support and allow parents a comprehensive view of non-academic performance.
    ● Whole-School Healthy Habits Framework: Establish explicit targets for MVP60 daily
       physical activity, sleep duration, and digital well-being. These will be integrated into
       classroom micro-practices to raise APASO "Healthy Living" scores.

    3. Elevating Leadership and Community Engagement
    ● Servant Leaders Academy (3-Tier Ladder): Formalize a progressive leadership framework
       to broaden student horizons. Tier 1 focuses on Class and Subject Monitors through training
       workshops; Tier 2 supports Club Chairs, Prefects, and the Student Association; Tier 3 offers
       elite students opportunities for external service, international training, and representing the
       school and Hong Kong.
    ● Deepening S1–S5 Service-Learning: Design structured, cumulative volunteer experiences
       partnering with environmental, elderly, and literacy NGOs. Require capstone reflections and
       align the internal Social Service Award with the Hong Kong Youth Association Volunteer
       Award.
    ● Upgrading External Partnerships: Rigorously review external speakers (partnering with
       bodies like TWGHs Heartline and the Family Planning Association) to ensure interactive,
       age-appropriate delivery for values and life education.

    4. Enhancing the Life Planning
    ● The Life Planning Committee will collaborate closely with Academic Departments to
       institutionalize individualized goal-setting mechanisms and post-secondary pathway
       coaching, aligning students' high educational aspirations with actionable, incremental
       learning strategies.

    5. Promoting Sports and Healthy Habit:
    ● Streamline student case-management protocols and clarify teacher roles across Student
       Support, Guidance, and Discipline teams to optimize pastoral capacity.
    ● Deepen tiered Positive Education and cognitive reframing interventions under the "4Rs
       Mental Health Charter" to strengthen students' stress-coping mechanisms, particularly for
       Senior Secondary students facing examination pressures.
    ● Address the physical fitness deficit by bridging the perceptual gap through structured fitness
       benchmarking (e.g., School Physical Fitness Award Scheme), expanded sports co-curricular
       offerings, and targeted health-literacy campaigns.

4.2. Major Concern 2: Strengthening students’ learning confidence

 Achievements
 1. To stimulate learning motivation and enhance students’ confidence in learning

 1.1. Substantial Enhancements in Instructional Quality, Teacher Support, and Academic
 Performance
 In the past academic year, the school made concerted efforts to enhance the instructional repertoire
 of teaching staff and build an encouraging academic culture. These endeavors yielded exemplary
 outcomes in system-level indicators. According to the APASO, the norm-referenced Q-Score for
 Clear Instructional Guidance reached a remarkable 114, placing the school within the top tier
 among all Hong Kong secondary schools. Concurrently, the Q-Score for Teacher Support stood at
 101, demonstrating that students deeply recognize our teachers’ commitment to scaffolded
 learning, approachable mentoring, and individualized classroom assistance.
 These positive findings are corroborated by the Year-end School-based Questionnaire, in which
 instructional quality and teacher support received strong positive ratings from students exceeding
 the 70% benchmark:

     ● Most teachers can cater for my learning ability during instruction. (Strongly Agree:
       15.94%, Agree: 56.18% — Total: 72.12%)
     ● Most teachers provide me with appropriate feedback during lessons. (Strongly Agree:
       19.39%, Agree: 52.59% — Total: 71.98%)
     ● Most teachers have a good grasp of my learning progress. (Strongly Agree: 15.94%, Agree:
       54.98% — Total: 70.92%)

 These pedagogical improvements directly translated into notable academic milestones. Most
 prominently, the HKDSE English Language passing rate recorded a substantial year-on-year
 increase, an achievement that affirms the effectiveness of our targeted curriculum adjustments and
 intensive baseline support. Qualitative observations across classrooms further indicate that
 students, especially at the Junior Secondary level, exhibit significantly greater confidence in using
 English for routine classroom discourse and simple presentations. In terms of talent development
 and public speaking, a growing cohort of students actively stepped forward to represent the school
 in open English speech contests, territory-wide competitions, and symposiums, reflecting a
 tangible boost in academic self-concept and expressive confidence.

 1.2. Broad Curriculum Competence, Generic Skills, and Information Literacy
 The school evaluated student performance across the 7LG through structured departmental and
 committee reviews. The data demonstrates that the curriculum successfully fostered comprehensive
 intellectual development:

     ● Generic Skills: Rated at 4.783 / 7.0 by Academic Departments and 4.875 / 7.0 by
       Functional Committees, reflecting well-honed collaboration, communication, and critical
       thinking abilities among students.
     ● Breadth of Knowledge: Evaluated at 4.762 / 7.0 by Academic Departments and 4.670 / 7.0
       by Functional Committees, confirming that our cross-disciplinary curriculum broadened
       students’ intellectual horizon beyond textbooks.
     ● Information Literacy (IL): Academic Departments rated students’ competence at 4.230 /
       7.0, categorized as a solid and proficient standard.

 The school also proactively modernized its digital education infrastructure. High-speed campus-
 wide Wi-Fi infrastructure was upgraded, dedicated school-based server databases were
 constructed, and enterprise-grade educational cloud platforms were procured. These technical

 1.3. Robust External Partnerships and Professional Communities of Practice (CoP)
 To foster evidence-based teaching methodologies, the school engaged external professional bodies
 to build cross-departmental Communities of Practice (CoP):

     ● CUHK Quality School Improvement Project (QSIP): Extensive collaborative lesson
       planning, curriculum mapping, and instructional workshops were conducted across diverse
       departments, including HMSC, English, Chinese, Chinese History, History, Life and
       Society (L&S), and CSC/CSD.
     ● EDB School-based Support Services (SBSS): The curriculum development officers
       provided specialized on-site consultation to optimize the school-based curriculum and

 These external collaborations cultivated a collegial culture of peer observation and pedagogical
 dialogue. Concurrently, a dedicated Digital Learning and AI CoP was formed internally, where
 pioneer teachers developed and shared AI-assisted formative assessment tools, structured Thinking
 Routines, and Self-Regulated Learning (SRL) strategies.

 1.4. Consolidation of Remedial Support and Learning Foundations
 The school systematically strengthened its academic intervention mechanisms for lower-progress
 learners. Through structured post-school tutorials, stratified learning materials, and customised
 assessment papers, junior-form students who previously struggled with academic transitions

5. Our Learning and Teaching
To enhance learning and teaching effectiveness and consolidate the major concern of "Strengthening
students’ learning confidence and motivation," a comprehensive series of school-wide pedagogical
initiatives, targeted tier-support programmes, and professional development platforms were
implemented throughout the 2025/26 academic year. The key operational details and achievements
are outlined below:

Professional Capacity Building: Assessment for Learning (AfL) and Questioning Skills
To strengthen teachers’ capacity to track student learning progress systematically, the Academic
Executive Committee (AEC) collaborated with the Quality School Improvement Project (QSIP) to
deliver whole-school training on Assessment for Learning (AfL) and classroom questioning
techniques during the First Staff Development Day. The sessions deepened teachers’ pedagogical
awareness in using formative assessment data and purposeful questioning to provide actionable,
timely feedback.

To embed these practices into everyday classroom routines, AEC established a cross-departmental
Community of Practice (CoP) comprising seven subject teachers (HMSC, English, Chinese, Chinese
History, History, Life & Society, and Citizenship & Social Development). Between October and
June, CoP members engaged in professional dialogues, collaborative lesson planning, and conducted
two open research lessons, culminating in a school-wide concluding sharing session. This initiative
established a culture of reflective practice, equipping teachers with the confidence to deploy
structured questioning and AfL tools to help students monitor their own learning milestones.

Digital Transformation, Pre-lesson Preparation, and Active Classroom Participation
In close partnership with the eLearning Team, the school actively explored digital tools and blended
instructional strategies to boost classroom engagement. Through professional development mini-
workshops and targeted resource sharing, teachers expanded their digital teaching toolkit.
A central emphasis was placed on pre-lesson learning and preparation enabled by eLearning
platforms. Subject departments incorporated interactive tasks, digital note-taking, structured group
discussions, and collaborative peer presentations into regular units. This shift successfully
transformed traditional teacher-led delivery into a student-centred environment characterized by
active inquiry and collaborative problem-solving.

Junior Secondary Transition and Study Skills Development
To support S1 entrants in adapting smoothly to secondary school academic demands, a structured
study skills workshop was conducted at the start of the academic year. The workshop achieved high
student agreement in helping newcomers understand their VARK learning styles, adopt effective
note-taking systems, and establish disciplined daily study routines. The learning style profiles will be
integrated into students' Personal Growth Portfolios to inform ongoing academic guidance.
To strengthen English-medium learning readiness across the curriculum, cross-curricular
collaboration was initiated among S1 subjects. Academic departments worked together to help
students build a solid academic vocabulary base across disciplines, scaffolding technical terms and
subject-specific language to boost their reading comprehension and expressive confidence.

Tiered Learning Support, Early Intervention, and Peer Tutoring
To ensure that no student was left behind, the school maintained a responsive early-identification and
multi-tiered intervention framework. Following the First Term Examination, students with repeated
unsatisfactory academic results attended structured goal-setting sessions with the Vice Principal to
diagnose learning bottlenecks and formulate actionable recovery plans. In parallel, teachers served as
mentors to provide individualised care, monitor academic progress, and nurture effective study
habits. To reinforce foundational knowledge and bridge persistent learning gaps, a dedicated Summer
Boost Programme was also organised for students requiring supplementary academic consolidation.

strategies focused on students’ physical, mental, and social well-being, as well as enhance reading
motivation through engaging supplementary materials and interactive sharing activities.

6.5. Mental Health
The Guidance Committee has been dedicated to promoting the 4Rs Mental Health Charter by
providing comprehensive mental health information and training for students, parents, and teachers.
To reinforce our support network, we aim to train the Guidance Prefect Team into "Student
Gatekeepers." Through systematic, school-based training supplemented by specialised thematic
workshops delivered by external professional organisations, our Guidance Prefects are equipped to
recognise common emotional and mental health issues. They also acquire practical skills in peer care,
responding to online help-seeking requests from peers, and assisting teachers in managing on-campus
crisis incidents. The establishment of peer support as a protective factor is well reflected in the SHS
(e.g. Q. 25), where students indicated that they maintain harmonious relationships with their peers. It
has become a common practice for students to proactively accompany peers in need of emotional
support to seek assistance from teachers or four school social workers.

A strong culture of "peer mutual support" was highly visible this academic year, with students actively
encouraging and accompanying peers facing emotional distress to participate in school activities. This
positive shift is closely tied to the Guidance Prefects' robust promotional efforts in advocating
"Relationship" across the campus. Furthermore, according to the "Guidance Prefect Training
Effectiveness Survey," 100% of the trained Guidance Prefects agreed that "the training enhanced my
confidence in conveying the message that students can seek help from trusted adults (such as teachers,
social workers, and parents) when facing difficulties," and that "after training, I feel more confident in
helping peers undergoing emotional distress (e.g., persuading or accompanying peers in need to seek
support/assistance)." These findings demonstrate that adequate training not only strengthens peer
relationships but also effectively fosters a spirit of mutual care and watchfulness. The fruitful outcomes
of positive teacher-student interactions are also reflected in the positive metrics of SHS (Q. 26 and 28).
Additionally, the committee launched the "Let’s Grow" lunchtime broadcasting interview series every
Wednesday. Through the personal stories shared by teachers and students, the program promotes a
growth mindset to combat adversity and enhance students' psychological resilience. In the post-
pandemic era, students' anxieties have gradually stabilised, a trend validated by the positive data
regarding student emotions in APASO (Q. 1 to 3).

To extend support to families, the committee deliberately organised two Friday evening parent
seminars. To align with our specific school context, these seminars were hosted by our own teachers
to enhance parents' understanding of adolescent development. The seminars received highly favorable
feedback; the post-activity survey indicated that 100% of the respondent parents agreed that "the
seminar allowed me to better understand my children's thoughts and feelings," and 100% stated that "I
feel I can apply what I learned tonight to real-life situations." Empowering parents with the confidence
to take "action" is crucial for them to effectively fulfill their roles as "Parent Gatekeepers."
Regarding staff development, the committee compiled the "School-based Teacher Gatekeeper

Manual" this academic year, aiming for "early identification and early intervention" to support students
with potential mental health risks. The manual is designed to aid colleagues in identifying students'
mental health needs and providing corresponding support, thereby boosting their confidence in
performing their duties as "Teacher Gatekeepers."

6.7. Career and Life Planning
The school has implemented a comprehensive Career and Life Planning (CEP) curriculum from S1
to S6, which is integrated across various subjects. The curriculum is designed to help students
understand their interests and strengths, explore multiple academic and career pathways, and
cultivate positive attitudes. Through classroom instruction and experiential activities, we aim to build
students' self-confidence and inspire them to pursue healthy and purposeful lives.

The CEP curriculum is delivered effectively at each key stage. For S1 and S2 students, learning is
integrated into Life Education lessons and Life-wide Learning Day, including visits to CUHK and
HKUST, which enable students to develop self-understanding and explore diverse opportunities. S3
students complete five sessions as part of the Taster Programme , actively engaging in values-based
reflection. Senior form students receive courses as scheduled, with highly positive feedback. Survey
results indicate that nearly 95% of S5 and S6 students are satisfied with the programme. Over 90%
agree that it helps them understand post-secondary options, set goals, and formulate preliminary life
plans, while 80% report increased confidence for future studies. Approximately 90% find individual
counselling helpful in making informed decisions.

A wide range of CEP-related activities has been successfully organised to enhance students' self-
awareness and confidence. Notable examples include the S6 Mock Results Release and tertiary
institution visits, both of which received over 90% satisfaction rates and significantly improved
students' preparedness. A CEP Education Day was organised for S2 to S5 students, featuring
university guided tours, experiential workshops on emerging STEAM careers, and site visits to
government organisations. The S6 Mentoring Scheme effectively supported students in exploring
diverse pathways, with 90% acknowledging its usefulness in making post-secondary choices.
Additionally, over 95% of students enrolled in Applied Learning Courses expressed satisfaction, and
88% reported increased confidence.

9. Feedback on Future Planning

Celebrating Milestone Achievements: Innovation, Leadership, and Academic Excellence
The 2025–2026 academic year marked a momentous milestone for Heep Woh College, defined by
remarkable academic breakthroughs, international recognition for school-based innovation, and the
vibrant cultivation of student leadership.

Academically, our graduating cohort achieved outstanding results in the 2026
HKDSE. Most notably, our intensive curriculum refinement and tailored baseline support delivered
an exceptional leap in English Language performance, underscoring our students’ rising academic
competence and language self-efficacy.

On the technological and pastoral front, our school-designed AI Personalised Growth Portfolio (PGP)
achieved global acclaim, capturing both the Gold Award and a prestigious ranking among the Top 10
Best Inventions at the International Invention Innovation Competition (iCAN). More than an award-
winning innovation, the PGP serves as a digital platform that holistically tracks student development,
actively safeguards their mental and physical well-being, and reinforces healthy digital habits.
Building upon this global success, we will upgrade the platform in the coming academic year with an
interactive AI-assisted reflection module to further scaffold students’ metacognitive abilities and
habit of deep reflection.

Our signature programmes have flourished into hallmarks of cross-disciplinary excellence and talent
development:
   ● The Miniature Arts Cross-Disciplinary Learning Programme has matured into an institutional
       flagship, empowering students to curate, research, and present Chinese culture and local
       history through bilingual guided tours and creative craftsmanship.
   ● The Religious Education Mentorship Programme was successfully institutionalized across
       Secondary 1, 5, and 6, pairing students with dedicated teacher-mentors to provide
       personalized spiritual guidance, character formation, and pastoral care during critical
       transitional years.
   ● The Servant Leaders Academy continued to unlock students’ leadership potential. Our student
       leaders distinguished themselves across prominent arenas, garnering honors such as the Top
       10 Outstanding Student Leaders of Hong Kong, Kowloon Region Top 10 Outstanding
       Students, Wong Tai Sin Top 10 Outstanding Students, and selection into the prestigious
       UNICEF Young Envoys Programme. Backed by dedicated scholarship support, student
       representatives participated in cultural exchanges at UK boarding schools and attended elite
       summer leadership institutes at the Universities of Oxford and Cambridge.

Central to these student achievements was our investment in teacher capacity. The school launched a
newly expanded Community of Practice (CoP) framework to nurture a collaborative culture of
professional inquiry. Across eight specialized clusters—AI in Learning and Teaching, Self-Regulated
Learning (SRL), Thinking Routines (Making Thinking Visible), Data Mindset, Assessment for
Learning (AfL), Miniature Project Learning, Enhancing Class Spirit and the Class Teacher’s Role,
and Nurturing Student Leaders—our teachers co-planned, trialed pedagogical innovations, and
shared best practices, establishing a unified professional language across subject panels.

Evidence-Based Reflection and Strategic Transition: The 2026–2029 Development Cycle
As this academic year concludes our 2023–2026 Three-Year Development Cycle, the entire teaching
faculty engaged in a thorough, months-long Planning-Implementation-Evaluation (PIE) review
during staff meetings and School Development Days. By rigorously triangulating stakeholder

Version 5: 2-9-2026 1153




perceptions, public examination performance, and affective outcomes, we evaluated our institutional
strengths and identified the core needs of our students.
Our diagnostic review revealed three distinct areas requiring concerted future action:
    1. From Awareness to Daily Practice: While health awareness and school belonging are
        exceptionally high, students require structured guidance to translate cognitive understanding
        into enduring daily routines—specifically regarding sleep hygiene, balanced digital usage,
        regular physical exercise, and refined social etiquette.
    2. From Passive Confidence to Proactive Autonomy: Despite heightened classroom confidence
        and improved baseline benchmarks, students must transition from teacher-dependent learning
        into proactive, self-directed learners who master systematic study routines, higher-order
        thinking, and continuous academic goal-setting to further enhance value-added performance
        across all subjects.
    3. From Identity to Humanistic Global Citizenship: While national identity is firmly embedded,
        students need broader platforms to connect national developments with international realities,
        utilizing technological literacy to champion social good, ethical values, and sustainable
        development.

In direct response to these student needs and the Education Bureau’s Blueprint for Digital Education
Development in Primary and Secondary Schools, our professional team forged a strong consensus on
the strategic roadmap for the 2026–2029 School Development Plan, balancing continuity with
intentional transformation across three Major Concerns (2026–2029):

●    Cultivating a Healthy Lifestyle and Daily Habit: Moving from health awareness to concrete
     habit formation by embedding MVP60 physical activity standards, sleep routines, and AI PGP
     tracking to nurture physical vitality and positive digital-social habits.
●    Nurturing Responsible and Proactive Learners: Progressing from baseline confidence to self-
     directed excellence through "AI + Curriculum" integration, high-participation classroom
     pedagogies, and structured study-skills coaching to maximize value-added growth.
●    Nurturing Talents with Digital Literacy and Humanistic Qualities: Deepening national
     identity and global perspective under the vision of "Technology for Good," combining UN
     SDGs, National Security Education, and flagship interdisciplinary projects to foster
     compassionate, digitally adept leaders.
We offer our deepest gratitude to God for His abundant grace, wisdom, and steadfast protection
throughout this past three-year cycle. We also extend our heartfelt appreciation to our devoted
teachers, supportive parents, active alumni, and diligent students whose united efforts have elevated
Heep Woh College to new heights.

Stepping with faith and confidence into the 2026–2029 triennium, we remain dedicated to our
calling: providing an inspiring, values-driven, and forward-looking whole-person education that
empowers every Heep Woh student to thrive, lead, and shine for the betterment of our community,
nation, and world.
