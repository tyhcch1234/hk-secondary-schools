Our School
The school is one of the 32 Sheng Kung Hui secondary schools in Hong Kong and is named after Bishop
Mok Sau Tseng. Founded in 1975, it has a history of 49 years and is a co-educational secondary school.


Mission
Our mission is to provide the best opportunities for the formation of Christian and humanistic values, to
foster constructive attitudes and develop a range of skills in order that our students can develop their
potential to participate fully in society and become a responsible citizen of the world.

We would endeavour to provide a caring and loving environment where everyone is regarded with
mutual trust, respect and love to make the years we spend in MST a most nurturing and happy
experience.

Evaluation of Annual School Plan 2023 – 24
Major concern 1: To enhance learning effectiveness
Intended Outcome (1): Embrace learner diversity and stretch students’ potential to the fullest

Achievements:
   Lift-off classes to cater for learner diversity
     -   S1-S3 pilot classes were arranged on Saturdays to cater for learners’ diversity in Chinese Language, English
         Language and Mathematics.
     -   S1-S2 elite programmes were implemented in 4 domains: 1) English Language and humanities, 2) Chinese
         Language, 3) Mathematics, and 4) Science
     -   Chinese Language department arranged writing classes for S5 students, supplementary classes on
         designated passage of classical Chinese for the under-achieved S4 & S5 students.
   Extra support was provided to S6 students in preparing for DSE examination
     -   Support measures in achieving better results:
          ‧     Supplementary classes for elective subjects on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays, and
                supplementary classes for Mathematics extended module on Thursdays
          ‧     Supplementary classes for low achievers in core subjects on Thursdays
          ‧     Academic mentorship programme was implemented for students with 26 points or above in the best 5
                DSE subjects in their S5 annual results.
          ‧     Oral practice sessions among our students and with students from other schools
     -   Support measures in coping with the pressure of the DSE examination:
          ‧     The Counselling and Guidance Committee set up “Cheer Up” stations for S.6 students and organized
                workshops and sharing sessions for their parents, offering tips on how to support their children in
                preparation for the HKDSE results release.
          ‧     To show support to our S.6 students who would take public examinations, the Alumni Association
                handed out soft ice-cream. The ice-cream van serving the event distributed a total of seven hundred
                ice-cream treats.
          ‧     Disney Theme Park Adventures can provide students with both a learning opportunity and a joyful
                escape from the pressures of preparing for the DSE.
   Students’ learning experiences was enhanced with activities beyond the classroom:
     -   Our subject departments have organized various activities, including visits, field trips, and experiential
         learning sessions.
     -   Our students have excelled in elections and competitions across music, visual arts, dance, sports, and
         academic events. Details of their achievements are on the last few pages of this report.

Evaluation:
   Lift-off classes to cater for learner diversity
     -   For the S1-S3 pilot classes, the overall attendance rate was 89% which is in line with the expectation. Most
         students reflected in the questionnaires that the classes are useful. Tutors observed that most students had
         good attitudes toward learning and well preformed in the classes.
     -   For the S1-S2 elite programe, students provided positive feedback on the elite programs, noting that the
         English Language and humanities class enhanced their public speaking, research, writing, questioning, and
                                                                                                                      7
             critical thinking skills. In the Mathematics program, participants achieved excellent results in competitions,
             with 16 students receiving awards in the semi-finals and 6 in the finals of the Hua Xia Mathematics
             Olympiad. Additionally, 7 students earned awards in the Asia International Mathematical Olympiad Open
             Contest Semi-Final. For the Chinese Language program, the majority of participants found the course lively
             and interesting, which cultivated their creativity and enhanced their writing skills. It also helped them
             understand the writing styles of Hong Kong authors. Additionally, they felt the tram tour was innovative
             and engaging, helping them learn about Hong Kong's cultural landscape and aligning with themes of
             literature, history, and social development. For the Science program, 72.73% of S1 students rated the
             program “excellent” and 27.27% S1 students rated the program “good”. Meanwhile, 50% of S2 students
             rated the program “excellent” and 50% S2 students rated the program “good”. Thus, both S1 and S2
             students agreed that the program was effective
        -    For the S5 Chinese Language writing classes, the students' feedback was positive. The final exam average
             writing scores for S5 students improved by 2.82 to 5.4 points compared to the previous semester's exam.
             This shows that the course has helped enhance students' writing potential.
   Extra support was provided to S6 students in preparing for DSE examination
        -    Focus group meetings with three groups of S6 students—outstanding, good, and average HKDSE results—
             were conducted after the results were released. The majority agreed that the extra support was helpful in
             managing the pressure of the DSE examination and achieving better results.
        -    Students reflected that the inter-school oral practice was very effective in improving their oral exam skills
             and their confidence.
        -    Most of those students joined the academic mentorship program found the program useful in helping them
             in preparing for HKDSE and choice of study pathways. Some students with above-average results in S5
             annual results reflected that they also want to join the mentorship program.
        -    Over 70% of low-achieving S6 students in core subjects showed improvements, and the percentage meeting
             the 332A requirements was very good.
        -    Our 2024 HKDSE results were good, reflecting that the support measures were effective.
   Students’ learning experiences was enhanced with activities beyond the classroom:
        -    In the subject and committee annual reports, teachers noted that students found the learning activities
             engaging, enriching their knowledge and broadening their horizons.

Feedback and Follow-up:
   The S.1-S.3 pilot classes were effective in helping low achievers, and we will expand the scale of these classes.
   The elite programs will be extended to S.3.
   For S.6 support measures, we will:
    -       expand the scale of the academic mentorship program,
    -       liaise with more schools to provide students with additional opportunities for inter-school oral practice, and
    -       keep all other S.6 support measures.

Intended Outcome (2): Develop a collaborative culture in learning

Achievements:
   Nurture a collaborative culture among students
     -   Collaborative learning strategies, like group work and group project, in learning and assessment are widely
         used in learning activities in various subjects.
     -   Revision classes and quizzes were conducted by class associations. Some classes made use of e-learning
         tools in the revision classes.
     -   S2 cross curriculum project day involving Chinese History, Geography, history and Life and Society was
         conducted. Students were divided into groups of five to six students. Each group was required to design a
         one-day historical tour of Tai Po.
   Nurture a collaborative culture and professional sharing among teachers
     -   Two major sessions and two minor sessions of TCLT were arranged for professional sharing.
     -   In addition to formal appraisals by the principal and department heads, over 50% of our teachers
         participated in peer lesson observations. Post-observation meetings facilitated professional sharing and the
         exchange of good practices.

Evaluation:
   Based on teachers’ observations, the collaborative learning approach has effectively achieved the goals of
    enhancing breadth of knowledge, language proficiency, and developing generic skills. By actively engaging
    students in hands-on activities, fostering discussions and peer interactions, and promoting self-directed learning.
    this approach has provided students with a comprehensive education and equipped them with vital skills for their
    academic and personal development.
   Besides TLCT and lesson observations, informal exchanges among teachers about teaching methods and
    common student mistakes were widely noted. The collaborative culture and professional sharing among teachers
    were strong.

Feedback and Follow-up:
   The atmosphere of peer learning among students could be further cultivated.

Whole Person Development
Our school emphasizes on whole person development of each students. Training programmes and
activities are organized to assist positive development in the following aspects:


Religious and Evangelical Affairs

     The Religious and Evangelical Affairs Committee aims to help students to develop a positive view
towards life, enhance their understanding of Christianity and encourage them to embody Christian faith
in daily life.

   Religious morning broadcasts and praise and prayer sessions in assemblies were held by various
    Christian teachers, with generous assistance given by the Revd. Tony Au Yeung Siu Kei and Mr.
    Oliver Chin Kan Yuen of HKSKH Church of Our Saviour, to broaden students' horizons
   The Revd. Au Yeung was invited to share messages at both our Christmas Service and Easter Service.
   Gospel Week was held in May. This year's theme was "Encountering God in Our Difficult Times",
    started off by an alumna who shared her testimony in a morning broadcast session. On specific days
    during the week, Christian hymns and praise songs were played, along with some sharing, to raise
    students' awareness of their intrapersonal and spiritual condition. In addition, HKSKH Church of Our
    Saviour organized a book fair that lasted 4 days to introduce students to different Christian
    resources. One of the highlights of Gospel Week was a music and drama performance held by
    students during lunch time. Moreover, Dr. Ho Lun, who evangelizes using magic, came and shared
    with our students in a whole-school assembly. Over 60 students either decided to give their lives to
    Jesus or expressed interest in being discipled. We praise God for all His work.
   Christian Fellowship gatherings were held during the academic year, providing opportunities for
    students to gain a deeper understanding of the Christian faith.
   Boys' Brigade gatherings were held to enhance the discipline and responsibility of participants and
    introduce Christianity to students.


Moral, Civic and National Education
    The Moral, Civic and National Education Committee aims at promoting values education, civic
education and national education.

   Values Education is an essential element of whole-person education which aims at fostering
    students' positive values and attitudes through the learning and teaching of various Key Learning
    Areas or subjects and the provision of relevant learning experiences. The Committee upholds the
    belief that "Everyone should have empathy". Cultivating care and empathy involves understanding
    and meeting the needs of others with concern and love. By promoting care and empathy in students,
    they can develop a friendly and caring attitude towards family, teachers, and friends. This can
    extend to caring for the welfare of vulnerable communities, even to loving and protecting all living
    things, and practicing "treating others with kindness, and loving all creatures". This can help prevent
    and eliminate bullying and animal cruelty incidents, and build a caring campus and harmonious
    society. An "MST Voluntary Service Team" programme was conducted for all S.4 and S.5 classes to
    serve different groups of needy people. Students gained valuable experience and developed
    positive values in relation to caring for others and dedication to the community.
                                                                                                        14
   The "Running Talents" programme was co-organized with the PE Department in this academic year.
    This programme used running as a theme, aiming to enhance the self-image of teenagers, boost
    their confidence, and foster social connections. Our school, in collaboration with another
    participating school, TWGHs Wong Fung Ling College, organized a 2-day-1-night training camp at
    the YMCA Tseung Kwan O Youth Camp in February. Under the guidance of coaches and teachers in
    charge, participating students explored landmarks in the Tseung Kwan O area through running
    during the training camp. These landmarks included Devil's Peak Battery, Tseung Kwan O Cross Bay

    fostered team spirit but also allowed students to venture beyond Tai Po, personally visit different
    communities in Hong Kong, and get to know their unique characteristics, thus broadening their
    horizons.
   In response to the need to cultivate students' positive values and capacity conducive to their whole
    person development, one form period on having a positive attitude towards assessments was
    organized for S.1 to S.5 students to empower them to overcome challenges and failures with
    courage and perseverance. Moreover, one form period was organized for all forms to alert students
    to the unhealthy elements in our society and enable them to make rational judgement.
   The Classroom Management Scheme was implemented to nurture students' sense of responsibility.
   The Personal Growth Profile Scheme, a joint effort of the Extracurricular Activities Committee, the
    Counselling and Guidance Committee and the Moral, Civic & National Education Committee, was
    implemented from S.1 to S.6 to cultivate students' self-directed personal development.
   With the aim to promote national education and enhance students' understanding of China, Flag
    Raising, Thematic Board Display and Thematic Book Fair were held on ceremony days, such as
    National Foundation Day, National Constitution Day and National Security Education Day. Moreover,
    our students continued to participate in the "Constitution and Basic Law Student Ambassadors
    Training Scheme". A national education assembly with the theme of China's minorities was held in
    March. With the assistance of the ambassadors, they shared the customs, religious beliefs, culture,
    traditional costumes and languages of the Miao, Naxi, Tibetan, and Dai ethnic groups. The students
    were encouraged to visit the magnificent mountains and rivers of our motherland to broaden their
    horizons and learn how to respect and appreciate the culture of minorities.

Academic Affairs
     The Academic Committee aims to develop students' academic potential, encourage students and
help them to pursue excellence.

   Results in internal examinations, as well as external assessments such as the HKDSE were analyzed
    to help enhance learning and teaching.
   Small group teaching in junior core subjects and, pilot classes in junior forms were held on Saturdays.
   To develop students’ advanced writing skills, higher order thinking skills and presentation skills, and
    empower them to master higher level mathematical and scientific reasoning, elite classes in
    Humanities, Mathematics and Science were held in S.1 and S.2.
   Nomination of students for gifted education programmes was coordinated and organized to cater
    for learners' diversity.
   Various support measures for S.6 students were implemented such as commencement of school
    lessons for S.6 students in mid-August 2023, regular supplementary lessons held after school on
