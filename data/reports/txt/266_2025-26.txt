(1)     Our School
Sha Tin Methodist College is an aided co-educational EMI secondary school founded in 1983. It is sponsored
by the Methodist Church, Hong Kong to serve the local community together with churches and service centres
in the Sha Tin parish. In 2025-26, there are a total of 722 students in 25 classes from Form One to Form Six.

Vision
To develop a holistic education based upon the Christian faith; to nurture our students to live an abundant life
through the preaching of the Gospel.
本基督精神，發展全人教育；藉宣講福音，培育豐盛生命。


Mission and beliefs
Provide whole-person education according to the spirit of Jesus Christ; Nurture abundant lives by teaching
the gospel.

We believe:

That each and every student is the love of God and endowed individually. We therefore respect each of them
and help them develop their potential to the fullest.

That through whole-person education on moral, intellectual, physical, social, aesthetic and spiritual aspects,
every student will be able to model themselves after Jesus Christ, to “grow in wisdom and stature, and in favor
with God and men.” (Luke 2:52)

That the meaning of life does not lie in material affluence but in the quality of life. The gospel of Jesus Christ
is the basis for development of a holistic life: “I have come that they may have life, and have it to the full.”
(John 10:10)

That education is teaching by word and deed, just as what Jesus Christ did to his disciples.

The two targets in Priority Task 2 were largely achieved, with positive responses from both students and

⮚    The first target, “Cultivate students’ self-awareness: Enhance students’ self-awareness;
     strengthen their understanding and expression of emotions, recognition of character strengths,
     and ability to apply them to promote greater self-acceptance and self-worth.” was successfully

     understanding of character strengths, data shows that interpersonal dynamics, parent-child
     relationships, and academic expectations remain closely linked to anxiety and depressive

     appreciation and affirmation to reinforce interpersonal relationships and social awareness.

     enhancing their executive functioning and self-management skills” was also largely achieved.
     Students have made measurable progress in goal setting, learning methodology, emotional
     regulation, and stress alleviation.

Feedback and follow up
⮚ This year's SEL initiatives have proven strategically sound and effective in enhancing self-awareness
  and self-management. Moving forward, room for growth remains in students' emotional regulation, self-
  management, and self-discipline.
⮚ This year marked the school's initial phase of implementing Social-Emotional Learning (SEL), with
  strategic focuses strictly placed on Self-Awareness and Self-Management. In the upcoming academic
  year, this framework will undergo a progressive expansion, shifting the pedagogical locus from the self
  to others. The school will advance to the next developmental stage by actively promoting Relationship
  Skills and Social Awareness across the student body.
⮚ Students have acquired a foundational understanding of character strengths, and the school will focus on
  deepening their practical application in the coming academic year. In alignment with the upcoming
  promotion of Social-Emotional Learning (SEL), with a specific focus on Relationship Skills, emphasis
  will be placed on encouraging both teacher-student and peer-to-peer identification and appreciation of
  each other's strengths. This initiative aims to enhance students' self-efficacy and foster a positive
  campus culture rooted in mutual appreciation.
⮚ Student anxiety and depressive tendencies remain key areas of concern. Students' academic
  expectations, peer dynamics, and parent-child relationships significantly impact their emotional well-
  being. In the upcoming academic year, alongside promoting Relationship Skills within the Social-
  Emotional Learning (SEL) framework, the school will introduce Social Awareness initiatives. These

English and Chinese) and committees like the counseling team, consisting of global and language issues and
positive education.

The Implementation of Language across Curriculum (LaC): To facilitate the implementation of Language
across Curriculum (LaC) at STMC, the LaC Committee collaborated with the Education Department Bureau
(EDB) and The Hong Kong Polytechnic University to co-organize professional development workshops. The
training enhanced the understanding of English as a Medium of Instruction (EMI) subject teachers, providing
them with valuable insights and practical knowledge on incorporating language skills into subject-specific
content.

Cross-curricular language learning materials, for example, LaC booklets, reading journals, LaC learning packs
and powerpoint slides have been developed to strengthen collaboration among the EMI subject panels and
facilitate classroom learning. Regular and systematic surveys and discussion meetings have also been
conducted on the examination and analyses of our student’s language needs in learning EMI subjects.

All the activities have showcased robust connection between English language and other EMI subjects and
exemplified the dedication of our school to cultivating interdisciplinary learning experiences for students.

2. Promoting STEAM Education
The STEAM curriculum is formally introduced at STMC in Form 1 and 2 to strengthen students’ ability to
integrate and apply knowledge and skills, nurture their creativity, collaboration, and problem-solving skills,
and foster their spirit of innovation, as required in the 21st century. Hands-on and minds-on activities, which
may be theme-based or problem-based, are incorporated to enable students to solve problems and create
designs through scientific investigation, coding, mathematical modeling, and design-and-make projects in
both formal lessons and extracurricular clubs. STMC students have participated in and won awards at local,
national, and international STEAM competitions, showcasing their interests and unleashing their potential.
This year, two students, Yeung Kwan Ngai (3B) and Anthony Leung (3C), represented Hong Kong in an
international competition in Korea, winning the Grand Prize in the “Inventor & Maker” division. Within the
school, STEAM Week, held annually in March, cultivates students’ interest and curiosity in science and
technology.

5. Stepping up Gifted Education
We believe that every student is a gift from God and our students are all gifted. The school has adopted the
Three-Tier Implementation Model of Gifted Education, i.e. three levels of engagement, including school-
based whole-class learning (Level 1), supplemented by school-based pull-out enrichment and extension
programs (Level 2), and off-site intensive support services (Level 3) since 2000. Pull-Out Gifted Education
Programs (Level 2) were held after school at STMC on Mondays and Saturdays. About 160 students of junior
forms are recruited to nurture their giftedness in 9 specific gifted classes, namely Chinese Language, English
Language(Junior / Senior), Mathematics, Science, PSHE, MUN (Model United Nations), STEAM and
Coding respectively. The learning experience, performance of students and reflections were kept as a kind of
student portfolio.

5. Strengthening Values Education
Positive and Christian values education has always been our main focus and is variously delivered across
different KLAs, subjects and committees at STMC. Students are nurtured to be a responsible National and
Global Citizen starting from F.1. Subjects and committees had embedded national security, 12 priority
values and Education for Sustainable Development naturally into formal and informal learning platforms.

To foster students’ national identity and love for our own culture, our school launched an inter-disciplinary
Chinese Culture Week from 9/2/26 to 12/2/2026. Game booths, calligraphy and couplets writing booth,
expert talk, lunchtime performance, PTH song dedication etc. were held throughout the week. The Chinese
Culture Week was a great success, as both teachers, students, parents, alumni and students from Shanghai all
gave very positive feedback. It fostered a deep sense of patriotism in our school.

STMC has incorporated e-learning into the school curriculum and developed e-resources such as eClass,
Google Classroom platforms, education apps and AI tools. All teachers are allocated iPads to facilitate
teaching. All students are entitled to “Bring Your Own Device” (BYOD), i.e. iPad as a learning tool to
facilitate self-directed learning, peer learning, assessment, and quick feedback. All classrooms and special
rooms have set up interactive TVs to facilitate learning.

Life Education

Our school aims to implement Life Education, which serves as a platform to provide Value Education.
Through the School-based Formal Curriculum which includes Form Teacher Lessons and Religious and Moral
Education, as well as the Informal Curriculum offers a variety of experiential learning activities and immerses
etiquette training to enrich the featured learning elements of Life Education, students are allowed to develop
holistically to fulfil the mission and values of our school. Aligned with the school development plan, socio-
emotional learning has been introduced this year. Social Emotional Learning (SEL) benefits students by
fostering self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-
making.

Form Teacher Lessons are designed based on Positive Psychology to address the developmental needs of
students at different phases. Topics covering Value Education include Perseverance, Respect for Others,
Responsibility, National Identity, Commitment, Integrity, Benevolence, Law-abidingness, Empathy,
Diligence, Unity and Filial Piety. The implementation of social emotional learning (SEL) is integrated into
Form Teacher Lessons.

Mindfulness lessons for F.1 students and Day 1 Mindfulness broadcasts was carried out to cultivate a positive
attitude through strengthening students’ self-awareness, emotional management skills and making favourable
choices in times of difficulty.

Experiential learning activities for Life Education are organized for students to build knowledge with
experiences, deepening the meaning of Value Education. The relevant activities include:
   F.1 Activity Day (Perseverance and positive class culture), Benediction Ceremony (Thankfulness and

   F.2 Inclusion Volunteer Service (Care and respect)

   F.5 Life Planning Workshop (Life Planning)
   F.6 Celebration of Adulthood (The spirit of becoming servant leaders)

Religious Education

Aiming to implement Religious Education, our school held the Christian Union in September and organized
the Gospel Week themed “A.I. Life” in March. Through hymns, sharing, and prayers, students were
encouraged to trust in God and live well according to their religious beliefs, especially during difficult times.
The most important goal is for our students to trust in God and to connect with Him in their daily lives.

For Student Fellowship and Girls’ Brigade, students have regular meetings every Tuesday and Saturday,
respectively. Through a wide range of featured activities and sharing sessions, students can benefit from
religious teaching and are encouraged to support their companions.

The Benediction Ceremony was held on 10 October 2025. F.1 students received blessings from God and well-
wishes for their secondary school life. The F.6 Celebration of Adulthood was held on 26 and 27 November
2025. During this event, the school principal, pastors, and teachers washed the graduating students’ feet as an
example of servant leadership. After this rite of passage, students gained maturity, and their self-centeredness
was greatly reduced.
