A. SCHOOL VISION, MISSION & OBJECTIVES

Vision and Mission

1.1 St. Francis Xavier’s College shall operate as a “Catholic” school. Its “Catholic” identity shall be
recognized in so far as it conforms with the criteria set by the Catholic Church [see Canon 803 §§1-3 and
related Canons 804-806 of the Code of Canon Law (promulgated in 1983), quoted in the Appendix; also
refer to Vatican II, Declaration on Christian Education (28 October 1965), 8-9; Congregation for Catholic
Education, Instruction “The Religious Dimension of Education in a Catholic School” (7 April 1988)].
With a determination to carry on the contribution of the Catholic Church to education, the School, as its
vision and mission, shall uphold and pass on the following core values to young people to prepare them
properly for their life and future responsibilities –
1.1.1 Truth: It is what the human intellect is searching for –
       (a) Human reason’s capacity for truth must be upheld, and the desire for truth, especially the truth
       about God and about the meaning of life, must always be encouraged and kept alive.
       (b) Wisdom, which enables a person to distinguish right from wrong, and good from evil, must be
       treasured above all other kinds of knowledge.
       (c) Honesty demands that a person tell the truth and put it into practice, even at the cost of making
       a great sacrifice.
1.1.2 Justice: It is the moral virtue that consists of a constant and resolute will to give to God and
        one’s neighbours their due –
        (a) Justice towards God is called the “virtue of religion”; and justice towards one’s neighbours
        disposes one to respect the rights of others and to establish in human relationships the harmony
        that promotes equity with regard to individual persons and to the common good.
        (b) Human dignity can be protected and promoted, and the wellbeing of society can be achieved,
        only if human rights are respected and individuals undertake their responsibilities for one another,
        for their own families, and for society.
1.1.3 Love: It is the greatest of all virtues –
       (a) God, the source of life and goodness, has created everything out of love, and has called the
       whole human family to be His children. As a member of God’s family, one’s goals in life are to
       share God’s happiness, to love God above all things and love one’s neighbours as brothers and
       sisters.
       (b) Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Saviour of humankind, is the model of selfless love and
       humble service to others.
       (c) The practice of all the virtues is to be inspired and motivated by love, so that all aspects of
       human life and interpersonal relationship may be bound together in perfect harmony.
       (d) Love surpasses the strict measure of justice and urges one to care for the poor and the needy,
       and to make a preferential option for the underprivileged and marginalized in society.
1.1.4 Life: It is a priceless gift from God and is sacred in itself –
        (a) Every human person is created in the image of God and has the right to life, which must be
        respected from its conception to its natural end.
        (b) In the spirit of the “Beatitudes” as taught in the Gospel, the tribulations and adversities in life
        are to be faced with serenity and hope.

                                                      P. 3
        (c) Every person is entitled to have whatever is necessary for a decent and dignified existence.
        (d) Only a society which respects human life can bring happiness to all.
1.1.5 Family: It is the basic unit of society –
        (a) Only pure love, the unreserved mutual self-giving between husband and wife, is truly
        gratifying; a happy, wholesome marriage is prepared by the practice of the virtue of chastity and
        sustained by fidelity and an indissoluble, lifelong commitment.
        (b) Inasmuch as sex is an integral part of conjugal life and has its own dignity, a balanced sex
        education must follow a holistic and in-depth approach, with emphasis on the virtues of self-
        discipline and mutual respect between a man and a woman.
        (c) Marriage is the foundation of a family; an intact and united family is a permanent support for
        husband and wife, and for parents and children, in achieving their goals in life; an intact and
        united family is likewise a most favourable setting for the upbringing of children and young
        people, and a necessary condition for the wellbeing of human society.


1.2 The School shall cultivate the above core values by –
1.2.1   upholding the time-honoured practice of respecting and collaborating with stakeholders with
          diverse backgrounds in achieving the School’s vision and mission as set out in this Article, in
          the awareness that the success of education depends on the joint effort of all parties concerned
          (refer to Canon 796§2 and Canon 800§2 of the Code of Canon Law, quoted in the Appendix);
1.2.2     providing a family environment imbued with mutual trust and love in the School; and
1.2.3     incorporating in the formal school curriculum Religious Education courses designed by the SSB
          (as defined herein), and fostering a Catholic spirit through religious practices held regularly in
          the School, such as morning prayers and religious activities held regularly for staff and
          students.


1.3 The School was established by “The Visitor in Hong Kong of the Institute of the Marist Brothers of
    the Schools” whose founder St. Marcellin Champagnat had passed on the following beliefs and ideals
    as an integral part of his educational vision and mission –
1.3.1      That we should make Jesus known and loved among the young and the children, especially the
           poor and the least favoured.
1.3.2      That to bring up children properly, we must love them, and love them all equally.
1.3.3      That each of our students would cherish the love of God and accordingly develop his potentials
           fully, lead a meaningful life as an individual, and take a contributing role in society.


1.4 With due regard for the core values set out in Article 1.2, the School shall also strive to put the
aforementioned beliefs and ideals into practice by inculcating in the school environment the following –
1.4.1    Opportunity be provided for the students to learn the Gospel of Christ in order to enrich their
         spiritual life.
1.4.2    Our students be nurtured with Marist Style of Education that they may get a whole-person
         education and develop their moral, intellectual, physical, social and aesthetic potentials.
1.4.3    That our students be instilled with the proper moral values so that they have positive goals of
         life, and have concern about others in the society.

B. OUR SCHOOL

Brief Introduction of the School

The Marist Brothers, our school sponsoring body, started the education of youth in China about 120 years
ago. One of the schools they served was St. Francis Xavier's College in Shanghai. In 1950, they came to
Hong Kong from China and gave religious instruction in St. Martin's English School. Two years later, the
Brothers were asked to take over St. Martin's. In 1954, with the help of the Education Department, new
school premises were built at the junction of Maple Street and Sycamore Street. On 9th December 1955,
classes were resumed in the new premises. At the same time, it was renamed St. Francis Xavier's College
as a token to mark the continuation of Marist education in China.

Medium of instruction

Ever since the beginning of the school, the English Language has been adopted as the medium of instruction
in our school. In March 1998, the HKSAR Government confirmed the suitability of our school to continue
using English as the medium of instruction.

Under EDB’s latest announced “fine-tuned MOI arrangements for secondary schools”, our school is entitled
to use English as the MOI until the year 2028.

C. OUR STUDENTS

Enrolment
There were 27 classes in total in the year 2023-2024. The government set the maximum numbers of students in F.1
to F.6 to be 33, 33, 33, 32, 32 and 32 per class respectively.

Under the Voluntary Optimization of Class Structure Scheme, the School was entitled to open 24 classes, 4 classes
in each level. On the other hand, the School used school funds to implement small class teaching in F.1, F.2 and F.3,
adding one extra class to each of these forms while keeping the number of enrolments unchanged. In doing so, the
School was able to reduce the class size to 24 to 28 per class in F.1 to 3. For the senior forms, there were sufficient
F.4 places to admit the whole population of F.3 students who were promoted to F.4, excluding the repeaters.

During the school term, we recorded quite a number of withdrawal from the School. Up to the last school day (15
July 2023), we have 694 students in total.

Teachers Development Days (TDD)

The First Teachers Development Day
The first TDD of the 2023-2024 school year, held on 13 October 2023, focused on Values Education at St. Francis
Xavier’s College. It began with a prayer and a group sharing session where teachers discussed their aspirations for
student development. A key activity was selecting six virtues aligned with the School's mission. The afternoon
involved drafting performance indicators for these virtues, ending with a reflective candlelight session and final
remarks from the observer.

The Second Teachers Development Day
Held on 8 March 2024, the second TDD aimed to enhance the School's strategic planning through The Enhanced
School Development and Accountability Framework. The morning featured an introduction to the framework and
a sharing session with Dr. Chu, principal of C.C.C. Heep Woh College. Teachers then evaluated our student
performances using the new framework.
In the afternoon, a session by Integrated Resources of Education (iRed) led teachers to explore the use of AI in
education, providing hands-on experience for teachers to implement these technologies in their classrooms.

The Third Teachers Development Day
The third TDD was held on 27 May 2024. The morning session centered on finalizing the 3-year school
development plan using the bottom up approach.
The afternoon session, organized with St. James’ Settlement’s Wellness Pro-Teacher Wellness Support Service,
included a health talk on traditional Chinese medicine and a Tai Chi workshop. These activities aimed at
promoting teachers' well-being, encouraging them to share these wellness practices with students.

E. ACHIEVEMENTS and REFLECTION on MAJOR CONCERNS; FEEDBACK and FOLLOW-UP

Major Concern 1:
To further stretch students’ academic potential through promoting self-directed learning.

     Achievements
 Development of Self-directed Learning Skills: A key target of this initiative was to foster self-
   directed learning (SDL) habits among students. Over the last year, notable progress was
   observed in this area. Many students developed a routine of taking notes during lessons, which
   is a positive step toward independent learning. This practice was encouraged and reinforced by
   teachers, as seen in regular subject panel meetings where they shared the importance of students
   documenting their learning process. This indicates that self-directed learning has begun to take
   root as part of students' learning routines.

 Teacher Engagement and Pre-training Practices: Teachers were actively involved in
   fostering SDL by using pre-training techniques to boost students' learning motivation and
   increase classroom participation. The use of pre-lesson preparation and activities that
   encouraged critical thinking helped stimulate student engagement. The integration of these
   strategies led to more lively participation in class, as students were better prepared and more
   motivated to engage with lesson content.

 Outside Classroom Learning Resumed: While the pandemic caused a temporary halt to
   outside-the-classroom learning activities, many of these opportunities were fully resumed in
   2023-2024. Activities such as field trips, competitions, and academic enrichment programs
   played a crucial role in offering students additional SDL opportunities, helping them apply
   classroom knowledge in real-world settings. These experiences were pivotal in stretching their
   academic potential.




     Reflection
 Insufficient Learning Motivation: Despite the progress in building self-directed learning
   skills, there remains a significant gap in students' intrinsic learning motivation. Teachers
   reported that although students had adopted note-taking and some self-management skills, their
   drive to go beyond the basic requirements of their studies was lacking. Many students appeared
   content with completing tasks without actively seeking to push their academic boundaries,
   leading to untapped potential. Thus, while the mechanics of self-directed learning were in place,
   the deeper motivation to excel was not yet fully realized.

 Pandemic-related Disruptions: The original vision for enhancing SDL included a wide range
   of outside-classroom enrichment activities designed to encourage independent learning.
   However, the global pandemic interrupted these plans, restricting students’ access to broader
   learning experiences. Although many activities were resumed in 2023-2024, the full impact of
   these disruptions was felt throughout the cycle, and the delayed return to normalcy meant that
   some planned initiatives could not fully materialize during the cycle.




                                                        P. 14
    Feedback and Follow-up

 Institutionalizing SDL as Routine Practice: Moving forward, the school will incorporate self-
  directed learning skills and habits as a routine part of students' academic life. This will involve
  continuous teacher reinforcement of independent learning strategies, such as note-taking,
  research, and critical thinking. The school also aims to expand the use of e-learning tools and
  technology to support SDL, ensuring that students have access to a variety of resources to aid
  their independent studies.

 Fostering Intrinsic Motivation: Recognizing the gap in learning motivation, the next school
  development cycle will focus on elevating students’ intrinsic motivation. This involves
  identifying strategies that go beyond academic tasks to inspire a genuine interest in learning.
  Initiatives such as goal-setting exercises, personalized learning experiences, and more frequent
  recognition of student achievements will be implemented to help students feel a sense of
  ownership and accomplishment in their learning journey.

Major Concern 2:
To promote the well-being of the Xaverian Family and equip them for challenges in life

     Achievements
 Promotion of Well-being through the PERMA Model: The school adopted the PERMA
   model (Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, and Accomplishment) as the
   foundation for promoting well-being among students, staff, and parents. Through a variety of
   initiatives, including workshops, extra-curricular activities, and themed form assemblies,
   students were introduced to vital knowledge and skills related to well-being. For example,
   mindfulness sessions, positive psychology workshops, and peer support groups were organized
   to enhance emotional resilience and mental health.

 Positive Response to Well-being Initiatives: The well-being initiatives extended beyond
   students to include teachers and parents, who were also involved in activities designed to
   enhance their understanding of well-being. Workshops were organized for parents on topics
   such as stress management and supporting their children's mental health, while teachers
   participated in professional development focused on promoting mental well-being in the
   classroom. Feedback from all stakeholders—students, parents, and teachers—was generally
   positive, with many participants reporting that they felt better equipped to handle emotional and
   mental health challenges.

 Improved Student Well-being: Although well-being promotion was partly hindered by the
   pandemic, students showed improvements in understanding the concepts of well-being and
   applying them to their daily lives. There was an increase in awareness and open discussions
   about mental health, with students engaging more in well-being activities and adopting
   practices like goal-setting and emotional regulation. The school observed progress in students'
   ability to manage stress, communicate more effectively with peers, and seek help when
   needed.




     Reflection
 Need for Better Self-management Skills: Despite the positive strides made in promoting well-
   being, it became evident that students needed further support in developing essential self-
   management skills. Teachers reported that while students had gained knowledge about well-
   being, they often struggled with practical aspects like time management, setting realistic goals,
   and staying organized. These gaps in self-management skills affected both their academic
   performance and overall well-being. Students were often overwhelmed with responsibilities but
   lacked the skills to balance them effectively.

 Fragmented Well-being Promotion: The well-being efforts, though impactful, were somewhat
   fragmented and lacked a cohesive, whole-school approach. Different committees and subject
   panels were involved in promoting well-being, but the absence of a centralized strategy meant
   that the initiatives were not always aligned. This limited the overall effectiveness of the well-
   being promotion efforts, highlighting the need for a more integrated and structured approach
   moving forward.


                                                        P. 16
    Feedback and Follow-up

 Introduction of School-based Values Education Curriculum: To ensure a more holistic
  approach to promoting well-being, the school will implement a School-based Values Education
  Curriculum starting from the next academic year. This curriculum will focus on embedding core
  values, including self-discipline, perseverance, and empathy, into everyday school life. By
  fostering these values, the school aims to help students build stronger self-management skills
  and emotional resilience. The curriculum will also provide structured support for teachers and
  parents to reinforce these values outside the classroom.

 Strengthening Teacher and Parent Involvement: Given the positive feedback from parents
  and teachers, the school plans to continue and expand its well-being initiatives. More
  professional development opportunities will be offered to teachers on integrating well-being
  into their teaching practices. Similarly, parents will be engaged through workshops and
  resources that equip them to support their children’s mental and emotional health. By fostering
  a stronger connection between home and school, the well-being of the Xaverian Family as a
  whole will be further strengthened.
