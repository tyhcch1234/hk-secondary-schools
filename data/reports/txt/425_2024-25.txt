  1.2 School Type

           Our school is a government subsidized whole-day EMI co-educational secondary school,
       established by the Yuen Long Merchants Association in September 1978. There were eight S.1
       classes in the first year, and gradually increased to 30 classes in September 1984, with six classes in

     S.1, S.2 and S.3, four classes in S.4 and S.5, and two classes in S.6 and S.7. From September 1998
     to July 2004, the school ran 29 classes from S.1 to S.7, with five classes in each level from S.1 to
     S.5, and two classes in S.6 and S.7. From September 2004 to July 2009, the total number of classes
     ran up to 30 again as the Education Bureau allocated one more class to our school as a one-off plan

     30 classes from S.1 to S.6, with five classes in each level from S.1 to S.5 and 2 classes in S.7.

 Major Concern 1：To boost the scholastic achievements of student


 1.1 To enhance students’ self-directed learning ability
 Achievement
 The implementation of innovative learning and teaching strategies across different subject departments transformed
 student engagement and participation in the learning process. These strategies included creating tasks that empowered
 students to select appropriate materials for their assignments, such as vocabulary and sentence pattern lists for English
 language writing tasks. Through this, a sense of ownership was fostered among the students, which was a key aspect
 of self-directed learning that encouraged students to take initiative in their educational journeys. In addition to
 allowing for material selection, departments introduced various learning resources that supported self-directed
 learning. For instance, pre-lesson tasks and activities on online learning platforms enabled students to prepare and
 engage with content at their own pace, while reflective "note-to-self" corners promoted self-assessment. These
 strategies encouraged active engagement as students learned to evaluate their progress and adapted their learning
 approaches accordingly. The positive shift toward more student-centered activities was reflected in the results of the
 SHS survey, which indicated an increase in the mean score for the statement "My students take the initiative to learn,"
 rising from 3.0 to 3.2 between the 2023/24 and 2024/25 school years. Similarly, parents observed a rise in their
 children's initiative, with the mean score for the statement "My child takes the initiative to learn" increasing from 3.5
 to 3.6, further illustrating the broader impact of these educational strategies. Moreover, students reported that their
 teachers frequently provided effective learning strategies, such as preparing for lessons, utilizing concept maps, and
 offering access to online resources. The increase in the mean score for this statement from 3.6 to 3.8 indicated that
 teachers actively supported self-directed learning through targeted guidance and resources. Overall, these findings
 underscored the effectiveness of the diverse strategies employed by various departments and demonstrated a
 commitment to guiding students toward becoming active learners.


 Reflection
 The data presented above indicate that our school has made some progress in adopting diverse learning and teaching
 strategies aimed at fostering active student engagement. However, the relatively low scores reported by teachers
 regarding students’ initiative to learn warrant further examination. These lower scores may stem from inadequate
 guidelines provided by teachers to help students successfully complete self-directed learning tasks, or the

                                                                                                                        8
opportunities available for students to develop their self-directed learning abilities may not be enough. Addressing
these issues will be crucial in ensuring that students are fully supported in their journey toward becoming more
autonomous and proactive learners.


Feedback and Follow-up
To sustain the momentum of these initiatives, subject departments will continue to implement strategies that actively
engage students in their learning. An environment that fosters curiosity and empowers students to take ownership of
their educational journeys will also continue to be created. This will involve designing tasks that enable students to
choose appropriate materials for their assignments and providing clear guidelines to help them navigate self-directed
learning tasks effectively. Resources that promote self-directed learning, such as pre-lesson tasks, online activities,
and “note-to-self” corners for reflection, will also be provided. Moreover, tailored resources will be developed to
address the diverse needs of students and allow them to learn at their own pace and revisit material as needed.



1.2 To Further Cultivate Students’ Reading Habits
Achievement
To enhance students' reading habits, our school implemented several strategies, including class-based sharing and
providing access to a diverse range of reading materials. For class-based sharing, teachers utilized morning reading
sessions to introduce tailored reading materials related to various subjects. To provide access to a diverse range of
reading materials, the LIGHTSAIL platform was employed in the S.1-2 English Department, while the Chinese
Department required students to engage with e-books at least once each term for their reading reports. Some students
even took the initiative to explore different e-reading platforms, such as LIGHTSAIL and School JoyReadClub, with
34 students receiving book coupons for their outstanding performance. Furthermore, nominated students were given
the opportunity to select and purchase new books that they were interested in, which was followed by a competition
called “Massians’ Best Picks Competition,” allowing for whole-school participation and enhancing the reading
atmosphere of the school. The achievements mentioned above were supported by the results from the SHS survey,
which showed that the mean score for the statement "My students like reading" increased from 2.7 in the 2023/24
school year to 3.1 in 2024/25 from the teacher's perspective. From the students' viewpoint, the statement "I often read
leisure materials and newspapers outside class" saw an increase in the mean score from 3.3 to 3.4, while parents also
reported an increase in the mean score for the statement "My child likes reading," which rose from 3.2 to 3.3.


Reflection
The data presented above indicated that our school made some progress in fostering students’ reading habits. Various
subject departments also developed engaging tasks that promoted interaction with reading materials. However,
despite these advancements, there was still room for improvement. The increases in scores were modest, reflecting
only a 0.1 rise in the perspectives of both students and parents, while the percentage of students who utilized e-
reading platforms only remained at an average level.


Feedback and Follow-up
To enhance access to diverse reading materials and foster a love for reading, the Reading Team and Library will
collaborate with different departments to improve the utilization rate of e-reading platforms. Recognizing the need to
engage students more deeply, it is suggested to encourage students to utilize e-books for their assignments particularly
                                                                                                                      9
in the stage of information search in conjunction with the theme-based project from the Chinese Department.
Moreover, an English book recommendation list for Secondary 1 to Secondary 2 students will be compiled to motivate
them to explore suggested e-books and use this list for their ERS book reports. To further cultivate their reading
habits, teachers will assist students in setting up SCMP accounts, enabling access to the Young Post and SCMP ePaper
on their iPads during morning reading periods, thereby encouraging reflection on current topics. Furthermore, all S1
students will regularly visit the library in groups to read English magazines, complete tasks on Microsoft Forms, and
earn rewards such as their favorite snacks or stationery. By implementing these strategies, it is hoped that a more
engaging reading environment can be created.

1.4 To empower students to develop the habit of mindful reflection on their learning
Achievement
To cultivate an environment where students can effectively reflect on their learning, our school has implemented
various strategies aimed at enhancing this vital skill. The Teacher Survey results indicate a mean score of 3.3 for the
statement, "Students reflect on and improve their learning." While this score suggests some progress, it highlights the
need for further enhancement in fostering a culture of reflection among students.
In contrast, teachers have rated their own performance highly, with a mean score of 4.1 for the statement, "I provide
students with specific feedback in a timely manner to help them reflect on and improve their learning." This
discrepancy suggests a gap between teachers’ perceptions of their feedback's effectiveness and the actual engagement
of students in the reflection process.


Furthermore, students reported a mean score of 3.7 for the statement, "Teachers often provide suggestions on my
performance and let me know how to improve," and a score of 3.6 for "I often reflect on my performance in class,
tests, and examinations, as well as teachers' suggestions." These results demonstrate a positive trend in students'
acknowledgment of feedback, yet they also indicate that there is still considerable room for improvement in
enhancing their reflective practices.

In addressing the increasing complexity of student-related data, we have turned to Microsoft Power BI, a powerful

gap suggests the necessity for a more structured approach to teaching reflection within the curriculum. By integrating
explicit instruction on how to reflect on feedback and learning outcomes, we can better support students in developing
their reflective skills. The incorporation of Microsoft Power BI into our analysis underscores this effort, as it allows
us to visualize trends and patterns in student performance, thus facilitating discussions around self-reflection and
personal growth.


Feedback and Follow-up
To address the identified gaps, the school will undertake a series of follow-up actions aimed at improving both the
quality and frequency of student reflection. This will include professional development for teachers focused on
strategies to encourage student reflection, ensuring that feedback is not only timely but also constructive and
actionable. Additionally, we will conduct workshops for students that teach them how to effectively utilize feedback
and reflect on their learning processes. Regular monitoring will be established through check-ins where students can
discuss their reflections and the impact of feedback with their teachers.
Collaboration among staff will play a crucial role in this initiative. Teachers will work together to share best practices
for fostering reflection and to create resources that can be used across different subjects. With the capabilities of
Microsoft Power BI, we will continue to analyze and interpret student data to inform these strategies, thereby creating
a robust feedback loop that empowers students to engage in mindful reflection and drive continuous improvement in
their learning experiences.

Achievement
To recognize students’ academic achievements, their accomplishments were celebrated through various initiatives.
These included organizing prize-presentation sessions during morning assemblies, displaying students’ excellent
work on boards, establishing a “Board of Fame” to highlight achievements in form tests and examinations, and
encouraging peer sharing of academic successes. By prominently showcasing these achievements, we aimed to
enhance the academic atmosphere on campus and motivate students to excel. The SHS report revealed that 17.3% of

indirectly affirming that students’ distinguished academic achievements were acknowledged. Furthermore, students

1.6 To Enhance National Security Education
Achievement
In the 2024–2025 academic year, Yuen Long Merchants Association Secondary School made significant progress in
national security education. National security content was embedded across all Key Learning Areas (KLAs),
including Chinese History, Citizenship and Social Development, Economics, Geography, History, Music, Physical
Education, Science, Tourism and Hospitality, and Visual Arts. Flag-raising ceremonies were enhanced with themed
speeches on national achievements and values, deepening students’ understanding of national symbols. APASO data
showed disagreement with “The national flag is important to me” dropped to 4.8% in senior forms, compared to
10.8% in junior forms, indicating stronger recognition of national symbols.


A variety of activities—including a National Security Poster Design Competition, Hong Kong Palace Museum visits,
lion dance classes, and Chinese Culture Academic Week—strengthened cultural identity. APASO findings revealed
that over 94% of senior students were willing to learn and pass on Chinese culture, and over 90% of students
developed a stronger sense of national identity. A cross-disciplinary open lesson, “Let the Terracotta Warriors Tell the
Story”, integrated 13 subjects using cloud technology and AI, fostering pride in national achievements. Disagreement
on national pride fell from 10.9% in junior forms to 6.8% in senior forms.


Administrative measures ensured compliance and accuracy of teaching materials. All new teachers passed the Basic
Law and National Security Law Test and attended EDB training, while professional development enhanced
confidence in delivering national security education. Mandatory flag-raising ceremonies improved discipline and
collective awareness, supported by APASO data showing disagreement with “Maintaining national security is my

                                                                                                                     13
responsibility” dropped from 11.9% to 4.8%. Home-school collaboration was strengthened through Parent Days and
cultural activities, with feedback indicating increased parental support.


Reflection
The school’s comprehensive approach enriched learning and strengthened patriotic values. APASO data confirms
high agreement on national responsibility, pride, and cultural continuity, with marked improvement from junior to
senior forms. Over 90% of students demonstrated a stronger sense of national identity, validating the school’s efforts.


Feedback and Follow-up
In 2025–2026, the school will continue embedding national security education, form a Flag-raising Uniform Group
with Chinese-style drills, and strengthen resource monitoring. External speakers will be vetted, staff contracts will
include security clauses, and professional development will expand. Home-school cooperation will be deepened
through cultural events and regular communication.



Major Concern 2: To provide holistic student development and uphold our whole-person education

2.1 To enhance teachers’ expertise in discerning students’ problems
Achievement
Within the school environment, teachers are an irreplaceable asset, acting as a vital protective factor for their students.
They are uniquely positioned to address core emotional needs while simultaneously nurturing the drive for growth
and self-actualization. Investing in the development of teacher expertise is therefore paramount to ensuring they can
meet this profound responsibility.


In this academic year, there are structured practices for both the Discipline and the Counselling Committee to achieve
the above target. Firstly, more comprehensive guidelines that outline roles and responsibilities of class teachers in
providing individualized support for students at both aspects have been clearly set out on teacher procedural manual,
with tiering mechanism of cases and relevant examples for support strategies included. All teaching staff are also
briefed on the content of the manual during the Staff Development Session in August. This initiative has established
a clear and unified framework, ensuring all staff can efficiently identify and address student needs. A follow-up
professional development (Counselling Workshop for all teachers 與學生正面同行) was also further conducted in
between the academic year to deepen teachers’ understanding of counselling skills.


In addition, sufficient communication platforms were developed to facilitate professional dialogues for both
conceptualization and formulation for support measures for students’ cases. At committee level, both the discipline
and counselling mistress held regular meetings with their committee members, with relevant          professionals      like
social workers and Educational Psychologist invited occasionally. This helped to identify challenges they face and
provide guidance, enhancing their classroom management skills and emotional support skills. The above practice
also provides solid foundation for the implementation for discipline/counselling teacher coordinator system,
empowering more and more staff manpower to get equipped and ready in student support work. More
interdisciplinary student case conferences are thus conducted timely and effectively.


The above strategies received general positive feedback in SHS (on a scale of 1 to 5), from both the teachers, students

This year's initiatives have holistically embodied our Massian values. Student-led campaigns and assemblies fostered

cultivated humble learners through reflective practices, while the 4R Wellness Week and strengthened support
systems successfully enhanced student wellbeing, as confirmed by APASO data.

Feedback and Follow-up
Looking ahead, the school could continue to strategically expand the experiential learning offerings to strengthen
student development. This includes introducing overseas field trips for cultural immersion and establishing an
adventure-based camp tailored for S1 students. These programmes are designed to nurture self-discipline, build
resilience, and foster a strong sense of self-appreciation at the critical start of their secondary school journey. By
providing these challenging and enriching opportunities, the school aims to build a solid foundation for their growth
as confident and capable global citizens.

2.3 To enhance students’ leadership skills
Achievement
The school aims to foster students’ leadership skills by introducing “Chill Club Friday” Scheme to provide platform
for students to practice decision-making and team management. A wide variety of 31 clubs ranging from academic,
cultural, sports to services aspects were formed. The Scheme was organized in structured format, with a student-
directed Club Promotion and Enrollment Day organized in late September, followed by 5 Club Days across the term
under Friday Assembly timetable. The regular Club Days set provided room for club advising teachers and student
leaders to think out of the box and organize cultural visits or sports activities to extend their exposure outside of
classroom.

The school has also newly established a Leadership Committee, initiating the formulation process for the school-
based leadership development blueprint, which includes Massian Leadership qualities and design for relevant
leadership programmes which nurture our students to be leaders with both vision and value. In addition, the school
is eager to offer opportunities for students to participate in international leadership progammes to broaden their

The programme consists of 8 sessions that enable members to develop their communication and leadership skills

for all stakeholders’ needs in students’ leadership development aspect.


Reflection
The school has created a well-rounded leadership program. The "Chill Club Friday" Scheme gives students a practical,
hands-on foundation to learn team management and decision-making. This is well supported by the new Leadership
Committee, which is planning for the future, and by excellent outside partnerships like the Toastmasters program.
Together, these efforts provide students with both immediate experience and long-term growth, effectively preparing
them to become capable and caring leaders.


Feedback and Follow-up
Moving forward, the Leadership Committee will be expanded into a Leadership and Student Advancement
Committee to better integrate student leadership with academic enrichment and global citizenship. This holistic
approach will ensure leadership development is coupled with academic growth and international exposure. The "Chill
Club Friday" initiative will continue to be a cornerstone, empowering student leaders to direct activities and   thus

is encouraged to lead, achieve, and engage with the wider world.
