(1) Our School

School Vision & Mission

School Motto: Morality, Wisdom, Health and Diligence

In keeping to this school motto, we are committed to:

        ⚫   providing an all-round education for the development of high morals and positive
            values in our students;
        ⚫   cultivating among our students’ kindness, a sense of responsibility, moral integrity and
            social awareness;
        ⚫   providing students with the knowledge and skills to cope with work and study and
            helping students develop independent thinking skills and become autonomous learners;
        ⚫   helping students maintain physical and mental health; and
        ⚫   encouraging students to make every endeavour to excel.



School Goals

        (1) To enable every student to have all-round and unique development in the areas of ethics,
            intellect, physique, social skills and aesthetics.
        (2) To enable every student to have a holistic and unique development in respect of
            character building. He/She should be self-disciplined, polite, rule-abiding,
            honest, caring, accepting, responsible, civic-minded, environment-conscious and
            able to tell the right from the wrong.
        (3) To enable every student to
            (a)   acquire a basic level of linguistic competence in Chinese and English, and
                  communicate with others in Cantonese, English and Putonghua.
            (b)   acquire basic skills in computation and in reading charts and diagrams.
            (c)   master self-learning skills, show competence in IT, and develop skills for
                  collecting and utilizing information.
            (d)   develop skills for innovation so as to cope with changes and solve problems.
            (e)   master high-order thinking skills, including the ability to understand, apply,
                  analyse, integrate and evaluate knowledge.
        (4) To cultivate students' interpersonal skills and foster their acceptance of differences
            and diversity.
        (5) To strengthen students’ sense of national identity and prepare them to serve and
            contribute to our society and country.

Core Values of Education


Everyone is uniquely gifted; our school strives to provide opportunities for students to actualise

their potential and develop their talents so that they can shine. With student-centred education as

our chief mission, we prioritise students’ growth needs and promote whole-person development. We

organise diverse activities and learning experiences to widen students’ exposure and nurture future

leaders who actively try, experiment, share, and contribute to our society and country with pride in

their national identity.



Morality is at the forefront of our school motto. For students to set good examples for one another,

our school expects them to be cultivated, well-mannered, and solicitous about the community. We

are committed to nurturing scholars whose curiosity drives them to learn proactively, whose passion

motivates them to enrich their knowledge. Physical and emotional well-being lays the solid

foundation for a purpose-driven life; therefore, we aim at promoting physical wellness and aesthetic

appreciation in our education, which are vital to students’ balanced growth. Attitude determines

altitude and the goals one can achieve; we firmly believe that students who are diligent and driven

will be able to scale great heights in their future.

School Facilities

Facilities include a computer room, a library, a student activity room, MMPT, a multi-purpose room,
4 laboratories, a Geography room and a Home Economics room. A fitness centre, a STEM Maker
Laboratory, a study room, and an air-conditioned canteen have recently been set up. A Chillax
Commons has been set up for students to relax, promoting their well-being. A new Chinese Academy
has also been established to host various Chinese cultural activities and promote traditional Chinese
studies and culture. The campus is well-equipped with WiFi network, and smart boards are installed
in all classrooms to enhance the effectiveness of learning and teaching. School Improvement
Programme (SIP) was completed in Sept 2005 to extend an extra usable area of 1,500 sq. M.

Major Concern 1: To empower students as positive, self-driven leaders for the
school and wider communities.

Achievements
1.   To empower students as active learner by bolstering students’ self-confidence
     1.1 Encourage students to actively participate in assembly or class sharing and provide
         trainings and feedbacks to participants

         ❖ The initial goal was to have 50 students conduct sharing; in the end, 80 students
           conducted sharing in 29 morning assemblies on 26 distinctive themes. Central
            recruitment was conducted for all announcers in both Chinese and English morning
            assemblies, with clearly outlined criteria regarding students’ conduct and ability. Class
            free talks were conducted in S1–S4 and some S5 classes, and the topics of each
            student’s sharing were recorded for evaluation. Selected students presented their free-
            talk sharing at the mid-term prize-giving ceremony, cultivating a culture of
            appreciation and showcasing exemplary performances. EDB officials expressed
            appreciation for Free Talk, noting its contribution to communication and collaboration
            and its role in identifying students with speech-related challenges.

     1.2 Encourage teachers to provide more opportunities for students to participate in lessons
         and demonstrate their learning outcomes.

         ❖ More than 90 lesson observations were conducted, with an average score of 3.07 out
           of 4. A significant increase in student participation was evident. Lessons were student-
           centred, with teachers serving as facilitators. The ESR report highlighted strengths such
           as clear learning objectives and effective utilization of tools and facilities in
           classrooms.

     1.3 Emit students to demonstrate learning outcomes of their experience and insights from
         non-academic activities in CTP and OLE lessons.

         ❖ Eleven senior-form groups shared during OLE/CTP periods, covering non-academic
            experiences related to science, humanities, and excursions. S2 students learned and
            hosted good game booths, and S1 Life Week game booths were launched.

     1.4 Provide opportunities for students to reflect on their learning experiences and keep their
         reflection in an organized manner.

         ❖ S1–S2 Learning Skills Workshops introduced note-taking, memorization, and general
           study skills prior to the first term test. A Reflection Logbook scheme for S4 students
           recorded reflections on learning experiences across activities such as Admission

            Information Parallel Sessions, Career Mapping, Careers Day, and Interview
            Workshops. Most student responses were positive. Students reported that they reflected
            on their learning and appreciated keeping records of reflections in an organized
            manner. Their documentation and organization of learning experiences improved.

2.   To instil proper values and attitudes in our students, and empower students to make
     informed decisions, act with integrity, and contribute positively to the society and our
     nation as a whole

     2.1 Implement a school-based compassion-inspired values education framework specific to
         the needs of our students.

         ❖ Senior form students were introduced to the school-based compassion-inspired values
           education framework during CTP and OLE lessons in the first term. Students’ attention
            was directed to the focus of each sharing at the beginning of events, and then to the
            framework’s positive values during debriefing. Students were invited to resonate with
            the experience shared and reflect on their own experiences. Junior form students
            reviewed questions about discrimination and were given qualitative questions for
            reflection.

     2.2 Hold programmes which provide students with information, aiding them in making
         informed decisions in the process of career and life planning

         ❖ The following programmes provided study- and/or career-related information and
           advice: S1 Career Express; S2 Little Society; S3 Life Auction; S4 & S5 Careers Day;
           S5 Mock JUPAS Programme Selection Workshops; and S6 Academic Counselling
            Scheme.

     2.3 Include learning content related to politeness and service spirits through different training
         workshops and Service-learning class.

         ❖ To enhance students’ service spirit and boost confidence, the Discipline Committee
           organized a Head Prefect Training Workshop, a New Prefect Training Workshop, and
           a Prefect Leadership Training Day Workshop. Over 90% of prefects agreed these
           workshops strengthened their awareness of serving peers and the school, and over 80%
           felt their confidence in performing duties increased. A Service-Learning Class and a
           Rehabilitation Programme were launched for students to serve the school after
            behavioral issues. Seven S1 and S2 students served as Politeness Ambassadors on
            Parents’ Day; they received praise and affirmation from some parents.

     2.4 Equip students with accurate sex knowledge and develop proper values towards sexuality

         ❖ Two staff training sessions on online sexuality traps and cyberbullying were provided
           to all teachers, with an additional session on cyberbullying for 10 teachers. S1–S6
           students received talks and workshops on sex education.

   1.1 To cultivate a learning atmosphere that engages students and encourages student

        1.1.1 To engage students in academic-related activities/ competitions
                 ❖ Over the past year, the school has made remarkable progress in fostering an
                     engaging and participatory learning environment. Students were encouraged

                     ranging from science and STEM to debate and artificial intelligence. Their

                     demonstrated the growth of a collaborative learning community. One

                     world’s top twenty for English debating and ranked among the top forty in
                     English writing, bringing immense pride to the school. Students also
                     continued to stretch their talents in territory-wide competitions such as Model
                     United Nations and other major tournaments, reinforcing their global outlook.

                     Forum created further platforms for intellectual growth. Students debated

Reflection
1. To engage students as active participants in a learning community that nurtures their
    intellectual growth

   ❖ While substantial progress was achieved in engaging students and enriching their
     participation, several areas for growth and improvement have been identified. The Civic
     Forum proved to be a valuable intellectual experience; however, it became apparent that
       students could benefit from more thorough preparation—specifically, by acquiring greater
       background knowledge about the motion prior to the event. This would help strengthen the
       depth of debate and foster more meaningful discussion.


   ❖ In Mathematics, the need for more targeted support was observed, particularly in S2 pull-
     out classes, suggesting that small group teaching could be an effective strategy to address
     individual learning gaps. Regarding academic competitions, the organisation could be
     enhanced by introducing a preliminary round before the main event, allowing teams to
     refine their designs and presentations and enabling the most suitable representatives to be
     selected for the school.


   ❖ The coordination of Science SBA assessments presented challenges, with multiple Physics,
     Chemistry, and Biology tasks scheduled within just two weeks, which led to considerable
     stress for some students. Improved coordination and scheduling can help distribute
     workload more evenly and reduce student pressure in future cycles.


   ❖ A key point of reflection concerns how the wider student body can benefit from the rich
     experiences of competition participants. More systematic opportunities for sharing, such
     as presentations, forums, or mentorship, should be promoted so that learning extends
     beyond direct participants to foster a more inclusive and supportive environment.


   ❖ Throughout the year, initiatives were undertaken to promote linkage between academic
     topics, school values, and values education, but further integration may enhance relevance
     and holistic development. Efforts in peer learning and sharing, as well as self- and peer-
     assessment practices, demonstrated positive steps toward accountability and collaborative
     learning, yet their overall effectiveness warrants regular review and refinement.

   ❖ Classroom discussions and student presentations have increased, reflecting a more active
     learning climate. However, the effectiveness of such participation —particularly the design

                                                                                                22
       of group tasks — needs ongoing improvement to ensure discussions lead to genuine
       collaboration and meaningful insights. Achieving this will require teachers to reconsider
       task structures and facilitate richer interactions.


   ❖ Finally, while coordination of Science SBAs has improved, the clustering of assessments
     over a short timeframe poses challenges, and efforts should be made to spread them more
     evenly to support student wellbeing and enhance learning outcomes.

      Our students consistently demonstrate a strong sense of camaraderie and mutual support,

Report on “AI for Science Education” funding programme

This report outlines our experiences and learning outcomes from the AI for Science Education
funding program, which involved five teachers: The program aimed to enhance the ability of junior
secondary science teachers to integrate artificial intelligence (AI) into their teaching, promoting
inquiry-based learning among students.
Overview

The funding consisted of two main components: (A) Short-Term Course and (B) School-Based
Support.


(A) Short-Term Course for teachers.
A 12-hour in-person course was conducted at The Chinese University of Hong Kong. 5 teachers
WMC, LPC, WKT, HTL, and LHK attended the course.

The content of the course included:
⚫    Fundamentals of AI: An introduction to basic concepts of AI, generative AI, and large
     language models (LLMs).
⚫    Practical Applications: Exploration of AI applications in junior secondary science education,
     including:
⚫    Image recognition for predicting plant health
⚫    Simulations of physical phenomena (e.g., optics and mechanics)
⚫    Predictions and evaluations of chemical reactions


Participants had hands-on experience with various AI tools, including ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Magic
School, Kaggle Dataset, and Teachable Machine.


The features of the short-term course are as follows:
⚫    Key Learnings
    Integration of AI in Education: The course highlighted how AI can enhance the teaching of
    biology, physics, and chemistry through practical applications. For example, using AI to
    analyse scientific data can significantly improve students' inquiry skills.

⚫    Hands-On Experience: Teachers gained valuable experience with AI tools, which will help
     them incorporate technology into their lessons more effectively. The practical sessions
     allowed them to experiment with AI applications relevant to their subjects.



                                                                                                56
⚫    Collaborative Development: Developing teaching examples in collaboration with peers
     strengthened their understanding of interdisciplinary approaches, fostering a supportive
     learning community.

⚫    Data-Driven Teaching: The introduction of data-driven instructional strategies will enable
     teachers to design inquiry-based activities that enhance students’ understanding of scientific
     concepts.


Future Plans after the teacher training.
Moving forward, we plan to utilize the knowledge gained from this program to design lessons for
S1 Chapter 3. A sharing session will be conducted during the staff development day to disseminate
insights and best practices among all teachers.


By fostering a collaborative atmosphere and integrating AI into our science curriculum, we aim to
enhance students' learning experiences and outcomes.



(B) School-Based Support
We still have approximately $50,000 in funding available. At the end of the academic year, we will
assess whether there is updated knowledge or advancements in AI education. If significant updates
are available, we may organize additional teacher training to further enhance our staff’s capabilities.
Alternatively, if no new developments arise, we may apply for school-based support to ensure
effective implementation of AI in our teaching practices.

Report On the Use of the Promotion of Reading Grant


The Major objectives for Promotion of Reading:

●   To turn the library to a well-organized resource information centre and an inviting place for self-
    learning.

●   To nurture a good reading culture in school.



Evaluation

➢   E-learning platforms have been set up in the library so that students can use the platforms for
    their self-learning.

➢   Subscribe School JoyRead Club, an e-reading platform, encourage students to cultivate reading
    interests, broaden their horizons, and develop reading habits through e-reading channels.
    Although we have promoted the E-reading platform through email, video, language teachers’
    recommendation, and reading reward program in October and February., the students' reading
    volume has not yet reached the ideal level. Promotion still needs to continue in the new school
    year.

➢   Book recommendations and sharing by 13 librarians and 3 students were organized for the whole
    academic year to nurture a culture of appreciation and other moral values.

➢   The Chinese and English book fairs were held successfully in Feb and March. The gross sales
    were $106,100 (Chinese book fair) and $33,600 (English book fair) respectively.

➢   Putonghua-related books were introduced to S1 students. Students borrowed books afterwards
    for their reading activities.

➢   Small-scale book exhibitions on different themes were held regularly this year, such as English,
    Chinese, PE. Exhibitions attracted students’ interest in reading and will be continued next year.

➢   It was a pity that Joint-school activities and Joint-club activities were not organized.
