(2) Achievements and Reflection on Major Concerns

Priority Task 1:     Enhance students’ life skills through diversified learning experiences

Achievements
Our school enhance students’ life skills through different activities and competitions in different
disciplines.
The Australia Immersion Program provided opportunity for students to stay in Australia from
15 July to 5 August to learn the unique Australian cultural exposure and broadened the
knowledge. There were about 60 G7 to G10 students and 6 chaperone teachers in the program.
Students were hosted by two famous schools from Brisbane, Sunnybank State High School and
Park Ridge State High School. A group of G11 students participated in the Rits Super Global
Forum in Japan to exchange their ideas on “Diversity and Inclusion” with students from
different countries through poster presentation, topic group discussions, product and design
sessions, keynote lectures, seminars by overseas teachers and presentation contests. The school
had given a unique Australian cultural exposure through different workshops and broaden the
knowledge of our students in the lessons. Besides, several inbound and outbound exchange
programs allowed students to host global peers and immerse them in the vibrant culture of our
city. Ten students and two chaperon teachers from Kamnoetvidya Science Academy (KVIS)
took part in the International Exchange program at GT in October. Seven students and one
chaperon teacher from Ritsumeikan Junior and Senior High School (RITS) participated in an
exchange program in our school in December. Accompanied by a chaperon teacher, ten students
travelled to Thailand in January for an exchange in the KVIS outbound trip. A group of 10
students participated in an exchange program to Invicta Grammar School in UK for 9 days to
experience a cultural immersion.


The MI Student Research Team has echoed one of the UNESCO themes in education for all,
and education for girls in UNESCO International Forum for the Future of Education (2-4
December) in its MI Female Interview Series (2024-25). Two interviews were held in October
and November with two eminent female scholars: Professor Nancy Ip from the HKUST and
Professor Rosie Young from the HKU. Students completed the interview sessions and feeling
motivated to contribute the fields and domains mentioned.


Tchoukball Teams broke the record by winning seven prizes at the International League of TGI
tournaments in Europe. It was held on the 14-15 December in Geneva, Switzerland.
Participating teams came from all around the world, including the host country Switzerland, the

United Kingdom, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Hungary and Poland. Our school represented

In April, fifteen students participated in an engaging scholar workshop organized by the Faculty
of Education, CUHK, focusing on the pros and cons of playing online games. The discussion
sparked a lively exchange of ideas as students shared their experience and reflected on the

projects and various competitions were widely used as learning outcomes for student learning.

Reflection
Our school open and provide diverse national and international learning experience for students.
These include different exchange programs, environmental activities, MI student research,
Tchoukball Geneva Indoors and scholar workshop which broaden students’ knowledge, skills
and international perspective in the learning subjects. International exchange programs offer
students advantages in cultural immersion, language skills, personal growth, and career
prospects. Students gain a broader worldview, develop independence and adaptability, improve
communication, and build a global network of friends and professional contacts.
Different international competitions allow students to communicate with global peers and
interface with global challenges. The performance of students were outstanding showing that
the learning curriculum, supports from supervising teachers and student efforts achieved high
standards. Students also performed well in project-based competitions in which the learning
processes developed their generic skills such as communication, team works, collaboration, etc
in addition to knowledge and skills. Furthermore, MI Student Research Team allows students to
explore insights from different industries and celebrities. Continuous supports will be provided
for students to explore and unleash their potential in different sectors.

Priority Task 2: Cultivate students’ positive values and develop good citizenship among
                 students

Achievements
Our school organize different programmes and activities to enable students to have a better
understanding about a sense of well-being and care for others.
The G7 Integration Program was held in August to engage students in ice-breaking activities,
group games and Campus Treasure Hunt to get themselves more familiar with the school
environment. These activities encouraged interaction among G7 students and their class
teachers. Students also cooperated with their classmates in an inter-class dodgeball competition,
developing strong team bond and class cohesion.
The G7 Orientation Camp took place on 19 and 20 September, providing new G7 students with
an opportunity to bond with one another and develop their teamwork skills. They participate4d
in a variety of team-building activities, including obstacle courses and group challenges that
encouraged communication and collaboration.
Zero Plastic League (ZPL) was in partnership with UNESCO in Greening Education starting
from 31 July in order deliver strong, coordinated and comprehensive action that will prepare
students to be a well-informed and responsible global citizen. Besides, ZPL was invited by the
RTHK to have a voice interview to share ZPL’s vision and work, problems and solutions to the
plastic pollution from different perspectives. Students shared their insights in organizing various
ZPL activities and their feeling towards spreading the message about environmental issues on
the air.
The G9 Leadership Training Camp took place in December at Po Leung Kuk Jockey Club Pak
Tam Chung Holiday Camp. The camp aimed to enhance leadership skills and teamwork among
students. It emphasized experimental learning, with students gaining insight into responsibility,
empathy, and the importance of listening as leaders.
The Student Union hosted a Dancing Contest in January to foster joyful atmosphere, spreading
entertainment and happiness throughout school. Besides, it organized the Game Booth Day to
create a memorable experience for primary school students.
In response to the growing concern over mental health issues in Hong Kong, our school
organized the School Wellness Carnival to educate students on the risks associated with mental
health in March. There were engaging game booths in the school hall, a lively and thought-
provoking drama and a discussion about how to comfort others and encourage their peers to
seek help when needed. This experience inspires students to thrive in a stress-free school
environment.
Eighteen G9 and G11 students joined a two-day camp held by Lions Clubs International District
303 in the Wu Kai Sha Youth Village in March. The aim was to prepare teenagers to be brave in
being their true selves and to pursue their dreams. Students were exposed to more than 40 booths
related to the government, education, society, entertainment, interpersonal skills and work
experiences.

In April, our school launched a value education program entitled “Gratitude Education for GT”
aiming at enhancing reflective competence among students from G7 to G11. Running from April
to May, the program focused on cultivating a mindset of gratitude towards various aspects of
life, including self, peers, teachers, parents and the wider community, such as their city and
country. In the program, class teachers helped students to engage in a series of sharing circles
with classmates, discussions in groups, card writing for parents and reflective exercises designed
to encourage them to appreciate the positive influences in their lives. The initiative endeavoured
to empower students to reflect on their experiences and emotions, helping them develop
resilience and a positive outlook.


Reflection
To cultivate students’ positive value and gratitude towards various aspects of life, “Gratitude
Education for GT” was launched for G7 to G11 students. With a User’s Guide, the whole school
is gearing up to improve their reflective competence for growth and excellence. ZPL was in
partnership with UNESCO to promote global citizenship education in environmental
sustainability. School Unions organized different teacher-student activities to foster harmonious
atmosphere in school and strengthen student bondings. G7 and G8 mentorship programmes
were continued to instill positive values and promote student well-being through different
activities such as discussion, family visits and volunteer works. The programmes also allow
students to exchanging ideas and sharing their life experiences with their class teachers and
classmates. Mainland study tours were organized to allow students to learn the recent country
development in order to nurture students with an affection for our country and Hong Kong and
equipped with global perspective, an aspiring mind-set and positive thinking.

Priority Task 3: Develop students’ lifelong learning of STEM literacy to keep pace with fast
changing technological development



Achievements
To enrich STEM literacy in school, our school invited different professionals to deliver talks to
share their insights and experience.
Our school invited Prof. He Tian, a member of the Chinese Academy of Science and a Fellow
of the World Academy of Sciences (TWAS) to give a science talk. In the talk, Prof. Tian
introduced the development of chemistry since ancient times and the elements used in historical
relics. His introduction aroused the interest of the students.
Our school invited Prof. Anderson Shum Ho-cheung, Vice President and Chair Professor of
Chemical and Biomedical Engineering of the City Univeristy of Hong Kong to share his
insights in a sharing session in January. Prof. Shum shared his experience and views about the
mindsets which drive his interests in research.
Our school provided opportunities for students to showcase their project ideas with global peers
for STEM knowledge and cultural exchange. Three G11 students attended the International
Students Science Fair (ISSF) 2025 organized by Mahidol Wittayanusorn School (MWIT) in
Thailand in January and the team was awarded “Outstanding Project Award”.
A team of three G10 students took part in the 2024 National Youth Space Innovation
Competition. Enrolled in the category of Microgravity, the team was awarded the First Runner-
up in the Hong Kong Preliminary Round and Second Runner-up in the National Final
Competition in August, held at Wenchang, Hainan.
Twelve G7 to G11 students represented Hong Kong in the 2024 Turkic International
Mathematical Olympiad (TIMO 2024), held in Istanbul, Turkey. Through their sharp
mathematical acumen and exceptional problem-solving abilities, the Hong Kong team managed
to clinch an impressive haul of 8 gold, 4 silver, and 3 bronze medals.
In 2024, Our school had successfully hosted The first international STEM project competition,
The GT College – Internatinal Science Project Competition (GTC-ISPC) 2024. The competition
aimed to promote sustainable development of STEM education locally, nationally and
internationally, and facilitates secondary school students to showcase their creativity and
exchange ideas on an international research platform.

Reflection

Our school provides diverse opportunities for students to develop STEM literacy and skills. For
example, our school invited university professors to give a science talk for students and organized
the GTC-ISPC to promote sustainable development of STEM education. Besides, there were a
variety of STEM courses relating to emerging technologies such as A.I., synthetic biology, drone,
robotics etc. Students were actively participated in different national and international STEM

Staff Development Days were held and be aimed at updating all teachers about the school
directions and achievements to create opportunities for teachers to come together for professional
exchanges which have enabled peers to grow their expertise and build harmony, trust and
supportive relationships among teachers.


In addition, a series of four new teacher training sessions (5 March, 9 April, 7 May and 11 June
2025) and 2 hours per session was held for new teaching staff members at our school. The aims
of the training sessions were to introduce school culture and history and share teaching and
learning for the MI students.


Workshops, seminar and conferences
Teachers are encouraged to attend the workshops, seminars and conferences organized by the
EDB, universities and education institutions for professional development throughout the year.


Teacher Training Program and mentors for new teachers
The Teacher training program is designated for newly recruited teachers. It emphasizes the skills
to teach gifted students which requires professional expertise and resources provided by the
school. The school provided appropriate support for new teachers such as the mentor program.
Each newly recruited teacher will also be assigned with a mentor and a subject coach who offer
help to new teachers to navigate and find their way through the school culture, facilitate their
teaching, and support their needs. The mentor allows the new teachers to observe lessons taught
by colleagues with excellent performance in teaching, thereby expediting their adaption to the
new environment and work.

(4) Support for Student Development
Gifted and Talented Programs
Our school offers four enrichment programs to nurture the gifted.
1. Clubs and Interest Group (C & I)
C & I group aims to develop students’ potential in non-academic areas and there are 31 courses
in the group.

2. Talent Class Program
The Talent Class program aims to develop artistic ability of students which is an important
capability for the development of creativity, open-mindedness, flexibility, and aesthetic
sensitivity. There are 26 courses in the group.

3. Elective Day
The program aims to enrich students’ academic knowledge and provide hands-on experience to
their subjects of interest. There are 26 courses in the group.


4. Sports Friday
The program aims to arouse students’ interest in sports and to develop their athletic ability. There
are 35 courses in the group.


Moral education
A holistic approach of life planning education was adopted to support the whole-person
development of students. There were assemblies every Monday and every Thursday for teachers,
students, and other organizations to share their personal experience, life values and attitude. In
order to help students develop a sense of belonging to the school, build leadership skills and get
along well with their schoolmates, one lesson is allocated for mentorship and two lessons are
allocated to adolescent growth for G7 to provide necessary support to students. For G8, 2 lessons
per week are allocated for the leadership training program in which external service is provided
to deliver leadership training to students. Teachers and other speakers are also invited to deliver
talks and briefing during the allocated time.

Various leadership training programs and community services were established to let students
develop their strengths, leadership, and problem-solving skills; community services to elderly
centres let students learn to care and love for others and make a contribution to the community.
Our school has built a positive and harmonious school environment by having a strong culture of
parent-school engagement. The Parent-Teacher Association (PTA) was very supportive to our
school. A number of school-wide activities were jointly organized by the Moral Education Group
and the PTA this year and there was a high number of parents and teachers participating in the
events.

School social worker
Our school works closely with the Hong Kong YWCA to support student development. The
organization appoints a social worker to our school, who provides counselling to students in need,
communicates and gives advice to students, parents and teachers, she also organizes and
coordinates the orientation camp, breakthrough camp and the leadership training programs.

Strong culture of parent-school engagement
The Parent-Teacher Association (PTA) was very supportive to our school. A number of school-
wide activities were jointly organized by the Moral Education Group and the PTA this year and
there were a high number of parents and teachers participating in the events. Besides the PTA
meetings, there were Parents’ Night for the effective communication of parents, class teachers,
and schools at the beginning of the academic year which was important for the holistic
development of students.

Channels of communication with parents
Our school has multiple channels to communicate with parents including the e-notice, G.T. Blog,
G.T. Newsletter, class representative communication, telephone conversation, e-mails, and the
WhatsApp group.

Financial Assistance
The school provides a financial assistance scheme for needy students. This year, 5.9 % students
were eligible for the assistance.

(7) Feedback on Future Planning
•   Our school open and provide diverse national and international learning experience for
students. These include different exchange programs, environmental activities, MI student
research, Tchoukball Geneva Indoors and scholar workshop which broaden students’ knowledge,
skills and international perspective in the learning subjects. Different international competitions
allow students to communicate with global peers and interface with global challenges. Continuous
supports will be provided for students to explore and unleash their potential in different areas.
•     With the increased connections between local schools and schools in Chinese Mainland, more
study tours and activities will be arranged to allow students to learn of recent development of the
Chinese Mainland. It also help to nurture students with an affection for our country and Hong
Kong and equipped with global perspective, an aspiring mind-set and positive thinking. Students
also had chance to participate community service in order to develop a good citizenship. More
related activities will be explored to let students to build sense of belonging to the country and
Hong Kong. Community services will be continued to develop students to be a well-informed and
responsible citizen.
•     To build cohesion and positive learning environment in school and to strengthen students’
mental and emotional health to foster holistic well-being, our school will continue to organize
more student-teacher activities. Besides, parents and alumni will also be invited in different school
activities. Our school will also stay connected with global networks for knowledge and cultural
exchange.
•     With the increasing use and potential benefits of Artificial Intelligence in teaching and
learning, teachers will be equipped to enhance teaching practices and explore tools for
personalized learning in order to maximize students’ learning outcome.
