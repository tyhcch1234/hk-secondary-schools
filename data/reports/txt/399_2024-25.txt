CHAPTER 1: Portrait of Heep Yunn School


School Background

By the Grace of God, Heep Yunn School is an Anglican school established in 1936, when two former Church Missionary Society institutions, namely
Fairlea School (1886) and Victoria Home and Orphanage (1887), were joined together. The name “Heep Yunn” means “united in grace” in Chinese.
Over the years, Heep Yunn has gradually grown from a Vernacular school into an English-medium school providing a high quality, all-round education
for girls in Hong Kong. In 2012, the school switched its status from a government grant-in-aid school to a direct subsidy scheme school.


The founders of Heep Yunn were committed to bringing education to young women and orphans in answer to the call of duty at a time of economic
instability and threat of war in 1930’s Hong Kong. They believed in empowering students to bring enlightenment to our nation and support to our
people. To this day, the school abides by its dedication to serve our fellow men in the love of Christ.



School Vision and Mission


It is the mission of the school to provide our students with a Christian education that develops the whole person in the following areas: moral,
intellectual, physical, social, aesthetic and spiritual; to instil in our girls a spirit of public service, and to build a firm foundation in both Chinese and
English.



School Motto

In faith and love we are committed

Campus and Facilities

The school is located on an 11,000 sq. m. campus consisting of six building blocks: the Lau Cheung Suet Ming Building (1936), the Chapel of St.
Clare of Assisi (1957), the Chau Hau Leung & Li Shiu Ching Building (1964), the Library (1966), the Jubilee Building (1986) and the Mr. & Mrs. Au
Sue Hung Building (2005). Outdoor facilities include a 25-meters swimming pool, 4 multi-purpose games courts, a Sorority Garden, a parking area
and a lawn. Other facilities include the School Hall, the School Canteen, Student Development Centre, 30 special rooms including the Black Box
Theatre, the Innovation Laboratory, the Visual Arts Room, the Music Room, the Technology & Living Room, and Lecture Theatre, 30 classrooms,
and a 3-storey high sports centre.

CHAPTER 2: School Development- Review on the Annual Major Concerns


3-Year Development Goal (1)


To facilitate students’ learning through enhancing teachers’ assessment literacy.


Annual Major Concern (1)
To promote quality formative assessment and adopt diversified assessment modes across various subjects.

Progress and Achievements
     Leveraging on two years of participation in the Quality School Improvement Project (QSIP) of The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK),
the School consolidated a shared understanding of quality assessment and built sufficient momentum to launch school-based professional learning
and systematic cross-departmental exchange. Our overarching aims this year were twofold: to raise assessment literacy among all teachers and to
embed diversified assessment modes in everyday classroom practice so that feedback is timely and capable of advancing learning. To initiate the work,
seed groups—the Personal, Social and Humanities Education (PSHE) Key Learning Area (KLA), the Science & Technology Department, and the
Junior Science Department—showcased concrete assessment-design practices during Professional Development Day 1 in August 2024, setting the
tone for a two-tier implementation plan to scale quality formative assessment across subjects.


   Tier 1 focused on teachers’ capacity building. On 21 March 2025 (Professional Development Day 2), the Professional Development Committee
and the Academic Committee organised a Professional Training on Quality Assignments. The Chinese Department shared its approach to designing
quality assignments, and teachers were guided through a newly developed self-evaluation checklist that outlined the key elements of effective
assignment design. Teacher survey results indicated strong endorsement of the training: 93.0% agreed the self-evaluation form was well structured
and clearly explained; 90.6% agreed the Chinese Department’s presentation was insightful and offered practical guidance; 91.7% agreed their
understanding of the requirements for quality assignments had been enhanced’ and 89.4% felt more prepared to design quality assignments following
the session.


     Tier 2 operated at the departmental level, where middle managers promoted quality formative assessment and diversified assessment modes as
set out in programme plans. Highlights include:

English Department: Targeting Form Four students undertaking the SBA, the department provided structured scaffolding for the Individual
Presentation, drawing on departmental materials and HKEAA assessment resources. An overwhelming majority (95.5%) of students found the
preparation process and trial SBA useful for the actual assessment. Specifically, 94.0% reported improved presentation skills, 97.8% said the process
heightened their awareness of the assessment criteria, and 95.5% agreed the strategies helped them identify strengths and weaknesses.


Life and Society / Citizenship, Economics and Society Department: Students completed self-reflections to consolidate learning after lessons. Google
Forms exercises were used to check understanding, and data-response questions were introduced to strengthen subject-specific writing skills. A cross-
curricular project for Form 3 students encouraged them to integrate knowledge across subjects. Students engaged seriously with the reflections, which
deepened their self-understanding of key concepts. The exercises consolidated learning and supported lesson preparation, and students performed
satisfactorily in both the writing tasks and the project, demonstrating the required knowledge and skills.


Chemistry Department: During lessons, new experiments were introduced for Form 4 (back titration) and Form 5 students (following the progress
of a reaction using colorimetry). In addition, ball-and-stick model-building tasks were assigned to Form 4 (diamond, graphite, sodium chloride), Form
5 (organic compounds and molecular substances with different three-dimensional shapes), and Form 6 students (cis-trans isomers and enantiomers).
Over 85% of students (Form 4: 85.4%; Form 5: 90.1%; Form 6: 88.0%) agreed that the experiments and model-building activities helped them
understand abstract chemistry concepts and improve their psychomotor skills. Beyond lessons, students photographed everyday items related to
current topics and explained the underlying chemical principles. Over 80% (Form 4: 87.4%; Form 5: 85.2%; Form 6: 82.3%) agreed that these
assignments highlighted the relevance of chemistry to daily life and increased their awareness of current science issues.


    In August 2025, all departments submitted their quality assignment designs to the Head of the Academic Committee for review. Many were
thoughtfully constructed and provided multifaceted evidence of student learning. Overall, the School made solid progress toward the major concern
of promoting quality formative assessment and adopting diversified assessment modes across subjects.


Reflection
     The School made substantial progress toward its major concern of promoting high-quality formative assessment and diversifying assessment
modes. Whole-school professional learning strengthened assessment literacy, while department-led initiatives translated shared principles into
concrete, subject-specific practices through authentic tasks, including trial SBA presentations, data-response writing, laboratory investigations and

model-building, to name but a few. These diversified modes generated richer evidence of learning and assessed students from multiple perspectives.
By and large, students showed clearer awareness of expectations and enhanced ability in demonstrating their learning. Teachers largely reported
feeling better equipped and were energised by collegial sharing. However, limited lesson time and teachers’ capacity remained notable constraints,
particularly for documenting quality-assignment design.


Future Planning
    The School’s goal of strengthening teachers’ assessment literacy across subjects to enhance student learning aligns with the Education Bureau’s
“Seven Learning Goals of Secondary Education”, notably the development of “generic skills”, “breadth of knowledge”, and “language proficiency”.
Greater awareness of, and a balanced approach to, formative and summative assessment have improved learning and teaching outcomes. Nonetheless,
an excessive focus on assessment results can place undue pressure on students, particularly those with perfectionistic tendencies. In the year ahead,
effective assessment practices will be embedded in routine teaching and learning. At the same time, the School will prioritise students’ well-being and
resilience in response to APASO-III findings that indicate persistently elevated levels of anxiety. In the next school development cycle, the School
will adopt the PERMA framework—Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, and Accomplishment—to cultivate positive emotion
and engagement, strengthen relationships with teachers and peers, deepen students’ sense of meaning, and celebrate their accomplishments.


3-Year Development Goal (2):


To cultivate positive values among students and nurture them into becoming responsible citizens with the commitment to serve.


Annual Major Concern (2):
To provide students with the opportunities to serve using their own initiatives.

Progress and Achievements
      It has always been the School’s goal to nurture students’ compassion and empower them to create positive impact in the community. In
partnership with non-governmental organisations (NGOs) and tertiary institutions, the Service-Learning Department delivered to all Form Four
students a comprehensive curriculum that builds empathy for disadvantaged groups and equips students with design thinking, entrepreneurship, and
a sustained commitment to serve. Students worked in authentic contexts to identify users’ pain points and apply interdisciplinary knowledge to design,
prototype, and test solutions to real-world problems.

     In 2024–2025, the School collaborated with the Hong Kong Sheng Kung Hui Welfare Council and introduced the concept of “servant
leadership.” To help students explore the needs of different service users—including elderly, ethnic minority communities, and people with
disabilities—students engaged directly with users at the following venues: Lok Man Alice Kwok Integrated Service Centre; Holy Carpenter Church
and Community Centre; United Court, Yuen Long; Providence Garden for Rehab; SKH Tseung Kwan O Elderly Services Building; SKH Wong Tai
Sin District Elderly Community Centre; Jockey Club Centre for Family Enhancement; and HKSKH Kowloon City Children and Youth Integrated
Service Centre. Following “Human Library” sessions at these sites, students designed and implemented service plans. They prepared lesson plans and
materials, created community maps, tested recipes, and organised games and activities. Feedback was gathered from social workers and service users.
Each cycle concluded with structured debriefing and student reflection.


      These arrangements effectively prepared students to serve the community and strengthened our partnerships with NGOs. Through site visits
and NGO-led workshops, students deepened their understanding of community needs. In the modified POSL (Process and Outcomes from Service-
Learning) survey, on a 10-point scale, students reported an average of 8.0/10 for “understanding the needs, potentials, and resources of the
community served,” and 8.2/10 for “empathy for disadvantaged people.” These results indicate that the programme offered meaningful opportunities
for learning about the community and cultivating empathy.


      By planning and implementing community service programmes under the guidance of teachers and social workers, students also strengthened
key competencies. The average score for “ability to apply knowledge and skills learned in school to real-life situations” was 7.3/10, and 7.9/10 for
“ability to work with others in a team to achieve common goals,” demonstrating that students were able to leverage their learning to serve the
community. Debriefings after each service and the collection of written reflections provided valuable opportunities for personal growth. The average
score for “understanding of your own values, strengths, and weaknesses” was 7.5/10, indicating that the reflective components prompted meaningful
self-assessment.


      To broaden horizons, the School also collaborated with Heep Yunn Primary School, Archireef “Here-in-Nature,” and Hong Kong Children’s
Hospital, enabling students to launch service projects in STEM and sports education, marine ecology, and care for paediatric patients. Pull-out
programmes offered further opportunities for community engagement. For example, three student teams joined the MTR “Train for Life’s Journey
2.0” Programme to design solutions that enhance the quality of life for underprivileged groups in Hong Kong; five students entered the PolyU
Igniting Social Innovation Competition 2025, using VR and HiVE technologies to raise awareness of public health issues; and eleven students served

     Overall, the year’s initiatives advanced the School’s major concern by providing structured, student-initiated service opportunities; developing
the much-needed empathy, teamwork, and real-world problem-solving; and fostering sustainable partnerships with community organisations.


Reflection
     In pursuit of our goal—to cultivate positive values among students and nurture them into responsible citizens committed to service—the School
has sought meaningful ways to weave service into students’ everyday learning. Given our students’ busy schedules, setting aside time to support those
in need has long been a challenge. Building on the optimisation of core subjects in the senior forms, the School has aptly introduced a Service-
Learning curriculum within regular Form Four lessons.


     Amid their pursuit of outstanding results in public examinations and a broad spectrum of co-curricular activities, students were given regular
time to pause, engage with people in need, and reflect on their role in building a better community. In addition to developing essential knowledge
and transferable skills for service, this deliberate creation of time and space to connect with society helps nurture responsible, upright and altruistic
citizens ready to serve.


     Through a comprehensive, student-led Service-Learning programme, the School has effectively cultivated the priority values and attitudes
advocated by the Education Bureau: “Perseverance”, “Respect for Others”, “Responsibility”, “Commitment”, “Integrity”, “Benevolence”,
“Empathy”, and “Unity”. We are pleased to witness our students’ growth and deep reflection throughout the programmes. Beginning with small acts
of kindness, we aspire for our students to grow into servant leaders distinguished by vision and virtue.


Future Planning
      Over the past three years, the School has strengthened students’ sense of “national identity” and advanced “healthy lifestyles” and “life planning”,
in alignment with the Education Bureau’s “Seven Learning Goals”. This progress was driven by a whole-school framework for national and national
security education, complemented by a wide range of values-based activities. Our efforts were reflected in consistently high APASO-III Q-scores,
peaking at 116 for national identity across all subscales— “responsibility and obligation”, “pride and love”, “national flag and anthem”, and
“achievements”. These effective practices will be embedded as regular provision and reviewed periodically to sustain national and values education.

Annual Major Concern (3):

Progress and Achievements
    To equip students with future-proof skills, the School has expanded STEM-related life-wide learning through the establishment of the
Innovation Laboratory and the launch of a school-based Science and Technology curriculum in the junior forms, supported by external funds.
Learning opportunities are intentionally structured at three levels—school, territory-wide, and international—to provide progressive challenge and
breadth of exposure.


     At the school level, every student gained hands-on experience through a whole-school STEM Fun Fair held on 19 January 2025. All Forms One
to Five classes designed and operated game stalls that integrated knowledge from the Forms One to Three Science and Technology curriculum. To
support preparation, the School produced a Fun Fair Game Design Guidebook and related resources, and arranged skill-sharing sessions. Students
reported that these supports were clear and useful for both planning and skills development, and guests were impressed by the interactive games
students built from scratch.

Reflection
     Aligned with the major concern—to equip students with future-proof skills through STEM-related life-wide learning—the concerted efforts
of STEM-related departments have created rich, engaging opportunities for participation. These experiences have kept learners abreast of
technological developments while sharpening critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity and innovation, self-directed learning, adaptability, and
effective teamwork—competencies essential for the future.


      The steady increase in student participation reflects the successful promotion of STEM education. At the same time, rising demand highlights
the need for additional resources—including equipment, materials, and teachers’ capacity—to sustain programme quality and safety. To continue
refining the curriculum, integrating emerging tools and methodologies, and expanding partnerships with industry and universities, sustained funding
is essential. We will work to ensure that Technology Education maintains its momentum so that students remain conversant with rapid technological
change.


Future Planning

“generic skills”, in line with the Education Bureau’s Seven Learning Goals. Looking ahead, in step with technological advances and educational trends,
the School will sharpen its focus on developing Artificial Intelligence (AI)-literate learners in the next school development cycle. We will invest in AI
platforms and targeted professional development for teachers to enrich learning and teaching. Through the responsible and effective use of AI, we
aim to keep the school community acquainted with rapid innovation and ready to meet future challenges.

Students’ Attitude and Behaviour


As a school founded on Christian values and a rich legacy, Heep Yunn is committed to fostering essential character strengths in our students, including
integrity, humility, unity, gratitude, fortitude, temperance, commitment, and altruism. We hope to see our girls grown up to be humble yet assured;
grateful and gracious; down-to-earth yet strong-willed; empathetic and inquiring- qualities that set Heep Yunn girls apart. To this end, we strive to
create meaningful experiences for our students, supported by apposite counsel and guidance, to promote their holistic development. This commitment
is aimed at unlocking their potential while helping them navigate the challenges that arise during their adolescence, a critical stage of their lives.


Learning in a community that encourages a growth mindset, our students make improvements and elevate their standards step by step. Our graduates
are equipped with future-proof skills and virtues necessary to achieve their aspirations, overcome difficulties, and pursue their callings and missions
with dedication and excellence, earning the trust of their peers and the wider community.


We believe that each student is graced with unique talents and gifts by our Heavenly Father. At the same time, we recognise shared traits among our
students, shaped by their collective experiences in our school. Notably, they understand and appreciate the significance of teamwork and public
service. The insights shared by our community members below are intended to provide a deeper understanding of student attitudes and behaviors
within our educational environment.

       Report on the Use of One-off Grant for Supporting the Implementation of the Senior Secondary Subject Citizenship and Social
                                                                 Development (2021-25)


1. Background:
In accordance with Education Bureau Circular Memorandum No. 83/2021 dated 2nd June 2021, the Education Bureau announced the provision of a One-
off Grant of $300,000 to support the implementation of the Senior Secondary Subject Citizenship and Social Development (CS Grant). Initially allocated
for a three-year period, the grant was extended for an additional year as stated in Education Bureau Circular Memorandum No. 174/2023 dated 25th
September 2023.


2. Objectives of the Grant set out by EDB:
The grant specifies uses in four areas:
    1. develop or procure relevant learning and teaching resources (including multi-media and e-learning resources), mobile applications and software, as
       well as reference materials for CS;
    2. subsidise students and teachers to participate in Mainland interflow activities or study tours relating to the curriculum of CS;
   3. organise school-based learning activities for enhancing the learning and teaching effectiveness of CS;
   4. organise or subsidise students to participate in joint-school / cross-curricular activities relating to the curriculum of CS conducted in Hong Kong
      or in the Mainland to promote interchange and dissemination of good practices.


3. Planning Principles set by School
It has been decided by the school that the use of the grant should:
   •   focus on experiential learning through exchange programs and study tours;
   •   prioritize cross-KLA and cross-subjects activities;
   •   balance themes to enrich students’ understanding of the culture, history, and development of our country.

4. Summary of the use of the Grant:
   •   Due to pandemic-related disruptions, only a small portion of the grant was utilised in the 2021-2022 school year.
   •   In alignment with the planning principle, the majority of the grant was used to support students’ participation in cross-curricular activities in Hong
                                                                                                                                     Updated 19 December, 2025 - 44
Heep Yunn School/ School Report 2024-25

           Kong and Mainland China. In Hong Kong, two activities were organized in collaboration with the Geography, History, and Tourism and Hospitality
           Studies departments, focusing on cultural heritage and its potential development. In Mainland China, three activities were conducted in collaboration
           with the Chinese History, Chinese Literature, Biology, Geography, and Tourism and Hospitality Studies departments. These included a study tour
           to Shanghai and Nanjing, highlighting key events in modern Chinese history, and a tour to Chengdu, exploring Sichuan's physical and biological
           landscape as well as its rich literature and history.
     •     A total of $291,715.20 was spent, with an unspent balance of $8,284.80 returned to the EDB. This financial report was submitted to the EDB on
