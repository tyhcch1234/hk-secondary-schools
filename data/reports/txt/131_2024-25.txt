    Foreword by the Principal
    Dear Parents, Students, and Members of the UCCKE Family,

    The school year 2024-25 marked the completion of our three-year School Development
    Cycle. It was a year where we continued to navigate evolving landscapes, finding that every
    challenge presented an opportunity for growth and learning – a true testament to the
    resilience and dedication of our students and staff. With God's grace guiding our path, we
    embarked on planned initiatives, made necessary adjustments, and consistently affirmed our
    belief that God is always in control, and our calling is to serve Him faithfully.

    This past year, our school theme, “Discern with Abounding Love,” anchored our
    collective journey. In an era increasingly shaped by Artificial Intelligence, our task
    extended beyond merely processing information. We focused on cultivating the ability to
    discern knowledge and insight in light of the Bible, ensuring that our judgments and
    decisions were always guided by God’s profound love. The theme Bible verse from
    Philippians 1:9-10 (NIV) — ‘And this is my prayer: that your love may abound more
    and more in knowledge and depth of insight, so that you may be able to discern what is
    best and may be pure and blameless for the day of Christ’ — served as a constant
    reminder for us all. It encouraged us to grow not just in intellect but also in compassion,
    understanding that true wisdom blossoms when infused with abundant love. We strived to
    create an environment where students could not only excel academically but also develop
    the moral compass needed to navigate a complex world with grace and truth.

    Everything we undertook, from new projects to daily lessons, was steeped in earnest prayer
    and whole-hearted effort to create truly meaningful learning experiences for our students.
    Witnessing your happy faces as schemes and projects came to fruition brought us immense
    joy. And when circumstances called for flexibility and adjustment, we approached these
    moments with patience and a spirit of adaptation. These accumulated experiences, both
    successes and learning curves, have enriched us all and will undoubtedly serve as invaluable
    motivation for the years ahead.

    As you read through this report, I hope you will join us in praising the Lord and giving
    thanks for His unfailing providence and blessings upon our School. I want to take this
    opportunity to express my heartfelt appreciation and gratitude: to my dedicated colleagues
    who worked tirelessly throughout the year; to our supportive parents who continually
    uphold the School; to our students who place their trust in us and allow us to walk alongside
    them; and to all KEians for your love, prayers, and unwavering support to our school
    community. I am certain that your rewards are being recorded in Heaven.

    To God be the Glory!

    Your Principal,

    Dr. Cheng Kin Tak Samuel

2. Achievement and Reflection on Major Concerns
    Our 2022-2025 school development cycle is one that builds on the foundation and strengths
    of the previous one, as well as a continuation and consolidation of areas requiring further
    development due to the constraints posed by the pandemic.

    Regarding the nurturing of self-directed learners, strategies conducive to blended learning,
    Reading across the Curriculum (RaC) and stretching the learning capacity of more able
    students are implemented at school and subject levels in a progressive manner. Other than
    academic pursuit, vigorous efforts are made to promote positive values education that leads
    to healthy growth of students, as well as the development of a stronger sense of citizenship
    and national identity. With teachers playing a critical role in students’ holistic growth, their
    professional development needs continue to be one of our major concerns. Through
    conducting action research, strengthening professional support for new teachers and middle
    managers, and adoption of differentiated appraisal system, teachers’ capacity and competence
    in supporting students’ learning, personal growth as well as leading subject/ functional group
    development would be enhanced.

    The three major concerns aim to promote students’ holistic and healthy growth, with the seven
    learning goals embedded in various parts of our work:
    •   National and global identity
    •   Breath of knowledge
    •   Language proficiency
    •   Generic skills
    •   Information literacy
    •   Life planning
    •   Healthy lifestyle

    Through the planning – implementation – monitoring – evaluation/ reflection, planning cycle,
    we are confident that a greater impact on students’ learning and holistic development will be
    witnessed through the adoption of effective strategies and measures.

    Impact on Teaching, Blended Learning, and Student Agency
    Alongside staff development, students in History, English, Physics, Mathematics, and
    Chinese had opportunities to collaborate with AI. They extended their learning, critically
    evaluated AI-generated outputs, co-created products with AI, and received more
    personalised feedback—strengthening choice and agency within a blended learning
    environment.
    Teachers’ efforts in sharing and showcasing innovative pedagogies during the school’s
    15th Curriculum Celebration further demonstrate the progress achieved in this area.
    According to the KPM Teacher Survey, teachers reported frequently engaging students
    in active inquiry and knowledge construction inside and outside the classroom (3.99/5)
    and employing blended learning strategies to develop self-directed learners (3.9/5).
    Students also reflected positively on their learning experiences, with a KPM Student
    Survey score of 3.79/5 for learning through interactive or collaborative class activities
    and 3.6/5 for the effective use of e-learning platforms to enrich learning. Parents also
    reflected that the school is able to actively develop different teaching methods such as e-
    learning and self-regulated learning (4.1/5). These results underscore our progress in
    creating a conducive environment toward AI-informed, blended practices that foster self-
    directed learning.

    Student Engagement and Digital Practices
    Most students now effectively use OneNote, Teams, Google Classroom, and eClass for
    both face-to-face lessons and after-school learning, reinforcing self-directedness during
    preparation, in-class activities, and consolidation. Collaborative spaces in OneNote and
    tools such as Padlet are used across subjects to promote collaboration and co-creation,
    aligning with the principles of self-directed learning—time, content, place, and pace—
    within a coherent blended learning environment.

    Reflection and Evaluation:
    Building on this solid foundation, the school aims to further strengthen both teachers’
    and students’ capacities to use AI and digital technology, with a focus on both the
    integration of and collaboration with AI to facilitate effective learning and teaching in
    the next developmental cycle, ensuring students are equipped as future-ready learners.
    Framework on the digital competence at organizational, teacher and student level would
    be devised, policies on purposeful and ethical use of AI would be formulated and
    systematically implemented at both subject and school level. Resources will also be
    deployed to create an enabling environment to develop student agency and autonomy in
    face of this rapidly changing world.

    Facilitation of RaC
    Fair progress has been made regarding the implementation of RaC, particularly in
    extending students’ learning in subjects such as History, Chinese History, and certain
    NLC subjects. Through subject-integrated learning tasks and subject-related reading
    schemes, students have demonstrated enriched understanding and the ability to conduct
    more in-depth analysis of subject content.

    and the ‘RaC X Library Cozy Reading Hub’, though the turnout for activities was not as
    high as expected.

    Infrastructure preparation
    Library construction has been making good progress. A total of four engagement
    exercises were conducted on Mar 26, 2025, Jul 3, 2025, Jul 4, 2025, Jul 10, 2025, to
    gather views from various stakeholders, including students, teachers, and parents, on the
    infrastructure, facilities, and design of the new 3-story library. Feedback collected during
    these sessions has been thoroughly analysed, and a preliminary design on the various
    purposes and zoning of library has been drafted to reflect the input and priorities
    identified by stakeholders.
    The design emphasizes creating a modern, collaborative, and inclusive learning
    environment, aligning with global trends in educational infrastructure that support digital
    literacy, self-directed learning, and knowledge co-creation. Construction work is
    scheduled to commence in the Autumn Session of the 2025-26 school year.

     Gratitude Week, held from Nov 4 - 7, 2024, was a collaborative effort involving the L&S
     Faculty, Moral and Civic Education Team, Student Support Team, and Discipline &
     Counselling Team. Activities included booths where students could write gratitude cards
     and play interactive games, as well as informational displays on love languages and
     emotions curated by SINCE Concept. A concert was organised featuring performances
     by S.6 students, CVELTD, and UNISON.HK. Artist Anton Fung shared his personal
     experiences, and an organ recipient was invited to speak in the assembly, inspiring
     students to face life’s challenges with positivity and resilience.
     These activities successfully cultivated a culture of appreciation and emotional
     awareness across the school. It is recommended that future thematic weeks continue to
     focus on promoting wellbeing. All junior form students participated in Positive
     Education activities organised by the Moral and Civic Education Team. These included
     the use of “My Journal”, “My VIA Journal”, and “Mind-roaming Cards” during Form
     Periods. Additionally, an art therapist from SINCE Concept and a director facilitated
     reflections on short films about family and friendship. These practices helped teachers
     better understand students’ emotional needs, fostered positive values, and supported the
     development of self-esteem.
     To further reinforce positive interactions, a set of school-based scripture cards designed
     by an illustrator were used by teachers to engage with students. Moreover, some teachers
     continued to use VIA mutual appreciation cards during GLocal Week to encourage peer
     recognition and support.
     Professional development sessions for teachers were held on Aug 19-20, 2025. Mr.
     Leung Pak Kin, Director of Breakthrough, encouraged teachers to walk alongside
     students in their growth journey. Mr. Pang Ching Hung and Ms Chan Bik Ling, authors
     and researchers, shared insights into the challenges faced by Hong Kong youth today.
     These sessions deepened teachers’ understanding and empathy towards students,
     fostering a more supportive and nurturing school environment.
     This year, we successfully strengthened the sense of ownership of positive education
     among students, teachers, and parents with the dedication and support by all stakeholders
     in fostering student wellbeing and positive development.

     Reflection and Evaluation:
     Our school has made significant strides in engaging all stakeholders in the development
     of positive education. A wide range of initiatives were implemented to cultivate a culture
     of wellbeing and character development among students, teachers, and parents.
     Gratitude Week successfully promoted emotional awareness and a sense of gratitude
     across the school community. These efforts, supported by various positive education
     tools, created a nurturing and inclusive school atmosphere.
     Overall, the collective commitment of all stakeholders has successfully embedded
     positive education into our school culture. Building on the success of our positive
     education journey, the next school development cycle will focus on enhancing students’
     physical, social, and emotional wellbeing. We will continue to engage stakeholders
     through structured programmes, experiential learning, and cross-disciplinary
     collaboration. Emphasis will be placed on strengthening resilience, promoting healthy
     lifestyles, and deepening emotional literacy to support holistic student development.

     5.5 Library as a Learning Centre
         One of the missions of the library is to equip our students to be information literate; and
         hence, students are provided with the opportunity to learn and acquire the skills of
         accessing information efficiently and effectively, reading and evaluating information
         critically and competently, and using information accurately and creatively. They are
         also expected to practice ethical behaviour in regard to the use of information and
         information technology.

         5.5.1 Explicit teaching of Library and Reading Skills
         Library Program was organized for junior forms and conducted during the Reading Time.
         Objectives of this program are to help students become effective users of information
         (both printed and electronic resources), develop the habit of reading for learning, become
         independent information seekers & life-long learners, and develop problem-solving and
         communication skills. Information Skills with AI implications was included in the
         program to promote AI Literacy.

         5.5.2 Extended Research
         Junior Form students were offered the opportunity to research on specific topics as
         assigned by the subject teachers using both printed and electronic resources. High-order
         thinking with reading & information skills learned in the Library Program were applied
         in these research units.

         5.5.3 Electronic Resources Workshops
         Workshops were conducted to introduce eResources to staff and students, which include
         Britannica School & Launch Packs, ProQuest SIRS Issues Researcher, HyRead,
         NowBook, Rosen eBooks and Flipster Digital Magazines, covering materials across the
         curriculum.

         5.5.4 School Library Webpage
         The library webpage in the school website serves as an eResources hub and also includes
         library updates/activities.

         5.5.5 School Library System
         The catalogue of the school library system covers not only printed items in the library
         collection, but also school subscribed eBooks, items available from HKPL and web
         resources; special features of the system have enhanced users’ experience in searching
         and using information

         Systematic learning and teaching of library/ information/ reading skills have been
         achieved through the Junior Form Library Program. Opportunities for skills application
         were provided in Extended Research Projects implemented in collaboration with
         different subject departments. Availability and accessibility of quality printed,
         multimedia and online resources facilitated the skills learning and application process.

     5.6 Reading for Learning
         Current research highlights the importance of reading in the success of student academic
         performance, with an emphasis on critical reading across the curriculum. Reading can
         also help students cultivate characters attributing to leadership, such as decision-making,
         problem-solving, and communication. Moreover, the high emotional intelligence
         acquired through reading helps readers maintain a happy and healthy life style. In view
         of the above, the Library puts a strong emphasis on reading promotion and helping
         students acquire proficient reading skills and develop strong reading habit.

        5.6.1 Reading Scheme
        Purpose of this reading scheme is to enhance student competence in in-depth reading,
        allow them to practise critical reading and polish their research skills. The reading
        assignments were integrated as pieces of HW in some subjects in the junior forms.
        Recommended books and worksheets to facilitate critical reading in different subjects
        were available.

        5.6.2 Reading Circles
        The S.1 Reading Circles groups, provided with Reading Circles book sets and worksheets
        to facilitate discussion, met in the Spring Session under the supervision of the English
        teachers. Reading Circles grades were given to students based on their performance in
        the meetings and the preparation work they completed. Reading Circles Student Leaders
        selected by teachers were given Good Names in recognition of the effort they put in
        helping other students to excel in the program.

        5.6.3 Book Week (Dec 2-6, 2024)
        This year's Book Week focused on igniting students' passion for reading. The week
        commenced with the Opening Ceremony of the Digital Reading Centre. The Readathon
        was launched to promote extensive reading across various subjects while raising funds
        for KE+. During lunch breaks, Teacher Book Talks provided students with engaging
        insights into a variety of texts. A local author was invited to speak at the Book Week
        Assembly, and a virtual author talk hosted by an overseas writer further broadened
        students' perspectives. Additionally, activities such as the "Guess the Legacy" Book
        Game, the Great Minds Alert Book Display, Historical Legends Scrapbooking, and the
        Geography Digital Scavenger Hunt encouraged students to share in the joy and
        excitement of reading.

        5.6.4 Battle of the Books (2024-2025)
        This interschool reading competition aims to introduce students to a variety of reading
        materials, build teamwork skills, and help students see reading as a fun activity. The
        questions asked in the competition were based on the books from the reading list assigned
        to the participants. The UCCKE BOB team, avid readers from S.1 to S.3, was formed in
        September. Team members had great fun reading and preparing for the competition.
