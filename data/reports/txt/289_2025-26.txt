(1) Our School

School Mission
Established in 1982 by The Hong Kong Sheng Kung Hui as an aided co-educational secondary
school, we strive to uphold the essence of the Christian spirit and ethos of whole-person education.
Dedicated to developing students spiritually, morally, academically, socially, physically and
aesthetically, we hope that our students will strive to establish and cultivate positive values, give
back to the community, and live up to the school motto – “acquiring knowledge and practicing
with determination”.

(3) Student Performance

HKDSE 2025
100 students sat for the Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education (HKDSE) in 2025. In all
subjects on average, the percentage of students attaining level 5 or above is 32.1%, that of level 4
or above is 68.1%, and that of level 2 or above is 98.9%. 93% of our students met the general
entrance requirements of UGC-funded institutions. A total of 91 students received JUPAS offers.

Part I: Evaluation on the activities
a. Mindful Parenting Course (靜觀教養課程)
      The first phase of the course consisted of 4 lessons, which were held on November 8, 15, 22,
      and 29, 2025. While 15 parents enrolled in the course, only around 5 parents attended all the
      lessons. Feedback from some parents was positive; they noted that the course helped them
      stay calm, raised their emotional self-awareness, and had a positive impact on handling their
      children appropriately. However, other parents found the course boring and only attended one
      or two lessons.
      Since parent attendance was unsatisfactory, the average cost per participant became very high.
      Therefore, it was decided not to proceed with the second phase of the course.

b.    Parents’ Talk: AI and Children’s Future (家長講座：AI 與孩子的未來)
      This activity was held on November 11, 2025. This was the first time the school hosted a
      parents’ talk online via ZOOM. Although the participation rate appeared higher than
      traditional in-person talks, some participants left very early. Additionally, there was virtually
      no interaction between the speakers/teachers and the parents. Therefore, this mode of hosting
      talks requires further consideration.
      Participants generally gave positive feedback on the content, with over 90% expressing that
      the talk helped them understand the importance of assisting their children in mastering AI
      skills.

c.    Fathers’ Group (「揮動爸爸」)
      The fathers’ group consisted of 6 sessions held between January 2 and February 6, 2026. Each
      session comprised two components: a 1.5-hour pickleball game for relaxation, followed by
      30 minutes dedicated to parenting skills and sharing. Fathers were eager to join, and the quota
      of 10 was fully met.
      The attendance rate was exceptionally high, averaging 95%, with 7 members achieving 100%
      attendance. Furthermore, 100% of the participants agreed that the activity raised their
      confidence in their parenting roles, and they particularly enjoyed sharing experiences with
      other members. They expressed a desire for more sports-related activities in the future (e.g.,
      basketball, badminton, tennis, martial arts, and canoeing).
      Based on this year's experience, combining sports with parenting skills proved to be an
      effective way to engage fathers. Given the highly positive feedback, it was agreed that this
      type of fathers’ group should be continued in the coming year.

d.    Parents’ Talk: How to Help Children Grow from Failure? (家長講座：「如何協助子女從
      失敗中成長」)
      The activity was held on January 30, 2026, with around 23 parents participating. Two

     speakers used their life experiences to help parents understand the importance of experiencing
     failure in a student's life. There was good interaction between the speakers and the participants.
     According to teachers’ observations and participant feedback, the parents were highly
     engaged in the activity and reflected deeply on the topic.

e.   Museum of Epic Failure Exhibition (失敗博物館展覽)
     This exhibition was held on Parents’ Day and showcased stories of failure and subsequent
     reflections from secondary students, university students, and parents. A small-scale book
     exhibition on the same theme was organized by the school library, allowing parents and
     students to borrow relevant books. Many parents were observed stopping to read the stories
     patiently. The activity successfully provided an opportunity for parents to reflect on the true
     meaning of failure and help their children build a growth mindset. It was agreed that the event
     was highly meaningful.

f.   Mental Health First Aid Course (精神健康急救課程)
     The course consisted of 4 sessions, each lasting 3.5 hours, held on March 21, March 28, April
     11, and April 18, 2026. Twenty-four parents enrolled. Attendance was satisfactory, and 7
     parents successfully obtained their certificates (criteria: 100% attendance and completion of
     a quiz).
     According to the questionnaire results, 100% of the respondents expressed satisfaction with
     the course content, which taught parents how to provide early intervention and initial support
     to adolescents developing mental health issues or experiencing a crisis until professional help
     is available. This indicated that they learned how to better support their children's healthy and
     balanced development. Parents’ sharing, their interactions with the instructors, and their
     questionnaire comments proved that they felt more prepared to guide their children through
     their developmental years.

g.   Parents’ Relaxation Workshop: Mosaic Lamp Making (馬賽克燈放鬆體驗)
     The activity was held on April 11, 2026, with 24 parents participating. 100% of the
     participants enjoyed the activity, expressing that they learned a practical way to relax and
     recharge their energy for their parenting roles.

     needs. Parents were invited to write down the most impressive parts of the talk, and their
     feedback demonstrated that they grasped the core message. Some parents expressed a desire
     for more in-depth discussions in the future.

     should continue in the coming year. For further improvement, the teacher-in-charge will
     explore ways to design an app that can easily record the accumulation of students’ exercise
     time so that they can see their own accomplishments more clearly.

     Mr. Patrick Wong reported that the S-miles Day was conducted successfully in March this
     year. The atmosphere was positive across all forms. However, there was no F.5 competition
