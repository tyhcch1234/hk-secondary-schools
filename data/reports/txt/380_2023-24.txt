1.   College Vision, Mission and Motto

     Year Founded: 2006

     Vision: It is our vision to provide the best nurturing opportunities that unleash students’
     potential, inspire excellence and cultivate lifelong learners and leaders of tomorrow.

     Mission:
     HKFYG Lee Shau Kee College (hereafter HLC) is dedicated to creating a positive
     environment where young minds are inspired and enthused to challenge themselves
     and strive for excellence inside and beyond the classroom. We are committed to
     nurturing students to be intellectually competent, with positive moral characters,
     values, conscientiousness and physical prowess.
     Passion for learning, knowledge and the broadening of horizons are the hallmarks of
     an education in HLC. We believe all these are essential for the greater development
     of inquisitiveness, curiosity, exploratory spirits and a global outlook, alongside
     academic pursuits.

     College Motto:
     Our College motto, “Wisdom in Action (智慧之源  求實求真),” inspires students
     to cultivate inquisitiveness and curiosity, not only in their studies but also in their
     approach to life and the world around them. To embody this motto, HLC fosters self-
     directed learning, self-discipline, and self-efficacy in three key areas: Wisdom in
     Learning, Wisdom in Relationships, and Wisdom in Living. These efforts are
     underpinned by six core values—Respect for Others, Perseverance, Integrity,
     Commitment, Benevolence, and Responsibility—which serve as the moral framework
     for shaping students’ character.

3.   Our College

     3.1 Basic Information
         We are a Direct Subsidy Scheme (DSS) school not participating in Secondary
         School Places Allocation (SSPA).

         Medium of Instruction:
         The College aims to help students become trilingual and bi-literate. All subjects
         except Chinese Language, Chinese Literature (S.4-S.6), Chinese History, Life &
         Society (S.1-S.2), Citizenship & Social Development Studies (S.4-S.6) are taught in
         English. English is widely used in assemblies and College activities. Putonghua
         is used in Chinese Language in S.1-S.2.

         Class Structure:
         The College operates 6 levels (Secondary 1 to Secondary 6), each with a maximum
         of 5 classes. The class size on average is below 32. In the 2023/2024 school year,
         we opened up to 30 classes with a total of 763 students.

     6.2 Academic Initiatives

         6.2.1    Teaching and Learning Digitally
                  The College has further developed blended learning in Self-Directed
                  Learning (SDL). With the successful launch of the BYOD (Bring Your
                  Own Device) program from S.1 to S.4 and the installation of digital
                  whiteboards in all classrooms, students have become adept at using iPads
                  extensively and skillfully during lessons. This extensive use of digital
                  devices is expected to expand further as the scheme extends to cover five
                  forms in the coming year.        Additionally, subject departments have
                  adopted SDL guided learning plans for at least 70% of the curriculum,
                  incorporating both core and extension parts to cater to learners’ diversity.

                  Throughout the year, various staff development opportunities were
                  arranged, focusing on supporting student learning with eLearning tools
                  and AI. With the introduction of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI)
                  during staff development days, all subjects had the chance to incorporate
                  GAI into their teaching and learning or observe its application in other
                  subjects. The English department collaborated with the Centre for Learning
                  Sciences and Technologies of CUHK to investigate the effectiveness of
                  using AI in English writing. All subject departments also conducted
                  lesson studies to explore how AI tools can enrich students’ learning, such
                  as through personalized learning, improving classroom engagement, and

    6.2.2   Catering for Diversities
            The College continued to advance STEAM education through both formal
            and informal curricula. Students achieved numerous awards in external
            competitions such as the “2024 Fun Science Competition” and “The 18th
            Infrastructure Building Competition for Secondary Schools,” enriching
            their learning experiences. Additionally, the College hosted a remarkable
            event, “Dialogue at Secondary Schools with Shaw Laureates,” where four
            Shaw Laureates visited our College to deliver an inspiring talk about future
            science careers and their personal journeys in science.

            For the 9th consecutive year, the College collaborated with The University
            of Hong Kong to introduce Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) to
            students, encouraging them to pursue courses from universities worldwide
            at their own pace. Teachers and students were honored to share their
            unique experiences at the Learning & Teaching (L&T) Expo held at the
            HKCEC in December 2023. The L&T Expo is a significant event for
            educators globally to exchange insights into their profession. The College
            integrated MOOCs into the school-based curriculum (MOOC x Reading),
            the promotion system known as the HLC Scholar Scheme, and the MOOC
            Award Scheme. All S3 students completed a MOOC course during the
            summer vacation, replacing traditional summer holiday assignments.

6.3 Academic Achievements

Our students performed outstandingly in inter-school academic competitions and
contests. The Public Speaking and Debating Team won the Championships and 1st
Runner-up in the Junior Division of the Hong Kong Secondary Schools Debating
Competition 2023/2024 Grand Finals. The English Drama Club won four awards in
the Hong Kong School Drama Festival. We had also got a number of awards in Hong
Kong Schools Speech Festival, Mathematics, Science and Liberal Studies competitions,
Sports competitions, Music contests and Harvard Book Prize.

2024 HKDSE Results
Our S.6 students obtained very good results in HKDSE. For the core subjects (Chinese
Language, English Language, Mathematics), the average percentages of level 2 or above
and level 4 or above were 98.3% and 43.1% respectively.

For all subjects, the average percentages of level 2 or above and level 4 or above are
98.4% and 42.4% respectively. Among all subjects, our students performed particularly
well in English Language, Mathematics, M1, M2, Physics, Chemistry, Information &
Communications Technology, History, Chinese History, Economics, Business,
Accounting & Financial Studies with over 40% of them obtaining level 4 or above.

7.   Student Development and Support

     7.1 Discipline and Guidance

     We prioritize promoting PERMA in Discipline and Guidance, creating an environment
     that nurtures positive emotions, student engagement, meaningful relationships, and a
     sense of accomplishment. Through various activities and counseling support, we equip
     students with tools for overall well-being.

     To foster students’ self-esteem and resilience, we introduced mindfulness activities
     during events like the Guidance Thematic Week and stress management workshops
     across different forms. These activities included creative pursuits such as alcohol ink
     painting, Scratch Art, planetary candle making, and harmonious pastels. At the
     conclusion of the thematic week, we also arranged for an ice cream truck to treat all
     HLCians, acknowledging their hard work following the First Examination.

     Students benefited from high-quality professional counseling services that effectively
     addressed their individual emotional needs. Our dedicated team, consisting of two
     Social Workers, a Student Counselor, and external professionals such as Clinical
     Psychologists, Educational Psychologists, and Speech Therapists, played a crucial role
     in providing essential support and guidance. The School Guidance Team regularly
     met with these professionals to facilitate coordination and alignment between academic
     and guidance initiatives. As a result, our students’ academic and personal development
     needs were met cohesively, fostering their overall well-being and enabling their success.

     Last but not least, the Teacher-Student Mentorship Program brought immense benefits
     to our students. All teachers became mentors, catering to specific needs across
     different areas. This program provided personalized support, fostering holistic
     development and strong teacher-student relationships, effectively nurturing academic
     growth and emotional well-being.

     7.2 Careers and Life Planning

     The “Careers and Life Planning” programs have been instrumental in guiding students
     towards successful academic and career pathways. By adopting a comprehensive
     approach that includes self-discovery, subject selection, real-world experiences, and
     collaborations with external organizations, our students have gained the knowledge
     and skills necessary to make well-informed decisions about their futures. These
     initiatives have empowered students, equipping them with essential skills for their
     forthcoming endeavors.

     Junior Form Programmes
     The programmes for junior forms have emphasized self-discovery and subject selection.
     Activities such as the Elective Subject Fair, Wisdom-In-Action (WIA) lessons, and
     informative sessions for parents have provided students with insights to make
     informed academic choices.

7.3 Moral and Civic Education (MCE)

The College places great importance on students’ moral development. Graded-
service learning programmes are implemented, allowing students to engage with
different target groups and learn the value of empathy and community service. These
experiences enable students to serve not only their local community but also contribute
to the betterment of the nation and the world. By instilling a sense of social
responsibility, the College aims to nurture students as moral leaders who can positively
impact society.

Additionally, the College conducted a thorough review and implementation of
National Security Education (NSE) across multiple aspects, including administration,
human resources, teaching and learning, discipline, counseling, and home-school
cooperation.   NSE was prioritized within the framework of Moral and Civic
Education, with morning assemblies serving as a platform for students to share the
importance of National Security, promoting awareness and responsibility. Moreover,
S.2, S.4, and S.5 students actively participated in the National Security Quiz
Competition, integrating it into their Life & Society (L&S) and Citizenship & Social
Development Studies (CS) lessons.

Furthermore, our S.3 and S.4 students taking Chinese History and History as electives
visited the Hong Kong Palace Museum for the “Hong Kong Jockey Club Series: The
Imperial Garden of Yuanmingyuan - The Royal Garden Culture of the Qing Dynasty”
exhibition during the post-exam period.       The exhibition showcases over 190
important artifacts related to Yuanmingyuan, including paintings, objects, and
architectural models, reproducing the former glory of this famous royal garden from
the Qing Dynasty.        The exhibition comprehensively presents the historical
appearance of Yuanmingyuan, allowing students to gain a deeper understanding of
Chinese garden art.

7.4 Other Learning Experiences (OLE)

The OLE Working Group at HLC provided a diverse array of extracurricular activities
aimed at nurturing student strengths, fostering an international outlook, developing
leadership skills, and enhancing unity among HLCians. Recognizing the importance
of broadening students’ horizons and facilitating life-wide learning experiences, the
college organized activities during Life-Wide Learning Week, including overnight
camps and trips to Mainland China. These initiatives not only fostered strong peer
relationships but also instilled self-discipline, life skills, and a global perspective.
Throughout the year, students engaged in various activities to explore their interests
and enrich their school lives.

To bolster students’ leadership qualities through student-led initiatives, the inaugural
“HLCian Love Concert” took place on 31 May 2024 during the Friday assembly. Led
by the heads of four performance groups, this event celebrated the achievements of
music performance groups in external competitions.               The student leaders
demonstrated exceptional organizational skills by coordinating the event, directing
rehearsals, and serving as conductors, providing a valuable platform for leadership and
coordination skill development.

Furthermore, the College’s commitment to language education is underscored by the
successful launch of Spanish, Japanese, and Korean language programs, currently
accommodating 105 students. Noteworthy is the introduction of a Public Exam
Course for Spanish students, resulting in two S.3 students excelling in the DELE A1
exam, qualifying them for DELE A2 classes for HKDSE. Dedicated external tutors
have enriched the learning experience by organizing engaging activities like the
Japanese festival day and visits to the Korean Cultural Centre. Additionally, students
in the Foreign Language Class Programme must pass a school-based test before
progressing to the second year, ensuring a solid foundation for their language learning
journey.

To further strengthen cohesion among HLCians, the OLE Working Group engaged
alumni and former club leaders in club training, benefiting CCA clubs such as the
Handbell Ensemble, Drama Club, Debate Team, Badminton Team, and Soccer Team.
This collaborative approach enhanced leadership development, provided students
with role models, and nurtured a sense of tradition and unity within the school
community.

7.5 Student Leadership

The College places a strong emphasis on nurturing future leaders. Leadership
development opportunities are available at various levels, including class and form
structures, interest clubs, service teams, and the student union. Students are
encouraged to engage in external programs like the “HKFYG Hong Kong 200
Leadership” project, which enhances their leadership skills and prepares them for
future responsibilities. Through the cultivation of leadership abilities, the college
aims to empower a cohort of leaders capable of effecting positive change within their
communities and society at large.

The student bodies, represented by the four houses and the Student Union, serve as
vital platforms for cultivating the leaders of tomorrow. These entities offer invaluable
experiences and training avenues for students to hone leadership capabilities, promote
teamwork, and enrich the school environment. In November 2023, the 1st “SU
Activity Week” which highly appreciated by both students and teachers was organised
by the Student Union.

During the Pre-S1 Orientation held in late August, our student leaders played a pivotal
role in welcoming newly enrolled HLCians. They organized engaging ice-breaking
activities and familiarized S.1 students and parents with the vibrant College culture,
fostering a welcoming and inclusive atmosphere.

HLCians participate in a diverse range of activities, including lunchtime performances,
annual singing contests, short-video film competitions, Christmas festivities, and
Sports Days.    The dedicated involvement of proactive student leaders further
enhances the sense of belonging and involvement among the student community,
nurturing a supportive and inclusive ethos.

The College is nurturing Leaders of Tomorrow!

8.   Home-school Cooperation

     The College has established an admirable partnership with parents, believing that a
     harmonious relationship between parents and teachers greatly enhances students’
     development.

     8.1 Communicating with Parents
         The College utilizes diverse channels to facilitate effective home-school
         communication. At the start of each term, the school conducts “Sunshine Calls” to
         connect with parents. Additionally, two Parents’ Nights are held to share school
         news, update students’ academic progress, and gather parental feedback on school
         policies. Parents also have the opportunity to meet with their child’s Form
         Teacher during Parents’ Day to discuss the student’s overall performance.
         Furthermore, the College leverages information technology to enhance
         communication, including the eClass Parent App and the frequently updated HLC
         Facebook page, which are well-received by parents and students. Every year, the
         College digitally distributes the HLCian Press and HLCian Link, providing the
         latest news on the College and student achievements. The College is grateful for
         the valuable input from student editors.

     8.2 The Parent-teacher Association (PTA)
         Founded in 2008, the PTA actively participates in College activities and provides
         substantial support. The PTA Standing Committee (PTASC) holds regular
         meetings, serving as a bridge between the College and parents. Parents assist in
         various ways, such as supporting student welfare and helping with Christmas
         party decorations. Two parents from the PTASC are elected as school managers
         of the School Management Committee (SMC), linking parents and the School
         Sponsoring Body.

         This year, the PTA organized numerous activities, including Yoga Classes, Parents’
         Talks, and a Christmas party, and worked closely with the Student Welfare
         Working Group on selecting service providers. On 19 May 2024, the PTA
         organized a picnic to Tai O with about 60 parents and students attending. It was
         the 1st PTA picnic after the pandemic, and the feedback was very positive. The
         PTA also organized events like Pilates Classes, a visit to the History Museum for
         the “天地之中一河南夏商周三代文明展,” and CPR & AED courses provided by St.
         John Ambulance.

         The PTA launched its first flag-selling fundraising activity for the Samaritan
         Befrienders Hong Kong. In September and October 2023, the PTA also organized
         its first Positive Mutual Support Group for new S.1 parents on five Saturdays,
         receiving very positive feedback.

         The PTA has been giving tremendous support to the College and organizing
         various activities for its members. Our heartfelt thanks to the PTA and parents
         for their unfailing support.

11. Future Planning

    The development and accomplishments of our College are evident through our strong
    track record of awards and achievements. In July 2020, the College received a highly
    encouraging ESR Report from the EDB, highlighting the professional leadership of the
    School Management, the effectiveness of our learning and teaching, and the
    outstanding academic and non-academic performances of our students.

    During the COVID-19 pandemic, our work became increasingly complex. However,
    with the leadership of SMC members and the dedication of our professional team, HLC
    remained committed to providing the best nurturing opportunities. We strive to
    unleash students’ potential, inspire excellence, and cultivate lifelong learners and
    leaders of tomorrow.

    As we conclude one three-year plan, we celebrate our success in unlocking potential
    and promising a brighter tomorrow. The successful implementation of Self-Directed
    Learning (SDL) has significantly enhanced our students’ learning habits, setting a
    strong foundation for their future growth. Looking ahead, we are excited to
    incorporate the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and eLearning into our curriculum,
    further enriching the educational experience and preparing our students for the future.
