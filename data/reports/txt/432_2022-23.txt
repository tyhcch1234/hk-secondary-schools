School Vision & Mission

To
        provide a holistic education;
        nurture people of excellence; and
        promulgate the Truth and lead students to an abundant life.




Our School

CNEC Christian College is a subsidised EMI school. Being a Christian school founded in 1973, it does
not only uphold the spirit of serving the community, but also practises its belief that young people
are to be educated and developed morally, cognitively, physically, socially, aesthetically and
spiritually if an all-round education is to be achieved. Consequently, CNEC Christian College
provides students with an environment whereby their academic performance can be improved,
their moral character can be edified and their interpersonal skills can be enhanced. Then, in
doing so, the school hopes that students will be inspired to know the Truth and lead a life of
excellence.


School Campus

Our school, with a floor area 100,000 square feet, comprises three teaching blocks with lift tower
installed, a hall, two covered playgrounds, two basketball courts, four volleyball courts, two
gardens and a five-lane running track.

Major Concerns 2022-2023 (Strategies, Success Criteria, Actual Progress & Follow-up Suggestions)
Major Concern 1: Nurturing students to be self-directed learners and catering for their diverse learning needs
Strategies

Area 1: Motivate students to self-direct their learning
 Curriculum development (revise school timetable, strengthen National Security Education and Values Education, review the existing curriculum).
 Encourage students to design their personalized learning method, provide tools and materials for students to be self-directed learners.
 Promoting reading through DEAR periods, reading across the curriculum in all subjects and library activities.
Area 2: Cater for the diverse learning needs of students
 Review the sequence of the content in the curriculum, optimize the pedagogies and resources, set appropriate teaching objectives to cater for students’ diverse learning
   needs.
 Establish peer learning groups among students.
 Reinforce assessment as learning and assessment for learning among teachers.
 Adopt various means of assessment tasks.
 Share effective pedagogies or good practices at department level.
 Arrange formal lesson observations and peer observations in the focus of catering for the diversified learning.

Success Criteria

Area 1: Motivate students to self-direct their learning
 Students can develop creativity, collaboration skill, problem solving skill and presentation skill through project learning and cross-curriculum learning.
 National Security Education and Positive Values Education are organically integrated and naturally connected into curriculum.
 Clear aims, objectives, scope of works, teaching and learning objectives of each section are stated in the teaching progress.
 Book sharing by teachers and students have been done during the DEAR periods.
 Reading across the curriculum has been done in all subjects.
 The borrowing rate of books in School Library has raised by 5 % compared with the school year 2021-22.
 In the Stakeholders’ survey, 70% of the students and 60% of the parents show their views that “I / My child often read materials such as leisure reading materials and
   newspapers outside class.”
 Workshops on note-taking skills for F.1 and F.2 students have been conducted, all teachers have joined the workshop and tried out the self-directed learning strategies
   within the lesson.
 Students can design their personalized learning methods such as pre-lesson study and post-lesson summary-making.
Area 2: Cater for the diverse learning needs of students
 Seminar on enhancing the teaching techniques for students with diverse learning needs is arranged for teachers and over 80% of teachers can apply the effective
   pedagogies introduced to teaching.
 Learning support programmes for gifted students and low achievers are provided.
 Over 90% of students can handle the graded exercises which suit their levels.
 Students have a self-evaluation by figuring out the mistakes and seeking improvements after tests and examinations.
 Students’ efforts are appreciated by an achievable reward scheme.

Actual Progress

Area 1: Motivate students to self-direct their learning

 Book sharing by teachers and students have been done during the DEAR periods,

 Workshops on note-taking skills for F.1 and F.2 students have been conducted (26/9/2022 and 3/10/2022) successfully. Most of the F.1 and F.2 students can apply the
   note-taking skills in their homework.
 The borrowing rate of books in School Library is 20.4 item /day in the school year 2022-23. The rate was 20.6 item / day in the school year 2021-22.
 In the Stakeholders’ Survey, 49% of the students showed that “I often read materials such as leisure reading materials and newspapers outside class.” and 48% of the
   parents showed that “My child likes reading.”.
 In the Stakeholders’ Survey, students have positive feedback on learning:
   - 54% of students showed that “I take an active role in learning inside and outside of the classroom.”
   - 48% of students showed that “I am confident in learning inside and outside of the classroom.”
   - 50% of students showed that “I enjoy learning inside and outside of the classroom.”
   - 54% of students showed that “I know how to set my own learning goals.”
   - 53% of students showed that “I know how to apply learning strategies, such as doing pre-lesson preparation, using concept maps and on-line resources.”
Area 2: Cater for the diverse learning needs of students
 All teachers have joined the professional development workshop about enhancing the teaching techniques for students with diverse learning needs. From the results of the
   survey,

   - 100% of the entire teaching staff agreed that “The workshop enhanced my knowledge of catering for learning diversity of students.”
   - 100% of the entire teaching staff agreed that “The workshop was stimulating and thought-provoking.”
   - 97.8% of the entire teaching staff agreed that “This workshop provided concrete and practical suggestions.”
   - 97.8% of the entire teaching staff agreed that “I will implement the knowledge gained from this workshop.”
   - In all, the overall impression of the teaching staff towards the workshop is positive.
 Learning support programmes for gifted students and F.1 to F.3 low achievers are provided by our school and external organizations such as HKAGE and HKUST.
 In the Stakeholders’ Survey:
   - 68% of students showed that “The teachers often teach us learning strategies, such as doing pre-lesson preparation, using concept maps and on-line resources.”
   - 58% of students showed that “Teachers often provide suggestions on my performance and let me know how to improve.”
   - 60% of students showed that “Teachers’ questions inspire my thinking inside and outside of the classroom.”
   - 62% of students showed that “Teachers often allow us to explore a variety of topics inside and outside of the classroom.”
   - 62% of students showed that “Teachers often organise different learning activities both inside and outside of the classroom, such as discussion, visits, sharing of insights
       gained from reading and oral presentation.”
   - 55% of students showed that “I often reflect on my performance in class, test and examination results, as well as teachers’ suggestions, such as their comments on my
       assignments, in order to reflect on and improve my learning.”

Follow-up Suggestions

Our school goes through a planning, implementation, and evaluation (PIE) process every year. By evaluating the 2022-23 annual plan, which is the first year of the Three-year
Development Plan (2022-25), follow-up actions are taken in the coming academic year.
 To improve students’ self-directed learning skills through reading and note-taking.
  - Workshops on note-taking skills for F.1 and F.2 students would be conducted by Chinese, English, Science and PSHE teachers.
  - Book sharing of teachers and students during the DEAR periods.
  - One reading period would be implemented into the school timetable for F.1 and F.2 students.
  - More reading activities and reading promotion from library.
 Strengthen the National Security Education, Values Education and the importance of 7 Learning Goals.
 7 Learning Goals would be integrated and naturally connected into curriculum. They would be clearly stated in the annual plan of all subjects.

Major Concern 2: Promoting positive values education and good mental health to help students live a purposeful life

Strategies

Area 1: To nurture students with Positive Values Education
 Elements of positive values have been instilled in all subjects. Reinforce elements of Positive Values Education in assemblies and OLE lessons.
 Whole-school activities and programs organised by action groups or task groups to promote positive values education.
 Activities organised by Moral and Civic Education task groups and subjects to promote Chinese culture and national identity.
 Create more opportunities for students to serve others and the community.
 Equipped class teachers with professional knowledge and enhance the effectiveness of classroom management.

Area 2: Promoting physical health and mental health by developing good habits and peer support
 Talks on developing healthy habits will be held to promote physical health.
 Activity of promoting physical ability will be held.
 Arrange talks on promotion of mental health for teachers, students, and parents.
 Organise special program to promote healthy lifestyle.
 Different schemes on peer support will be organized to promote peer support.
 Organise thanksgiving activity to nurture grateful attitude.



Success Criteria

Area 1: To nurture students with Positive Values Education
 The elements of positive values education have been included in all subjects and have been shown in the teaching progress.
 Whole-school activities and programs have been organized to promote positive Values education.
 Students are willing to abide by the law and regulations, their recognition of national identity is uplifted.
 40% of the Junior form and 25% of the Senior form students have taken part in voluntary service.

Area 2: Promoting physical health and mental health by developing good habits and peer support
 Students’ awareness of the significance of their physical health is aroused and their physical fitness is uplifted.
 Students’ awareness of the significance of their own mental health is aroused and the score of negative emotions is lowered and the score of self-concept is uplifted.
 Students are more willing to share with others and ask for help when they are in need.
 Positive feedback from teachers and students for the assembly of moral education.
 Students are willing to practice thanksgiving by writing records of gratitude.

    Years                        Details of the participation of Social Services
  2021/2022    Members of Girl Guides joined 2021-2022 ‘Make A Difference’ Community Service.
                They have learnt knitting from the retired Guide members, and gave those finished
                knitted items, made by themselves, to the elderly of the community.



  2022/2023    Three flag-selling activities were organised for all F.3 students throughout the year.
               Six F.3-F.5 students actively participated in a yearly pilot programme named ‘Voices
                of the Children Phase’ organised by Plan International Hong Kong. With the
                support of our students, children in the Shek Lei community were helped voice
                their views and put forward their innovative solutions to issues about
                transportation and play space.
               Collaborated with Adventure Ship Trainers and The Hong Kong Society of the Blind,
                Counselling Action Group arranged the ’Adventure Ship Buddies Program’ which
                aimed at providing an opportunity to our students and people with special needs
                to have a positive interaction. The programme included a volunteer-service training
                workshop, an inclusion orientation program between our students and the
                members of The Hong Kong Society of the Blind, an adventure ship training day
                and, the evaluation and sharing session.
               Members of Girl Guides participated in a raffle selling day, two flag hoisting
                ceremony on 4 May and 1 July 2023, and social inclusion with the elderly, through
                which they learnt to serve the community with a sense of responsibility and
                gratitude.
