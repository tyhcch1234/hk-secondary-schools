(1) Vision and Mission
Tak Oi Secondary School is sponsored by the Missionary Sisters of the Immaculate Conception
(M.I.C.). Through a whole school approach, we aim to provide quality Catholic Education to lead
our young girls into a life of joy and thanksgiving.


Love and Truth, our school motto, are the guiding principles for developing our girls and the
whole school. Amazed at God’s gracious love, we nurture in our girls a heart of appreciating all
that is good, true and beautiful. Acknowledging Jesus as the ultimate Truth, we enlighten our
girls to strive for excellence in their pursuit of knowledge and righteousness in life.


We are committed to fostering a caring, respectful and joyful environment with a rich array of
learning opportunities for our young girls to develop their unique potentials in all aspects and
to become a whole person.


We embrace the call to educate our girls to become responsible, resilient, creative, passionate
and collaborative young women who dedicate themselves to self-growth and to contributing to
the common good of the universe.

Achievements and Reflections on Major Concerns

The academic year 2025 – 2026 was the second year of the 3-year development cycle
(2024/25-2026/27) with the theme “Towards a Sustainable Well-being School Community”,
aiming to enable students to become more independent and flexible learners, to strengthen the
capacity to lead a more active and healthier lifestyle, and to enhance the sense of connectedness
with community, nation as well as the world for the common good of all.

Major Concern 1: To enable students to become more independent and flexible learners

Strengthening students’ learning competence was the prime objective of school development.
In the 3-year development cycle, the school would mainly focus on strengthening students’
creative problem-solving skills and empowering students with better self-management skills
particularly in task prioritization, decision making and expectation management.

Priority Task 1: Strengthening students’ creative problem-solving skills

Students were expected to become a generation of independent, flexible learners equipped to
navigate the complexities of their educational journeys and beyond through integrating
thought-provoking questions or authentic situations in school curriculum as well as learning
and teaching, and creating opportunities for students to express their opinions, ask questions,
discuss for solutions or suggestions in learning and teaching.

 Achievements

 The school made continuous progress in strengthening students’ creative problem-solving
 skills through lessons and learning beyond classroom that emphasized questioning,
 application of knowledge to authentic situations and reflection. Riding on the measures
 adopted in the previous year, the school further extended students’ reflection to their
 learning beyond the classroom. Reflection tasks were assigned to students to encourage them
 to express their opinions and conduct personal reflection on their daily life after engaging in
 whole-school sharing assemblies. In S1 and S2, the “Read-Think-Talk” Programme supported
 students to think beyond learning and reading, discuss ideas and reflect on their
 understanding after reading while Value’s Corner provided an open platform for students to
 express opinions and develop their thinking. The positive progress is reflected in the MCP
 Survey results in which students reported higher willingness to express ideas and ask
 questions, and they continued to strengthen their ability to think of different solutions to
 problems.

 Teachers’ professional development and instructional capacity were further enhanced to
 strengthen students’ creative problem-solving skills. Teacher sharing of best practices was
 fostered through structured professional dialogues, including open classrooms where all
 teachers observed or opened their lessons for exchanging professional dialogues. Selected
 commendable lesson plans and learning materials were collected and published in a good
 practice booklet, providing teachers clearer directions and practical exemplars aligned with
 key school focuses. In addition, the school collaborated with the Education Psychologist to
 support teachers in adopting SQ3R pedagogy to enhance students’ learning, particularly in
 reading comprehension. The pedagogy was initially introduced to S1 teachers and students
 and received positive feedback. It was further promoted to all teachers by the Education

Tak Oi Secondary School   School Report 2025-2026   Page 3
 Psychologist in a staff development workshop to empower other teachers to apply SQ3R
 flexibly across subjects in different forms, thereby creating more opportunities for students
 to think, solve problems in reading and communicate. There was a clear year-over-year
 improvement from the SHS (Teachers) in asking questions of different levels to inspire
 students’ thinking inside and outside of the classroom.

 Reflection

 Based on the evaluation findings, the school made progress in strengthening lesson
 engagement and students’ readiness to think and respond, contributed by teachers’
 improved awareness and use of questioning strategies of different levels as well as students’
 sustained development in problem-solving skills. These achievements were supported by
 structured reflection opportunities and question-led learning routines, which increased
 students’ willingness to raise questions inside and outside classrooms. However, the
 evaluation indicated that more deliberate alignment is needed to ensure questioning leads to
 clear application outcomes and is consistently experienced by students at different readiness
 levels, while questioning was increasingly diversified.

 Consolidating our experience from PIE, the school will strengthen a whole-school policy for
 lesson structure and lesson questioning by explicitly focusing on designing and sequencing
 questions based on students’ needs and levels, accompanied by appropriate scaffolding so
 that students can respond effectively as well as strengthening students’ learning routine in
 thinking after learning. The school will also extend the collection of good practices from
 teachers to assignment and assessment design in which good design and sequence of
 questions can be observed.

 Feedback and Follow-up

 The school will implement targeted follow-up measures to help students further enhance
 their creative problem-solving skills.
    • The Student Growth Journal will be redesigned into a Gratitude Journal for S1 and S2
         in which students will complete short, guided reflection promotes after key learning
         tasks, recoding what they learned, how they contributed, what they are grateful for
         and one take away they will try. This creates a continuous record of personal growth
         that teachers can use to provide timely encouragement and guidance.
    • To translate strong classroom questioning into visible learning evidence, teacher
         professional sharing will be extended to lesson planning exchange. Teachers will work
         together in departments to develop effective lesson plans and exchange professional
         views to uphold the learning and teaching effectiveness. The good practice booklet
         will also extend to assignment and assessment design, in which teachers can learn
         from each other on setting diversified assignment tasks and meaningful thinking
         questions.
    • A Digital Education Task Force Group will be set up to plan and guide the integration
         of digital education in school curriculum. English Language and Science will be the
         pilot subjects that use digital tools to provide AI-enabled challenge tasks that require
         students to generate ideas, test solutions and explain their reasoning so that creative
         problem-solving is strengthen through authentic practice and assistance of AI.
    • The school will reiterate the whole school approach of 3-in-1 structure and
         enrichment of students’ lesson preparation among the teaching force, encouraging
         teachers to use questioning as a lesson preparation task and provide students
         consistent opportunities to respond in the next lesson. In this way, students will be

Priority Task 2: Empowering students with better self-management skills particularly
task prioritization, decision making and expectation management

Empowering students with self-management skills enhanced their independence and flexibility
as learners. By organising school-based workshops or learning experiences on self-
management skills as well as promoting effective teaching pedagogies or tools for students to
organise and manage their learning, students would be better to assess tasks, make informed
choices, and set realistic goals, fostering resilience and confidence in their educational journeys.

 Achievements

 The school made steady progress in empowering students with better self-management
 skills with practical opportunities for students to plan, organise and improve their learning.
 The revision of S2 note-making skills using authentic situations helped students practise how
 to capture key information, organise it into usable knowledge and decide what mattered
 most during learning and revision. Teachers were also reminded in staff meetings to provide
 Sufficient practice opportunities and share effective approaches in class, ensuring students
 do not only understand note-taking procedures and skills, but could apply them consistently
 to meet learning expectations and organising their learning.

 Self-management skills were further strengthened through student programmes, especially
 S4 Leadership Curriculum – SPARK extending to community service projects. By requiring
 students to plan and execute ideas in real contexts and conduct workshops for S1 students
 on Experiential Learning Day, students learnt to set manageable goals, make decisions,
 sequence tasks and monitor their own progress. This curriculum not only heightened
 students’ awareness and reflection on important leadership qualities but also created an
 authentic platform for S4 students to manage their academic learning and other personal
 development. S5 students were also provided with a study skill workshop in which they
 acquired skills on time management and task prioritisation, bringing them alternatives in
 planning their studies and preparing for the public examinations.

 The Student Council Constitution was under review to balance students’ academic
 expectations and responsibilities, proposing to clarify duties of leadership posts, increased
 flexibility for junior students to take up Level 1 roles and engaged more students through
 small task forces. The school would collect students’ opinions through public consultation
 and followed the procedures of the Constitution to decide whether the proposal would reach
 consensus in the school community.

 Evidence from MCP Survey also supported the achievements by demonstrating students’
 ability to allocate time according to urgency and importance improved, their ability to
 retrieve necessary information online increased. Meanwhile, the SHS (Students) results
 reflected a strengthened culture of self-reflection in response to class performance,
 tests/exams, and teachers’ feedback. The evidence indicated that students were increasingly
 able to monitor progress and adjust strategies – an essential foundation for effective self-
 management.

 Reflection

 skills driven by authentic, practical learning contexts. Teacher alignment also ensured
 consistent application of skills in different subjects and class. However, the evidence also
 suggested that goal setting and learning strategies could be more sufficiently embedded or
 sustained across different year levels.

 With the clear school’s direction on students’ holistic development and its experience in the
 past two years, it reflects that effective empowerment of self-management skills requires

 The school will embed study skills from S1 to S4 according to their learning needs with

 Senior form students will acquire skills of better use of Error Skill Book and Online Question
 Bank by HKEdCity. Overall, this approach ensures students receive the suitable level of
 learning skills at the right time, increases practice and feedback, and helps students develop
 transferable self-management habits across junior and senior forms. With English Language
 and Science as the pilot subjects for promoting AI in Education in school, students will learn
 how to use apps for personalised feedback from the AI-driven learning platforms,
 empowering them better skills for managing their learning.

Major Concern 2: To strengthen students’ capacity to lead a more active and healthier
lifestyle

A vibrant and supportive school environment would be sustained through empowering
students to improve their mental and physical health, foster social connections, motivate
students to find meaning in learning and life, and make informed choices under the wave of
information in the modern world. Students were expected to share their active and healthy
energy with others and create sustainable impact in the school community.

Priority Task 1: Strengthening students’ interpersonal skills for positive and healthy
relationships in school

The school has implemented measures to strengthen students’ interpersonal skills for positive
and healthy relationships in school. Through diverse activities and initiatives, students are
cultivated with important values of respect for others, responsibility, empathy, unity,
benevolence and law-abidingness.

 Achievements

 Progress in strengthening students’ interpersonal skills for positive and healthy relationships
 is evident in both students’ self-reported learning outcomes and school climate indicators,
 aligned with the school’s target values of respect for others, responsibility, empathy, unity,
 benevolence and law-abidingness. The school’s whole-school approach on the theme

 “Together we hope, united in joy” as well as other values-aligned activities and programmes
 organised particularly by the Value Education Team created a learning culture where
 students practised these values in daily interactions. In the MCP Survey, students consistently
 reported strong growth in development healthy peer relationships and overall relationship-
 building attitudes remained consistently strong across terms. Students also reported
 improved teacher support and encouragement, supporting a caring environment where
 empathy and benevolence were modelled and learned.

 The concerted effort of different subjects and teams to integrate the values of hope, joy and
 unity in students’ learning experiences, APASO findings further demonstrated the
 effectiveness of the whole-school culture. Satisfaction with life at school rose together with
 the satisfaction with relationships with teachers. Correspondingly, KPM indicators on school
 connectedness improved with a significant increase in belonginess and not lonely, showing
 that students feel more included and supported in the school community.

 The whole school impact was also strengthened by parallel work on staff well-being and
 collaboration. Teachers participated in well-being workshops and staff development

Priority Task 2: Enhancing students’ engagement and motivation in learning

The school emphasizes the importance of cultivating a sense of meaning, ownership and
purpose through enhancing students’ engagement and motivation in learning. With a stronger
connection with their education and growth, students will develop an intrinsic motivation that
fosters a vibrant school community through shared goals and collaborative experiences.

 Achievements

 The school successfully strengthened Open Classroom teaching practices to share effective
 pedagogies and good lesson plans among teachers for enhancing students’ engagement and
 motivation in learning. Teacher better linked authentic learning tasks to students’ needs and
 interests, and more effectively create learning opportunities for students to engage in the
 process of knowledge construction that sustained students’ learning motivation. The
 enhanced Talent Pool measures, various one-off EDB funding sources and experiential
 opportunities for talent development especially S4 Experiential Leadership Programme
 helped identify students’ strengths and expose them to a wider range of learning
 opportunities beyond school as well as some external competitions. This deepened students’
 relevance to the meaning of learning and developing their potential.

 Apart from learning, a rich array of activities and programmes were organised to cultivate
 students the spirit of resilience and positive emotions that supported students to overcome
 difficulties or challenges and to regain motivation to continue their growth. The school also
 successfully applied for a grant to create a space in school named Bounce Back Centre in the
 coming two years in which students and teachers would be given opportunities to develop
 their resilience and positive emotions through different tools and workshops. Students
 would improve coping after setbacks, build self-efficacy and persistence, support healthier
 responses to challenges and promote meaning in effort.

 These outcomes were also reflected across different KPM, APASO and MCP surveys, for
 example, KPM Meaning in Life index increased significantly, indicating that students
 increasingly perceive learning and life experience as purposeful. The notable increase among
 junior form students also reflected a stronger overall positive orientation in the school year.
 APASO results supported this upward trend in students’ motivation with an increase in the
 proportion of students who were satisfied that they have clear meaning/ purpose. Students
 reported improved discovery of satisfactory meaning and clearer understanding of what
 gives life meaning. These findings suggested that students were not only more willing to
 engage, but also more able to connect learning with personal purpose – an essential driver of
 motivation across the seven learning goals. MCP data showed improvements in day-to-day
 learning engagement and emotional support. Students’ willingness to participate in lessons
 slightly increased overall while perceptions that learning is meaningful remained
 consistently high and improved marginally. Importantly, students expressed that they felt



Tak Oi Secondary School   School Report 2025-2026   Page 8
 supported by the school to stay positive and helped sustain their motivation and
 participation.

 Reflection

 The school’s progress in enhancing students’ engagement and motivation in learning was
 contributed by the relevance of students’ learning to real-life situations which was
 considered as an important motivator that helped students to find real meaning from their
 learning and interests in engaging in the learning process. Also, the consistent cultivation
 of positive values and the encouragement from teachers increased students’ readiness to
 engage in their learning. Students felt safe, steady and more capable of learning when they
 were recognised of their effort and developed more positive emotions. Enhanced and
 continuous professional development culture among teachers supported teachers to
 develop more appropriate and effective teaching pedagogies and learning activities that
 could improve how lessons were delivered, supported and adjusted to students’ needs as
 well as how students could receive clearer expectations, better feedback and learning tasks
 that felt more accessible and meaningful. However, the school noticed that student’s
 motivation could be further sustained through day-to day learning in every subject and
 context for stronger continuity. Their learning motivation could further extend to their
 assignment and assessment in which they could also notice their effort and improvement.


 Feedback and Follow-up

 The school will implement the following follow-up measures to reinforce motivation through
 authentic engagement, recognition and enhanced lesson routines. All teachers will be
 reminded of the consistent implementation of “3-in-1” routine across classroom so students
 experience predictable lesson flow, clearer expectations and more structured opportunities
 to participate and engage in knowledge construction. The time for students to activate their
 prior knowledge and connect learning to purpose as well as the embedded consolidation for
 understanding and quick reflection to maintain learning momentum will sustain students’
 willingness to participate and make their learning to be more visible. The whole-school
 academic quiz competition organised by the Academic Prefects as well as the theme-based
 KLA challenges with authentic questions prepared by different subjects do not only build
 wider engagement of students but also to provide extra opportunities for students to apply
 subject knowledge to solve a practical task. Students’ achievements and effort in learning will
 also be recognised and displayed at the Jubilee Court to strengthen a culture of sharing and
 pride in learning. Students’ motivation will be boosted through recognition and belonging,
 reinforcing positive emotions and encouraging students to engage more consistently.

Priority Task 4: Cultivating a stronger awareness of information literacy through whole-
school approach

A whole-school approach is expected to be gradually developed to empower students to make
informed choices about their growth and education. By developing their critical thinking skills,
students can navigate information effectively and make decisions that can benefit their growth
and lead to healthier and more active lifestyle decisions.


 Achievements

 The school’s achievements in cultivating stronger awareness of information literacy through
 a whole-school approach was evidenced by improvements in students’ self-reported abilities
 across key information targets such as assessing information online, evaluating reliability or
 authenticity, developing ideas, protecting privacy and resisting inappropriate online
 behaviours. The holistic review of the information literacy curriculum clarified the roles of
 different departments and teams in the school and ensured that the important knowledge
 and skills of the curriculum were covered in suitable subject content and lessons, mainly the
 Information and Communication Technology as well as Citizenship, Economics and Society.
 Teachers of other subjects also included instructions and suitable learning content to
 reinforce students’ awareness on reliability checking and transforming information into
 original thinking.

 The Values Education Team also organised programmes to further enhance students’
 awareness and understanding of information literacy such as Intellectual Property
 Interactive Drama for S1 – S3 students. The Discipline Team also collaborated with the Police
 Force to organise talks on preventing online scams and proper use of online information to
 update students with the most recent cases and to enhance students’ skills in protecting their
 privacy. All the above measures and programmes empowered students to locate necessary


Tak Oi Secondary School   School Report 2025-2026   Page 11
 online information, made a positive progress on students’ evaluation, and sustained students’
 digital responsibility.

 Reflection

 The whole-school approach successfully made information literacy as a part of daily learning
 so that students were exposed to routines of searching and use of online sources across
 lessons and different subject contexts. With the consistently high students’ digital
 responsibility, the workshops or talks linked with practical examples or authentic situations
 were found to be effective in shaping attitudes and behaviours. The approach of making good
 use of lesson learning tasks supplemented with interactive and authentic workshops or talks
 was suitable to increase students’ information literacy. However, students showed the need
 to further enhance their skills on evaluating the source as well as applying higher-order
 thinking skills in the use of information online. Students required ongoing practice and
 reminders to maintain the habit and awareness. Further focus could also be put on
 empowering students to synthesize, compare viewpoints and form justified original ideas.

 Feedback & Follow-up

 The school will keep the successful whole-school integration and practical digital safety
 reinforcement, strengthen higher-order components by embedding structured tasks and
 sufficient guidance for students to compare multiple sources and to create new information,
 and monitor closely on students’ use of online information in their learning. A Digital
 Education Task Force Group will also be set up to support the implementation of digital
 education in school through shaping the digital roadmap, driving teacher professional
 development, and implementing student wellbeing and digital safety initiatives. Information
 literacy will remain as a significant task to continue while promoting digital education in
 school.

Enhancing students’ awareness of community, national, and global issues fosters a strong sense
of connectedness. By understanding the broader context of their actions, students become
active, responsible citizens. This awareness builds a sustainable school community, where
collaboration and empathy thrive, ensuring collective well-being and a commitment to positive
societal change.

 Achievements

第三部分：成效檢討

1. 考察活動預期目標：
   •   透過參觀澳門或珠海市區深化學生對城市規劃及旅遊業發展的認識
   •   參觀澳門旅遊大學，掌握餐飲及款待業的最新趨勢與前景
   •   透過西餐餐桌禮儀體驗，提升學生對餐飲業的理解及社交場合應對能力
   •   參觀自然景觀與生態旅遊地點，認識兩地之生態旅遊發展
   •   並透過體驗活動了解自然地理及高新娛樂科技對旅遊與娛樂業的影響

2. 考察活動達到的成效：
   •   參觀澳門市區、主要旅遊景點、橫琴粵澳深度合作區規劃建設博物館及國家方志館大
       灣區分館，使學生了解澳門與珠海的主要城市規劃要點（如交通、用地分配與旅遊商
       圈），並認識兩地的發展方向與機遇。
   •   參觀澳門旅遊大學及其教學酒店，讓學生深入了解酒店與餐飲業的營運模式，掌握業
       界發展機會，並認識從業所需的關鍵能力與素質。
   •   在澳門旅遊大學教學餐廳完成西餐禮儀體驗後，學生能正確示範基本餐桌禮儀（包括
       餐具擺放、用餐順序與餐前／餐中禮節），並在餐飲場合中自信應對。
   •   參觀澳門黑沙海灘與橫琴芒洲濕地公園，提升學生對當地生態與海岸地貌的認識，並
       理解城市發展對自然環境與生態旅遊的影響。
   •   在國家地理探索中心參與體驗活動，增進學生對科技應用的理解，並示例說明高新娛
       樂科技（如 AR/VR 導覽、智慧導覽系統）在旅遊與娛樂業的實際應用與效益。


3. 考察活動可以改善或繼續推展的地方：
   • 強化體驗式學習：除西餐餐桌禮儀體驗外，建議安排學生於澳門旅遊大學之教學酒店
     參與崗位實務觀摩與輪值體驗，以了解酒店不同職務之工作內容與所需技能，並提升
     職場適應力與實務理解。
   •   於校內推廣餐桌禮儀：建議由參訪學生製作海報或小冊子，在校內宣導餐桌禮儀，將
       學習內容向全校擴散，促進禮儀內化為日常行為，並強化學生之職場軟實力（儀態、
       社交禮節與溝通能力），以提升就業競爭力。
   •   加強與業界人士交流：於每場參訪（如澳門旅遊大學及其教學酒店、國家地理探索中
       心等）安排業界從業者進行經驗分享與座談，或設置問答與小型訪談環節，促進學生
       與業界直接互動，使學生更全面理解產業現況並掌握實習與就業資訊。

    student attitude, loan of books, engagement in reading activities)

      a.    Loan of Books
            i.   Total book checkouts reached 3,114 in 2025–2026, circulation remained steady and satisfactory overall. This solid baseline confirms
                 that continuous investment in book procurement successfully maintained strong student reading habits across the school.


      b.    Student Attitude
            i.   S1-S3: Exhibited high enthusiasm, active participation, and positive attitudes when reading was tied directly to interactive classroom
                 tasks (e.g., Read-Think-Talk Programme, Rac Programme, Author Talk, etc.).
           ii.   S4–S5 Cohorts: Showed a mature, goal-oriented reading attitude, actively utilizing newly purchased reference books and literature to
                 support their Chinese and English SBA project requirements.


      c.    Engagement in Reading Activities
            i.   The Reading Week Challenge (Christmas & World Book Day) and the Book Exhibition held alongside Parents' Day drew high student
                 participation. Guest author talks (Albert Tam, Reena Bhojwani and Ah KIU) also achieved high student engagement and critical
                 thinking.

           i.   The budget was directly allocated toward purchasing books integrated into classroom instruction (such as 'Read-Think-Talk' sessions

                into scheduled class times.
