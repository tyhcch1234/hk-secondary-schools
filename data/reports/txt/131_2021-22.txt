Foreword by the Principal
The school year 2021/22 marked the completion of our three-year School Development Cycle
started in 2019/20. We have encountered many new challenges under the pandemic and we found
that challenges brought opportunities which were the nutrients for students and teachers to grow.
We planned and paved the path for student development and teacher professional training three
years ago with adjustments made from time to time; we have learned that God is always in control
and all we could do is to serve Him faithfully. Therefore, ‘God’s Will is Our Zeal’ was an
appropriate school theme for 2021/22. The theme Bible verse from Romans 12:11-12 reminded us
to be joyful in hope, patient in affliction and faithful in prayer.

Whatever we planned, we prayed earnestly; we did whole-heartedly to create meaningful learning
experiences for students. When we successfully completed any schemes and projects, we felt joyful;
when we had to cancel any activities after all the preparation work done due to the tightening of
epidemic prevention measures, we kept our patience. That was what we have learned in the past
couple of years and we will cherish these accumulated learning motivation and experiences for the
years to come.

Through reading this report, I hope you will praise the Lord with us and give thanks to Him for His
providence and blessings upon the School. I want to take this opportunity to express my heartfelt
appreciation and gratitude to my colleagues who have been working diligently throughout the year;
to parents who have supported the School unceasingly; to students who always trust us and allow
us to walk with them; and to all KEians for your love and prayer support to the school community.
I am sure your rewards have been recorded in Heaven.

To God be the Glory!


Your Principal,


Dr. Cheng Kin Tak Samuel

    Capacity building
    This year, professional exchange on AaL has taken the form of subject-based exchange of good
    practices. In the 12th Curriculum Celebration ‘Subject Sharing’ parallel sessions held on
    8/7/2022, some subjects shared curriculum materials or assessment practices in relation to self-
    assessment, peer assessment or feedback. Teachers in general found the sessions stimulating and
    inspiring and treasured the opportunity to learn from each other.

    Strategies and practices of AaL
    Most subjects incorporated AaL tasks or practices into their curriculum, teaching and learning,
    and students were found to get accustomed to the following:
         Class-based bonus marks (based on student/ class-initiated self-learning tasks)
         Unit/ lesson checklist for goal-setting, monitoring understanding as well as progress
         Rubrics for self or peer assessment and feedback

            Learning guides for self-assessment
            Use of reflection questions for learning tasks/ assessments
            Use of reflection questions for action planning
            Use of online platforms or apps for giving instant feedback to students
            Checkpoints for projects/ extended tasks

    Training for students
    Due to time constraint and special class arrangements throughout the year, formal training on
    AaL was not offered to students. Instead, senior form students were invited to share some
    practical experience in the school assembly in September 2021 on how to monitor and assess
    their own learning. Form-based training, particularly for junior form students, will be
    strengthened and conducted in the FP next year.

    At subject level, the adoption of different AaL strategies and materials such as the promotion of
    peer assessment, the use of rubrics for self-reflection, among most of the subjects was evident.
    Such practices will be sustained in the coming year to equip students to be self-directed learners.

    Reflection and Evaluation:
    As the concluding year of the current school development cycle, changes in students’ learning
    motivation and self-regulation have been gradually witnessed, no matter from the observation
    and feedback by teachers, ESR team and data shown in the KPM questionnaires.

    The reform of academic structure launched three years ago has indeed created an environment
    conducive to the facilitation of Assessment as Learning. Students, in general, have
    demonstrated their initiative in their own learning, as observed in lesson as well as in
    assignments. Measures taken to enhance students’ intrinsic learning motivation through the
    facilitation of Assessment as Learning have begun to bear fruit, as seen from students
    demonstrating more ownership of their study and the ability to regulate and assess their
    learning.

    In the next SDC, efforts to incorporate AaL into our daily learning and teaching practices will
    be sustained, and new initiatives will be implemented to empower students as self-directed
    learners.

     2.2.1 Objective 1: To further promote the school atmosphere for the learning of positive
                         characters

     Strategies and Progress:
     To further promote the school atmosphere for the learning of positive characters, the Positive
     Education Team expanded with three new members (Miss Ma Yin Ting, Mrs. Tsang Lee Wing
     Yee, Miss Choy Wai Yan Vivien). Besides, all members were equipped with skills and
     knowledge of positive education by attending positive education training held by Geelong
     Grammar School and Bei Shan Tang Foundation.

     For professional development, the team organized two PD sessions for all teachers in summer
     2021. Team members shared their vision and learning throughout the year. The team also offered
     a mindfulness program in September 2021 for teachers who are interested. There were 15
     participants in total and positive feedback received.

     A pilot scheme of positive education programs in S.3 was launched, with a teacher training
     workshop held by Bei Shan Tang Foundation for S.3 teachers.

     The team also launched several programs for the whole school. Students of all Forms completed
     the character strength inventory and posted the result on the character strength board in each
     classroom. At Form and Class level, the team designed a set of game cards (Mind-roaming cards)
     for class-based sharing. Furthermore, several chapels about character strengths and failure
     education were held in different Forms. This year, the team also tried to hold a “WeZone
     Competition”, aiming to create a positive atmosphere in classroom, but the competition was
     cancelled due to lesson suspension. At individual level, students were required to review their
     character strengths before each Parents’ Day. An official Instagram account was set up to
     promote positive messages at school. Students are also encouraged to share with the team on
     Instagram, creating an alternative channel for the school to distinguish students who are at risk
     preliminarily. To further enhance the use of positive phrases and comments in students’ learning
     reflection, My Journal 3.0 was used to facilitate class sharing, even during school suspension.

     Reflection and Evaluation:
     My Journal (21-22 Revised Version) received positive feedback from Form Teachers as it was
     a good communication channel with students. The WeZone competition which was called off
     this year will revive in the coming year to promote the positive character learning. For the
     positive descriptors in report cards, effort has to be done so that there is more internalization
     of students on character strengths. The Positive Education Team will strengthen students’
     positive values by facilitating the implementation of character strengths.

    2.2.2 Objective 2: To cultivate the culture of mutual respect in school

    Strategies and Progress:
    A systematic whole-school sex education curriculum that further emphasizes mutual respect in
    interpersonal relationship was implemented. Two activities were organized in each form.

    It was hoped to strengthen mutual respect among S.1 students through dorm programs. Though
    they stayed shorter in dorm this year due to intermittent school suspension, dorm programs with
    the aim of promoting mutual respect were carried out successfully.

    The ‘Project Gabriel’ (Big Brother and Sister Scheme) also aimed at further strengthening the
    teaching of mutual respect between S.1 students and Prefects. The meetings were held
    successfully in Spring Session when face-to-face teaching was allowed. D&C teachers shared
    mutual respect with Prefects, who in turn designed games and activities for S.1 students. They
    also shared the topic of mutual respect with them in small group chatting.

    The promotion of mutual respect was also done in S.1 & 2 Camp which was postponed to July
    2021-2022 (day camp) due to the pandemic and tightened government policy. Yet, team-
    building activities were organized and successfully implemented with the aim of promoting
    mutual respect.

    Mutual respect was promoted to transfer students as well. D&C Team offered a form-based
    program while student counsellors held some games to build their relationships and attachment
    to school. Students were also assigned to small groups to meet the student counsellors and
    mutual respect was achieved through chatting.

    Reflection and Evaluation:
    Positive and encouraging feedback was received from both students and teachers. S.5 students
    were especially engaged in the relationship card game.

    For Project Gabriel conducted by D&C Team through Prefects, students enjoyed the game
    time and chatting with their big brothers and sisters.

    S.1-2 students also enjoyed and participated actively in the day camp activities at school. They
    learned the importance of mutual respect in interpersonal relationship through team-building
    activities.

     2.2.3 Objective 3: To widen students’ global perspective through curriculum, trips and
           service opportunities

     Strategies and Progress:
     It was planned that a whole-school environmental protection program would be refined.
     Unfortunately, the intermittent suspension of face-to-face teaching has put a halt to the whole-
     school energy saving campaign and recycling activity.

     It was also hoped that GLocal Weeks trips to different parts of the world could be organised
     to widen students’ global horizons and enrich their life experiences. Nevertheless, all the
     overseas trips could not be arranged under the pandemic. Yet, a group of students joined an
     online gathering organized by Global Christian Schools Network. It provided a chance for our
     students to share our service trip experiences and learning with other students over the world.

     To minimize the impact of health issues on the tours, instead of having overseas trips, local
     activities were organised during the post-exam period of the year 2021-2022. Eighteen activities
     were held in late July, 2022. After suspending for two years, almost all S3-S5 students joined.

     On the other hand, we witnessed more service opportunities for various target groups in the local
     community. For class service, we served 3 elderly centres, 1 special school and 1 children and
     youth centre. To raise students’ awareness of the needs of the local community in Hong Kong,
     a big Peacebox campaign was arranged with Hong Kong Church Network for The Poor.
     Altogether 80 boxes were collected and sent to the neighbour churches for distribution.

5.5 Library as a Learning Centre
     One of the missions of the library is to equip our students to be information literate; and hence,
     students are provided with the opportunity to learn and acquire the skills of accessing
     information efficiently and effectively, reading and evaluating information critically and
     competently, and using information accurately and creatively. They are also expected to
     practice ethical behaviour in regard to the use of information and information technology.

     5.5.1 Explicit teaching of Library and Reading Skills
     Library Program was organized for junior forms and conducted during the Reading Time.
     Objectives of this program are to help students become effective users of information (both
     printed and electronic resources), develop the habit of reading for learning, become

                                                                               God’s Will is Our Zeal 31

     independent information seekers & life-long learners, and develop problem-solving and
     communication skills. The library sessions during the face-to-face suspension period were
     conducted online.

     5.5.2 Extended Research
     Junior Form students were offered the opportunity to research on specific topics as assigned
     by the subject teachers using both printed and electronic resources. High-order thinking with
     reading & information skills learned in the Library Program were applied in these research
     units.

     5.5.3 Electronic Resources Workshops
     Workshops were conducted to introduce eResources, like Literacy Pro Library, LightSail
     Digital Reading Program, HyRead, eRosen eBooks and Flipster to staff and students.

     5.5.4 School Library Webpage
     A library webpage in the school website was created and launched in September 2021 which
     serves as an eResources hub and also includes library updates/activities.

     5.5.5 School Library System Upgrade
      The catalog of the upgraded school library system covers not only printed items in the library
      collection, but also school subscribed eBooks, items available from HKPL and web resources;
      new features of the system have enhanced users’ experience in information search.

     Systematic learning and teaching of library/ information/ reading skills has been achieved
     through the Junior Form Library Program. Opportunities for skills application were provided
     in Extended Research Projects implemented in collaboration with different subject
     departments. Availability and accessibility of quality printed, multimedia and online resources
     facilitated the skills learning and application process.

5.6 Reading for Learning
     Current research highlights the importance of reading in the success of student academic
     performance; reading can also help students cultivate characters attributing to leadership, such
     as decision-making, problem-solving, and communication. Moreover, the high emotional
     intelligence acquired through reading helps readers maintain a happy and healthy life style. In
     view of the above, the Library puts a strong emphasis on reading promotion and helping
     students develop a strong reading habit.

     5.6.1 Reading Scheme

     polish their research skills. The reading assignments were integrated as pieces of HW in

     in September. Students took the Lexile Test at the beginning of the school year to have their

     their Lexile growth.

6.5 Life Planning and Career Guidance
     Life planning education at UCCKE aims to help students understand themselves and explore
     various further study opportunities and the working world, so that they can make informed
     decisions about their future study and career, ultimately contributing to the society through
     utilizing their God-given talents. Close collaboration between Life Planning Committee and
     other committees and external organizations has been witnessed throughout the year to align
     students’ academic and non-academic pursuits with their aspiration.

     Full implementation of the enriched Life Planning Curriculum was witnessed this year, with
     programs and activities delivered to all S.1 to S.6 students based on the objectives and
     developmental needs for each form. Activities were organized in various scales and modes,
     ranging from mass programs, workshops, small groups to individual support, with an aim to
     cater for the diversified needs of students. Activities were also delivered in both virtual and
     on-campus settings to cope with the changing situation brought by Covid-19.

     With the release of lesson time brought by senior secondary subject optimization, a new
     subject named OLE (Life Education) was launched this year, comprising of moral and civic
     education, life planning and value education. Various important topics in relation to CLD as
     well as visits and activities were infused into the curriculum, giving students one further
     platform to explore their aspiration and pathways.

     Offering students with individualized support remains our top priority. Individual counseling
     was offered to S.5 and S.6 students, with teachers in the committee as well as FC/ FTs/ AFTs
     meeting students individually or in pairs and providing them with guidance and advice on
     both their aspiration exploration and academic pursuit. NLC students were also given
     individualized guidance on their non-JUPAS and overseas university application process. For
     S.5 students, individual counseling (i.e. career exploration coaching) was scheduled from
     February to May 2022, conducted initially on Zoom and later on campus after school
     resumption. For S.6 students, 2 sessions of counseling were offered. The first session was held
     from September to November 2021, which mainly dealt with their choice of university
     programs while the second one in late July 2022 (August for 6F), aiming to take good care of
     students’ emotional needs in face of the unpredictable circumstances and more importantly,
     help students make informed decision on their further study options after the release of
     HKDSE/ IAL results. To better support S.3 students’ needs in choosing their senior secondary
     subjects, group counselling was offered in Winter with the assistance of FC, FT and AFT.
     Students generally found the Counseling Handbook and group counselling meeting useful in
     helping them identify their interest, ability and strengths and thus the suitable stream and
     subjects for the next learning stage.

6.12 Boarding Life
      Boarding at UCCKE is not simply living away from home. It is a bridge between students’
      comfort zone and a challenging vibrant school community. The experience will remain a
      positive influence for years to come. What makes residential life at KE unique is our
      dormitory training curriculum. The curriculum included cooperative games, adventure
      games, sports climbing, drama training, pastoral caring by senior teachers and church pastor,
      and a formal dinner. The Dormitory and Facility Extension Block enables the school to
      enrich students’ learning experience.

      Due to COVID-19, this year we rescheduled the dormitory programme to all students who
      failed to participate in it in the past two years. Finally, 6 sessions have been held for S3, 2AE,
      2BD (half), 1E, 1AC and 1BD students. They have completed the dormitory program for
      around 3 weeks.

      S1 Students’ interview indicates that over 70% students think they have learnt to be more
      independent during the dormitory period. And almost 90% of them agree their friendship
      with classmates has been improved. From the perspective of parents, over 90% of parents
      agreed that the communication skills have been improved.

      For the dormitory personnel, tutors not only played a proactive role in students’ learning,
      they also spent time playing with students.

     6.14.1 Communicating
     Our school provides different opportunities for parents to communicate with the Principal, the
     Form Teachers and Subject Teachers. The regular Principal’s letters, e-class notices &
     announcements, electronic mails and individual phone calls are very important
     communication channels in our school. These channels keep parents updated of school events
     and allow parents to have easy access to teachers.

     According to the questionnaire analysis, parent interviews and through observation, Parent-
     Teacher Happy Hour (PTHH) is always one of the most popular school events for parents.
     Parents and teachers can have informal chat in a relaxing atmosphere. There were a total of
     three PTHHs in the school year.

     It is our tradition that all new parents of S1 were cordially invited to have breakfast with the
     Principal by class. The breakfast gatherings were held from September to October, which
     offered a good chance for parents to share their concerns and comments. Our Principal also
     invited individual parents of other forms to have breakfast with him throughout the year.

     The Parents Teachers Association (PTA) in our school is a very important channel to facilitate
     communication between school and all parents. According to the Constitution, the term of
     office for parent officers is a two-year period from January 1 to December 31, the officer team
     is composed of seven parent officers and four teacher officers. Active parent participation is
     highly valued as we work together to build an ideal campus for our children.
