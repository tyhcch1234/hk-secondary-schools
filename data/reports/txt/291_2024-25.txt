Our School
The school is one of the 32 Sheng Kung Hui secondary schools in Hong Kong and is named after Bishop
Mok Sau Tseng. Founded in 1975, it has a history of 50 years and is a co-educational secondary school.


Mission
Our mission is to provide the best opportunities for the formation of Christian and humanistic values, to
foster constructive attitudes and develop a range of skills in order that our students can develop their
potential to participate fully in society and become a responsible citizen of the world.

We would endeavour to provide a caring and loving environment where everyone is regarded with
mutual trust, respect and love to make the years we spend in MST a most nurturing and happy
experience.

Evaluation of Annual School Plan 2024 – 25
Major concern 1: To enhance learning effectiveness
Intended Outcome (1): Embrace learner diversity and stretch students’ potential to the fullest
Achievements:
    Lift-off classes to cater for learner diversity
     The pilot program for S1 and S2 has demonstrated a significant positive impact, with over 70% of students
     showing improved academic standings across the core subjects of Chinese Language, English Language, and
     Mathematics. At the S3 level, outcomes revealed a more diverse pattern of achievement, offering valuable
     insights for program refinement. Improvement was most pronounced in Mathematics, with 76.5% of students
     showing progress. Chinese Language results were also strong, with 66.7% of students demonstrating
     advancement. The results for English Language, at 36.4%, highlight a specific area for focused review and
     development.
    Junior form elite program
     In the English Language and Humanities stream, post-program evaluations revealed overwhelmingly positive
     feedback. Over 80% of Secondary 1 participants reported increased confidence in applying new study skills to
     their academic subjects, and more than 70% of Secondary 3 students felt more confident in their ability to
     undertake independent learning. Their high-quality collaborative work showcased exceptional creativity, critical
     inquiry, and mastery of self-directed investigation skills.
     The Mathematics program produced outstanding tangible results, with 40 junior form students qualifying for the
     semi-finals of prestigious competitions including the Hua Xia Cup and the Asia International Mathematical
     Olympiad. Our students secured 17 awards in the Hua Xia semi-finals, 8 in the finals, and 12 awards in the Asia
     International semi-finals. A particular highlight was one student earning a Gold Honor at the 2025 AIMO Final
     in Tokyo.
     In Chinese Language, an overwhelming majority of participants (89.5%) agreed that the course enhanced their
     understanding of the genres and evolution of classical Chinese prose, while 84.2% reported a heightened
     interest in the subject matter.
     Similarly, the Science programme received outstanding appraisals, with over 90% of participants rating it as
     “excellent” or “good” for its ability to inspire and promote scientific literacy beyond the standard curriculum.
     Six S2 students from the program will be nominated to represent the school at the International Junior Science
     Olympiad (IJSO).
    Extra support for S6 students preparing for the DSE examination
     Feedback from focus group meetings with our S6 graduates confirmed the value of the comprehensive support
     programs. The vast majority of students agreed these initiatives were instrumental in helping them manage the
     pressure of the HKDSE and achieve their academic goals. Students specifically highlighted the exceptional
     effectiveness of the inter-school oral practice sessions, which significantly enhanced their examination skills
     and confidence. Participants in the academic mentorship program also found it highly beneficial for their DSE
     preparation and subsequent choice of study pathways.
     Our students attained a commendable level of excellence in the 2025 HKDSE examination. Overall
     performance marked a significant institutional milestone, with 31.7% of entries achieving Level 5 or above—
     our best result since the inception of the HKDSE in 2012. Furthermore, 71.3% of entries attained Level 4 or
     above, representing our second-highest achievement on record.
    Enriched learning experience
     Beyond public examinations, student excellence was demonstrated through distinguished accomplishments in
     prestigious external competitions. Notably, twelve students participated in the 2024 International Chemistry
     Quiz, earning two High Distinctions, five Distinctions, and five Credits. A further eighteen students excelled in
     the International Children and Youth Biology Competition, securing six Special Gold Awards, eight Gold
     Awards, and four Silver Awards. Student innovation was also showcased through successful entries in the 56th
     and 57th Joint School Science Exhibitions and the Hong Kong Science Fair 2023. For details, please refer to
     P.27 to P.52 of this report.

Reflection:
    Lift-off classes to cater for learner diversity
     The outcomes strongly support the program’s core objective of stretching student potential. Strong S1 and S2
     outcomes affirm the effectiveness of the program’s foundational support, while less significant improvement in
     English language, particularly at the S3 level, highlights the need to adapt pedagogical approaches and content
     to accelerate progress in English language acquisition.
    Junior form elite program
     The resounding success of the Elite Program across all core subjects demonstrates a highly effective model for
     engaging and challenging our most able students. The positive student feedback indicates a significant boost in
     both academic confidence and metacognitive skills, which are critical for lifelong learning. The remarkable
     external competition achievements in Mathematics and Science validate the program’s rigour and its capacity to
     prepare students for the highest levels of academic competition. The high rates of student engagement and
     appreciation in Chinese Language confirm that the curriculum successfully marries depth of knowledge with
     fostering genuine intellectual curiosity.
    Extra support was provided to S6 students in preparing for DSE examination
     The outstanding HKDSE results affirm the effectiveness of our holistic strategy in nurturing student potential
     and preparing them for high-stakes assessment. The positive feedback from our recent graduates provides
     qualitative evidence that our support systems—including intensive oral practice and personalised mentorship—
     are successfully meeting student needs. The expressed desire from students to start supplementary classes and
     join the mentorship program is a strong indicator of its perceived value and underscores the importance of
     timely academic and pastoral intervention.

Feedback and Follow-up:
     -   To ensure lasting impact, we will incorporate these initiatives into our standard operating procedures.
     -   This includes expanding support systems to better accommodate increasing learner diversity, while
         simultaneously implementing strategies to enhance study motivation among students.
     -   The school will also investigate strategies to enhance student learning by consolidating study habits and
         skills, as well as optimizing the use of artificial intelligence tools.


Intended Outcome (2): Develop a collaborative culture in learning
Achievements:
     A strong and multifaceted collaborative culture has been successfully nurtured among both students and
     teachers.
     For students, this is strongly validated by the latest Stakeholder Survey (SHS), where over 70% of students
     agreed that “My schoolmates and I help each other and get along well.” Collaborative learning strategies were
     deeply embedded across the curriculum through group discussions, projects, and peer-learning initiatives. Key
     achievements include the highly successful S2 PSHE project day, where students designed a historical tour of
     Tai Po in groups, integrating knowledge from Chinese History, Geography, History, and Citizenship,
     Economics and Society. The students’ final outputs were a testament to their highly developed generic skills,
     with their capabilities in collaboration and communication being particularly noteworthy. Another example is
     the S3 Science investigation projects, where students worked in group to design and implement their
     experiments. Senior form students established independent study groups, and peer mentoring was evident as S3
     students shared study tips with S2.
     Among teachers, collaboration was robust and sustained, a fact corroborated by the SHS, in which over 80% of
     teachers agreed that “The staff are on harmonious terms” and “cooperate with one another at work happily.”
     This strong relational foundation has enabled meaningful professional exchanges among them. Under effective
     leadership, this collaborative spirit was demonstrated through school-wide initiatives such as the Chinese
     Culture Festival, which involved the participation of multiple departments, committees, teams, and clubs—
     including the Chinese Department, Physical Education Department, Moral and Civic National Education
                                                                                                                 8
     Committee, Student Union, STEM Team, Chess Club, and Astronomy Club—as well as the co-production of
     the MST Anthology by the English and Chinese Departments.
     Annually, two major and two minor Teacher Common Learning Time sessions were arranged for professional
     sharing, including reviewing assessment data and its implications for learning and teaching, collaborative lesson
     planning, and exchanging pedagogical best practices. More than half of all teachers engaged in peer lesson
     observations, while the Staff Development Committee arranged for the filming of exemplary teaching segments
     to further support professional growth and collaborative learning.

Reflection:
     The professional culture among teachers extends beyond formal structures like TCLT to include a vibrant
     ecosystem of informal, daily sharing of teaching methods and insights into common student errors.
     Collaboration among departments in different KLAs varies; we will create more opportunities to facilitate
     professional exchanges among departments in different KLAs.

Feedback and Follow-up:
     These successful initiatives will be incorporated into our routine practice. To further deepen this culture, we
     will enhance project-based learning for junior form students by providing more structured guidance and
     scaffolding to solidify their core competencies. Within the classroom, we will adopt targeted strategies to
     encourage even more active student participation in discussions and collaborative tasks.



Intended Outcome (3): Foster students’ interest and enrich their knowledge in STEM education
Achievements:
     During the 2024-25 academic year, our school successfully implemented a comprehensive and multi-tiered
     STEAM programme, significantly enhancing student engagement and proficiency. At the Tier 1 level, all
     students received universal exposure through subject-specific initiatives. In Computer Literacy, S1 students
     engaged in 3D modelling, printing, and AI projects with Micro:bit, while S2 focused on Python programming
     and AI. S3 students explored computer networks, databases, and advanced AI image recognition applications.
     The Mathematics department conducted inquiry-based activities across all forms; S1 students estimated court
     areas, S2 applied trigonometry to measure school hall heights with iPads, and S3 used GeoGebra to investigate
     triangle properties. S4 students deduced the period of a pendulum using logarithms. Cross-curricular projects
     were a highlight, such as the S1 collaboration between Computer Literacy and Science where students designed
     AI-powered "Teachable Machine" projects to solve science-related problems.
     Tier 2 and 3 programmes provided deep enrichment and competition opportunities. The STEM Team
     participated in the “GenRobo AI Application Programme” and the “Inno expo@Tai Po 2025”. The STEM
     Society hosted workshops on laser cutting, drone swarms, robotics, and forensic science, and organised a
     Zhuhai STEM Technology and Culture Tour. The Science Society enhanced knowledge through activities like
     the “Luminol glow” chemistry experiment. Our students excelled externally, with finalist placements in the
     “Low Carbon Energy Invention Competition” and a “Best Presentation Award” at the Global Marty Challenge
     (Python). They also achieved Merit Awards at the Greater Bay Area STEM Excellence Award and the 9th
     “Elite Cup” STEAM Challenge. Furthermore, projects were selected for exhibition at the 58th Joint School
     Science Exhibition and the Hong Kong Science Fair 2025.

Reflection:
     The breadth and depth of the STEAM programme have been effective in sparking student curiosity and building
     substantial knowledge. The hands-on, inquiry-based approach in core subjects successfully made abstract
     concepts tangible, fostering a genuine interest in STEAM fields. The strong performance in local and
     international competitions is a clear indicator of the enhanced knowledge and applied skills our students have



                                                                                                                  9
     developed. The high levels of participation and achievement across all tiers confirm that our strategy is
     effectively catering to a wide spectrum of interests and abilities.

Feedback and Follow-up:
     STEAM education will remain a major strategic focus in our next development cycle. We will strengthen cross-
     departmental collaboration, systematically coordinate all related teaching activities, and further embed STEAM
     principles to enhance students’ interdisciplinary understanding and real-world application skills.
     To create a more immersive learning environment, we will expand school-based initiatives such as the
     introduction of an S1 STEAM Activities Day—beginning in 2025/26 with a micro:bit Legged Robot Fun
     Day—and an enhanced STEAM Week. Strategic partnerships with external institutions will provide specialized
     training and field visits, offering students meaningful opportunities to apply STEAM knowledge in
     assignments, projects, and competitions. To reflect the integral role of aesthetic design in innovation, the STEM
     team and club will be formally renamed as the STEAM team and STEAM club. The new STEAM Creative
     Studio, funded by the QEF, will open in the coming academic year and enable students to explore technology
     integration in the performing arts through hands-on experiences.
     Outstanding student achievements and school experiences will be showcased to celebrate success and inspire
     broader participation. These efforts collectively aim to nurture innovation, critical thinking, and sustained
     passion for STEAM among all students.




Major Concern 2:      To cultivate students’ positive values and capability conductive to their whole
                      person development
Intended Outcome (1): Students embrace Christian values

Achievements:
     In 2024-25 school year, we focused on nurturing the Christian value of Empathy (同理心) through a variety of
     school-wide initiatives. Morning programmes and assemblies featured dedicated sharing sessions, while
     Religious Education lessons intentionally integrated this value into their curriculum. Additional activities were
     organised by the Christian Fellowship and The Boys’ Brigade, further reinforcing the theme. Reading materials
     related to empathy were prepared for use during morning sessions and RE lessons, and teachers shared hymns
     and Bible verses during assemblies to deepen students’ reflection.
     Based on minutes from form meetings and annual reports from committees and departments, teachers widely
     reported that students were highly engaged in these activities and demonstrated a better understanding of the
     value of empathy. There were reports of students actively demonstrating empathy in their daily interactions—
     showing compassion, offering support to peers, and engaging thoughtfully with the experiences of others.
Reflection:
     Feedback from staff confirms that students responded positively to the structured emphasis on Christian values,
     particularly empathy. Their active participation and increased awareness reflect the effectiveness of a
     coordinated, cross-departmental approach in value education.
Feedback and Follow-up:
     Fostering students' Christian values remains a core mission of our school. In the next development cycle, we
     will continue to prioritise values education, with an enhanced focus on the Christian virtues of Patience (忍耐),
     Fraternal Affection (友愛), and Moral Virtue (德行). These values will be thoughtfully integrated into morning
     assemblies, RE lessons, co-curricular activities, and pastoral programmes to further support the whole-person
     development of every student.

Intended Outcome (2): Enhance students’ sense of national identity, nurturing students as good law-
abiding citizens

Achievements:
     Our school has made significant strides in nurturing a strong sense of national identity among students, as
     clearly reflected in the recent APASO III survey results. Students attained outstanding performance across key
     domains, with a remarkable score of 113 in National Identity (Responsibility, Obligation) and an impressive
     collective Q-value of 116 across the sub-domains of National Identity (Pride, Care), National Identity (National
     Flag, National Anthem), and National Identity (Achievement). These results affirm our students’ deeply
     ingrained understanding and appreciation of their civic identity.
     This success is underpinned by a structured and immersive programme of activities. The weekly National Flag
     Raising Ceremony, attended by all teachers and students and often accompanied by a dedicated speech, has
     been instrumental in reinforcing national belonging. Our annual Chinese Cultural Week further enriched
     students’ understanding of nationhood and patriotism. During the 2024-25 school year, six mainland study tours
     provided students with profound, firsthand insights into the nation’s development, greatly enhancing their
     connection to and pride in their country.
Reflection:
     The exceptional APASO III scores demonstrate the effectiveness of our holistic approach to values education.
     The combination of daily practices, cultural immersion, and direct experiential learning through mainland
     exchanges has successfully fostered a meaningful and personal sense of national identity among our student
     body. This model of integrating formal and informal learning experiences proves highly effective in cultivating
     informed, responsible, and proud citizens.
Feedback and Follow-up:
     We will continue to systematically enhance students’ sense of national identity and their development as law-
     abiding citizens. This objective will be deeply embedded into the routine work of all subjects and co-curricular
     activities. The foundational practices of national flag-raising ceremonies and thematic cultural events will be
     maintained and enriched. We are committed to providing further authentic learning experiences, including study
     tours and cross-cultural exchanges, to ensure our students continue to develop a profound, respectful, and
     informed connection to our country and its values.



Intended Outcome (3): Students are to be perseverant

Achievements:
     Despite students self-reporting lower confidence in learning through APASO surveys, the school has
     implemented a multi-faceted approach to foster perseverance and well-being. Talks and activities focused on
     enhancing physical and mental resilience were organized for students, alongside talks for parents addressing
     youth behavior and potential problems. Form-teacher periods dedicated to self-appreciation were conducted for
     both junior and senior forms, aiming to promote self-confidence and an appreciation of beauty and excellence.
     While survey results indicate some growth in perseverance, it remains an area requiring continued focus.
Reflection:
     The data suggests that while initiatives are in place and recognized, cultivating deep-rooted perseverance
     remains a complex challenge. The gap between activity implementation and student self-perception highlights
     the need for more impactful, evidence-based strategies that connect effort to tangible outcomes and build
     genuine self-efficacy.
Feedback and Follow-up:
     Student well-being and perseverance will remain a strategic priority in our next development cycle. We will
     institutionalize the whole-school 4Rs Mental Health Charter (Rest, Relaxation, Relationship, Resilience) across

     informal curricula and class ethos building. We will promote healthy lifestyles and evidence-based time
     management strategies to strengthen self-regulation. To make perseverance visible and rewarding, we will
     establish dedicated platforms to showcase students’ physical and aesthetic accomplishments to the wider school
     community.


Intended Outcome (4): Students become more aware of the unhealthy elements in our society and
learn to resist temptation

Achievements:
     The school has proactively addressed modern challenges, notably cyber addiction, through targeted talks and
     activities during form-teacher periods that raise awareness of the negative impacts of video games, social
     media, and electronic devices. Complementary workshops were organized for parents to strengthen their skills
     in identifying and managing potential problems. Surveys indicate that most participating students, teachers,
     and parents agree that these initiatives successfully achieved their awareness-raising objectives.
Reflection:
     The positive feedback confirms the value of these educational efforts. However, the pervasive nature of
     technology and evolving societal pressures necessitates that this education be continuous and adaptive.
     Awareness is the first step; empowering students with practical resistance strategies is the ongoing work.
Feedback and Follow-up:
     This initiative will be incorporated into our routine work. Building on the raised awareness, we will
     continuously educate students about evolving unhealthy influences and equip them with practical strategies to
     resist temptation. This effort will be synergized with our broader well-being program, which promotes healthy
     lifestyles, balanced technology use, and strong mental health support systems.

activities are organized to assist positive development in the following aspects:

        The Religious and Evangelical Affairs Committee aims to help students to develop a positive
view towards life, enhance their understanding of Christianity and encourage them to embody Christian
faith in daily life.

     Christian teachers, with generous assistance given by the Revd. Tony Au Yeung Siu Kei and Mr.
     Oliver Chin Kan Yuen of HKSKH The Church of Our Saviour, to broaden students' horizons
    The Revd. Au Yeung was invited to share messages at both our Christmas Service and Easter

Moral, Civic & National Education
    The Moral, Civic & National Education Committee aims at promoting values education, civic
education and national education.
   Values Education is an essential element of whole-person education which aims at fostering students’
    positive values and attitudes through the learning and teaching of various Key Learning Areas or
    subjects and the provision of relevant learning experiences. The Committee upholds the belief that
    “Everyone should have empathy”. Cultivating care and empathy involves understanding and meeting
    the needs of others with concern and love. By promoting care and empathy in students, they can
    develop a friendly and caring attitude towards family, teachers, and friends. This can extend to caring
    for the welfare of vulnerable communities, even to loving and protecting all living things, and
    practicing “treating others with kindness, and loving all creatures”. This can help prevent and
    eliminate bullying and animal cruelty incidents, and build a caring campus and harmonious society.
