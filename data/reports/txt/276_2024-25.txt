 MOTTO MISSION GOALS AND ETHOS
MOTTO
寅畏上主是為智之本
(The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom)

MISSION
The offering to Chinese youths of a modern, liberal education in the English language (but including the
subject of Chinese language in the curriculum) upon Christian principles, Protestant and Evangelical, as
professed by the Sheng Kung Hui.

GOALS
The educational goals of the College, in accordance with its mission, may be described as:
• To cultivate a healthy attitude to life and the world and to expose students to the Christian message.
• To inculcate civic awareness in students and to develop them into responsible and useful citizens of
    community with respect for intellectual property, human rights, freedom and justice.
• To enable students to develop their intellectual potential fully, to think logically and creatively, to
    study and solve problems independently, and to communicate effectively in English and Chinese.
• To develop students’ skills and abilities in Information Technology and to arouse the interest in life-
    long learning.
• To develop students’ physical and musical skills and abilities and to encourage enjoyment in physical
    development and aesthetic appreciation.
• To encourage the appreciation of the arts and development of artistic talents and skills.
• To promote respect for the views and opinions of others, harmonious relationships in school, the
    family and the community, and participation in community affairs.
• To develop in students the ability to cope with adverse situations and emotional problems.

ETHOS
Our ethos is summed up in the College song: “Brothers here we all stand together, all for each and each
for all”. The College strives to provide an active, caring, happy and healthy environment for learning
based on mutual respect and trust.

5.      CONTINUING PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Throughout the past year, educators embarked on a transformative journey of professional growth,
significantly enriched by specialised online programmes offered by the International Boys' Schools
Coalition (IBSC) for new teachers and middle managers. Concurrently, the Education Bureau and the
Education University of Hong Kong have meticulously designed programmes to cater to the unique
learning needs of students with diverse educational backgrounds.

This comprehensive approach was further enhanced by immersive school visits within Mainland China
and overseas, fostering a global perspective and enriching pedagogical practices. The subsequent
paragraphs will explore the notable highlights and impactful outcomes of these diverse professional
development initiatives.

5.1     Staff Development Days

5.1.1   Exciting Collaboration on AI in Education with SSGC this Academic Year!


On the morning of the Mid-Autumn Festival 17 September 2024, St. Stephen's Girls' College and our
College, in partnership with the Apple Education Team, kicked off Part 3 of our Joint-School Staff
Professional Development journey focused on "Theory to Practice" in AI education.
A special shout-out to the dedicated Apple Team and our enthusiastic student ambassadors for their hard
work and commitment. Together, we are bridging theory with practice by integrating Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs) into our educational activities.
In the following months, we continued our collaboration by developing learning activities that utilise
iPads and apps to enhance student engagement. Teams from both schools worked together to create
tangible educational experiences for targeted student groups on specific subjects, aligned with the SDGs
for a sustainable future.
In May 2025, Part 4 of our Joint-School Staff Professional Development journey focused on "Theory to
Practice" in AI education was a celebration of collaborative efforts and innovative practices. The session
began with a check-in exercise before sharing experiences from various departments. Highlights included
the two Geography Departments’ use of co-created resources, SPC Integrated Science Department's AI-
driven student interactions with SSGC’s students, SSGC Junior Science Department's GenAI for self-
directed learning, and SPC English Department’s MagicSchool AI for English writing marking trial in Form
Three. The event concluded with discussions, feedback, and closing remarks.
The key outcomes in this academic year included enhanced collaboration between the schools, improved
teaching practices through AI integration, and the fostering of self-directed learning among students. The
sharing of experiences and outcomes by various departments was expected to inspire new approaches
and methodologies, while the discussions and feedback sessions provided valuable insights for future
initiatives. Overall, this professional development journey aimed to create a robust framework for AI
education that benefits both teachers and students.

5.1.2 Empowering Sustainability: Educators Leading the Path in Collaboration with WWF Hong
Kong

The staff wellness development day on 18 October 2024 served as a catalyst for change, equipping our
teachers with the knowledge and tools needed to champion sustainability in education.

In a landmark collaboration with WWF Hong Kong, our College commenced the day with a workshop
focused on sustainable lifestyles. This workshop aimed to raise educators' awareness of pressing
environmental issues, such as marine conservation, climate change, and sustainable development. During
the discussion, teachers shared valuable insights on sustainable practices for our College, such as "think
before we print," implementing a food waste processor, incentivising recycling, and promoting the use of
personal utensils to reduce waste. These suggestions highlight our educators' commitment to creating a
more environmentally conscious and sustainable campus.

Moreover, teachers selected breakout activities of their interest to further explore the
interconnectedness of personal well-being, societal values, national security, and global sustainability.
These activities included a self-aromatherapy workshop, a coffee and 3D latte art workshop, a blue and
white porcelain disk drawing workshop, a preserved flower wreath workshop, sports tournaments in
basketball and badminton, and hiking.

We have now established a foundation for incorporating sustainability practices into our daily lives and
education, aligning with Sustainable Development Goals, National Security Education, and Values
Education. This effort not only enhanced our well-being but also served as a model for our students and
the wider community.

5.2.8   Participation in IBSC Annual Conference 2025

Our College participated in the IBSC Annual Conference 2025 at Belmont Hill School in Boston. One of our
English teachers and a member of the Education Research Team, Mr. Allan Jolly, attended workshops
focused on reading in the digital age, classroom engagement, AI in learning, and character formation.

A major theme was facing the challenges of heavy screen time for boys, including reduced attention,
problem-solving, emotional regulation, vocabulary growth, and social skills. Research shows digital
reading can lower comprehension and encourage superficial processing.

In order to tackle this issue, cultivating a biliterate brain was highlighted, which is about balancing print
and digital reading with explicit deep-reading routines. Strategies included short prediction writes after
passages, vocabulary-substitution rewrites, and multimodal learning (text, video, audio, gra phics,

interactive tasks). The use of visual organisers such as story maps, timelines, and annotated diagrams
were suggested to deepen comprehension.

Workshops also addressed the power of shared reading. Practical ideas included curated reading lists,
home discussion questions, school book events, and father-son reading challenges to model good habits,
strengthen bonds, and improve literacy.

To improve classroom discussion, the R.E.A.L. framework (Relate, Excerpt, Ask, Listen) could be used.
The framework connects lesson content with students’ prior experiences and current contexts, grounds
claims in evidence, advances inquiry through purposeful questions, and encourages understanding
through attentive listening.

Overall, the 2025 IBSC Conference offered practical, research-informed approaches that our College can
adapt to strengthen reading, leverage technology, and structure richer discussions in service of our boys’
learning.

1.3     INTERNATIONAL ADVANCED LEVELS (IAL)

Commencing in the year 2017-2018, St. Paul’s College has diversified its senior curriculum and subject
choices for senior students. The IAL gives our students an opportunity to pursue an alternative
curriculum based on their skills and proficiencies. While the overwhelming majority of SPC students will
continue in the DSE curriculum in their senior years, the IAL enables our students to follow the
International A Level and GCE exams. This provides a viable alternative to the DSE curriculum, which
may allow a more suitable pathway for our budding scientists.

The IAL is a recognised qualification by all universities in the United Kingdom and the European Union,
and widely accepted in USA, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, as well as many Asian countries. All
Hong Kong UGC universities recognise the IAL qualification. Hong Kong students can be assessed as a
non-JUPAS candidate. Each university has a separate mechanism for evaluating students, just as they do
for DSE candidates. In addition, many Hong Kong universities have an early-admission scheme based on
prior exam results or predicted grades, as is the case in the United Kingdom.

3.    PROVISION OF CO-CURRICULAR ACTIVITIES

3.1 Assembly and Worship Services

Assemblies and worship services continue to play a very important part of the spiritual growth and
development of the students here at the College. Daily assemblies are held every week except Tuesdays.
A flag raising ceremony is held either before the first or second assembly on Mondays. Assemblies are
vital information sharing sessions that are crucial to the life of the school well received by all in
attendance. The past year’s schedule was comprehensively balanced with talks and presentations from
the Principal, Chaplain, Teachers, student groups and visiting speakers. As is our custom, the College was
pleased to welcome a number of notable alumni including Dr. Anissa Chan Wong Lai-Kuen and Professor
Ip Pak Keung Patrick as part of our distinguished Prefect alumnus series in November 2024.

The assembly schedule included many inspiring presentations by students covering a wide variety of
projects that they were involved in within the fabric of the school. This year, a number of our music
groups, under the direction of Mr. Stephen Lam – our Music Director, performed a wide repertoire of
music for their musically themed assemblies. There were also presentation sessions where students were
rewarded for their hard work and congratulated for their success in athletics, academics and extra-
curricular involvement. Bi-weekly eucharist services continue in the College Chapel on Thursdays at
lunch time. These services were attended by a small group of staff members and students alike. A group
of dedicated Altar Servers assist with the worship event offering liturgical support.

The Form One Heritage Service was held at St. Paul’s Church at the beginning of the school year as is our
custom. Remarks were given by our Principal which were well received by our new students entering the
College. The Christmas service was held at St. Stephen’s Church this year and we were pleased to have
the Revd Francis Yu, Curate of St. Stephen’s as the preacher. The traditional Easter service was held in
our school hall and we welcomed the Revd Professor Philip Wickeri, Archivist of the Province, to be our
preacher for the service. He delivered a thoughtful sermon in Putonghua explaining to the boys the
essence of the Easter story.

Worship, prayer, fellowship and sacred music are integral parts of the Christian life and its many different
platforms are well represented here at the College.

3.2   Pastoral Care Team

The aims of the Pastoral Care Team are to foster Christian values among students to help them navigate
life with a strong moral compass, to support students and teaching staff in their spiritual journeys, and
to maintain connections between HKSKH and the College through the coordination of students’ and
teachers’ activities.

The Pastoral Care Team coordinated and organised all religious activities at SPC. For Christian leaders to
plan Christian activities, there are four divisions supervised by Teachers in Pastoral Care Team: Senior
Christian Union, Junior Christian Union, Worship Team and Altar Server Team.

3.2.1 Activities and Functions

During the 2024–2025 academic year, the team’s work is guided by the mission to foster Christian values,
support spiritual journeys, and maintain strong ties with HKSKH and its affiliated churches.

Junior and Senior Christian Union fellowship

Both the Junior and Senior Christian Unions met bi-weekly throughout the year, from October 2024 to
June 2025, engaging students in fellowship, prayer, and thematic studies such as “Faith in God” and “The
Fruit of the Spirit.” Attendance remained high, with Junior Christian Union meetings averaging 65
students and Senior meetings averaging 37. Committee training and cell groups were held bi-weekly
throughout the year to strengthen Christian leaders.

Services

Regular eucharist services were conducted twice monthly in the chapel between October 2024 and April
2025, with the Altar Server Team providing essential support. New students participated in the F.1
Heritage Service in September 2024, where Christian Union Committee members took on key roles. The
Carol Service was held at St. Stephen’s Church in December 2024, while the Easter Service was held in
April 2025, led by guest preacher Reverend Professor Philip Wickeri, encouraging reflection on the
spiritual significance of Easter. Lastly, the F.6 Graduation Thanksgiving Service took place in June 2025
to round up and give thanks to God for leading the spiritual journey of the students.

Gospel Week

Gospel Week, a highlight in December 2024, included hymn busking, art workshops, song dedications,
and the featured chalk walk at the upper playground, culminating in festive meals and gift exchanges. In
the middle of the week, the Glorious Feast co-organised with the PTA was held in School Hall with more
than 40 parents participating to serve our students. For the most encouraging event, more than 200
students joined the faith sharing sessions. Special thanks go to our Principal, Mr. Dennis Yuen and
teachers who had supported the event.

Connection with Church

Education Sunday was held at St. Stephen’s Church in September 2024, drawing teachers and students
together for worship. The St. Stephen’s Church Fun Day in November 2024 featured interactive games
led by committee members. Broader community engagement was evident in the team’s participation at
the HKSKH Provincial Education Day Evensong at Holy Trinity Cathedral in October 2024, where a
Christian Union member served as flag bearer.

Connection with The Primary School

Outreach initiatives extended to the primary school, where worship services and interactive games were

Other featured events

In summary, the Pastoral Care Team’s Christian activities in 2024–2025 were marked by vibrant
participation, spiritual growth, community outreach, and enduring fellowship, with all major events and

Centre and a private tour of a Mercedes-Benz operational plant not normally open to the public. A

and management tips with our future leaders.

Following the EDB disbursement of the “One-off Grant for Mental Health of Parents and Students” of
$20,000, our PTA organised four art and craft workshops focused on the mental health of students and
parents from March to June 2025. These included: (i) Fluid Acrylics Workshop and Zentangle Workshop
on 8 March 2025, (ii) Japanese Alcohol Ink Art Workshop on 15 March 2025, and (iii) Handmade
Horticultural Bonsai Workshop on 21 June 2025. Each workshop had 25 parent and student participants
who learnt how to relieve anxiety and stress through art creation and mindfulness exercises.
Special edition red packets for this year’s Lunar New Year, the Year of the Snake, were designed and the
PTA received pre-orders in December 2024, as well as organising an open-stall sale on the Forms One to
Five Parents’ Day on 8 February 2025. The red packets were available in two styles and snapped up
quickly by parents. Also, a special edition leather card holder with a neck strap was produced, and all
students received one for free in June-July 2025.
The PTA once again partnered with our counterparts from King’s College to organise a Joint School Talk
on 5 July 2025 and a bowling activity on 12 July 2025. The Talk, held at our school, was attended by 160
parents and students from both schools. Mr. Dick Ho（何偉文先生）, General Manager of Bank of China
Credit Card (International) Co., Ltd., who has two sons studying at King’s College and our school, and is
currently a committee member of the King’s College PTA, gave a talk on financial planning strategies
and how to avoid financial scams. The other speaker, Mr. Rocky Lok （駱英棋先生）, Consultant and
former Chairman of the Hong Kong Harmonica Association and an alumnus of King’s College, led the
audience in a workshop on playing tunes with a 10-hole diatonic harmonica. The audience was given the
10-hole diatonic harmonica as a souvenir. The bowling activity between the two schools was hugely
popular, but was limited to 35 parents and students from each school. The bowling games provided
relaxation and opportunities for socialising.

As part of the school community, the PTA was actively involved in numerous school events and
celebrations. Between 15 and 21 November 2024, the PTA and the school’s Student Sustainability Society
jointly organised a campaign to collect used school uniforms. On 27 November 2025, PTA parents
volunteered to set up side-show stalls for food and games during the “Glorious Feast”, which was part of
the School Gospel Week. On 29 November 2025, many PTA parent committee members attended the
College Speech Day ceremony to celebrate student graduation and excellent performances. During the
annual Sports Day on 9 December 2024, the PTA organised teams to participate in relay races. The parent
teams won a silver medal in the 4 x 100 m Relay Race against the teacher teams, while thirty-three parent-
child pairs joined the Parent-Child 2 x 100 m Relay Race.

This year, the PTA collaborated with the SPC Alumni Association on two events. The first took place on
10 November 2024 and featured a movie appreciation of “An Abandoned Team”, starring Mr. Cheng Tan
Shui, a.k.a. “Dan Ge”, a celebrity and SPC alumnus. The second event was a gala dinner party celebrating
the retirement of our Principal, Mr. Dennis Yuen, on 21 June 2025, at a Chinese restaurant in Tsim Sha
Tsui. The dinner had a huge turnout of almost 360 guests, including school council members, alumni, past
teachers, parents and current students, all present to appreciate Principal Yuen’s 32 years of contribution
to our beloved College.

This year, the PTA photography team was formed, with seven parent photographers volunteering to
capture memorable moments of PTA activities. A video exhibition showcasing these moments was held
in the Gallant Ho Hall between 7 and 16 July 2025. The PTA issued and distributed its 33rd volume of the

achievements the PTA accomplished this year. Among these achievements, three dedicated parent

with the Outstanding Parent Volunteers Award, organised by the Federation of Parent Teacher
Associations Hong Kong Island on 1 July 2025. This award celebrated their significant contributions to
fostering strong home-school cooperation.

Major Concern 1

  To develop a future-focused curriculum that integrates educational technology,
  especially the use of generative artificial intelligence, into learning and teaching practices
  to prepare students for the changing education landscape in the second quarter of the
  21st century

Achievements

  To promote a more vibrant reading culture for students, teachers and parents
  - Promoted cross-curricular reading by sharing curated booklists with parents and
     students to encourage self-directed learning at every level.
  - Dedicated a monthly Form Teacher Period to “Time for Page Turner”, promoting and
     valuing consistent and enjoyable reading habits among students.
  - Engaged teachers in reading advocacy by inviting them to share their favourite books,
     strengthening leadership in reading and inspiring students through personal
     recommendations.
  - Designated April as Reading Month, creating an immersive reading culture through
     AR and VR experiences, book exhibitions, sales, talks and a campaign aligned with
     International Reading Day.
  - Strengthened the reading programmes in Chinese and English Departments through
     centralised subject-based reading award schemes

  To boost curiosity in learning
  - Restructured a Masterclass series for students to learn from the best and to inspire
     learning, namely Scholar-in-Residence, Artist in-Residence (Music), Artist-in-School
     Programme (Art).
  - Conducted more interactive lecture series on different topics of interest to students
     in both Junior and Senior Forms
  - Provided students with job shadowing opportunities through alumni networks,
     allowing students to gain insights from successful Paulines and broaden their career
     perspectives.
  - Promoted the in-house Academic Integrity Policy which provides students and
     teachers with clear guidelines for the safe and responsible use of AI in learning and
     teaching

  To enable more autonomy in learning and accelerated a paradigm shift in teaching
  through a greater use of education technology such as generative AI




                                                                                          Page 104
  -   Formed a teacher research team to encourage teachers to do action research to refine
      their teaching practices. Two research papers were developed and shared on
      different occasions for professional development.
  -   Encouraged teachers to communicate their research findings through open-access
      forums such as online platforms and international conferences. Research was
      presented at the IBSC conference and at EDB network
  -   Organised joint-school professional development programmes and professional
      learning communities for teachers to understand how AI works, including its
      applications, limitations, and ethical considerations
  -   Supported teachers taking part in conferences to allow them to interact with teachers
      in other boy schools both locally (e.g., Learning and Teaching Expo) and overseas (e.g.,
      IBSC Annual Conference)


Reflection

  ● Promote a rich reading culture
    It remains a challenge that boys often choose activities other than reading, although
    this issue is not unique to our school. The low library borrowing rate and the
    relatively subdued reading atmosphere are further reflected in the SHS results.
    As there are different reading online platforms, we should seek out a structured one
    allowing students and teachers to utilise these resources further.
    Although an increasing percentage of students report that they often read materials
    and newspapers outside class, this remains a concern. The survey results suggest that
    the College may need to foster a more positive attitude towards reading among
    students.
  ● Arouse curiosity in learning
    Some still believed that the Academic Integrity Policy could not effectively guide
    students and teachers in responsible use of AI. Perhaps there are still areas where
    further training or clearer examples are needed.
  ● Promote autonomy in learning and a paradigm shift in teaching through a greater use
    of education technology such as generative AI
    As we move further into the AI era, it is essential for teachers to equip themselves
    with effective strategies and skills to harness the power of AI in their teaching.
    Recognising this need, the College will place greater emphasis on professional
    development to ensure teachers can confidently integrate AI tools into their practice.
    Teachers should not only acquire new knowledge but also translate it into meaningful
    classroom applications. If we do so, students will benefit from enriched learning
    experiences that better prepare them for a rapidly evolving world.
    The College remains committed to strengthening students’ awareness of AI ethics. In
    view of the growing impact of artificial intelligence on society, we will further deepen
    and broaden the school-based curriculum to include meaningful discussions on real-
    world ethical dilemmas related to AI. These enhancements will aim to equip students




                                                                                        Page 105
    with the critical thinking skills needed to navigate complex issues such as fairness,
    privacy, and accountability in the digital age.

    In response to the rapid development of artificial intelligence and the implementation
    of the school’s Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) policy, the College will organise more
    activities focused on information literacy and the responsible use of social media.
    These initiatives are designed to help students critically evaluate online content,
    maintain digital safety, and engage ethically in virtual spaces.


Feedback and Follow-up


 While the 2024–2025 school year saw many achievements, we have identified key areas
 for further improvement in the coming year:
 ● Enrich home-school relationships around reading by holding Book Fairs and the use
     of online platforms, esp the latter as these provide an excellent opportunity for
     parents to read with their sons/wards.
 ● To make Reading Month in April a truly whole-school initiative, we aim to encourage
     greater participation from departments, teams, and groups beyond the language
     subjects. This collaborative approach will help ensure that reading is embraced as a
     shared responsibility across all disciplines.
 ● Introduce an AI literacy solution platform of enhanced digital books and news articles
     updated every weekday to motivate, monitor, and manage students’ independent
     reading practice
 ● Cultivate students as active agents rather than passive recipients of information
     through the SPC Curriculum: Project ACE for junior secondary students and the
     Common Core (Extended Studies) for the senior secondary students
 ● Provide teachers with more access to regional and international workshops,
     seminars, conferences, and training programmes so that they can stay up to date with
     the latest pedagogical approaches and education practices
 ● Build capacity by working with local universities to organise various professional
     development programmes to support staff in integrating AI into teaching, learning
     and school management/operations
 ● Review the Information Literacy curriculum in vertical alignment based on the
     “Information Literacy for Hong Kong Students” Learning Framework 2024
 ● Make effective use of data to inform educational practices for more personalised
     learning
 ● Invest in modern teaching methodologies and advanced technology infrastructure by
     updating learning resources, laboratories, library, music, and sports facilities to
     create a conducive 21st century learning environment that fosters innovation, critical
     thinking, and collaboration



                                                                                     Page 106
In summary, the identified areas for growth represent strategic priorities that will guide
our development in the upcoming year. By strengthening collaboration between home
and school, expanding reading initiatives across all disciplines, integrating cutting-edge
digital and AI literacy tools, and continuously supporting professional development, we
aim to cultivate a vibrant and forward-looking learning community.

Through thoughtful curriculum review, data-informed teaching practices, and
investment in modern facilities, we are committed to providing an education that equips
our students with the skills and mindset required for success in the 21st century. We
appreciate the collective effort and ongoing dedication of all staff, students, and parents
as we strive toward these important goals.

Major Concern 2

To strengthen pastoral care for the development of positive character traits in students and for
fostering an inclusive school culture

Achievements

  To promote teachers’ and students’ well-being

  Fostered a positive school / classroom culture
  ● Included student leaders and representatives in discussion during the review process
     of BYOD Policy of the College
  ● Acknowledged students’ achievements and efforts consistently through Campus TV,

  ● Organized the Gratitude Campaign to promote the values and power of thankfulness
  ● Arranged in-house seminars or workshops, especially for new teachers, to enhance
     their awareness and skills in supporting students with special needs

  Strengthened peer support network (Peer mentors, SA officials, prefects, wellness
  ambassadors)
  ● Invited Dr. Carina Leung to share with our students and their peers from other
     schools in Hong Kong on the topic of humanitarian work, spreading the message of
     self-help, peer support and social network in challenging times in LOHAS Festa
  ● Organised joint-school sustainability initiatives like low-carbon eating habits, and
     collaborative treasure hunt to promote “SDG 3: good health and wellbeing”
  ● Invited alumni prefects to deliver assembly talks to share their experiences at school
     and in the workplace (Prefects)
  ● Organized Graduation Week for Form Six students to enhance school spirit (SA
     officials)
  ● Organized Form One Breakthrough Camp to help Form One students adapt to their
     new school life (Peer mentors)
  ● Provided Wellness Ambassadors and Peer Mentors with 100-hour leadership
     training, including the Mental First Aid course
  ● Paired newly-admitted students in Forms Two to Four with peers to help them adjust
     to school life (Class buddies)




                                                                                          Page 108
  Provided a supportive work environment for staff

  ● Designated a Staff Development Day on teachers’ wellness. Over 95% of colleagues
    found that the breakout sessions were enjoyable and created space for mindfulness
    and social time with colleagues
  ● Opened the Fitness Centre and Indoor Swimming Pool for staff and students to use
    during their leisure time

  Promoted a connected school community for parents

  ● Encouraged form teachers to make “sunshine calls” to parents at the beginning of the
    semester
  ● Invited parents to participate in various school functions, such as showing support
    for their sons/ wards during the Swimming Gala and contributing their stories during
    Gratitude Week.
  ● Arranged for parents in need to receive support through the school’s purchased
    counselling services


Reflection

  The progress made in promoting students’ positive character development and fostering
  an inclusive, supportive school culture is commendable. Initiatives such as engaging
  student leaders in policy discussions, consistent recognition of student efforts, and
  launching peer support programs have demonstrated promising impacts on student
  well-being and school spirit. Collaborative events which extend peer networks beyond
  the school and integrate alumni contributions have further enriched this supportive
  environment.

  Nonetheless, opportunities remain to deepen the impact of our pastoral care framework.
  Reflection has suggested a need to enhance our continuous monitoring and evaluation
  mechanisms to measure the effectiveness of well-being initiatives more systematically
  given the fact that our society is becoming increasingly complicated.

  Strengthening the links between student support services and academic departments
  could further facilitate holistic student development. The College will continue to use
  structured feedback from students, parents, and teachers on pastoral care programmes
  to target improvements.

  On staff well-being, while the introduction of wellness-focused professional development
  and recreational facilities has been well received, ongoing efforts should aim to embed a



                                                                                     Page 109
 culture of wellness more deeply into daily practices and leadership approaches.
 Encouraging open dialogue, peer mentoring among staff, and resilience-building
 workshops should augment the current support infrastructure.

 Finally, parental engagement initiatives have laid a solid foundation. Moving forward,
 expanding these channels through regular workshops, digital communication forums,
 and targeted support services could enhance family-school partnerships and contribute
 significantly to student well-being.


Feedback and Follow-up

 In summary, while meaningful strides have been taken to strengthen pastoral care and
 promote holistic student development, sustaining and advancing this mission will
 require a more systematic approach to evaluation, deeper cross-departmental
 collaboration, and stronger engagement with all stakeholders.

 Looking ahead, a key priority will be the introduction of Systems Thinking Tools within
 Education for Sustainable Development (ESD). This initiative aims to provide a shared
 language and framework that helps students better understand their own thoughts and
 emotions, while fostering empathy and perspective-taking in the classroom. By
 equipping students with the ability to recognise interconnections and anticipate
 consequences, Systems Thinking will not only enhance emotional intelligence but also
 empower learners to address complex sustainability challenges collaboratively and
 responsibly. Students will also have opportunities to apply Systems Thinking in authentic
 contexts, such as interdisciplinary projects and community engagement initiatives.

  •   Adopted a whole-school approach to arrange NSE events with cultural exhibitions,

  •   Organized the Celebrating Diversity Day and related programmes to promote and

  This year marked significant progress in fostering both national identity and global

  multiple subjects, including IH, CS, English, IS, Biology, and Visual Arts. This

Part 1: Evaluation of reading resources and programmes

E-Resources

The College continued to shift from physical to digital platforms to promote reading.

As in previous years, the College subscribed to 智爱中文平台 for the Chinese Department to help improve
students’ proficiency in reading in the Chinese language. As a school, we completed 39,539 reading
exercises in 2024-2025, a slight decrease from the previous year. In 2023-2024, the whole school
completed 41,244 exercises. The implementation would be reviewed to increase the usage in the coming
school year.

The College continued to subscribe to WiseSearch by Wisers Information Limited and Britannica School
by Encyclopæ dia Britannica (UK) Ltd.. The usage of the database was closely monitored to determine the
need to renew our subscription to this resource this year. With the rise of various AI platforms, the College
Library would not continue to subscribe to Britannica School by Encyclopæ dia Britannica (UK) Ltd. in
2025-2026 for learning and teaching.

In 2024-2025, the College subscribed to 100 e-books through HKEdCity’s eRead Scheme. The app,
EdBookShelf, was installed in students’ iPads to make access to the platform easier. The subscription was
also made known to parents through an e-notice and through their Chinese teachers, English teachers
and form teachers. In addition, the introduction of the Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) scheme in junior
forms also helped the promotion of e-books among students, especially Form One students.

Besides procuring resources that would benefit students directly, the College also subscribed to SCMP for
teachers in order to provide them with more relevant and updated resources they could use in their
teaching. A total of 65 accounts were shared among different departments. Teachers teaching English,
Economics, Integrated Humanities, and Citizenship and Social Development found these SCMP accounts
particularly useful when looking for suitable reading materials to complement their subject textbooks.

Besides subscribing to the above e-Reading platforms, a number of activities were also held to promote
reading. The 2024–2025 school year marked an exciting new chapter in fostering a love for reading
among students. As part of Reading Month, the school launched an innovative initiative designed to make
literature more accessible and engaging. Central to this effort was the introduction of the Short Story
Dispenser, a creative tool that delivers bite-sized stories at the push of a button. This dispenser offered
students a quick and enjoyable way to immerse themselves in literature, whether during breaks or
between classes.
