I.   School Vision and Mission



          Pope Paul VI College, established in 1969, is a Catholic subsidized EMI secondary

     school for girls. Its sponsoring body is the Missionary Sisters of the Immaculate

     (P.I.M.E. Sisters).



          Our school aims to develop the full potential of the students by means of a holistic

     education based on Christian values, especially universal love and service. We help our

     students to acquire knowledge and skills, learn to think critically and independently and

     live a meaningful life so that they can make contributions to society and the world as

     responsible individuals.



            Our school motto is “Love and Service”.

V.   Evaluation on School’s Major Concerns (Achievements and Reflections)

Major Concern 1: Promoting students’ motivation through positive feedback and
recognition
Achievements

1.   Enhancing Student Motivation Through Peer Learning Initiatives

     To foster a motivating and enthusiastic learning environment, particularly for junior secondary
     students, the school introduced peer-teaching activities such as the PPVIC SpeakUp! event and
     the Academic Fair. These initiatives aimed to build confidence and collaboration by
     encouraging students to present ideas, seek peer feedback, and refine their abilities in structured
     settings. The PPVIC SpeakUP, a Ted Talk-style activity, fostered motivation as speakers
     proactively sought feedback and refined skills post-event, sparking interest in public speaking
     among S1 students and confirming its role in peer-driven learning. The Academic Fair provided
     a platform where Padlet reflections confirmed students gained valuable insights through peer
     presentations, with a school survey indicating 54.1% of presenting students reported increased
     confidence and motivation.

     The expansion of DEAR time periods reflected institutional commitment to reading culture,
     supported by 71% of teachers acknowledging improved access to reading materials via Science
     journal articles, Chinese booklets, Mathematics/English curated articles for self-directed
     learning. APASO survey excellence, with top-tier scores in "Reading (Keen On)" and "Reading
     (Good Strategies)," underscored effective whole-school literacy strategies, cultivating an
     environment where students actively engaged in self-improvement, leveraged peer learning, and
     mastered reading strategies.



2.   Enhancing Student Recognition Through Showcasing Achievements

     To showcase students' achievements across various platforms, we organized a series of student-
     led displays and events designed to foster motivation through public recognition and peer
     engagement. The Academic Fair served as a key platform, where students presented projects in
     subjects such as science, mathematics, and language arts, with Padlet reflections confirming
     that participants gained valuable insights through peer interactions, validating the event's role
     in collaborative learning. A school survey revealed that 54.1% of presenting students reported
     increased confidence and motivation after presenting their work. Over 70% of teachers agreed
     that the showcased works were of high standard and reflected in-depth understanding of topics,
     as evidenced by 71% acknowledging enhanced access to related materials through initiatives
     like S.1 Integrated Science journal articles and curated Mathematics/English resources.

     Complementing this, the Pope Paul Soundscape during Musical Week provided a supportive
     busking environment, with 87% of participants reporting growth in confidence and 91% of
     teacher feedback noting enhanced motivation, while Science Week activities, including the Got
     Science Talent Competition and team projects, allowed students to publicly demonstrate real-
     world applications, deepening interest and interpersonal skills. APASO survey data further
     highlighted top-tier literacy scores, underscoring how these showcases cultivated an
     environment of self-improvement and mastery.

3.   Extending Academic Engagement Through Structured Learning Opportunities

     The initiative to extend academic subjects has successfully fostered students' deeper interest in
     subject knowledge across Key Learning Areas, enhancing motivation through positive feedback
     and recognition.

        In Science Education, programs like Science Week, the Got Science Talent Competition,
         and iGEM participation enabled real-world applications, boosting curiosity and skills such
         as critical thinking and research, with students reporting heightened accomplishment from
         public showcases and peer teaching.

        In English Language Education, peer teaching in reading and grammar, supported by
         logbooks for progress tracking, encouraged self-directed goals and external competitions
         like the Speech Festival (S.1 First Place, S.2 Third Place) and Drama Fest (Outstanding
         Awards), increasing confidence among shy students.

        Technology Education extensions, including S.1 cookbook research (53.7% exceeding
         targets) and competitions like the Digital Stock Trading Guru Inter-School Competition
         (54.38% participation), improved engagement and habits, especially for low-achievers.

        In Citizenship and Social Development, the S.4 museum enquiry project on Chinese heritage
         yielded 85.3% achieving Grade C or above, with improving in independent thinking and
         data skills and strengthening national identity (85.7% enhanced).

     Some complementary measures, such as S.6 after-school elective tutorials, S.1 after-school
     homework sessions, and study tours like the Yunnan natural exploration (May 2025) and Tokyo
     technology trip (July 2025), which extended classroom learning. S.1 self-directed lessons
     covered note-taking, time management from "超凡學生手記," and exam analysis, while S.2
     STEAM curriculum emphasized design thinking and iterative prototyping. Data from
     stakeholder surveys and APASO III analysis confirm increased discussion depth and interest in
     advanced topics.



4.   Recognizing Student Efforts Through Appreciation Cards

     The school implemented a strategy of designing and distributing appreciation cards to
     acknowledge students' efforts in various activities, aligning with the major concern of
     promoting motivation through positive feedback and recognition. This initiative was central to
     the Big Sister Scheme under the Counselling Team, where little sisters wrote personalized cards
     to big sisters at the final lunch meeting, achieving all agreement among Counselling Team
     Assistants (CTAs) that their efforts were recognized, as evidenced by team surveys.
     Achievements show a 25% increase in participation rates in lunch and team meetings compared
     to the prior year, based on observational data. The cards extended to complementary programs
     like the Pope Paul Soundscape, a student-led busking event by the Publicity Committee, where
     informal card-like recognitions contributed to 96% of teacher audience viewing it as a
     meaningful expression outlet. All performers reported skill growth in teamwork, time
     management, and self-expression, as per post-event surveys.

5.   Implementing Student Award Scheme to Praise Students' Good Behaviors

     The implementation of a student award scheme to praise students' good behaviors was carried
     out by the Values Education Committee through five Monthly Star elections, awarding a total
     of 266 students across themes including courtesy and respect, inquisitiveness, optimism,
     perseverance and fostering class unity. Teacher questionnaires indicated that approximately
     80% of awarded students demonstrated more active manifestation of good virtues, while 65%
     showed overall improvement in conduct compared to the beginning of the semester. Class
     teachers observed increased student attention to personal and peer conduct, with active
     nominations particularly evident in unity themes, including recognition of a student facing
     serious illness. This scheme encouraged a culture of mutual appreciation, aligning with
     stakeholder survey results where 93.5% of teachers agreed that highlighting accomplishments
     boosts motivation.



6.   Encouraging Teachers to Provide Positive Feedback and Recognition During Lessons
     and Extracurricular Activities

     This initiative was advanced through the Staff Development Committee's workshops on
     managing group dynamics and providing constructive feedback, such as "Providing Positive
     and Constructive Feedback to Students" and "Understanding Interpersonal Dynamics through
     MBTI." Surveys revealed that 84% of teachers agreed their awareness of providing positive
     feedback had increased, consistent with the broader stakeholder survey findings of 83.9%
     teacher agreement on this item. This heightened awareness was reflected in activities like
     reading programs, where positive reinforcement led to more library visits and book borrowing,
     and tutorials for S.1 and S.6 students, which cultivated effective study routines and improved
     punctuality and self-management. Attendance data from these sessions suggested success, with
     fewer students requiring weekly attendance.



7.   Organizing More Student-Led Opportunities in School Events to Highlight Student
     Accomplishments

     In our Admission Talk and Staff Development Day this year, we aimed to highlight student
     accomplishments through the Publicity Committee's initiatives, including the student-led
     Instagram account launched in November, which published various posts reaching over
     hundreds of followers in our School Ambassadors’ activities. This platform boosted participants'
     confidence in planning, digital communication, and media production. Additionally, the Pope
     Paul Soundscape busking event provided a supportive outlet for talent expression, with 96% of
     teacher feedback indicating it was meaningful and 87% observing increased confidence among
     participants. All performers reported growth in teamwork, time management, and self-
     expression. Stakeholder surveys corroborated this, with 54.6% of students agreeing that peer
     teaching aided recognition and confidence, 56.4% feeling pride after presentations, and 54.1%
     reporting increased motivation post-presentation.

8.   Organizing More Student-Led Opportunities to Foster a Culture of Praise

     Discipline Committee organized a Mock Trial Competition this year and positive feedback
     during training and competitions were found to boost student confidence, engagement, and
     skills in legal reasoning, public speaking, and teamwork. This created a supportive environment

9.   Implementation of Structured Reading Programs to Enhance Literacy Skills

     The school implemented structured reading programs to enhance literacy skills, including the
     Drop Everything And Read (DEAR) Time and Reading Awards initiatives for S.1 to S.6 students
     throughout the academic year. DEAR Time encouraged regular reading, with principal reports
     noting an increase in library visits as more students borrowed reading materials, though some
     confusion arose regarding permissible materials. The Reading Awards program recognized
     student engagement, awarding drink coupons and library prizes monthly, which led to
     heightened library attendance and prize redemption visits. Survey data indicated that 68.5% of
     students reported improved reading habits, while teacher feedback highlighted a 25% increase
     in book borrowing rates. These efforts collectively fostered a stronger reading culture, supported
     by positive parent feedback, noting improved literacy engagement at home.



10. Organization of Reading-Related Competitions and Events to Boost Engagement

     The school organized reading-related competitions and events to boost engagement, including
     the "抗日戰爭勝利紀念日主題書展" (3-13/9/2024), "全港即興寫作比賽" (30/10/2024), and
     "Detective Riddles" (12/2/2025 - 31/3/2025) for S.1 to S.6 students. The book exhibition and
     writing competition attracted broad participation, with 60% of students attending at least one
     event and 45% submitting entries, reflecting increased interest in literary activities. However,
     the Detective Riddles event saw limited uptake, with only one student participating, indicating
     a need for better promotion. The "One-Day Manager" events (27/2/2025 - 15/5/2025) involved
     new teachers in the library, resulting in a 20% increase in book borrowing on those days, driven
     by student excitement over teacher-autographed stickers and recommended books. Teacher
     surveys showed 78% agreement that these events enhanced student motivation, while student
     feedback indicated 55.2% felt more confident in reading comprehension, demonstrating the
     programs' success in engaging diverse learners.

Reflection

  Facilitating Factors

        Structured lessons and programs provided clear frameworks for skill development, with a
         significant percentage of students demonstrating improved competencies and help-seeking
         behaviors.

        Student-led initiatives and peer-driven activities empowered participants, fostering
         confidence and responsibility through collaborative feedback and leadership opportunities.

        Cross-disciplinary collaborations and the use of supportive tools enhanced self-monitoring
         and resilience, as observed in classroom activities and principal evaluations.

        External partnerships brought real-world relevance to learning experiences, boosting
         participation and engagement in various school programs.

        Consistent scheduling of structured activities created a supportive environment, encouraging
         regular participation and reinforcing positive behaviors.

        Teacher involvement in engaging roles facilitated stronger student-teacher interactions,
         contributing to increased participation and enthusiasm.

        Alignment of resources with curriculum goals ensured targeted support, leading to
         measurable improvements in student skills and understanding.

        Parental involvement reinforced learning outcomes at home, creating a cohesive support
         system that enhanced overall student progress.


  Hindering Factors

        Limited reach and uneven motivation levels were evident, with a notable portion of students
         reporting lower enthusiasm and confidence, alongside challenges in addressing diverse
         learning needs.

        Showcase events lacked inclusivity, with participation skewed toward high achievers,
         leaving foundational gaps for some learners and logistical challenges due to tight timelines.

        Time constraints and resource shortages in senior forms and other areas impeded effective
         coordination and participation in peer-led activities.

        Inconsistent follow-up after key events reduced the potential to sustain motivation, with
         varying student preparedness impacting group dynamics and broader recognition efforts.

        Misunderstandings and inappropriate material use during structured sessions led to
         inconsistent engagement, necessitating additional guidance to maintain focus.

        Low participation in specific initiatives highlighted inadequate promotion and engagement
         strategies, indicating a need for more accessible formats.

     Lack of sustained activities post-competitions limited long-term engagement, with many
      students discontinuing pursuits after initial involvement.

     Scheduling conflicts and varying interest levels affected attendance, hindering the uniform
      effectiveness of programs across all student groups.



Overall Reflection

  The overarching success in enhancing student motivation through peer learning initiatives, such
  as PPVIC SpeakUp! and the Academic Fair, is evident that 54.1% of presenting students reporting
  increased confidence and motivation, alongside top-tier APASO scores in reading engagement.
  Showcasing achievements via events like the Pope Paul Soundscape and Academic Fair, with
  87% of participants and 91% of teachers noting confidence and motivation gains, further
  underscores the effectiveness of public recognition in fostering a positive learning culture. The
  extension of academic engagement through structured opportunities, including Science Week and
  study tours, has deepened subject interest, while recognition strategies like appreciation cards and
  the Monthly Star awards have boosted participation and virtue demonstration by 25% and 65%,
  respectively. Teacher-led feedback initiatives and student-led events have consistently elevated
  engagement, with 84% of teachers reporting increased feedback awareness and 54.6% of students
  valuing peer teaching.

  However, challenges persist that necessitate strategic adjustments. The limited reach of
  motivation, with only 40.4% of students feeling more motivated and 36.7% more enthusiastic,
  highlights disparities in engagement across the cohort. Inconsistencies in participation, such as
  the low uptake in Detective Riddles and dominance of high achievers in showcases, indicate
  inclusive gaps that require targeted interventions. Logistical constraints, including tight
  preparation timelines and resource shortages, have impacted the uniformity of outcomes, while
  limited follow-up mechanisms have hindered sustained impact, as seen with only 45% of students
  continuing literary pursuits post-competition. The experience derived from this cycle emphasizes
  the need for enhanced planning, inclusive design, and consistent reinforcement to ensure
  equitable benefits and long-term growth. These insights will guide the proposed follow-up
  measures to address identified shortcomings and build on established successes.

Major Concern 2: Developing students’ time management skills

Achievements

1.   Conducting Student Workshops During SDL Lessons on Time Management
     Techniques

     The school conducted student workshops during Self-Directed Learning (SDL)
     lessons to help students master time management techniques, focusing on skills like
     prioritizing tasks and setting personal goals. These sessions were integrated into the
     curriculum for junior forms, with subject teachers rotating to provide personalized
     support.

     Attendance data showed high participation, with 95% of S.1 students attending all
     scheduled workshops. Survey results indicated that 78% of participants reported
     improved abilities in seeking help when facing learning difficulties, as per self-
     evaluation surveys. Teacher observations confirmed a 20% reduction in late
     assignment submissions in the first term, demonstrating the workshops' effectiveness
     in equipping students with practical tools for punctuality and self-management.



2.   Implementing S.1 After-School Sessions to Complete Homework and Other
     Tasks

     The implementation of mandatory after-school sessions for S.1 students from 4:15
     p.m. to 5:15 p.m., starting with the Winter Timetable on September 24, 2024, aimed
     to foster disciplined homework completion and task management. Tutors and
     alumnae volunteers provided guidance, helping students cultivate effective study
     routines. Evaluation data from early December showed that 85% of students
     completed assignments on time, with a 15% increase in early dismissal eligibility at
     4:45 p.m. Stakeholder surveys revealed 72% of teachers noting improved self-
     management in S.1 students, while parent feedback highlighted reduced home study
     stress, underscoring the program's success in building foundational time management
     habits.



3.   Educating Students on Time Management for iPads and Providing Strategies to
     Reduce Screen Time

     Education on iPad time management was delivered through dedicated sessions,
     providing strategies to reduce screen time and prioritize educational use. Students
     learned techniques like app timers and focused work modes, integrated into IT and
     class lessons. Review of DC records showed a 15% decrease in screen-related
     distractions. Class teacher feedback indicated that over 70% of students agreed they
     had a better understanding of screen time strategies, as per surveys. This initiative
     demonstrated a commitment to balancing technology with productive habits,
     promoting healthier digital behaviors among students.

4.   Revising the BYOD Policy to Prioritize Educational Use and Minimize
     Distractions

     The revision of the Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) policy emphasized educational
     prioritization and distraction minimization, with updated guidelines distributed

     Spirit
