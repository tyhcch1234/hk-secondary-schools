1. Information about the school
1.1 School Profile
    1. CCC Ming Kei College was founded in 1967 and celebrated its 50th Anniversary in the year
        2016-2017. It is an aided co-educational secondary school directly operated by the Hong
        Kong Council of the Church of Christ in China (HKCCCC). The school has been using
        English as the medium of instruction since its inauguration.
    2. In September 1998, the HKSAR Government began to adopt the language policy of mother-
        tongue teaching for public sector schools. Only those secondary schools with a proven
        record of using English as the teaching medium successfully were allowed to retain the use
        of “English as the medium of instruction” (EMI) at the levels of Secondary 1 to 7. CCC
        Ming Kei College was one of the 112 secondary schools in the territory which were
        approved that year to continue to be EMI Schools.
        In September 2010, the Education Bureau implemented the fine-tuning policy for the
        medium of instruction in secondary schools. CCC Ming Kei College continued to use
        English as the teaching medium for all students.
    3. The school has implemented measures, in stages over the years to respond proactively to
        education reforms, particularly for the curriculum reform which was initiated by the
        Education Commission in 2000.            The major reform measures which have been
        implemented since the 2000-2001 school year are as follows:
        <2000/01> (1) Reform of the examination system at the S.1 & S.2 levels;
                     (2) Incorporation of “project learning” in the formal curriculum;
                     (3) Development of school-based curriculum for S.1 Integrated Humanities
                         and S.1 Chinese Language; and
                     (4) Issue of annual Non-academic Reports recording the activities joined by
                         and prizes won by students as part of their annual “learning profiles”.
        <2001/02> (1) Offering of one S.2 class which uses Putonghua as the medium of
                         instruction to study Chinese language.
                     (2) Launching of the Young-old Partnership Project for S.2 as a cross-
                         curricular project learning programme; and
                     (3) Development of school-based curricula for S.2 Integrated Humanities and
                         S.2 Chinese Language.

                         six 55-minute periods per day to create more time available for teachers

                     (3) Offering of one S.3 class which uses Putonghua as the medium of
                         instruction to study Chinese language; and

        <2003/04> (1) Introduction of a 20-minute Morning Reading Session before the first
                         period every school day to promote reading of English books;

1.3 School Campus and Facilities
    1. The school is located at the junction of Oak Street and Cherry Street in Tai Kok Tsui.
        With an area of more than 4,500 square metres, it is composed of two independent
        buildings, the school hall and the main building. The school hall is air-conditioned and
        has a seating capacity of 1,300 which can accommodate all teachers and students of the
        school for assemblies. The six-storey, L-shaped main building has 31 classrooms of
        standard size and 5 laboratories (including the Physics, Chemistry, Biology and two
        Integrated Science laboratories), all of which are fully equipped. It has also 12 special
        rooms, namely, Computer Room, Multi-media Laboratory Centre, Language Laboratory,
        Computer Assisted Learning Centre, Library, Geography Room, Art Room, Needlework
        Room, Home Economics Room, Music Room, Ceramics Room and English Corner.
        All classrooms and special rooms are air-conditioned, providing students with an
        effective learning environment.
    2. The school has a sufficient number of lockers for the use of every student. The campus
        also boasts one basketball court, one covered playground, one table-tennis court and an
        Art Gallery which was established in the 1999-2000 school year under sponsorship of
        the Quality Education Fund. The Art Gallery does not only provide a place for
        displaying students’ outstanding paintings and ceramics works, but also serves as a space
        used by the S.6 students for self-studying during regular school hours and after school.
    3. There are three Multimedia Learning Centres and one computer room. Computers and
        projectors are installed in the library, classrooms and special rooms. The school has a
        total of about three hundred computers which are networked together and can access the
        internet through broadband link. Students can make free access to the computers after
        school on weekdays and on Saturday mornings. This would help facilitate the
        implementation of school-based information technology education.
    4. With the EDB’s sponsorship of an approximate amount of forty million dollars, the
        school has its School Improvement Project (SIP) undertaken during the school years of
        2003-2004 and 2004-2005 and completed by the end of Summer Vacation in 2005. The
        project provides a new six-storey annex for the school with an additional 1,058 square
        metres of working and studying area. It comprises six more classrooms, one Computer
        Assisted Learning Room, one Conference Room, one new Computer Room, one Student
        Activity Centre, one Language Learning Room and a number of small rooms for specific
        purposes. Besides, a fireman lift has been built simultaneously for the use of both the
        staff and students with physical disability.
    5. With the sponsorship of the CCC Kei To Mongkok Church, the school has converted the
        table tennis court into a multi-purpose mini-hall, which can be used for small-scale mass
        programs and other learning activities during the summer vacation in 2011.
    6. With the sponsorship of the CCC Kei To Mongkok Church, the school has converted the
        Multi-media Learning Centre at ground floor into a Religious Education Resource
        Centre / Study Room, which can be used for religious education and self-study place for
        students in 2014.
    7. The old systems of audio-visual facilities and air-conditioning in the school hall were
        replaced in the year 2016-2017. The school hall was named after the school’s Founding
        Principal to become “The John Keith Walls Hall” during the 50th Anniversary
        Thanksgiving Service held on 10 March 2017.

1.4 School Mission and Goals
    1. The mission statement and school goals were set out collaboratively by all teachers and
        then endorsed by the SMC in June 1999. They can be summarized as follows:
        Embracing the HKCCCC’s mission for education, the school is committed to holistic
        education with a person-centred curriculum and extra-curricular activities which address,
        with equal emphasis, the moral, intellectual, physical, social, aesthetic and spiritual
        needs of our students. Through such provisions:
         we strive to nurture our students in becoming individuals who are able to extend their
            love for themselves to their neighbours and to God;
         we guide our students towards becoming honest, courteous, law-abiding people who
            are respectful to teachers and parents, caring to the young ones, and willing to share
            with and serve others;
         we prepare our students for assuming responsibility for their own lives, facing
            challenges with courage and being able to respond to the needs of our society; and
         we encourage our students to be involved in improving our society, contributing to
            the nation and serving the world.
        It is our aspiration that the lives of our students will give expression to the school motto:
        “It is more blessed to give than to receive”.
    2. The school implements its mission and goals as well as the school motto through the
        provision of both formal and informal curricula, particularly by taking the following
        measures:
         Setting up a String Orchestra through the sponsorship of the QEF in 1999-2000 to
            nurture students’ interest in music by encouraging them to play musical instruments.
         Setting up an Art Gallery through the sponsorship of the QEF in 1999-2000 for
            displaying outstanding artworks by students in order to promote aesthetic education
            and nurture students’ abilities in the appreciation of art;
         Launching the MKC Award Scheme in 2000-2001 to boost students’ participation in
            various extra-curricular activities to help them develop in the moral, intellectual,
            physical, social, aesthetic and spiritual aspects;
         Joining the Integrated Education Scheme in 2000-2001 and adopting a whole school

         Launching a service-centred project, “The Young-old Partnership Project”, for all S.2

2. Evaluation for Implementation of Major Concerns
Major Concern 1: To enhance students’ learning effectiveness for higher level of academic
attainment

Achievements
Target 1: To equip students with basic and essential learning skills

The school built upon existing measures while introducing new strategies to further support
students’ learning. These included designing pre-lesson and post-lesson tasks to support
classroom learning, promoting ethical use of information technology, enhancing students’
language competence, providing targeted support for junior form students, and fostering self-
management and responsibility in learning. Compared to previous years, these measures have
produced encouraging results, with students demonstrating greater engagement, improved
learning habits, and better academic performance across various areas.

1. To design pre-lesson and post-lesson tasks which can facilitate and consolidate classroom
   learning
    Pre-lesson tasks, such as pre-lesson worksheets, information collection tasks, reading
       materials and topic-related videos, were designed to help students recall prerequisite
       knowledge and prepare for new topics. Based on lesson observations and feedback from
       both teachers and students, most students were able to complete the pre-lesson tasks. This
       preparation equipped them with a foundational understanding of key concepts, which in
       turn enhanced their comprehension and participation during lessons.
    Post-lesson tasks, including summary tables, concept maps, chapter reviews, and DSE past
       paper questions were given to reinforce learning and apply newly acquired knowledge.
       According to homework inspection and feedback from teachers and students, most students
       were able to complete the post-lesson tasks. The tasks effectively facilitated the
       consolidation of learning, enabling students to apply what they had learned and reinforce
       their understanding of the new concepts.
    While most students benefited from the approach, some found the tasks challenging,
       highlighting the need for differentiated support and scaffolding to cater to diverse learning
       needs.
2. To promote wise and ethical use of information technology for learning
    The school established clear iPad usage rules for all students to ensure appropriate use during
       lessons. Students were allowed to use iPads during lessons strictly for educational purposes
       such as note-taking, accessing learning apps, searching for study-related information and
       interactive lesson activities. Based on lesson observation and teacher feedback, most students
       followed the rules and used iPads responsibly. To address occasional misuse of iPads during
       lessons, the school will continue to enforce clear usage rules next year to promote responsible
       and focused use of technology in the classroom.
    The school actively encouraged students to use information technology to enhance learning
       both inside and beyond the classroom. Teachers adopted interactive teaching approaches
       supported by e-learning tools such as Padlet, Google Slides, Jamboard, and Kahoot!, which
       fostered student interaction and engagement in lessons. All subjects set up Google
       Classroom to share a wide range of learning materials, including notes, supplementary
       graded exercises, answer keys, sound clips, and links to educational websites and YouTube
       videos. Some subjects also provided additional exercises through platforms like the Online

                                                                                                    16
       Question Bank from HKEdCity. Teachers also introduced reliable websites to support self-
       learning. From lesson observation and feedback from teachers and students, students were
       actively engaged in lessons and were able to learn more effectively with the support of e-
       learning tools. In addition, they were able to make good use of the online resources
       provided, which supported their independent learning and helped cater to different learning
       needs.
      To further promote responsible use of technology, the school also incorporated digital
       ethics and online safety into student learning experiences. Selected social issues related to
       digital ethics and responsible technology use were integrated into the Computer Literacy
       lessons. In addition, various activities were organized, including a drama show on
       intellectual property, talks on identifying fake information and anti-doxxing, and a
       workshop on digital and AI literacy. Feedback from students and teachers showed that the
       drama was well-received for its engaging and interactive format. While the talks and
       workshop were informative, some students found them overly content-heavy. To enhance
       the effectiveness of future activities, it is recommended to incorporate more interactive
       elements such as live polls, short quizzes, and group discussions to improve student
       participation and learning outcomes.
      According to the APASO results, the Q-scores for the subscale “Information Technology
       (No Addiction)” in KPM 17 increased from 102 in the previous year to 108. This reflects
       an improvement in students’ ability to use information technology in a balanced and
       healthy manner.

3. To enhance students’ language competence for more effective learning
    All S.1 to S.3 students subscribed to the English self-learning platform ‘English Builder’.
      Some classes achieved high completion rates, with students receiving certificates for
      outstanding performance. Notably, one student was awarded ‘Star Speaker’ among all school
      users in Hong Kong. However, the overall completion rate was around 51%, which was
      below expectations. Some students were reluctant to complete exercises on time, affecting
      overall engagement. Despite this, feedback from both students and teachers indicated that
      the programme remains conducive to learning, particularly for students who are motivated
      to explore a variety of themes and text types. The subscription to the platform will continue
      in the coming school year. To improve engagement, the English Department will adopt new
      measures, such as allocating class time for platform activities and setting rules to help
      students meet their progress targets.
    English teachers incorporated specific reading skill types into both teaching and assessments
      for S.1 to S.3. The selected skills and question types were appropriate for each level, and
      teachers agreed that students were generally able to handle them effectively. Evidence of
      improvement was observed, indicating that the approach successfully supported the
      development of students’ reading skills. This will continue in the coming school year.
    All EMI subjects provided language support by designing learning materials that enhance
      students’ ability to learn in English. These resources include vocabulary lists, exercises on
      commonly used grammar items across subjects, as well as guidelines and structured writing
      tasks to support the development of academic writing skills. Based on homework inspection
      and teacher feedback, many students have shown improvement in spelling, appropriate use
      of subject-specific vocabulary, and greater confidence and ability to express their ideas in
      writing.
    As scheduled, the English Activity Days and the English Week were successfully held with
      the support of trained English Ambassadors. While student participation in English Activity
      Days was generally lukewarm, engagement during English Week was noticeably stronger.
      To further enhance participation, a wider variety of activities could be explored, and targeted
      student groups may be invited to take part in future events.

Target 2: To facilitate students to set and achieve their learning goals
Continuing established practices with new measures, the school supports students in setting
and achieving their learning goals. The class streaming methods in junior forms were reviewed
and refined to better suit students’ learning needs. Differentiated learning materials and
assessments remained a key strategy for promoting self-directed learning and cater to diverse
learning abilities. Supplementary lessons and study groups were arranged to enhance
academic performance, while additional support was provided to low achievers to help them
progress. New strategies were introduced to guide students in understanding their learning
progress, encouraging self-reflection and goal-setting. Students’ academic achievements and
improvements were also recognized to foster motivation and a positive learning culture.
1. To review and revise the class streaming methods of junior forms
    This year, the school adopted new class streaming methods for S.1 and S.3. The arrangement
      aimed to better cater to learner diversity and foster a more positive learning atmosphere
      within each class. The Academic Committee evaluated the effectiveness of this strategy
      through teacher feedback and analysis of examination performance. Findings indicate that
      the new class allocation strategy has been effective in supporting student learning and
      enhancing the learning atmosphere.
    To assess the effectiveness of these measures, we compared the performance of S.1 students
      in the lower ability classes (S.1A, S.1B & S.1C) in the 2024 Pre-S1 Attainment Test and the
      second-term examination of the 2024–2025 school year. The standard score difference for
      the overall result was 0.44. This positive value suggests that students with lower academic
      abilities have benefited from small-class teaching and demonstrated improvement in their
      academic performance.
2. To provide differentiated learning materials and assessments to cater for self-directed
   learning and diverse learning needs
    A differentiated teaching syllabus was implemented for English Language in S.1 and S.2.
       The English Department developed three schemes of work: core, enhancement, and
       remedial. Each scheme of work is accompanied by a set of teaching materials with different
       focuses, specifically designed to cater to the varying learning needs of students in each ability
       group.
    Various subject departments developed and implemented graded exercises tailored to
       students' different ability levels. More challenging materials were provided for high-
       achieving students, while those with lower academic performance were given resources
       focused on strengthening foundational knowledge. Graded supplementary materials with
       answer keys were provided, allowing students to select tasks appropriate to their learning
       needs.
    Various subject departments included challenging questions or bonus questions in
       assignments, tests and examinations.
    Lesson observations, homework inspections, and feedback from teachers and students
       indicate that this differentiated approach proved effective. Students showed greater
       confidence, with less able learners successfully completing basic tasks and more able
       students engaging with advanced challenging tasks. The graded materials enabled self-paced
       learning and helped students revisit subject contents as needed. Students became more
       proactive, demonstrating initiative by completing optional tasks.
    The school has provided additional support for gifted students. For example, the
       Mathematics Department offered a variety of training courses and opportunities to participate
       in competitions. Many students have achieved commendable results, with some ranking
       within the top 5% in international challenges. These accomplishments not only affirm their
       mathematical talents but also inspire a strong motivation among these elite students to
       explore more advanced topics and further develop their skills.

Overall Evaluation
To address the school’s major concern of enhancing students’ learning effectiveness for higher
academic attainment, a range of strategies has been implemented, with the Stakeholder Survey
results reflecting steady progress. Students’ overall perception of their learning increased from a
mean score of 3.2 last year to 3.4 this year. Although it remains the lowest among all survey aspects,
this progress is encouraging as it suggests that the school’s strategies are having a positive impact.

To enhance learning effectiveness, the school has focused on equipping students with essential
learning skills. According to the Stakeholder Survey, students consistently acknowledged that
teachers often teach learning strategies such as conducting pre-lesson preparation, using concept
maps, and utilizing online resources, with the mean score remaining at 3.7 for both 2023-2024 and
2024-2025. Students’ agreement with the statement “I know how to apply learning strategies”
showed a slight increase, with the mean score rising from 3.3 last year to 3.4 this year. This modest
progress indicates that the strategies adopted are effective, and the school should continue its efforts
to support students in applying learning strategies in their studies. In addition to promoting learning
skills, teachers have consistently provided appropriate support to help students achieve higher
academic attainment. In the Stakeholder Survey, teachers indicated that they are able to provide
timely and specific feedback to help students reflect and improve, with a mean score of 4.3 in both
this year and last year. Meanwhile, students’ agreement with the statement “Teachers often provide
suggestions on my performance and let me know how to improve” increased from 3.7 to 3.8 this
year. Furthermore, teachers agreed that they adjusted teaching strategies based on students’ learning
progress, with a mean score of 4.3 — the highest-rated item in teachers’ perception on teaching in
both this year and last year — demonstrating their awareness of catering to learner diversity. These
findings reaffirm the school’s commitment to fostering a supportive and adaptive learning
environment that empowers students to learn more effectively and perform better academically.

The school has also placed emphasis on helping students set learning goals and build confidence.
The Stakeholder Survey results reflected improvements in goal setting and perseverance among
students. The mean score for “I know how to set my learning goals” rose from 3.2 to 3.4, while the
mean score for “I try my best to overcome learning difficulties” increased from 3.6 to 3.7. Teacher
support also strengthened, with the score for “Teachers often encourage us to learn and try our best
to overcome learning difficulties” rising from 3.6 to 3.7. Recognizing that confidence in learning is
a foundational element for academic success, the school has made efforts to enhance this quality
among students. The Stakeholder Survey results show a slight increase in both student and teacher
responses. Students’ mean score for the statement “I am confident in learning inside and outside of
the classroom” rose from 3.1 to 3.2, while teachers’ mean score for the statement “My students are
confident in learning” increased from 3.2 to 3.3. Although confidence in learning remains one of the
lowest-rated items in the Stakeholder Survey, the improvement is a positive sign. The school will
continue its efforts to help students set learning goals and boost their confidence in order to enhance
their academic performance.

        The Integrated Education Committee (IEC) was formed in September 2003 to oversee

        students) to solve problems and/or handle difficulties in their daily school lives,
        especially with regard to social, behavioural and learning aspects. Special Educational
        Needs Coordinator (SENCO) and Special Educational Needs Support Teacher (SENST)

        implementing the Whole School Approach to IE in order to further cultivate an
        inclusive school culture and enhance the effectiveness of the support given to students
        with SENs.

       Before the beginning of this academic year, the IEC had a discussion about certain SEN
       students with the resource teachers of Ebenezer School and SEN Coordinators of

       information as to the handling of those cases and made a number of valuable
       suggestions for the school.

       school life. They are primarily concerned with the promotion of a caring school culture.

       promote acceptance and support.

       The IEC organised school-based training sessions on Integrated Education for teachers
       who were directly involved in the teaching of the SEN students in early August 2024.

       known to the teachers. Most teachers found the suggestions made by the IEC to be
       practical.

      Chinese Language sessions teaching basic classical Chinese.

     Additionally, 94% of the students found that the teachers’ explanation interesting. As noted
     by the teachers, the programme was less formal than regular school lessons, allowing for the
     inclusion of enjoyable games that engaged students and facilitated learning.

     bottom 25% of them, were identified. The school arranged an 8-day English Enhancement
     Class to provide additional support for these students. The main areas of focus included
     reading skills, basic grammar items and vocabulary building. According to the survey
     conducted after the English Enrichment Class, 93% of the S.1 students found the class useful
     and they could understand the teachers’ explanation in English. The teachers also indicated
     that students had participated actively in class and shown improvement in the interactive
     activities.

i.   The S.1 Bridging Programme was originally scheduled to take place every morning from 18 th
     to 22nd August 2025, and the S.1 English Enhancement Class every afternoon from 18 th to
     27th August 2025. However, both sessions on 18th August were cancelled due to the
     government’s announcement of school suspension in response to adverse weather conditions.
     As a result, the S.1 Bridging Programme was held from 19th to 22nd August 2025, and the
     English Enhancement Class from 19th to 27th August 2025.

     demonstrates the effectiveness of these pre-S1 activities in preparing our students for using
     English as the medium of instruction.
