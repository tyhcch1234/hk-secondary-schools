     Major Concern 1: Customize learning experiences: To enhance learning outcomes through
      diversified and personalized educational practices

    Achievements
    In 2024–2025, subject panels and academic units actively implemented a wide range of
    strategies to diversify and personalize student learning. Academic differentiation was
    addressed through both enrichment and remedial measures. Gifted students benefited
    from targeted programmes such as the Physics Olympiad training, HKUST Dual Program,
    elite Visual Arts training at the Arts and Technology Education Centre, Putonghua recitation
    classes with external tutors, and participation in local and international competitions in
    STEM, humanities, and the arts.

    Support for less able students was provided through enhancement classes, small-group
    tutorials, and structured post-lesson consolidation activities. For example, several panels
    conducted additional tutorials before assessments, offered regular feedback on
    assignments, and tailored teaching resources via Google Classroom. While attendance at
    optional remedial classes varied, students with consistent participation generally showed
    improvement.

    Experiential and cross-curricular learning were prominent. Highlights included
    interdisciplinary study tours to Germany and the Netherlands, local ecological fieldwork,
    Chinese Culture Activity Day co-organised by Putonghua, Chinese, Chinese History, and
    Science panels, and STEAM-infused projects such as F.1 logo design and F.2 zodiac laser
    engraving. Project-based learning was used to connect knowledge with real-life contexts,
    such as Biology’s ecological investigations, History’s heritage visits, and Junior Science’s
    design cycle tasks.

    Technology integration expanded, with most panels making regular use of online
    platforms, AI tools, and simulations for pre-lesson preparation, collaborative research, and
    skill practice. Library-led reading programmes, thematic book displays, and cross-panel
    literacy initiatives further diversified learning modes. Student leadership was fostered
    through roles such as student-librarians, peer mentors in gifted programmes, and team
    leaders in competitions.

    Reflection
    Personalisation in learning yielded clear academic and engagement gains. High
    participation was seen in AI-driven research tasks, cross-curricular projects, and external
    competitions. Differentiated strategies helped both high-achievers and students needing
    reinforcement. However, attendance in voluntary remedial programmes remained uneven,
    limiting their impact on some target groups. Teacher readiness in advanced technology
    integration varied, leading to uneven application of AI tools. Large-scale projects delivered
    strong learning experiences but required substantial resources, raising sustainability
    considerations.

    Feedback and Follow-up
    To build on the progress made, we can:

    •     Increase participation in remedial and enhancement programmes by incorporating
          certain remedial sessions into the regular timetable or linking them with compulsory
          formative tasks to raise attendance. Varied scheduling options should also be offered
          to accommodate students’ commitments;
    •     Broaden accessibility of enrichment activities through the design of entry-level
          competitions and workshops for junior forms. Enrichment programmes should be
          promoted via taster sessions, peer sharing, and parent briefings to encourage wider
          participation;
    •     Strengthen consolidation after experiential learning by building structured follow-up
          into the activity design. This may include reflective journals, presentations, or cross-
          subject assessment tasks to ensure learning is captured and applied;
    •     Enhance teacher capacity in AI and technology integration by expanding professional
          development workshops with hands-on AI and e-learning applications. Regular peer-
          sharing sessions between panels should also be organised to showcase successful
          practices;
    •     Maintain sustainability of cross-curricular projects by prioritising scalable, lower-
          resource models without compromising quality. This could involve alternating major
          projects across years or sharing coordination between panels; and
    •     Leverage student leadership for customised learning by formalising student leader
          roles in academic and co-curricular programmes. Student leaders can help drive peer
          learning, mentor participation, and assist in activity organisation.


       Major concern 2: Live up to the core values of Catholic Education: To strengthen individual
        and community responsibility and foster active citizenship

    Achievements
    In 2024–2025, the school integrated Catholic core values across academic, co-curricular,
    and pastoral programmes to nurture responsible individuals and active citizens. Values
    education was embedded in classroom teaching, assemblies, and extra-curricular activities.
    The Ethics and Religious Education Panel delivered lessons directly linked to Catholic
    values, guiding students to reflect on moral responsibilities, respect for life, and the
    common good. The Civic Education Section and Discipline Committee reinforced civic
    awareness through assemblies and talks on national security, anti-drug education, crime
    prevention, and community responsibility.

    Service learning was promoted through diverse activities. Students contributed to charity
    initiatives such as the Caritas Bazaar, participated in community cultural projects like the
    Visual Arts “Better Community, Better Life” walk, and offered service in leadership roles
    such as student-librarians, sports team captains, music ensemble leaders, and prefects.
    Peer Mediators, Inclusive Ambassadors, and senior students mentored younger peers in
    academic, cultural, and religious activities.

   National and civic identity were strengthened through heritage site visits, museum trips,
   and cultural celebrations such as the Chinese Culture Activity Day. The interdisciplinary
   study tour to Germany and the Netherlands fostered intercultural understanding, heritage
   preservation awareness, and global citizenship. Career and Life Planning programmes from
   F.1 to F.6 guided students to consider their personal values, responsibilities to society, and
   long-term contributions to the community.

   Staff also modelled Catholic values by participating in the Catholic School Leadership
   attachment programme and organising school-wide moral education initiatives. The
   Student Support Team implemented Individual Education Plans for SEN students,
   emphasising responsibility, empathy, and cooperation between home and school.

   Reflection
   Authentic contexts, such as service roles, cultural celebrations, and civic projects,
   effectively reinforced Catholic values. Strong turnout in signature events reflected the
   appeal of values-based programming. Intercultural and heritage experiences deepened
   students’ sense of identity and responsibility. However, opportunities for values
   application were more frequently taken up by established student leaders, and integration
   of civic themes across all subjects varied in depth. Sustaining enthusiasm for community
   service throughout the year remained a challenge amidst competing priorities.

   Feedback and Follow-up
   To build on the progress made, we can:

   1. Expand participation in service learning by creating entry-level, low-barrier activities
      targeted at junior forms. These should provide meaningful opportunities for students
      to contribute to the community and develop responsibility from an early stage.
   2. Increase the frequency and variety of values-focused experiential learning, including
      local service projects, intercultural exchanges, and heritage visits. All activities should
      include structured pre-activity preparation and post-activity reflection to consolidate
      learning.
   3. Strengthen cross-panel collaboration to embed Catholic values and civic responsibility
      more consistently within subject-based curricula, ensuring alignment between
      academic learning and the school’s mission.
   4. Develop a progressive leadership pathway for students from junior forms upwards,
      providing training and linking roles explicitly to Catholic core values to foster long-term
      commitment to service and citizenship.
   5. Provide ongoing recognition and celebration of students’ service and citizenship
      contributions throughout the school year to sustain motivation and highlight role
      models for the wider student body.
   6. Continue professional development for staff on effective integration of values
      education into lessons, co-curricular activities, and pastoral care, ensuring a coherent
      approach across the school.

     Major concern 3: Promote whole-person wellness: To advocate for holistic health and
      lifelong well-being

    Achievements
    In 2024–2025, the school promoted physical, mental, and social well-being through a
    coordinated programme of academic, co-curricular, and pastoral initiatives. The PE Panel
    expanded sports participation across forms, with school teams competing successfully in
    inter-school athletics, football, basketball, and swimming events. Physical fitness
    programmes and sports days were well attended, fostering regular exercise habits and
    team spirit.

    Mental health and emotional resilience were addressed through activities such as the
    “Failure Experience Day,” stress management workshops, and mindfulness sessions
    organised by the Student Guidance Committee and Student Support Team. Peer Mediators
    and Inclusive Ambassadors received training to support classmates, promote empathy, and
    address interpersonal issues.

    Nutrition and healthy living were promoted through talks by health professionals, thematic
    library displays on wellness, and integration of healthy lifestyle topics into subjects such as
    Science and Life Education. Career and life planning programmes included components on
    balancing work and personal life, resilience, and adaptability.

    Cultural and recreational activities also contributed to whole-person wellness. The Music,
    Visual Arts, and Activity Sections offered creative outlets through performances,
    exhibitions, and art-making workshops, helping students develop aesthetic appreciation
    and self-expression. Participation in interflow study tours and local cultural activities
    further broadened students’ horizons and strengthened their social networks.

    Reflection
    A broad spectrum of wellness activities ensured students with varied interests could
    participate, fostering a culture that values health alongside academics. The integration of
    wellness topics into curricula strengthened long-term awareness. Peer support networks
    enhanced the school’s capacity to respond to well-being needs informally. However, the
    level of participation varied between forms and activity types, with some programmes
    attracting primarily senior students or those already active in sports and leadership.
    Feedback from several sections indicated that sustained engagement in wellness activities
    could be challenged by academic workload and competing commitments. Additionally,
    while mental health awareness has increased, some students were hesitant to seek help
    proactively, suggesting a continued need to normalise open discussion of emotional well-
    being.


    Feedback and Follow-up
    To build on the progress made, we can:

     1. Broaden access to wellness activities by introducing more entry-level, recreational
        sports and creative programmes aimed at junior forms and less active students.

         ensuring reinforcement of physical, mental, and social health messages in multiple

      4. Develop year-round wellness campaigns with recurring events and challenges to
