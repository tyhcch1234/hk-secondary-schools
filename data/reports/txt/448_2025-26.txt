1. Information about the school
1.1 School Profile
    1. CCC Ming Kei College was founded in 1967 and willccelebrate its 50th Anniversary in the
        year 2016-2017. It is an aided co-educational secondary school directly operated by the
        Hong Kong Council of the Church of Christ in China (HKCCCC). The school has been
        using English as the medium of instruction since its inauguration.
    2. In September 1998, the HKSAR Government began to adopt the language policy of mother-
        tongue teaching for public sector schools. Only those secondary schools with a proven
        record of using English as the teaching medium successfully were allowed to retain the use
        of “English as the medium of instruction” (EMI) at the levels of Secondary 1 to 7. CCC
        Ming Kei College was one of the 112 secondary schools in the territory which were
        approved that year to continue to be EMI Schools.
        In September 2010, the Education Bureau implemented the fine-tuning policy for the
        medium of instruction in secondary schools. CCC Ming Kei College continued to use
        English as the teaching medium for all students.
    3. The school has implemented measures in stages over the years to respond proactively to
        education reforms, particularly for the curriculum reform which was initiated by the
        Education Commission in 2000.            The major reform measures which have been
        implemented since the 2000-2001 school year are as follows:
        <2000/01> (1) Reform of the examination system at the S.1 & S.2 levels;
                     (2) Incorporation of “project learning” in the formal curriculum;
                     (3) Development of school-based curriculum for S.1 Integrated Humanities
                         and S.1 Chinese Language; and
                     (4) Issue of annual Non-academic Reports recording the activities joined by
                         and prizes won by students as part of their annual “learning profiles”.
        <2001/02> (1) Offering of one S.2 class which uses Putonghua as the medium of
                         instruction to study Chinese Language.
                     (2) Launching of the Young-old Partnership Project for S.2 as a cross-
                         curricular project learning programme; and
                     (3) Development of school-based curricula for S.2 Integrated Humanities and
                         S.2 Chinese Language.
        <2002/03> (1) Implementation of a new timetabling system featuring a 7-day cycle with
                         six 55-minute periods per day to allow more time for teachers and students
                         to teach and learn;
                     (2) Development of the school-based curriculum for S.3 Chinese Language;
                     (3) Offering of one S.3 class which uses Putonghua as the medium of
                         instruction to study Chinese language; and
                     (4) Offering of ASL Liberal Studies to S.6 students as an optional subject.
        <2003/04> (1) Introduction of a 20-minute Morning Reading Session before the first
                         period every school day to promote reading of English books;
                     (2) Introduction of two test cycles per school term to help enhance students’
                         learning; and

                         “learning and teaching”.
        <2004/05> (1) Introduction of Integrated Humanities to S.4 students as an optional subject;
                         and

1.3 School Campus and Facilities
    1. The school is situated at the junction of Oak Street and Cherry Street in Tai Kok Tsui.
       Occupying an area of over 4,500 square metres, it comprises two independent buildings:
       the School Hall and the Main Building. The fully air-conditioned School Hall, with a
       seating capacity of 1,300, accommodates all teachers and students for assemblies. The
       six-storey, L-shaped Main Building houses 31 standard-sized classrooms and 5 fully
       equipped science laboratories (Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and two Integrated Science
       Laboratories). Additionally, there are 12 special rooms, including the Computer Room,
       Multimedia Laboratory Centre, Language Laboratory, Computer-Assisted Learning
       Centre, Library, Geography Room, Art Room, Needlework Room, Home Economics
       Room, Music Room, Ceramics Room, and English Corner. All classrooms and special
       rooms are air-conditioned, providing an optimal learning environment.
    2. The school provides sufficient lockers for all students. Facilities also include a basketball
       court, a covered playground, a table-tennis court, and an Art Gallery, which was
       established in the 1999–2000 school year with funding from the Quality Education Fund.
       The Art Gallery not only showcases outstanding student artwork and ceramics but also
       serves as a self-study space for S.6 students during and after school hours.
    3. The school is equipped with three Multimedia Learning Centres and one computer room.
       Computers and projectors are installed in the library, classrooms, and special rooms. A
       total of approximately 300 networked computers provide broadband Internet access.
       Students may use the computers free of charge after school on weekdays and on Saturday
       mornings, supporting the implementation of school-based information technology
       education.
    4. With the Education Bureau’s funding of approximately HK$40 million, the School
       Improvement Project (SIP) was carried out during the 2003–2004 and 2004–2005 school
       years and completed by the end of the 2005 summer vacation. A new six-storey annex,
       providing an additional 1,058 square metres of learning and working space, was
       constructed. This annex comprises six classrooms, a Computer-Assisted Learning Room,
       a Conference Room, a new Computer Room, a Student Activity Centre, a Language
       Learning Room, and several purpose-specific rooms. A fireman’s lift was also installed
       for the use of staff and students with physical disabilities.
    5. In 2011, with sponsorship from CCC Kei To Mongkok Church, the former table-tennis
       court was converted into a multi-purpose Mini Hall for small-scale programmes and
       various learning activities, especially during the summer vacation.
    6. In 2014, also with the support of CCC Kei To Mongkok Church, the ground-floor
       Multimedia Learning Centre was converted into a Religious Education Resource
       Centre/Study Room, serving both as a facility for religious education and a self-study
       venue for students.
    7. In 2016–2017, the School Hall’s audio-visual and air-conditioning systems were
       upgraded. During the school’s 50th Anniversary Thanksgiving Service on 10 March 2017,
       the hall was renamed “The John Keith Walls Hall” in honour of the founding principal.
    8. The STEM Learning Centre was established in 2016–2017 and named “The Wong Hin
       Hing STEM Learning Centre” on 10 March 2017 in memory of Mr H.H. Wong, a beloved
       Mathematics teacher who passed away in 2015.
    9. A Quality Education Fund (QEF) grant of approximately HK$2 million was approved for
       a Cantonese drama project and the establishment of a gymnasium.

1.4 School Mission and Goals
    1. The mission statement and school goals were set out collaboratively by all teachers and
        then endorsed by the SMC in June 1999. They can be summarized as follows:
        Embracing the HKCCCC’s mission for education, the school is committed to holistic
        education with a person-centred curriculum and extra-curricular activities which address,
        with equal emphasis, the moral, intellectual, physical, social, aesthetic and spiritual
        needs of our students. Through such provisions:
        ⚫ we strive to nurture our students in becoming individuals who are able to extend their
            love for themselves to their neighbours and to God;
        ⚫ we guide our students towards becoming honest, courteous, law-abiding people who
            are respectful to teachers and parents, caring to the young ones, and willing to share
            with and serve others;
        ⚫ we prepare our students for assuming responsibility for their own lives, facing
            challenges with courage and being able to respond to the needs of our society; and
        ⚫ we encourage our students to be involved in improving our society, contributing to
            the nation and serving the world.
        It is our aspiration that the lives of our students will give expression to the school motto:
        “It is more blessed to give than to receive”.

2. Evaluation for Implementation of Major Concerns
Major Concern 1: To enhance students’ learning effectiveness for higher level of academic
attainment

Achievements
Target 1: To equip students with basic and essential learning skills

The school built upon existing measures while introducing new strategies to further support
students’ learning. These included designing pre-lesson and post-lesson tasks to support
classroom learning, promoting ethical use of information technology, enhancing students’
language competence, providing targeted support for junior form students, and fostering self-
management and responsibility in learning. Building upon the successful practices of previous
years, the majority of the measures implemented this year have proven effective in supporting
student learning and development. Positive outcomes have been observed in students'
engagement, learning habits, and academic performance across various areas.

1. To design pre-lesson and post-lesson tasks which can facilitate and consolidate classroom
   learning
   • Pre-lesson tasks, such as pre-lesson worksheets, information collection tasks, reading
       materials and topic-related videos, were designed to help students recall prerequisite
       knowledge and prepare for new topics. Teaching resources were uploaded to online learning
       platforms such as Google Classroom to facilitate self-directed learning. Based on lesson
       observations and feedback from both teachers and students, most students were able to
       complete the pre-lesson tasks. This preparation equipped them with a foundational
       understanding of key concepts, which in turn enhanced their comprehension and
       participation during lessons.
   • Post-lesson tasks, including summary tables, concept maps, chapter reviews, and DSE past
       paper questions were given to reinforce learning and apply newly acquired knowledge.
       According to homework inspection and feedback from teachers and students, most students
       were able to complete the post-lesson tasks. The tasks effectively facilitated the consolidation
       of learning, enabling students to apply what they had learned and reinforce their
       understanding of the new concepts.
   • While most students benefited from the approach, some lower-achieving students still
       required additional guidance and scaffolding to complete independent learning tasks
       effectively.

2. To promote wise and ethical use of information technology for learning
   • The school continued to enforce clear guidelines on the use of iPad during lessons. Students
      were allowed to use iPads during lessons strictly for educational purposes such as note-
      taking, accessing learning apps, searching for study-related information and interactive
      lesson activities. Based on lesson observation and teacher feedback, most students followed
      the rules and used iPads responsibly. To address occasional misuse of iPads during lessons,
      the school will continue to enforce clear usage rules next year to promote responsible and
      focused use of technology in the classroom.

Target 2: To facilitate students to set and achieve their learning goals

The school continued to support students in setting and achieving their learning goals. The
class streaming methods in junior forms were maintained and continued to cater for students’
diverse learning needs. Differentiated learning materials and assessments remained a key
strategy for promoting self-directed learning and cater to diverse learning abilities.
Supplementary lessons and study groups were arranged to enhance academic performance,
while additional support was provided to low achievers to help them progress. New strategies
were introduced to guide students in understanding their learning progress, encouraging self-
reflection and goal-setting. Students’ academic achievements and improvements were also
recognized to foster motivation and a positive learning culture.
1. To review and revise the class streaming methods of junior forms
   • This year, the school continued to implement the class streaming method adopted in the
      previous year for all S.1 classes. The arrangement aimed to better cater to learner diversity
      and foster a more positive learning atmosphere within each class. The Academic Committee
      evaluated the effectiveness of this strategy through teacher feedback and analysis of
      examination performance. Findings indicate that this class allocation strategy has been
      effective in supporting student learning and enhancing the learning atmosphere.
   • To assess the effectiveness of these measures, we compared the performance of the bottom
      20% of junior form students in the first term examination and the second term examination
      of the 2025-2026 school year. The standard score differences for the English Language
      subject were 0.07, 0.13 and 0.18 for S.1, S.2, and S.3, respectively. Similarly, the standard
      score differences for Mathematics in S.1, S.2, and S.3 were 0.47, 0.40 and 0.40 respectively.
      These positive values indicate that students with lower academic abilities have benefited
      from small-class teaching and have shown improvement in their academic performance.

Overall Evaluation
To address the school’s major concern of enhancing students’ learning effectiveness for higher
academic attainment, a range of strategies has been implemented, with the Stakeholder Survey
results reflecting steady progress. Students’ overall perception of their learning increased from a
mean score of 3.4 to 3.5 this year, showing continuous improvement from 3.2 in 2023-2024. This
progress is encouraging as it suggests that the school’s strategies are having a positive impact.

To enhance learning effectiveness, the school has focused on equipping students with essential
learning skills. According to the Stakeholder Survey, students consistently acknowledged that
teachers often teach learning strategies such as conducting pre-lesson preparation, using concept
maps, and utilizing online resources, with the mean score remaining at 3.7 for both 2023-2024 and
2024-2025. Students’ agreement with the statement “I know how to apply learning strategies”
showed a slight increase, with the mean score rising from 3.3 last year to 3.4 this year. This modest
progress indicates that the strategies adopted are effective, and the school should continue its efforts
to support students in applying learning strategies in their studies. In addition to promoting learning
skills, teachers have consistently provided appropriate support to help students achieve higher
academic attainment. In the Stakeholder Survey, teachers indicated that they are able to provide
timely and specific feedback to help students reflect and improve, with a mean score of 4.3 in both
this year and last year. Meanwhile, students’ agreement with the statement “Teachers often provide
suggestions on my performance and let me know how to improve” increased from 3.7 to 3.8 this
year. Furthermore, teachers agreed that they adjusted teaching strategies based on students’ learning
progress, with a mean score of 4.3 — the highest-rated item in teachers’ perception on teaching in
both this year and last year — demonstrating their awareness of catering to learner diversity. These
                                                                                                     26
findings reaffirm the school’s commitment to fostering a supportive and adaptive learning
environment that empowers students to learn more effectively and perform better academically.

The school has also placed emphasis on helping students set learning goals and build confidence.
The Stakeholder Survey results reflected improvements in goal setting and perseverance among
students. The mean score for “I know how to set my learning goals” rose from 3.2 to 3.4, while the
mean score for “I try my best to overcome learning difficulties” increased from 3.6 to 3.7. Teacher

to overcome learning difficulties” rising from 3.6 to 3.7. Recognizing that confidence in learning is

among students. The Stakeholder Survey results show a slight increase in both student and teacher
responses. Students’ mean score for the statement “I am confident in learning inside and outside of
the classroom” rose from 3.1 to 3.2, while teachers’ mean score for the statement “My students are
confident in learning” increased from 3.2 to 3.3. Although confidence in learning remains one of the
lowest-rated items in the Stakeholder Survey, the improvement is a positive sign. The school will
continue its efforts to help students set learning goals and boost their confidence in order to enhance
their academic performance.

identified, along with insights gained from the planning, implementation, and evaluation

Facilitating Factors:
A. Strategic use of school resources and administrative support:
    Supplementary lessons, study groups, and tutorial classes have provided targeted support,
       especially for DSE candidates and junior form low achievers.
    The S.1 Summer Orientation and English Enhancement Programmes have effectively

    Split-class and small group teaching arrangements, such as the English remedial group in
       S.4 and smaller class sizes in S.1, have provided better support for students with diverse
       learning needs.

      Elements of national security education and values education have been integrated

       effectively nurturing students’ national identity and positive values.
      Differentiated learning materials have been incorporated into the curriculum of various
       subjects to support students with varying needs and abilities.
      Cross-subject collaboration in curriculum design and assessment has been promoted to
       enable students to make meaningful connections across subjects.
      A wide range of life-wide learning activities have been arranged to extend students’ learning
       experiences beyond the classroom.

C.    Teachers’ professional competence and commitment to student learning:
      Teachers have demonstrated increasing awareness of learner diversity and have adapted
       their teaching strategies to meet students’ varied needs.
      Teachers are proficient in using e-learning tools to facilitate interactive learning and provide
       supplementary materials that support self-directed learning.
      Teachers consistently encourage students and help them build confidence through positive
       reinforcement and personalized guidance.

D.    Students’ capability and positive learning attitudes:
      Students are familiar with e-learning tools and online platforms, which support interactive
       learning and self-directed learning.
      Most students, especially high achievers, demonstrate a positive attitude towards learning.
       They follow teacher guidance and complete assigned tasks with diligence and consistency.

Hindering Factors:
A. Limited resources for expanding targeted support:
    Split-class teaching (e.g. remedial and pull-out classes) and tutorial classes have proven
     effective, but the school lacks sufficient manpower and resources to extend these
     arrangements to more students in need.
    Some workshops and talks (e.g. study skills, digital ethics) were conducted by external
     organizations, and it is difficult to ensure consistent quality and alignment with students’
     needs.
    The number of suitable service providers remains limited, leaving the school with few
     options when arranging learning programmes, particularly in areas such as study skills
     development.

B. Challenges for teachers arising from learner diversity
   The wide range of learner diversity poses challenges for teachers. While self-directed
    learning is promoted, many students still require close guidance and regular monitoring.
    Teachers must frequently review and revise teaching materials to cater to diverse learning
    needs.
   Student motivation remains uneven, especially among low achievers. Teachers need to
    explore effective strategies to help students set achievable goals, recognize their progress,
    and boost their confidence.
   Teachers need to go beyond supporting low achievers and proactively identify and nurture
    gifted students through enrichment opportunities.

C. Students’ learning weaknesses
   Some low-achieving students lack motivation and initiative, which limits their engagement
    with learning tasks and self-directed activities.
   Despite the availability of digital tools (e.g. iPads) to support learning, some students
    struggle to stay focused and use them appropriately, which affects their concentration and
    learning outcomes.
   Many students have a weak language foundation and lack effective learning skills, which
    makes it difficult for them to grasp subject content and apply appropriate learning strategies.
   Students’ interest in reading remains low, with limited participation in reading activities and
    underutilization of reading resources such as classroom reading corners.

3.2 Integrated Education and Multiple Intelligence Education
<a> The Formation of the Integrated Education Committee
     The Integrated Education Committee (IEC) was formed in September 2003 to oversee all
     matters pertaining to the implementation and promotion of Integrated Education (IE) in the
     school. Its function is to help the students with special educational needs (SEN) to solve
     problems and/or handle difficulties in their everyday school life, especially in the social,
     behavioural and learning aspects. Special Educational Needs Coordinator (SENCO) and
     Special Educational Needs Support Teacher (SENST) were assigned in 2019/2020 to lead
     the IE Committee in planning, coordinating and implementing the Whole School Approach
     to IE in order to further cultivate an inclusive school culture and enhance the effectiveness
     of the support given to students with SEN.
<b> Case Conference with Paramedics
     In August 2025, before the beginning of this academic year, the IEC had a discussion with
     the resource teachers of Ebenezer School and the SEN Coordinators of primary schools
     about certain SEN students. Those teachers gave the IEC members essential information as
     to the handling of those cases and made a number of valuable suggestions for the school.
<c> Integrated Education Ambassadors
     The IEC recruited 18 IE ambassadors (IEA) to assist the SEN students in adapting to school
     life. They are primarily concerned with the promotion of a caring school culture. This year
     they assisted the committee in organizing parent-child activities during Parent's Day and
     helped organize activities that aimed to raise awareness of the challenges faced by people
     with different needs. Students engaged in lectures, displays, and booth games that promoted
     acceptance and support.
<d> School-based Training Sessions for Teachers
     The IEC organised school-based training sessions on Integrated Education for teachers who
     were directly involved in the teaching of the SEN students in early August 2025. The special
     educational needs of each student and the related pedagogy were made known to the
     teachers. Most teachers found the suggestions made by the IEC practical.
<e> Individual Education Planning Conferences
     The IEC held 10 Individual Education Planning Conferences for 6 SEN students in the last
     academic year to review their academic progress, reduce their examination pressure and
     improve their social skills at school. Ideas were fruitfully exchanged between the
     participants of the conferences.
<f> Special Examination Arrangements
     Examination time was extended for the visually-impaired students, attention deficit
     students, autistic students, students with special learning difficulties, and students with
     metal health problems. For the students with hearing impairment, their marks in the
     listening and speaking papers were adjusted so as to encourage them to take the
     examinations.
<g> Caring Week
     Caring Week was held from March 23 to March 27. An array of activities was organized
     by the IEC, including a song dedication activity, a disabilities quiz booth, experiential
     activities, and sharing by Wong Ming Yan, a visually impaired singer. Through these
     activities, students not only learned about the barriers faced by others but also experienced
     their challenges, fostering empathy and promoting a caring culture at school.
<h> Stress relief activities for students
     The committee collaborated with the school social workers to provide students with various
     activities to distress on different days, including board games, handicraft making, darts etc.
     The Salvation Army the Playground Association, PS33 and chill lab were also invited to
     promote their activities at our school. All students were welcome to participate. The
     activities were very popular among students. Through participating in different activities,
     it was hoped that students could relieve their academic pressure.
