                                        Our School

1.1 School Mission
    It is our belief that our students are created in God’s image and they are unique, respectable
    and valuable. Therefore, it is our mission to educate our students to have respect for life as
    well as develop their abilities and potential given by God. We believe that education is a
    means to practice the Christian faith. Based on the love of Christ, we are dedicated to educate
    our students with love and care.

1.2 Mission Statement
    Our main objective is to promote self-esteem, passion for life, concern for others, social
    responsibility and lifelong learning among students with an emphasis on virtue, wisdom,
    physical development, social ability, aesthetic appreciation and spirituality. To achieve the
    objective, our school strives to reinforce the foundations of “knowledge”, “character and
    value” as well as “skill and quality” for our students. The following mission statement, in
    line with our belief and aspiration, serve as a blueprint for the long-term development of
    our school. “Based on the teaching of the Bible and through the practice of love, we are
    committed to create a learning environment filled with creativity, initiative, care and trust.
    We aim to help students build harmonious relationship with others, and to develop their own
    unique potential in the areas of intellect, character and temperament. We aspire to cultivate
    young people with a sense of responsibility, rationality and affection, who pursue excellence
    in life with vision and passion.”

1.3 Introduction
    ECF Saint Too Canaan College, a Christian co-educational secondary school joining DSS,
    commenced operation in September 2003, with the aim of providing quality Christian
    education for students. With a millennium school campus in Kwun Tong, our school
    provides a good learning environment with comprehensive facilities for our students, which
    include 33 classrooms, 4 laboratories, 1 STEM room, 1 computer room, 7 special rooms,
    library, English Corner, health center, 1 basketball court and 1 football court. Moreover, our
    school has set up a Campus TV Studio in order to provide a platform for students to develop
    their oral training and creativity.

   During the summer, a renovation of new Learning Resources Centre has started. Hopefully
   it would be more convenient for students to borrow learning resources and to promote
   learning atmosphere. Moreover, major renovations, including the restoration of classrooms,
   in hopes of providing students with a better learning environment.

(2) Achievements and Reflection on Major Concerns; Feedback and
    Follow-up
    Priority Task 1: The major concerns were discussed in the School Administration Committee
    meeting. The following two major concerns were adopted for 2024-2027, School 3-year
    Development Plan:

    Major Concerns
    1. To foster the development of positive values and promote the holistic well-being of students.
    2. To empower students’ uniqueness.

    Major concern 1: To foster the development of positive values and promote
    the holistic well-being of students.
    1.1 To empower students to cultivate a growth mindset.
    1.2 To inculcate a constructive learning attitude among students.
    1.3 To strengthen the integration of Christian principles into the educational experience.
    1.4 To bolster students' sense of national identity and civic responsibility.
    1.5 To nurture students with STCC's core values (Respect, Responsibility, Resilience).

 Achievements
 Achieved.

 In respect of students’ well-being, in light of the environment as a whole, the emotions of
 urban residents deserve attention. Therefore, Major Concern 1 focuses on students' well-being.
 Schools emphasizes students' rest balance and stress relief.

 This year, the first two weeks of September was Transition Week, during which students only
 attended half-day classes to help them adapt more easily to the new school year.

 Additionally, activities/venue for students’ mental, physical, and spiritual well-being, such as
 Healthy Soul Day, counseling services, Soul Place and social workers’ support were organized.

 The school also provided talks related to parents regarding emotional education. Additionally,
 in courses such as OLE, several experiential activities have been newly introduced, such as
 Canaan Coffee House and Human Library, aimed at enhancing the mental health of S4 students.

 Moreover, in April of this year, the school organized the Life-wide learning programme. For
 junior students, it focused on local activities such as camping, while for senior students, it
 offered opportunities for overseas exchanges, promoting relationships between teachers and
 students as well as integrating new students. OLE Day with different activities was organized
 in recent years.

 In response to Target 1.1 (growth mindset), as reflected from the survey for teachers, more
 than 95% of teachers agreed that they had instilled a growth mindset in students through their
 lessons and curriculum.

 In response to Target 1.2 (constructive learning attitude), according to the survey for
 teachers, more than 70% of teachers agreed that effective learning strategies across the
 curriculum have been implemented.

In response to Target 1.3 (Christian principles), this year, there has been hymn-singing
during Morning Assemblies. On Tuesdays and Thursdays, prayers have been led by teachers
and students. Moreover, before Revision Test week and examinations, CMT team were singing
songs and delivering small gifts for encouragement.

The Gospel Week was highly successful, resulting in 90 students accepting Jesus as their
personal savior. Gospel Café and the teacher-student mini concert were highly popular.

In response to Target 1.4 (national identity), a Value Education Day assembly was organized
focusing on empathy and responsibility. Water Rocket Competition was held, enhancing
students’ understanding of China's aerospace technology. Weekly flag-raising ceremonies were
held. Moreover, APASO III data showed students’ national identity recognition was a strength
(P-score around 85).

In response to Target 1.5 (STCC's core values), volunteer service activities fostered social
responsibility and allowed students to experience the joy of helping others. The Students Union
(SU) and Four Houses operations demonstrated satisfactory performance (SU Rating 5.3;
Houses Rating 4.7). Committee members showed enhanced leadership abilities (over 85%
positive response).
Reflection
A holistic approach has also been adopted to promote positive values and well-being of
students. Most teachers agreed that students have ample input on Christian education while
value education has carried out through both the curriculum design inside the classroom and
activities outside the classroom.

With a more well-established class structure set, teachers are more capable of handling and
teaching different levels of students. Teachers are committed and working hard to inculcate a
constructive learning attitude among students. Though there is challenge of learning diversity
has become more obvious and complicated as their differences are not only shown in terms of
learning capacity but languages and cultures.

Teachers perceived that students showed eagerness in participating in activities, understood
their national identity, and showed more empathy. APASO data confirmed strengths in
students’ honesty (P-score ~80) and national identity recognition (P-score ~85). Though the
sense of belonging and class cohesion remain low post-COVID may be a challenge.

 Feedback and Follow-up

 A holistic approach will be continued to be adopted in the coming year to inculcate positive
 values to students. The school will focus on unity and commitment, two out of twelve values
 promoted by EDB. These two values should be instilled to students through education in lessons
 and being the themes of activities outside the classroom.

 The school will focus on value education Theme: the development of responsibility and
 perseverance. To increase the frequency of dedicated Form Teachers Lessons for value
 education. Boosting Well-being and 3Rs by strengthening support programs for emotion
 control, self-discipline, and resilience.




Major Concern 2: To empower students’ uniqueness.
     2.1 To build self-efficacy in students.
     2.2 Adopt an evidence -based approach to inform our curriculum, instructional planning
         and assessments.
     2.3 To unlock students’ reading potentials.
     2.4 To create a wide variety of opportunities for students to showcase their unique talents
         and strengths.

 Achievements
 Achieved.

 In addition to pursuing academic achievement, our school equally values holistic development.
 This year, there is a focus of fostering students' uniqueness, including both academic and non-
 academic development. Our school emphasizes the importance of building students' confidence
 and enriching their lives with meaningful experiences.

 In response to Target 2.1 (self-efficacy), good work from students was displayed on both the
 Info Day and 1st Parents’ Day. This built their self-efficacy through boosting their confidence
 and intrinsic motivation.

 In response to Target 2.2 (evidence -based approach in instructional planning), our school
 is currently applying for the IAL curriculum. Detailed planning on curriculum and assessments
 has been carried out. The practice of holding S.5 counseling sessions after the Mock Results
 Release was effective for teachers to carry out evaluation and discussion based on students’
 performance.

 In response to Target 2.3 (reading potentials), this summer, our school has been working on
 renovations of learning resources centre. It will be moved to 1/F for students’ easy accessibility.
 Moreover, reading activities were run. Good books were recommended by teachers in Chinese
 and English lessons.

 In response to Target 2.4 (showcase talents), the school organized various activities for
 students to showcase their talents and learning achievements, such as Canaan Show, L&T and
 SD Assembly. Singing Contest was held twice a year, and this year's Teacher-and-Student

competition was particularly exciting, with teachers playing an indispensable role. According
to the survey for teachers, more than 95% of teachers agreed that ample opportunities are
created for students to showcase their talents.

This year, participation in the Sports Day reached an all-time high, with students breaking 20
records.

A total of 63 societies/school teams/service groups and interest classes were offered. The ECA
rating was 4.9 (success criteria >3.5). Over 74% of students reported being able to develop
potential through ECAs.

As revealed from students’ stakeholder survey, 56% of students agreed that “through the
activities the school provides , they can develop interests and life skills”.

Reflection
In general, the awareness among teachers to cater for learning diversity so as to allow individual
students to shine has been stronger this year. The award system has been refined to enable
students to be awarded with conduct marks more easily while students have opportunities to
showcase their abilities, both academic and non-academic. In terms of summative assessments,
it is believed that a lot more can be done to distinguish the learning capacity of students more
effectively though.

APASO reported low scores in “Satisfaction” (P-scores ~20). Students generally lack
confidence, especially those less academically able.

Feedback and Follow-up

More attention should be paid to learning across the curriculum. Project work, e.g. the
redevelopment of the Choi Hung Estate will be carried out in junior forms. It involves not just
the teaching inside the classroom but the activities outside the classroom as well.

To work more effectively on catering for learning diversity, the teaching team has been advised
to set higher goals for students especially the high achievers. It can be achieved by modifying
the curriculum so as to raise the awareness of teachers and highlight the importance of catering
for the needs of higher achievers and to enhance students’ breath of knowledge when it comes
to lesson observations.

In order to boost students’ confidence and build their self-efficacy, to refine the 'Yellow Paper'
Reward Scheme by increasing credit value (2 slips = 1 credit). Implement strategies to publicly
recognize student achievements and efforts outside the classroom.

(3) Student Performance
3.1 Students’ academic performance (KSH)
   We focus on training students to have intrinsic motivation for learning. For junior forms, we
   emphasize increasing their interest and enriching their learning experiences both inside and
   outside the classroom. It is inevitable for students to face real-world challenges and keen
   competition when they are promoted to senior forms. Our teaching team works diligently to
   shape and prepare them for the HKDSE so that they can meet the Hong Kong standard. It is
   true that there is still room for improvement in some subjects, however.

   We have seen significant improvements in most subjects for the HKDSE. Around 90% of our
   HKDSE candidates this year received offers for tertiary studies. Among them, students who
   received UGC-funded offers were admitted to local universities like The Chinese University of
   Hong Kong and The City University of Hong Kong, while others were accepted into courses
   offered by local tertiary institutions or chose to study overseas.

   Our teaching team is dynamic and willing to adopt different approaches. The school will
   continue to explore various strategies and strengthen its policies to enhance teaching
   effectiveness in the future.

3.2 Students’ personal growth
   Our students have satisfactory performance in physical education and arts development. They
   participated in various activities and competitions to widen their exposure. Moreover, their
   efforts to and perseverance in practicing paid off in the end and many students won different
   awards in the competitions.

   A total of 63 extra-curricular activities in our school and students have full chance to explore
   their interests and develop their potential. AIMS programmes were developed recently to
   facilitate students to devise their activities and career plan to achieve whole-person
   development. Moreover, it enables the school to further record and recognize students’
   activities and achievement in an organized manner. In the questionnaire, over 64% students
   showed they chose their ECAs according to the AIMS. About 70% students showed they can
   uphold good attitude in joining ECAs and learn some useful skills. In addition, 74% of students
   are able to develop potential through participating in ECAs.

   Different leadership training workshops and camps were held to teach the communication and
   leadership skills to student leaders. For committee members of the ECAs, over 85% students
   responded positively which showed their leadership has been enhanced.

(5) Appendix
                               ECF Saint Too Canaan College                                         Appendix I

                       2024-25 Capacity Enhancement Grant Report


 1.     Aims:
        In order to relieve teachers’ workload, 2 Teaching Assistants are employed with the use of
        the Capacity Enhancement Grant in 2024-25.

 2.      Major duties of TAs

        2.1.    KLA administrative work (lesson material preparation, KLA activity coordination,
                taking KLA meeting minutes, etc)
        2.2.    Administrative and clerical support (taking meeting minutes, statistical analysis for
                surveys, administrative work for the Learning & Teaching Department and Student
                Development Department, etc)
        2.3.    Lesson substitution (Total no. of substitution periods: Around 155 periods)
        2.4.    Exam Invigilation (Total no. of invigilation hours: Around 223 hours)
        2.5.    Other duties include school patrol, assisting in large-scale school activities (e.g.
                Speech Day, Sports Day) and external activities (e.g. Speech Festival, inter-school
                competitions)

 3.     Evaluation
        According to the statistics, it is found that about 100% respondents show a positive attitude
        towards the roles of TAs in relieving their workload. A majority of teachers agree that TAs
        can share their non-teaching duties. The statistics also show that TAs can provide necessary
        administrative and clerical support for teachers. As such, teachers agree they have more
        time for lesson preparation. From the figures, all respondents support the school to employ
        TAs with use of CEG for the coming year.
        For details of the statistics, please refer to the Appendix.


 4.     Recommendation

      The school continues to employ TAs with the CEG.

Part 1: Evaluation of the Effectiveness

1. Evaluation of achievement of the objectives: (e.g. reading culture of the whole school, students’ reading attitude, book borrowing
   situation and students’ engagement in reading activities)
   We would like to enhance student learning capacity through promotion of Reading across the Curriculum (RaC) by assigning
   extended reading tasks to students in different subjects with the Reading Booklet and Reading Time. The annual schedule of
   reading components of different subjects and activities for students has been recorded in the Reading Time schedule. However, the
   promotion of Reading across Curriculum and reading activities like visiting bookstores or public library may be restricted in the
   regular school timetable to facilitate students’ deep reading and reading skills. Paper books and e-reading of different subjects can
   be integrated to the reading booklet and students can use the booklet efficiently to review reading progresses. Reading Scheme can
   be strengthened to encourage students to finish the tasks and get the reading awards.


   Evaluation of strategies: (e.g. implementing diversified and motivating activities to promote reading, reading across the curriculum
   and home school co-operation)
   Different reading activities are held during the Reading Days. Several activities are taken place and students are satisfactory
   especially towards the book exhibition and writer’s seminar during the assembly. These popular activities are significant to promote
   reading atmosphere in the school. Various reading activities such as book exhibition, book crossing and Reading Time activities
   can be promoted in a larger extent to initiate students’ reading interest and extended reading. More students, teachers and parents
   can participate in the reading activities during the Reading Days.
