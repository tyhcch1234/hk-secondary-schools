1.   College Vision, Mission and Motto

     Year Founded: 2006

     Vision: It is our vision to provide the best nurturing opportunities that unleash students’
     potential, inspire excellence and cultivate lifelong learners and leaders of tomorrow.

     Mission:
     HKFYG Lee Shau Kee College (hereafter HLC) is dedicated to creating a positive
     environment where young minds are inspired and enthused to challenge themselves
     and strive for excellence inside and beyond the classroom. We are committed to
     nurturing students to be intellectually competent, with positive moral characters,
     values, conscientiousness and physical prowess.
     Passion for learning, knowledge and the broadening of horizons are the hallmarks of
     an education in HLC. We believe all these are essential for the greater development
     of inquisitiveness, curiosity, exploratory spirits and a global outlook, alongside
     academic pursuits.

     College Motto:
     Our College motto, “Wisdom in Action (智慧之源  求實求真)” guides students
     towards a greater inquisitiveness and curiosity, not just with regard to their studies,
     but also towards life and the world around them. To actualize the College motto:
     Wisdom in Action, HLC develops students’ self-directed learning, self-discipline and
     self-efficacy in the spheres of Wisdom in Learning, Wisdom in Relationships and
     Wisdom in Living, with the cultivation of the 5 core values: Respect the others, Caring
     for others, Perseverance, Integrity and Responsibility, as the moral framework to
     mould students’ characters.

3.   Our College

     3.1 Basic Information
         We are a Direct Subsidy Scheme (DSS) school not participating in Secondary
         School Places Allocation (SSPA).

         Medium of Instruction:
         The College aims to help students become trilingual and bi-literate. All subjects
         except Chinese Language, Chinese Literature (S.4-S.6), Chinese History, Life &
         Society (S.1-S.2), Citizenship & Social Development Studies (S.4-S.5) and Liberal
         Studies (S.6) are taught in English. English is widely used in assemblies and
         College activities. Putonghua is used in Chinese Language in S.1-S.2.

         Class Structure:
         The College operates 6 levels (Secondary 1 to Secondary 6), each with a maximum
         of 5 classes. The class size on average is below 32. In the 2022/2023 school year,
         we opened up to 30 classes with a total of 722 students.

         School Fee:
         In the 2022/2023 school year, the school fee was $25,540 per annum for S.1-S.3,
         $38,310 per annum for S.4-S.6 (Local & Integrated Curriculum) and $45,000 per
         annum for S.5 (International Curriculum), which is payable in 10 monthly
         installments for S.1-S.5 and in 7 installments for S.6. We offer HLC Scholarships
         to students who attain high academic results and have good conduct, and fee

     6.2 Academic Initiatives

         6.2.1    HLC Curriculum

                  The College further developed blended learning in self-directed learning
                  (SDL).      With various staff development opportunities arranged
                  throughout the year, the College focused on supporting student learning
                  with eLearning tools. To further maximize the effectiveness, electronic
                  whiteboards were installed in all classrooms with subsidy from the Quality
                  Education Fund (QEF). BYOD (Bring Your Own Device) has been
                  launching smoothly from S.1 to S.3, and students were used to using iPads
                  in both face-to-face and online lessons extensively and skillfully. The
                  scheme would be extended to S.4 in the following year. Moreover, subject
                  departments have adopted SDL guided learning plans for at least 60% of
                  the curriculum, with core and extension parts to cater for learners’ diversity.



                  To cater different interests and needs of students, the College offered ample
                  choices in the local curriculum, including Music, Visual Arts and Literature
                  in English as HKDSE electives. For the International Curriculum, namely,
                  the International Advanced Levels (IAL) Curriculum, 20 students were
                  admitted to the Integrated Curriculum in S.4 in 2023-2024 as a bridging
                  curriculum to the IAL curriculum in S.5 and S.6. Our Mathematics and
                  Science teachers actively participated in professional development events
                  to share our experience with other DSS schools and learn from IAL teachers
                  from other schools.

        It was the 8th year that the College collaborated with the University of Hong
        Kong (HKU) to introduce the Massive Open Online Courses (MOOC) to
        students. MOOC was extensively promoted to all S.1 students. S.1 to S.6
        students who were interested in MOOC were supported by teacher mentors
        and a series of lectures in HKU. All S.3 students were required to
        complete a MOOC as their summer holiday assignment. Moreover, a new
        and unique MOOCxReading curriculum (MOOC 2.0) was implemented in
        S.4. 7 HLC students have shared with HKU professors and other
        secondary school students about their learning journey in the S.4 MOOC x
        Reading Curriculum in which English teachers connected language
        learning and global issues by supporting students in project-based learning
        about the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) of the United Nations.

6.2.2   Catering for Diversities

        Two 13-year-old S.1 students, Paul Liu and Tom Liu, following in their
        brother Peter Liu’s footsteps, have accepted the offer from the University of
        Hong Kong for early admission, due to their outstanding results in the
        International A-Level Exam. HLC has offered extra support to them for
        their whole-person development and personal growth.

        Having met the requirements of the HLC Scholar Scheme, another S.1
        student, Arnold Choi, would be promoted to S.3 as he has demonstrated
        impressive self-directed learning capabilities in MOOC and maturity, and
        obtained outstanding results in internal exams.

        To meet the needs of talented students, the HLC Scholar Scheme was
        revised to allow S.3 students who are good at Mathematics and Sciences to
        be promoted to S.5 IAL stream.

        To cater for learning diversity, teachers have been evaluating students’
        progress using assessment data.       Subject departments have devised
        strategies to help students with different needs, e.g. designing graded
        questions, discussing learning difficulties in co-planning sessions,
        arranging extended programmes for enhancement and remediation,
        promoting collaborative learning and understanding students’ needs by
        pre-tasks and post-tasks.

        Furthermore, teachers were equipped with mentoring skills to support high
        achievers and late developers in different workshops and students in need
        were well-supported by the teacher mentorship programme.

6.3 Academic Achievements

Our students performed outstandingly in inter-school academic competitions and
contests. The Public Speaking and Debating Team has won the Championships and
1st Runner-up in the Junior and Senior Divisions of the Hong Kong Secondary Schools
Debating Competition 2022/2023 Grand Finals respectively. The English Drama Club
has won a number of awards in the Hong Kong School Drama Festival, including the
Adjudicators’ Awards. We had also got a number of awards in Hong Kong Schools
Speech Festival, Mathematics, Science and Liberal Studies competitions, Sports
competitions, Music contests and Harvard Book Prize.

2023 HKDSE Results
Our S.6 students obtained very good results in HKDSE. For the four core subjects, the
average percentages of level 2 or above and level 4 or above are 99.7% and 45.7%
respectively. For all subjects, the average percentages of level 2 or above and level 4
or above are 96.6% and 42.7% respectively. Among all subjects, our students
performed particularly well in English Language, Mathematics and Chinese History,
with 100% of them obtaining level 2 or above and over 50% of them obtaining level 4
or above.

Academic Value-added Information:
English Language ranked top 4% of value-added performance among all schools in
Hong Kong for 12 consecutive years (2012-2023), outperforming all other DSE subjects
in the College. Mathematics, Core 4 and Best 5 also ranked top 4% among Hong Kong
schools.

7.   Student Development and Support

     According to the ESR Report 2020, “A comprehensive school-based framework is
     developed to promote positive education. Co-ordinated efforts are made to nurture
     students’ whole-person development through well-structured programmes. Ample
     opportunities are created to foster students as leaders of tomorrow with global
     perspectives.   Students’ leadership potential is effectively maximized through
     abundant experiential learning.”

     7.1 Discipline and Guidance

         At HLC, we prioritize promoting PERMA in Discipline and Guidance, creating an
         environment that nurtures positive emotions, student engagement, meaningful
         relationships, and a sense of accomplishment. Through various activities and
         counseling support, we equipped students with tools for overall well-being.

         To foster students' self-esteem and resilience, we have introduced mindfulness
         activities as part of our efforts during events like the Guidance Thematic Week and
         stress management workshops. These activities encompassed a range of creative
         pursuits, including alcohol ink painting, Chinese ink painting, Scratch Art,
         planetary candle making, harmonious pastels, and twist ball. Furthermore, the
         Friday Assembly incorporated a Fun Fact Quiz to engage students, and we have
         also curated a collection of Positive Psychology Books in the school library, aimed
         at promoting overall well-being.

         Students were benefited from the provision of high-quality professional
         counseling services that effectively addressed their individual emotional needs.
         Our dedicated team, consisting of two Social Workers, a Student Counselor, and
         external professionals such as Clinical Psychologists, Educational Psychologists,
         and Speech Therapists, played a crucial role in providing essential support and
         guidance to students. The School Guidance Team regularly met with these
         professionals to facilitate coordination and alignment between academic and
         guidance initiatives.      As a result, our student academic and personal
         development needs were met cohesively, fostering their overall well-being and
         enabling their success.

         Last but not least, the Teacher-Student Mentorship Programme has brought
         immense benefits to our students. All teachers became mentors, catering to
         specific needs across different areas. This program provided personalized
         support, fostering holistic development and strong teacher-student relationships.
         It has nurtured academic growth and emotional well-being effectively.

7.2 Careers and Life Planning

    The “Careers and Life Planning” programs have been instrumental in guiding
    students towards successful academic and career pathways. Through a holistic
    approach that includes self-discovery, subject selection, real-world experiences,
    and collaboration with external organizations, our students have gained the
    knowledge and skills needed to make informed decisions for their future. These
    programs have empowered students, equipping them with essential skills for their
    future endeavors.

    Junior Form Programs: The junior form programs focused on self-discovery and
    subject selection. Through activities like the elective subject fair, Wisdom-In-
    Action (WIA) lessons, and informative sessions for parents, students gained
    insights to make informed academic decisions.         The subject fair allowed
    exploration of interests, while WIA lessons and parent sessions consider
    individuality, strengths, and aspirations.     These initiatives lay a strong
    foundation for career and life planning.

    Senior Form Programs: The College collaborated with external organizations to
    provide enrichment programs for senior students. The HKPASEA conversation
    series and workplace visits offered insights into career fields, while the
    comprehensive Careers Day equipped students with practical skills and guidance
    for higher education and career decisions.

7.3 Moral and Civic Education (MCE)

    The College has conducted a thorough review and implementation of National
    Security Education (NSE) across multiple aspects, including administration,
    human resources, teaching and learning, discipline, counseling, and home-school
    cooperation. NSE was prioritized within the framework of Moral and Civic
    Education, with morning assemblies serving as a platform for students to share
    the importance of National Security, promoting awareness and responsibility.
    Moreover, S.2, S.4, and S.5 students were actively engaged in the National Security
    Quiz Competition, integrating it into their Life & Society (L&S), and Citizenship &
    Social Development Studies (CS) lessons. Additionally, a visit to the National
    Education exhibition enhanced S4 students’ understanding of Chinese culture and
    appreciation for National Education.

    MCE organized various activities and competitions to enrich student learning
    experiences. The form-based Service-Learning Program encouraged community
    engagement and fostered social responsibility and empathy. Our students
    achieved remarkable success as Champions in the “Youth Empowerment &
    Community Innovation Case Competition” and they shared their achievements
    and inspired fellow students through an activity held within the school.

7.4 Other Learning Experiences (OLE)

    The OLE Working Group provided a wide range of extra-curricular activities with
    the purpose of developing student strengths, cultivating an international
    perspective, fostering leadership skills, and promoting cohesion among HLCians.

    The College’s commitment to language learning was evident through the
    successful introduction of Spanish and Japanese programs. The second-year
    Spanish program led to 10 advanced students scheduling the DELE exam,
    validating their skills. The first-year Japanese program has also generated
    significant student interest, showcasing the College’s dedication to diverse
    language options. The College is going to offer Korean program in the following
    year.

    The Arts-in-School Partnership Programme 2223, organized in collaboration with
    the Hong Kong Arts Development Council and the College’s Visual Arts
    Department, offered students captivating artistic opportunities. In-campus
    workshops such as the Saori Class and Inclusive Dance Class provided hands-on
    experiences and opportunities to showcase their work. The Saori and Touch Arts
    Exhibition, held at our venue, exposed students to diverse creative arts, fostering
    a vibrant artistic environment. Collaborative programs with the Arts Unit
    further nurtured artistic talents and strengthened the connection with the arts.

    The Life Wide Learning Days, a widely popular program, provided a two-day
    experiential learning opportunity for students. S.1 and S.2 students participated
    in the Discipline Training Camp and Arts Day Camp, while S.3 to S.4 students
    joined the Local Day Tour and “Future Leader” program by HKFYG Leadership
    Institute. S.5 students took part in an exciting 2-day Adventure Camp. These
    activities enhanced student educational journeys, promoting teamwork,
    confidence, and exploration skills.

    The OLE working group promoted cohesion among HLCians by engaging alumni
    and former club leaders in club training. CCA clubs like Handbell Ensemble,
    Drama Club, Debate Team, Badminton Team, Soccer Team, and others benefit
    from their expertise. This approach enhanced leadership training, provided role
    models for students, and fostered a sense of inheritance and cohesion within the
    school community.

8.   Home-school Cooperation

     The College established admirable partnership with parents. It is believed that a
     harmonious relationship between parents and teachers would greatly enhance students’
     development.

     8.1 Communicating with Parents

         The College provided varied and convenient channels to facilitate home-school
         communication. Sunshine calls were done at the beginning of the term. Two
         Parents’ Nights were held to disseminate school news, update students’
         performance and collect parents’ views on school policies. Parents could also
         meet their Form Teachers during the Parents’ Day for their child’s overall
         performances in the College. The College also made very good use of information
         technology to further enhance communication with parents such as the eClass
         Parent App. The instant update of the HLC Facebook Page was welcomed by
         parents and students. This year, the HLCian Press and HLCian Link which
         provided latest news of the College and student achievements were disseminated
         to parents in “Digital” form. Thank you very much for the students’ valuable
         input, especially the Student Editors.

     8.2 The Parent-teacher Association (PTA)

         The PTA was founded in 2008. The PTA Standing Committee (PTASC) and
         parents actively participated in College activities and provided adequate support
         to the College. Regular PTA meetings held by the PTASC served as a bridge
         between the College and parents. Parents provided assistance to support the
         College, e.g. student welfare and decoration for Christmas party. Two parents
         from the PTASC were elected to be the school managers of the School Management
         Committee (SMC) and served as a link between parents and the School Sponsoring
         Body.

         was held on 30 September 2022 with our School Supervisor, Ms Helen Hsu’s
         presence. Mr Leung Wai Nok was elected as the Chairman. The PTASC
         altogether held five meetings. They organized numerous activities such as Yoga
         Class, Parents’ talk and Christmas party etc., and worked closely with the Student
         Welfare Working Group on the selection of service providers.

         Our heartfelt thanks to the PTA and parents for their unfailing support.
