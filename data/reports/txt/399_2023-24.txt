CHAPTER 1: Portrait of Heep Yunn School

School Background
By the Grace of God, Heep Yunn School is an Anglican school established in 1936, when two former Church Missionary Society institutions, namely
Fairlea School (1886) and Victoria Home and Orphanage (1887), were joined together. The name “Heep Yunn” means “united in grace” in Chinese.
Over the years, Heep Yunn has gradually grown from a Vernacular school into an English-medium school providing a high quality, all-round education
for girls in Hong Kong. In 2012, the school switched its status from a government grant-in-aid school to a direct subsidy scheme school.


The founders of Heep Yunn were committed to bringing education to young women and orphans in answer to the call of duty at a time of economic
instability and threat of war in 1930’s Hong Kong. They believed in empowering students to bring enlightenment to our nation and support to our
people. To this day, the school abides by its dedication to serve our fellow men in the love of Christ.



School Vision and Mission

It is the mission of the school to provide our students with a Christian education that develops the whole person in the following areas: moral,
intellectual, physical, social, aesthetic and spiritual; to instil in our girls a spirit of public service, and to build a firm foundation in both Chinese and
English.



School Motto

In faith and love we are committed

Campus and Facilities

Our school is located on an 11,000 sq. m. campus consisting of six building blocks, each with its unique history and purposes. These include the Lau
Cheung Suet Ming Building (1936), the Chapel of St. Clare of Assisi (1957), the Chau Hau Leung & Li Shiu Ching Building (1964), the Library (1966),
the Jubilee Building (1986) and the Mr. & Mrs. Au Sue Hung Building (2005). Additionally, our outdoor facilities include a 25-metres swimming pool,
4 multi-purpose games courts, a rest garden, a parking area and a lawn. Indoors, we have the School Hall, Dining Room, Student Activity Centre,
Student Development Centre, 30 special rooms including the Drama Laboratory, the Innovation Laboratory, and the Lecture Theatre, 30 classrooms,
and a 3-storey high sports centre.

CHAPTER 2: School Development- Review on the Annual Major Concerns

3-Year Development Goal (1)

To facilitate students’ learning through enhancing teachers’ assessment literacy.


Annual Major Concern (1)
To advance students’ learning and teaching effectiveness by enhancing the classroom assessments.


Progress and Achievements
Stepping into the second year of collaboration with the Quality School Improvement Project (QSIP) under the Hong Kong Institute of Educational
Research (HKIER) of the Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK), the School had moved from analysing summative assessment to bolstering
classroom assessments in the academic year 2023-24. As was last year, a three-tiered staff development programme was devised to achieve the goal:


The first tier involved all teaching staff in a structured professional development programme arranged by the Staff Development Committee. It
began with a kicking-off briefing, complemented by a self-learning resource pack on the art of questioning, followed by a structured peer lesson
observation exercise with the focus on questioning technique. Deep reflection and genuine feedbacks from teachers indicated that the team embraced
growth mindset to learn from each other. As opined by participants, “the lesson observation yielded success and learning opportunities, which will
inform and enrich his/her future practices.”. For instance, teachers were reminded the importance to gauge students’ understanding and learning
progress, thereby providing apt scaffolding, and build on students’ prior knowledge or zone of proximal development to promote higher-order
thinking.


The second tier concerned the middle-managers, a special workshop was held in January by the QSIP Team on the topic “Lesson observation and
post-lesson debriefing skills of subject panel heads”. The significance of panel heads as the gatekeeper to exercise instructional leadership was
highlighted. All Heads of Departments agreed unanimously that the theme of the workshop addressed his/her needs and concerns over his/her
work, and the workshop provided concrete and practical suggestions to aid their work.

Selected seed groups including the Personal, Social and Humanities Education (PSHE) Key Learning Area (KLA) and the Science and Technology
and Junior Science Departments were involved in the third tier. Focused meetings and shadowing were arranged by the QSIP team. The strategies to
promote cross-curricular project and to develop school-based curriculum were discussed. To consolidate their experiences, promote the sharing of
best practices and enhance professional communication, the pioneer groups shared their insights gained in an end-of-term experience-sharing session
in August, guided by the QSIP, for all teaching staff. The presentations were delivered with clarity and openness, focusing not only on the success but
also reflections on their limitations and possible way-outs. Survey revealed that 98.7% of respondents agreed that the sharing by selected departments
improved their understanding of the related topic; 97.4% of participants agreed that the strategies and practices introduced by their colleague(s) were
stimulating and thought-provoking; 97.4% of them thought that the sharing by their colleague(s) could facilitate professional development on subject-
departmental/ school level. Overall, the primary goal of building teachers' capacity for the effective use of assessment data with the focus on
enhancing classroom assessments was by and large achieved.


Reflection
Leveraging support from the Quality School Improvement Project (QSIP) of the Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK), our dedicated team
of teachers advanced from mastering summative assessment data to enriching classroom assessment practices this year. Through a renewed,
structured and focused peer lesson observation initiative, our teachers capitalised from each other’s unique strength, co-creating a collaborative
learning environment.


However, some participants expressed the view that while the intensive professional development programme was meaningful, it required a substantial
time commitment. Given the demanding schedules of teachers, they found it challenging to engage in more authentic interactions with students and
to provide tailored support to each individual, particularly when they had spent more time to pen the reflection gained.


Future Planning
The above strategies are in alignment with the promotion of the “seven learning goals of secondary education” advocated by the Education Bureau,
in particular with the pursuit of “breath of knowledge”, “language proficiency” and “generic skills”. Instead of exploring assessment literacy in the
co-curricular programmes as originally stated in the school development plan, the School has fine-tuned the major concern to “promoting quality
formative assessment and adopt diversified assessment modes across various subjects” for the third year. This change was intended to keep the good
momentum in the academic departments in advancing different forms of assessment. Having delved into summative assessment and classroom

3-Year Development Goal (2):

To cultivate positive values among students and nurture them into becoming responsible citizens with the commitment to serve.


Annual Major Concern (2):
To promote students’ understanding of our community and our country’s development and nurture them into becoming responsible citizens.


Progress and Achievements
Heep Yunn is committed in building lasting characters and mindsets among students. Cultivating proper values among students through both regular
lessons and outside classrooms has consistently been a priority for the School.


In 2023-24, value education maintained its vigor across all departments, with a sharper focus on national education. A whole school approach was
adopted to the launching of school-based national education framework, in which all subjects infused elements of national education or national
security education in the curriculum. This was further amplified in the national education fortnight spanning late February and early March. The
following are some selected inter-department measures adopted: Form Two students were guided to create flour figurines during their Visual Arts
lessons, drawing from a character they learnt in the school-based Chinese curriculum. In Form Three, a series of “Chinese Literary Walks”, jointly
organsied by the Chinese Department and the Chinese History Department, took place in Tokwawan. Through comprehensive reading and guided
writing, teachers led students outside the classroom to experience community culture and learn about our heritage and history. This enhanced their
understanding of social and national development, fostering a sense of belonging to our country. Survey results indicated that 95.6% of students felt
their understanding of the community’s history and development had deepened, while nearly 90% expressed greater concern for their community.
They also reported a willingness to contribute to community development and environmental conservation. As for Form Four, students explored

beginning to yield positive results. Meaningful collaboration between departments has effectively promoted national education, linking regular lessons
with authentic experiences beyond the classroom, thereby providing students with optimal opportunities to explore the beauty of Chinese culture.

Reflection
With the hardware enhancement, our girls had also made shown improvements in their soft skills. Although the procured tools may become obsolete,
we were pleased that students were equipped with future-proof skills, including critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, innovative thinking,
active learning, adaptability, and effective teamwork. These skills had been fostered through the STEM-related curriculum and activities conducted in
the Innovation Laboratory.


Recognising the importance of keeping pace with the latest scientific developments and technologies, our school provided comprehensive support
for students participating in STEM-related competitions. Each year, the school’s science teachers encourage students to form teams for these
competitions and offer funding to acquire the necessary equipment and chemicals. Each participating team is assigned one to two science teachers
for guidance, along with laboratory technicians who provide technical support. Furthermore, the school values the concepts of “sisterhood” and
“mentorship” in promoting STEM development, encouraging experienced senior students to share their knowledge and guidance with new teams,
thereby fostering mutual learning and exchange. This year, we were happy to see the scope and intensity of STEM exposure and experience for
students had been expanded with the establishment of the Innovation Laboratory. While we have provided a more conducive environment for
learning for students, and witnessing the increased demand for STEM related training, however, the number of STEM teachers remained stable. To
further enhance STEM development, additional resources and opportunities should be allocated to enable them to elevate STEM initiatives to new
heights. If additional manpower is not feasible, outsourcing the courses might be an option.


Future Planning
As a result of the aforementioned measures, the “information literacy” and “generic skills” of students, as outlined in the “seven learning goals”
proposed by the Education Bureau, have been significantly enhanced. From prior experience, we observed that offering hands-on and maker
experiences for students in learning STEM-related subjects is essential. Therefore, the school will organise a whole-school STEM-based Fun Fair in
the upcoming academic year, allowing all students to integrate what they learn in STEM lessons into their class stalls. Building on previous successes,
we will intensify our efforts to encourage students to participate in more STEM-related competitions and activities, along with providing additional
opportunities for students to showcase their inventions and research achievements on campus. It is hoped that STEM education will not be confined
to a select few but will be widely disseminated throughout the school.

Students’ Academic Performance

Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education Examination (2024)
Among the 154 Form Six students who took the 2024 Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education (HKDSE) Examination, 33 of them had
