School Vision

The vision of our school is to nurture future leaders with the following qualities:

- good communicative skills.

- knowledge breadth, critical thinking, and good writing skills.

- an international perspective.

- an understanding of Chinese culture, history, and heritage.

- positive attitude and values.



School Mission

The mission of our school is to nurture talents and future leaders for society. Our education

objectives are love, creativity, and task commitment.

(2) Achievements and Reflection on Major Concerns

Priority Task 1:     Enhance students’ life skills through diversified learning experiences

Achievements
Our school open and provid diverse learning experiences for students to enhance their life skills
through competitions in different disciplines and innovative curriculum in the Gifted and
Talented Programs. Geography department encourage students to engage in different
environmental competitions to enhance their awareness and knowledge in environmental
education. In the “Blue-Green Partnership Programme Water Wise Student Ambassador
Scheme”, 2 students were awarded “Gold Award”, 3 students were awarded “Silver Award” and
2 students were awarded “Bronze Award”. The school was awarded a bronze award. A student
obtained a Merit award in the “Building the Islands of the Future Design Competition Study on
the Artificial Islands in the Central Waters.
6 G8 to G11 students participated in a very prestigious annual competition “Hong Kong Young
Writers Awards” which foster excellence in creativity. 2 students were awarded a Winner prize
in the Bauhinia Club Non-Fiction and Bauhinia Club Poetry respectively. 2 students were
awarded Honourable Mention in the Creative Writing Fiction and 2 students were shortlisted in
the Creative Writing Fiction.
3 G11 and G12 students were awarded Merit Awards in the mediation essay competition 2022
organized by the Department of Justice, the Education Bureau and Asia Conflict Resolution
Institute Limited. The competition aims to raise awareness on the use of mediation in daily life
to solve conflicts through essay writing.
In November, 10 G11 students participated in an annual climate change Model United Nations
forum “Good Cop Bad Cop” to give a presentation on climate change from their experience in
Hong Kong and to debate under the role of UK delegates.
In December, two students from Green Committee joined the Blue Society Summit (2022)
organized by the Chinese Manufacturers’ Association of Hong Kong to discuss the major
concerns of marine eco-systems with representatives from different countries such as Australia,
Fiji, Mexico, South Africa, etc. Also, 33 students participated in the Tchoukball Geneva Indoors
2022 which was held in Geneva and the teams won “1st runner-up” in M12 group, “Champion”
and “2nd runner-up” in M14 Boys, “1st runner-up” in M14 Girls and “1st runner-up” in M18.
Moreover, our school’s Economics students have achieved remarkable success in the
International Economics Olympiad Winter Challenge 2023, winning a total of 19 medals
including 2 gold medals, 7 silver medals and 10 bronze medals.

In April, 9 students joined the Greater Bay Area Youth Exchange Program in Guangzhou to
visit universities and firms to learn the latest technological developments of the area, exchange
with students in Guangzhou and went to cultural heritage sites.
Different project-based learning activities in different subjects were provided for students to
develop their social skills, generic skills and self-directed learning. In the Consumer Culture
Study Award (CCSA) co-organized by the Consumer Council and the Education Bureau since
1999, students needed to select a local consumption topic they were interested in and conduct
in-depth studies and submited reports on selected consumer behaviours, attitudes and values. A
team of 5 G12 students was awarded “Second runner-up” in the competition. In a project study

competition “將軍 O 的足跡” which promote the history and culture of Tseung Kwan O

District through various activities, and promote the concepts of caring for others and protecting
the community to young people, a team of 4 G11 students obtained a Merit award and creativity
award.
International Exchange Programmes allow students to exchange knowledge and culture with
global peers. This year, several online and physical international exchange activities were held.
12 G8 and G9 students participated in the online exchange programme co-organized by High
School Affiliated to Beijing Institute of Technology (BIT) and our school in September to allow
students to explore the local culture and experience the teaching style of the two regions. A
variety of online lessons, including discussions, online competitions and video presentations
were conducted to connect students with their peers in Beijing. Besides, a team of 3 students
participated in the international student conference organized by Ritsumeikan High School in
Japan to learn of worldwide food problems and solutions with global peers. In February, 10
students joined the GTC-KVIS exchange programme to go to KVIS, a leading science high
school in Thailand. In July and August, a month-long Australian Immersion program was held
for 80 selected G7 to G10 students to live with Australian host families, attend school with local
buddies and explore different areas of wildlife and nature in addition to taking classes taught by
Aussie teachers. This was a great chance for students to learn about the Australian culture while
improving their English language skills. In the end of April, the GTC-MWIT- and -KVIS
exchange programme was held. In a week-long visit, our students brought their Thai buddies to
different excursion activities in addition to the normal classes in school which made a rewarding
journey for Thai students.

The 2022 Gifted and Talented Programs (GTP) including Club & Interest (C&I), Talent Class
(TC), Elective Day (ED) and Sports Friday form a crucial component of the curriculum,
allowing students to pursue different disciplines, STEM and Third Languages, arts and sports
with an integration of the leading and innovative learning elements into the contents. Nearly all
the students were matched with their top choices in each program which allow them to engage
in subjects that they truly passionate about.
The curriculum of Accelerated and Enrichment (AE) classes of all subjects including Chinese
Language, English Language, Mathematics, Science, Music and Sports was refined to
encourage the increase use of technology in learning. Authentic performance tasks such as
project video, press release, drama show, podcast, Youtube video, performances, research
projects and various competitions were commonly used as learning outcomes for student
learning.
Reflection
This year, school open and provide diverse learning experience for students both nationally and
internationally. These include different environmental competitions, creative writing
competitions, mediation competition, United Nation climate change forum, Tchoukball Geneva
Indoors and International Economic Olympiad which enhance students’ knowledge and skills
in the learning subjects. The competitions allow students to interface with global challenges,
new sports and Olympiad events and earn awards to recognise their efforts. The performance of
students were outstanding showing that the learning curriculum, supports from supervising
teachers and student efforts achieved high standards. Students also performed well in project-
based competitions in which the learning processes developed their generic skills such as
communication, team works, collaboration, etc in addition to knowledge and skills.
Furthermore, international exchange programmes were resumed both physically and online
which provided opportunities for students to have exchange with global peers. With the Third
Language courses in the GTP, students were able to communicate and learn from their global
peers. Continuous supports will be provided for students to explore and unleash their potential
in different areas. This year, a team of students joined the Greater Bay Area Youth Exchange
Program in Guangzhou to visit universities and firms to learn the latest technological
developments of the area, exchange with students in Guangzhou and went to cultural heritage
sites. More visits to different cities of Greater Bay Area (GBA) will be arranged to build up
connection between students in different GBA and to allow students to learn of recent
development of the GBA.

Priority Task 2: Cultivate students’ positive values and develop good citizenship among
                 students

Achievements
Our school organize different programmes and activities to enable students to have a better
understanding about a sense of well-being and care for others.
To develop students to be a well-informed and responsible citizen, Green Committee in school
organized several activities to promote the concept of green environment and sustainable
development, including game booths and assembly presentation. There were over 200 students
participated in the events. Green Committee also organized the Mooncake Box Recovery
Programme to promote the concept of recycling.
To develop students strong sense of belonging and positive values and attitudes, several alumni
and students activities were organized to foster the support and build networks between students
and alumni. In the end of September, careers and university consultation night was organized
for G12 students and parents. 19 alumni were invited to share their work experience and
professional development after graduation with G12 students. In January 2023, an alumni New
Year Gathering & Sports Competition for alumni students and teachers was held to build up
friendship and allow alumni to reunite in the event.
To nurture our students with an affection for our country and Hong Kong and equipped with
global perspective, an aspiring mind-set and positive thinking, activities to meet different policy
makers were arranged. In January, 10 G10 and G11 students attend a programme to have a
dialogue with Miss Alice Mak, the Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs. Besides, to deepen
students’ understanding on macroeconmic issues such as government expenditure and the
economic development of Hong Kong, all G10 and G11 Econmics students had a dialogue with
Financial Secretary Mr. Chan Mo Po at the HKFYG Building.
On the occasion of the 73rd National Day of the People’s Republic of China, our school invited
Ka Wah Tong, a senior counsel and convenor of Path of Democracy to give his perspective on
“One Country, Two Systems”. The talk gave students the opportunity to optimize their exposure
to different topics in national education and strengthen their awareness of their national identity.
In G7 Mentorship and G8 Leadership Training programmes, our school customized moral
education curriculum to focus on students’ problem solving, presentation and communication
skills. Students’ feedback were positive throughout the programmes.
To let students realize the importance of emotional health, the Moral Education Group held a
series of teaching sharing to teach students how to be a responsible citizen and develop positive
values. To foster harmonious campus atmosphere, a few teachers were dressed in various
characters, gave out candies and took photo with students in a Halloween costume wearing day.
Also, singing Contest was held in December in which both students and teachers demonstrate
their talents on the stage.

With the loosening of Covid preventive measure, more activities were held in school to foster

Priority Task 3: Develop students’ lifelong learning of STEM literacy to keep pace with fast
changing technological development



Achievements
Our school open new STEM events and competitions for students in order to allow them to
learn of new technological development and make contribution to the society. A new student-
led environmental group “Zero Plastic League (ZPL)” was launched with the vision to use
science and technology to address the plastic pollution problem. The team is consisted of 1
supervising teacher, 8 GT students and 2 students from Sing Yin Secondary School. This year,
ZPL organized a variety of activities which received many positive responses from participants
and partnership invitations. The activities including creation of a YouTube Channel to educate
the public about updated environmental issues such as UN Treaty on plastic pollution; a
webinar on 12 November with Mr. Sidhant Gupta, the founder of Clearbot and Prof. Christelle
Not, the Assistant Professor from the University of Hong Kong and our school iGEM team as
guest speakers. There were 250 students participate in the webinar. The team also hosted
sharing sessions in Sing Yin Secondary School on 26 November, in our school assembly on 23
March and in International College Hong Kong on 10 May. Besides, ZPL students organized a
course “Data Science Experience Course” to teach students how to annotate plastics from
photos collected in a dataset of plastics from an open-source tool using CVAT platform. Two
workshops were held on 7 February and 26 April and 3,000 images were annotated by student
volunteers. ZPL was invited by the Environmental Protection Department to join the “Bye Bye
Microbeads” scheme and promote online challenge for students. There were 117 students

cross-cultural exchanges in the activity.
In February 2023, three G11students joined the Thailand International Science Fair (TISF) to

Prof. Lo Yuk Lam, President of Hong Kong Bio-Med Innotech Association to deliver a talk to
share how the National Policy and the HKSAR Government Policy provide opportunities for
the development of bio-economy.

(4) Support for Student Development
Gifted and Talented Programs
Our school offers four enrichment programs to nurture the gifted.
1. Clubs and Interest Group (C & I)
C & I group aims to develop students’ potential in non-academic areas and there are 29 courses
in the group.

2. Talent Class Program
The Talent Class program aims to develop artistic ability of students which is an important
capability for the development of creativity, open-mindedness, flexibility, and aesthetic
sensitivity. There are 26 courses in the group.

3. Elective Day
The program aims to enrich students’ academic knowledge and provide hands-on experience to
their subjects of interest. There are 29 courses in the group.


4. Sports Friday
The program aims to arouse students’ interest in sports and to develop their athletic ability. There
are 32 courses in the group.


Moral education
A holistic approach of life planning education was adopted to support the whole-person
development of students. There were morning assemblies every Monday and every Tuesday for
teachers, students, and other organizations to share their personal experience, life values and
attitude. In order to help students develop a sense of belonging to the school, build leadership
skills and get along well with their schoolmates, one lesson is allocated for mentorship and two
lessons are allocated to adolescent growth for G7 to provide necessary support to students. For
G8, 2 lessons per week are allocated for the leadership training program in which external service
is provided to deliver leadership training to students. Teachers and other speakers are also invited
to deliver talks and briefing during the allocated time.

Various leadership training programs and community services were established to let students
develop their strengths, leadership, and problem-solving skills; community services to elderly
centres let students learn to care and love for others and make a contribution to the community.
Our school has built a positive and harmonious school environment by having a strong culture of
parent-school engagement. The Parent-Teacher Association (PTA) was very supportive to our
school. A number of school-wide activities were jointly organized by the Moral Education Group
and the PTA this year and there was a high number of parents and teachers participating in the
events.

School social worker
Our school works closely with the Hong Kong YWCA to support student development. The
organization appoints a social worker to our school, who provides counselling to students in need,
communicates and gives advice to students, parents and teachers, she also organizes and
coordinates the orientation camp, breakthrough camp and the leadership training programs.

Strong culture of parent-school engagement
The Parent-Teacher Association (PTA) was very supportive to our school. A number of school-
wide activities were jointly organized by the Moral Education Group and the PTA this year and
there were a high number of parents and teachers participating in the events. Besides the PTA
meetings, there were Parents’ Night for the effective communication of parents, class teachers,
and schools at the beginning of the academic year which was important for the holistic
development of students.

Channels of communication with parents
Our school has multiple channels to communicate with parents including the e-notice, G.T. Blog,
G.T. Newsletter, class representative communication, telephone conversation, e-mails, and the
WhatsApp group.

Financial Assistance
The school provides a financial assistance scheme for needy students. This year, 7.1% students
were eligible for the assistance.
