A. SCHOOL VISION, MISSION & OBJECTIVES

Vision and Mission

1.1 St. Francis Xavier’s College shall operate as a “Catholic” school. Its “Catholic” identity shall be
recognized in so far as it conforms with the criteria set by the Catholic Church [see Canon 803 §§1-3 and
related Canons 804-806 of the Code of Canon Law (promulgated in 1983), quoted in the Appendix; also
refer to Vatican II, Declaration on Christian Education (28 October 1965), 8-9; Congregation for Catholic
Education, Instruction “The Religious Dimension of Education in a Catholic School” (7 April 1988)].
With a determination to carry on the contribution of the Catholic Church to education, the School, as its
vision and mission, shall uphold and pass on the following core values to young people to prepare them
properly for their life and future responsibilities –
1.1.1 Truth: It is what the human intellect is searching for –
         (a) Human reason’s capacity for truth must be upheld, and the desire for truth, especially the truth
         about God and about the meaning of life, must always be encouraged and kept alive.
         (b) Wisdom, which enables a person to distinguish right from wrong, and good from evil, must be
         treasured above all other kinds of knowledge.
         (c) Honesty demands that a person tell the truth and put it into practice, even at the cost of making
         a great sacrifice.
1.1.2 Justice: It is the moral virtue that consists of a constant and resolute will to give to God and
         one’s neighbours their due –
         (a) Justice towards God is called the “virtue of religion”; and justice towards one’s neighbours
         disposes one to respect the rights of others and to establish in human relationships the harmony
         that promotes equity with regard to individual persons and to the common good.
         (b) Human dignity can be protected and promoted, and the wellbeing of society can be achieved,
         only if human rights are respected and individuals undertake their responsibilities for one another,
         for their own families, and for society.
1.1.3 Love: It is the greatest of all virtues –
         (a) God, the source of life and goodness, has created everything out of love, and has called the
         whole human family to be His children. As a member of God’s family, one’s goals in life are to
         share God’s happiness, to love God above all things and love one’s neighbours as brothers and
         sisters.
         (b) Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Saviour of humankind, is the model of selfless love and
         humble service to others.
         (c) The practice of all the virtues is to be inspired and motivated by love, so that all aspects of
         human life and interpersonal relationship may be bound together in perfect harmony.
         (d) Love surpasses the strict measure of justice and urges one to care for the poor and the needy,
         and to make a preferential option for the underprivileged and marginalized in society.
1.1.4 Life: It is a priceless gift from God and is sacred in itself –
         (a) Every human person is created in the image of God and has the right to life, which must be
         respected from its conception to its natural end.
         (b) In the spirit of the “Beatitudes” as taught in the Gospel, the tribulations and adversities in life
         are to be faced with serenity and hope.
         (c) Every person is entitled to have whatever is necessary for a decent and dignified existence.
         (d) Only a society which respects human life can bring happiness to all.

                                                   P. 3 of 25
1.1.5 Family: It is the basic unit of society –
        (a) Only pure love, the unreserved mutual self-giving between husband and wife, is truly
        gratifying; a happy, wholesome marriage is prepared by the practice of the virtue of chastity and
        sustained by fidelity and an indissoluble, lifelong commitment.
        (b) Inasmuch as sex is an integral part of conjugal life and has its own dignity, a balanced sex
        education must follow a holistic and in-depth approach, with emphasis on the virtues of self-
        discipline and mutual respect between a man and a woman.
        (c) Marriage is the foundation of a family; an intact and united family is a permanent support for
        husband and wife, and for parents and children, in achieving their goals in life; an intact and
        united family is likewise a most favourable setting for the upbringing of children and young
        people, and a necessary condition for the wellbeing of human society.

1.2 The School shall cultivate the above core values by –
1.2.1   upholding the time-honoured practice of respecting and collaborating with stakeholders with
        diverse backgrounds in achieving the School’s vision and mission as set out in this Article, in
        the awareness that the success of education depends on the joint effort of all parties concerned
        (refer to Canon 796§2 and Canon 800§2 of the Code of Canon Law, quoted in the Appendix);
1.2.2   providing a family environment imbued with mutual trust and love in the School; and
1.2.3   incorporating in the formal school curriculum Religious Education courses designed by the SSB
        (as defined herein), and fostering a Catholic spirit through religious practices held regularly in
        the School, such as morning prayers and religious activities held regularly for staff and
        students.

1.3 The School was established by “The Visitor in Hong Kong of the Institute of the Marist Brothers of
    the Schools” whose founder St. Marcellin Champagnat had passed on the following beliefs and ideals
    as an integral part of his educational vision and mission –
1.3.1      That we should make Jesus known and loved among the young and the children, especially the
           poor and the least favoured.
1.3.2      That to bring up children properly, we must love them, and love them all equally.
1.3.3      That each of our students would cherish the love of God and accordingly develop his potentials
           fully, lead a meaningful life as an individual, and take a contributing role in society.

1.4 With due regard for the core values set out in Article 1.2, the School shall also strive to put the
aforementioned beliefs and ideals into practice by inculcating in the school environment the following –
1.4.1    Opportunity be provided for the students to learn the Gospel of Christ in order to enrich their
         spiritual life.
1.4.2    Our students be nurtured with Marist Style of Education that they may get a whole-person
         education and develop their moral, intellectual, physical, social and aesthetic potentials.
1.4.3    That our students be instilled with the proper moral values so that they have positive goals of
         life, and have concern about others in the society.
1.4.4    That our students may build up with confidence, interpersonal relationship and leadership skills
         so that they are able to meet future challenges and changes.

B. OUR SCHOOL

Brief Introduction of the School

The Marist Brothers, our school sponsoring body, started the education of youth in China about 120 years
ago. One of the schools they served was St. Francis Xavier's College in Shanghai. In 1950, they came to
Hong Kong from China and gave religious instruction in St. Martin's English School. Two years later, the
Brothers were asked to take over St. Martin's. In 1954, with the help of the Education Department, new
school premises were built at the junction of Maple Street and Sycamore Street. On 9th December 1955,
classes were resumed in the new premises. At the same time, it was renamed St. Francis Xavier's College
as a token to mark the continuation of Marist education in China.

Medium of instruction

Ever since the beginning of the school, the English Language has been adopted as the medium of instruction
in our school. In March 1998, the HKSAR Government confirmed the suitability of our school to continue
using English as the medium of instruction.

Under EDB’s latest announced “fine-tuned MOI arrangements for secondary schools”, our school is entitled
to use English as the MOI until the year 2028.

C. OUR STUDENTS

Enrolment
There were 27 classes in total in the year 2024-2025. The government set the maximum numbers of students
in F.1 to F.6 to be 33, 33, 33, 33, 32 and 32 per class respectively.

Under the Voluntary Optimization of Class Structure Scheme, the School was entitled to open 24 classes, 4
classes in each level. On the other hand, the School used school funds to implement small class teaching in F.1,
F.2 and F.3, adding one extra class to each of these forms while keeping the number of enrolments unchanged.
In doing so, the School was able to reduce the class size to 24 to 28 per class in F.1 to 3. For the senior forms,
there were sufficient F.4 places to admit the whole population of F.3 students who were promoted to F.4,
excluding the repeaters.

During the school term, there were a few student withdrawals, while some new students also joined midway
through the academic year. As of the last school day (11 July 2025), the total student enrolment stood at 717.

Middle Manager Training
Over the 2024–25 school year, St. Francis Xavier’s College successfully delivered a three-part Middle
Management Capacity Building Program led by Dr. Cheung Wing Ming.

Session 1 (23 Aug 2024) focused on “How to Lead Team Members,” introducing visionary and servant
leadership, alignment with school mission and Marist values, and leadership thinking and courage. Participants
were tasked to craft and share a personal leadership vision.

Session 2 (25 Oct 2024) deepened the theme with “How to develop team members’ passion at work,”
emphasizing meaning, control, and capability as drivers of passion. Practical frameworks covered effective
delegation, feedback aligned with the P-I-M-E cycle, and success criteria for self-directed performance. Staff
collaborated in supervised groups and applied learning using iPads.

Building on this, the third session (28 Mar 2025) consolidated practice through small-group presentations of
real delegation plans, structured feedback, and whole-group Q&A, reinforcing accountability and peer learning.

All the sessions were held smoothly and the outcomes met our set objective. This series of training provided
coherent progression from theory to application. Teachers showed their active engagement in the trainings
conducted.

Teachers Development Days (TDD)
The First Teachers Development Day (25/10/2024)
Identifying and Supporting Students with Mental Health Needs
To help colleagues better understand students' learning needs, the school’s educational psychologist organised
a workshop for all teachers. Through group activities, colleagues learned strategies to address students' learning
needs, which enhanced their teaching effectiveness and fostered a greater concern for students' needs.

China’s Intangible Cultural Heritage Art and Cultural Safety
To enhance teachers' understanding of national security, our school organised a two-hour workshop for
colleagues. The theme of the lecture is cultural security, and it was conducted interactively to help everyone
gain a better understanding of the richness and intricacy of Chinese culture.

The Second Teachers Development Day (28/3/2025)
School Crisis Management Workshop
In the event of a crisis, knowing how to respond to sudden situations is crucial, and the methods and processes
for handling them are essential. Our school invited a clinical psychologist to deepen understanding of the
Education Bureau's "School Crisis Management: Handbook of aftermath intervention and psychological
support ".

Through group discussions, role-playing, and other activities, colleagues were able to better grasp the necessary
skills, ensuring they could take care of their own needs while also providing better support for various
stakeholders, such as students and parents.

The Third Teachers Development Day (26/5/2025)
Procurement Procedures in Aided Schools
Our school was pleased to invite Dr. Cheung Wing Ming to share insights on procurement procedures in aided
schools. Leveraging his extensive experience as a school manager and supervisor, Dr. Cheung addressed
potential issues that could arise during procurement processes. This discussion aimed to enable colleagues to
focus on critical details, thereby facilitating smoother workflows.

Positive Psychology through Zentangle® art
The physical and mental health of teachers is a priority for our school. To help colleagues relax amidst their
heavy workloads, we organised a workshop on "Positive Psychology through Zentangle® Art." Through
Zentangle® art, colleagues were able to find calm, focus on their drawing, and create a unique piece of art that
belongs to them. Not only did they feel relaxed during the process, but they also felt proud of their creativity.

Internal sharing：Enhancing Students’ Motivation through Catering for Learner Diversity
Enhancing student motivation and promoting self-directed learning are key priorities for our school. By
inviting colleagues to share their successful teaching experiences, teachers can adopt techniques that they can
implement in their lessons to cater to learner diversity and enhance both teaching and learning.

E. ACHIEVEMENTS and REFLECTION on MAJOR CONCERNS; FEEDBACK and FOLLOW-UP

Major Concern 1: Elevating the learning motivation of Xaverians and fostering self-directed learning to
achieve academic growth

Achievements

During the year, our subject panels made concerted efforts to enrich classroom learning by providing students
with authentic experiences, including field trips, visits, competitions, and peer learning groups. These activities
helped widen our students’ exposure and sparked their curiosity and interest in learning. It was encouraging to
learn that students enjoyed the activities provided. At the same time, teachers have become more aware of the
importance of making lesson objectives explicit, and it was observed that students became more focused and
on-task.

Goal setting has been implemented for a number of years in our school. In this 3-year cycle, we made efforts
to strengthen the practice by involving various committees in collaboration — namely AAC, LTE, and LCC.
While AAC revised the practice and provided Form Teachers with more guidelines to facilitate implementation
in their classes, LTE helped collect feedback and analyse data to gain insights for further improvement. LCC
organized activities, including talks during Form Assemblies, to expose students to different career options,
helping them make more informed choices and become clearer about their career goals. Additionally, the
application of IT in facilitating the follow-up of the goal-setting practice was piloted in two classes and is
planned to be extended to two forms in the coming year.

In this 3-year cycle, one of the strategies devised to elevate learning motivation was to provide students with
choices. Subject panels varied the number, mode, or type of assignments/learning tasks, and students valued
the flexibility given. However, whether the choices provided contributed positively to students’ learning
motivation is still yet to be determined.

Recognition of effort and improvement was also more visible. It has become more common for subject panels
to share and recognise students’ good works, and offer gifts and praise for improvements in tests and
assignments. Students, especially more capable ones, were invited to take part in various inter-school
competitions to stretch their potential and showcase their talents. It is encouraging to see that students became
more motivated in learning after these exposures.

As for professional development, LTE coordinated an important teacher training workshop on strategies to
elevate students’ learning motivation on 26 August 2024. All teaching staff agreed or strongly agreed that they
better understood the factors affecting students’ learning motivation and gained examples of effective strategies.
They reflected on their own teaching and planned to adopt various strategies introduced in the workshop. In
particular, offering prompt/timely feedback, providing learning materials at students’ levels, and adopting
group learning were the most popular strategies. In collaboration with the AAC Head, a sharing session was
conducted on the third TDD. On a 5-point scale, 96.7% of responding colleagues gave 4–5 points in agreement
that the objective of the sharing session aligned with the school’s major concern; and 96.7% agreed that the
session included practical strategies for catering to learner diversity.

The use of IT and online platforms has become more widespread, with many subjects adopting AI and
interactive apps to engage students. Peer lesson observations provided colleagues with the opportunity to learn
from one another, especially in designing lessons that highlight meaning, relevance, and achievement. Taken
together, these initiatives are believed to have contributed to a more engaging, student-centred learning
environment.

Reflection

The major emphasis for the first major concern of this 3-year cycle is to elevate the learning motivation of our
boys and foster self-directed learning. It has long been our goal to uphold students’ academic excellence as a
traditional EMI school. Subject panels were aware of the importance of elevating students’ learning motivation,
which is believed to be a means of achieving this goal. It can be learnt from different subject panels that they
                                                     P.15 of 25
had devised their subject-specific strategies in this regard.

While it is encouraging to observe, e.g. from lesson observations, that more emphasis is now placed on
elevating motivation, it still takes time for this culture to spread and create more notable impact. It is therefore
believed that we can maintain our current direction and reinforce practices through sharing good examples
during co-planning lessons, informal and formal subject panel meetings, and staff meetings in the years to
come.

Feedback and Follow-up

Building upon our achievements and learning from our reflection and evaluation, concrete follow-up strategies
are as follows:

   1. Inviting all subjects to review and enhance their strategies in providing learning tasks/experiences that
      can engage students and arouse their interest. Teachers will be encouraged to seize more opportunities
      to obtain feedback from students to ensure the learning tasks/experiences are engaging and meaningful.
   2. Continuing to stress the importance of making the learning objectives of each lesson clear and explicit
      to students.
   3. Strengthening coordination among different committees to improve the goal-setting practice —
      involving Vice Principals, AAC, LTE, LCC, and the IT Department.
   4. Requiring all subjects to keep exploring the use of technology and to provide more choices/a wider
      range of learning tasks for students to better cater to their interests, needs, and learning styles.
   5. Continuing to create more opportunities to recognise students in order to develop their sense of
      achievement — not only in academics, but also in motivation, participation, and attitude.
   6. Conducting peer lesson observations focused on raising students’ learning motivation and organising
      workshops and sharing sessions to spread good practices.



Major Concern 2: Adopting a whole-school approach to Values Education with a focus on enhancing the
self-management ability of Xaverians

Achievements

This year, we successfully launched a whole-school Values Education Framework integrating Catholic Core
Values, Marist traditions, the EDB’s 12 priority values, and our school-based theme of Family Spirit:
Commitment, Respect, and Brotherhood. This framework provided a unifying direction for our pastoral,
academic, and co-curricular work.

Whole-school assemblies were utilised to disseminate and reinforce the target values. The Principal introduced
the major concerns to the whole school at the school opening ceremony on the first school day, and the values
and performance indicators were incorporated into the admin panel’s sharing during their weekly morning
assemblies. The target values were also included in daily morning prayers led by Catholic students and teachers
in turns. A whole-school reflection on the values of the year was planned for the closing ceremonies, but
unfortunately, the session was cancelled due to the EDB’s announcement of school suspension.

Aside from these occasions, Masses, retreats, and pastoral programmes helped bring the framework to life.
Students had regular opportunities to reflect on values and experience them in practice.

Subject panels also embedded values into classroom learning. For instance, English promoted mutual respect
through debates and drama; RS deepened recognition of values through reflection and prayer; Humanities
subjects linked values to fieldwork reflections; PE promoted commitment and perseverance; and Visual Arts
gave students opportunities to express values through creative work. Our student leadership and service
flourished through the House system, student committees, and service projects, while whole-school events —
particularly those connected to the 70th Anniversary — celebrated family spirit and strengthened the sense of
belonging.
                                                     P.16 of 25
This 3-year cycle puts much emphasis on self-discipline, which includes the concept of self-respect, which
requires them to make the best use of the talents given by God, and take good care of their physical and mental
well-being. Considerable work has been done in this regard. For instance, the counselling committee has
established a team of Gatekeepers, who are student representatives promoting well-being at campus. A total of
16 wellness activities were organized during the academic year, including but not limited to wellness booths,
activities, giveaways, and exhibitions. The discipline committee has organized 6 discipline talks, each targeting
a specific form, in the first cycle, which placed strong emphasis on promoting the 3-Self Spirit (i.e. Self-respect
and respect for others, self control and self-initiative, which has been the tradition of our school. From teachers’
observation, while all students are aware of the 3-self spirit of our school, they still need to be reminded from
time to time.

In terms of professional development of values education, two subjects and two committees — namely the
English panel, Mathematics panel, LCC committee, and LWL committee — were involved in lesson planning,
observation, and evaluation processes to incorporate values education into their activities and lessons under
the guidance of the Catholic Education Office. The teachers involved agreed that while it definitely takes time
and experience, the process was fruitful.

Reflection

The implementation of our whole-school Values Education Framework was smooth, largely due to the
professional advice and support from the consultation services provided by the Catholic Education Office.
They not only guided our core team in forming our school-based framework, which will serve as a blueprint
for values education for the next six years, but also equipped the team with valuable experience in classroom
implementation.

As the support service will no longer be available, our team will need to continue the implementation
independently. Hopefully, with the experiences gained through the involvement of two subjects and two
committees, the CDC will continue its effort to spread the practices and benefit from professional exchanges
among committee members. In the coming year, the committee can further collaborate and extend the impact
to more colleagues. Sharing sessions should also be held to allow other teachers not yet involved to learn from
the experiences.

One major area for improvement identified is promotion. Even though the target values have been incorporated
in and reinforced through daily prayers and weekly sharing, there are limited opportunities for students and
teachers to visually "see" the framework around campus. More work in this area is needed in the coming year.

Support for Form Teachers can also be strengthened. Instead of solely providing documents, more elaboration
and classroom support could be offered — for example, meaningful videos promoting the values, and guided
questions to facilitate discussion and reflection.

Feedback and Follow-up

In 2025–26, we will transition from the theme of Family Spirit to a new focus on Self-Discipline. This shift
will allow us to build on past efforts while guiding students to take greater responsibility for their own conduct.
Concrete follow-up measures include:

   1. Strengthening the promotion of our school-based Values Education Framework through:

              Producing posters and presentation slides to be displayed at the school entrance and in each
               classroom throughout the year.
              Providing support for Form Teachers to consolidate the values with their classes using guided
               questions.
              Introducing the performance indicators in detail at staff meetings with reference to both Catholic
               and Chinese cultural values to build staff consensus and understanding.
              Producing an A4 folder with the target values of the year for the whole school.

            Assemblies and pastoral programmes will place more emphasis on the practical application of
             values in daily school life.

   2. Continuing to request all subject panels and committees to incorporate school-based values in their
      lessons and extracurricular activities.
   3. Continuing to organise experiential learning opportunities to reinforce self-discipline and perseverance.
   4. Organising activities to improve both students’ physical and mental health.
   5. Encouraging students to take part in competitions and activities to recognize and develop their potential.
