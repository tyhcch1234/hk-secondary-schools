   School Mission
   In pursuance of the Christian ideal of love and equality, True Light has engaged in women’s education
   with the aim of providing Christian education for the whole person and in the spirit of the school motto
   ‘Thou art the light of the world’.

   It is part of our tradition to promote among our students the importance of creativity, innovation, self-
   sacrifice, concern for others and the pursuit of excellence. At the same time, we always embrace the
   belief that ‘education is life’. Through participation in diverse school activities, our students can achieve
   balanced development in various domains, namely moral, intellectual, physical, aesthetic, social and
   spiritual.
   The following qualities are what our students strive to attain through education provided by us:
  - To have physical fitness and good health.
  - To have a spirit of endurance and perseverance.
  - To be a humble, courteous, honest and law-abiding person.
  - To be dedicated, eager to learn, prudent, critical in thinking and determined in taking action.
  - To have talents in all aspects and always work for the betterment of the environment.
  - To be equipped with professional knowledge and skills.
  - To have awareness of the needs and realities of their time.
  - To be able to co-operate with others actively for the benefit of society.
  - To be just and unprejudiced, and willing to sacrifice and serve.
  - To be able to enrich other people’s lives.

Strategy 2: Thinking Routines
Enhancement of our most commonly used strategies:
While cooperative learning allows students to address their blind spots and enrich their perspectives
within small groups—instilling confidence in their output before sharing with the wider class—
thinking routines provide structured frameworks that reduce cognitive load, preventing students
from feeling overwhelmed when constructing knowledge.
Together, these two approaches empower students to become confident, proactive thinkers.
Following our first year of implementation, we have categorized the most commonly used thinking
routines into three primary dimensions:
    ● Action Routines (e.g., Think-Pair-Share):
       These routines guide students in systematically structuring their workflow when engaging
       with an issue. They establish a clear sequence: beginning with individual reflection or
       reading (input), progressing to peer discussion and refinement in small groups, and
       concluding with whole-class sharing. This cycle continuously enriches personal thinking and
       elevates learning outcomes.
    ● Cognitive & Analytical Routines (e.g., See-Think-Wonder):
       These routines scaffold step-by-step personal reasoning. For instance, in See-Think-Wonder,
       which is commonly used across subjects, students first observe objective facts (see), interpret
       and form hypotheses based on evidence (think), and finally raise deeper questions or

                                               3
        inquiries (wonder), guiding them from simple observation to higher-order critical thinking.
        Another such routine would be Connect-Extend-Challenge used in the English Department,
        where ultimately students are to bring an impact to their communities and the world.
    ● Subject-Specific Thinking Routines:
        Tailored to discipline-specific inquiry, these frameworks cater to the unique analytical needs
        of different subjects. Here are some examples:
            o Biology: Utilize Flowcharts to map processes, systems, and logical procedures.
            o History & Chinese History: Emphasize chronological sequencing and cause-and-
                effect analysis while fostering empathy across diverse perspectives (utilizing tools like
                Circle of Viewpoints and Timelines).
            o Economics & Geography: Use tailored approaches for specialized data
                interpretation, such as efficiently decoding geographical maps and economic graphs.
            o Visual Arts: Incorporate Gallery Walk to enable peer critique, cross-learning, and
                spatial evaluation of artwork.
  Evidence of Achievement in Thinking Routines Notably, students now recognize the school's
  intent behind emphasizing these frameworks. Student application of thinking routines saw a
  positive gain, rising from 81.11% to 83.13%, demonstrating a deeper integration of self-regulated
  and collaborative learning habits across all grade levels. They demonstrated a clear understanding
  that mastering subject knowledge at this stage should move away from rote memorization toward
  a structured, systematic way of thinking.

Target 2. Cultivating students’ self-regulated learning skills and learning habits.
Strategy 1: To cultivate students’ learning habits
Recognizing that effective learning begins with systematic organization, our school has implemented
a multi-tiered approach to cultivate robust, lifelong learning habits across all form levels and
disciplines.
    ● Establishing Foundational Systems in Junior Forms For our S1 and S2 cohorts, the
        transition to secondary education requires explicit instruction in self-management. We
        prioritize routine practices, such as the systematic tidying and organization of subject
        binders. This tactile practice serves as a crucial stepping stone, instilling baseline executive
        functioning skills and ensuring students can efficiently retrieve, categorize, and utilize their
        learning materials.
    ● Subject-Specific Approaches to Knowledge Management Building upon this foundation,
        different departments have customized their approaches to align with their specific
        pedagogical objectives. By doing so, they guide students to establish structured, discipline-
        specific systems for managing their learning:
            o Metacognitive Reflection in Religious Studies: Emphasizing holistic personal and
                moral growth, the Religious Studies department utilizes reflective journaling (心靈札
                記). This practice encourages students to internalize their learning, engage in self-
                dialogue, and track their emotional and ethical development over time, thereby
                fostering a deep sense of self-awareness.
            o Knowledge Consolidation in Mathematics: To manage cognitive load and enhance
                analytical retention, Mathematics teachers actively guide students in creating
                                                4
            comprehensive chapter summaries. This trains students to distill complex concepts,
            connect interrelated formulas, and visually map out their understanding—a critical
            habit for independent revision and long-term academic mastery.
        o Cross-Curricular Organization: Across various other disciplines, teachers
            effectively incorporate the use of checklists and indexing tools. These resources serve
            as essential scaffolding, helping students monitor their own task progress, manage
            multi-step assignments, and maintain consistent, daily organizational habits.
  ● Subject-Specific Folder and Filing Management Providing a subject-specific folder with
    explicit filing management guidance is a common and highly effective strategy utilized
    across departments to cultivate students' learning habits:
        o Music: S1-S2 students were introduced to organized file management starting in
            Term 1, learning to systematically document sound exploration drafts, worksheets,
            and concert reports. This resulted in high punctuality in assignment submissions, with
            S2 students demonstrating particular diligence in maintaining class notes. Concert
            reports reflected not only proper filing but also thoughtful listening reflections.
        o Science: Assignment reviews in junior forms (S1-S2) indicated that students
            consistently kept their teaching materials neatly ordered in their Science folders,
            showing a marked improvement in their organizational skills.
        o Geography: For S1-S3, providing a dedicated folder and learning material list,
            coupled with uploading study materials to Google Classroom, proved highly
            successful. Teachers observed that most students neatly organized their notebooks,
            workbooks, and assessments for easy retrieval, developing a strong habit of
            referencing digital PowerPoints for revision. In S4-S5, the introduction of a new
            partitioned zipper folder helped most students neatly manage notes and past paper
            booklets, proving the continued effectiveness of physical organization in senior
            forms.

Evidence of Achievement in Learning Habits The effectiveness of these organizational and
metacognitive strategies is well-supported by qualitative departmental observations and
quantitative school-wide data.

In the recent Major Concern Questionnaire concerning learning habits, 86.14% of students agreed
that they have actively used the self-regulated learning strategies mentioned above to aid their
learning. This represents a positive, school-wide upward trend from 85.56% in the 2024-25
academic year. The growth is particularly encouraging among our senior cohorts; specifically, S5
students showed a significant increase in agreement from 87.06% to 92.04%.

Impact on Student Autonomy By integrating these diverse, subject-tailored strategies, we are
successfully shifting the cognitive load and responsibility of learning from the teacher to the
student. The survey data and classroom observations collectively confirm that these practices
empower students to move beyond passive reception, enabling them to build and refine their own
personalized learning frameworks. Ultimately, this strategic focus not only improves daily
academic efficiency but also cultivates the autonomy, motivation, and resilience required for
continuous, self-directed learning.

Target 3. Developing students’ reflective abilities and the capacity to improve their learning.

A critical component of cultivating lifelong learners is ensuring that students can accurately evaluate
their own progress and adjust their strategies accordingly.

 Strategy 1: To discuss relevant learning methods with students and provide them with reference
 materials and exercises, thereby broadening their understanding of these methods.
Integrating Reflection into Daily Learning Across various subjects, targeted measures were
implemented to weave reflection directly into the curriculum.
    ● Teachers actively showcased high-quality student work within classes, allowing students to
       benchmark and reflect upon their own performances.
    ● Note-taking practices were also reformed; rather than passively copying text verbatim,
       students were trained to use note-taking as an active, reflective tool.
 Furthermore, several subjects successfully fostered peer-to-peer evaluation, where students critically
 commented on each other’s work. This collective reflection prevented cognitive blind spots and
 helped students leap forward in their academic mastery.

Strategy 2: To invite students and alumnae to share their study methods at booths, allowing for
free exploration and the exchange of learning ideas.
   ●   S1 & S2 Peer-to-Peer Power-up: In Term 2, high-achieving S2 students were paired with
       S1 peers requiring targeted subject support across approximately five voluntary sessions.
       Post-activity surveys confirmed that these mentors successfully guided their S1 mentees in
       grasping core subject knowledge, while simultaneously building the confidence of S2
       mentors who initially doubted their coaching abilities.
   ●   S5 Leap to (DSE) Success: Recently graduated university alumnae returned to campus to
       conduct focused sharing sessions with our S5 cohort. Program questionnaires recorded 100%
       agreement that the initiative helped students overcome DSE learning difficulties and master
       essential exam skills.

Strategy 3: S1 – S2 Leap Higher Award Scheme
Incentivizing Active Engagement To encourage junior students to take proactive steps in their
learning, the S1 – S2 Leap Higher Award Scheme was introduced. This stamp-collecting reward
program incentivized active participation in classroom activities and responses to teachers' questions.
Reviews demonstrated that highly engaged students utilized the scheme effectively, collecting stamps
as a tangible reward for taking ownership of their classroom learning.

REFLECTION
To conclude the second year of implementation of Major Concern 1, sections under the Academic
Affairs Team as well as all subject panels have secured progress in gradually transforming students to
proactive, self-regulated and reflective learners. At the same time, such work needs to be deepened
and optimized. Evidence from stakeholder survey, APASO, Major Concern Questionnaire and
qualitative analysis all offer insights guiding this self-evaluation process. Based on systematic review
of our work, the following points to areas require further adjustments:

1.    Encouraging “Proactive Learners”: Optimizing cooperative learning strategies and
      deepening thinking routines continuously
     Building on established cooperative learning practices across all subjects, future efforts must
     focus on guiding students to recognize the value of peer feedback alongside teacher input as
     students are still reluctant to comment and respond on peers’ ideals. Furthermore, as students
     begin to understand that subject mastery requires structured, systematic cognitive processing,
     subject-based thinking routines should be sustained and deepened through habitual practice to
     make their thinking processes visible. More so, cross-subject thinking routines may also be
     adopted so as to foster interdisciplinary cognitive transfer and consolidate a holistic, school-wide
     metacognitive framework.

2.    Fostering “Self-regulated Learners”: Elevating Learning Habits and Expanding the ELA
      Structure:
     Disciplined, self-regulated learners equipped for lifelong and life-wide learning are nurtured
     through strong study habits and active participation in ELAs. On the one hand, the routine
     practice of organizing learning materials must transcend basic filing to become a meaningful,
     reflective process that actively consolidates knowledge. On the other hand, our well-
     structured framework for learning activities ensures equitable opportunities for all students to
     deepen their classroom learning in authentic contexts, while also allowing highly motivated or
                                                9
     gifted students to further apply their skills in competitive, real-world scenarios.
     Authentic off-campus competitions that extend beyond classroom instruction are an effective
     means to help students actualize their learning and visualize their achievements.
     Moving forward, this type of immersive, experiential learning must be scaled up into a
     comprehensive, whole-school cross-curricular framework. Broadening the scale and scope
     of these initiatives will make our students’ learning processes more visible and impactful, both
     on and off campus.

3.    Nurturing “Reflective Learners”: Aligning Extrinsic Learning Methods with Intrinsic
      Motivation
      Extrinsically, teachers provided explicit instructional methods and exemplars, complemented by
      subject-sharing sessions led by senior students and examination strategies offered by alumnae.
      While students acknowledged reviewing their performance and adapting their learning strategies
      based on timely teacher feedback, test scores and assessment results, overall impact can still be
      further enhanced.
     Extrinsic incentives yield limited success when implemented in isolation without cultivating
     intrinsic motivation; therefore, making learning progress visible to students is essential to help
     them autonomously drive their own development. This necessity is reflected in the limited impact
     of the Leap Higher Award Scheme on passive learners, with Major Concern Questionnaire data
     revealing that only approximately 70% of S1 and S2 students felt the extrinsic reward initiative
     meaningfully influenced their learning.

4.    Empowering “Competent Teachers”: Honing Skills in Cooperative Lesson Design and Peer
      Evaluation
     Dr. Ho’s focus group training could be expanded to a broader cohort of teachers, equipping
     them with expertise in designing cooperative learning lessons to promote inter- and intra-group
     collaboration alongside content-directed peer evaluation. Concurrently, targeted onboarding and
     tailored coaching must be established for new teachers; while they bring fresh perspectives,
     structured support is essential to ensure full alignment with the school’s overarching mission
     of nurturing confident learners.


FEEDBACK and FOLLOW-UP
 As we commence the third year of this major concern, our focus will be on optimizing the areas that
   fell short of targets, while scaling up our proven, successful initiatives.

 1. Cooperative learning and thinking routines would continue to be incorporated as a usual part
    of students’ classroom experiences to nurture them into proactive learners. Grounded in previous
    findings, focus should be especially placed on inter-group learning.
    As each subject has already identified one to two thinking routines that is deemed most effective
    for learning the respective subject matter, it is advised that those routines should be repeatedly
    used, turning them into regular practices, so students could establish that particular thinking
    habit. Useful tools would continue to be developed in each subject to facilitate these in-class

                                               10
  learning. Adopting a holistic, school-wide metacognitive framework is worth exploring to
  enhance cross-learning.

2. Seeing that some students are taking the initiative to step outside the classroom to put what they
   have learnt into actions in the real-world setting, we would like to offer more opportunities so
   that eventually this could become part of our learning culture.
   Cross-curricular and cross-departmental initiatives shall be made to help students transfer
   their knowledge to other areas. Also, more off-campus activities shall be offered so students
   could be challenged to upgrade their self-regulated learning skills when they are being placed
   into an unfamiliar environment.

3. Introducing varied reflective tools to promote intrinsic motivation is to improve students’
   reflective abilities and learning. We find that effort should be made to move students from
   extrinsic (i.e. relying on teachers’ input) to intrinsic (i.e. internalizing reflection as an essential
   part of their learning).
   Besides our alumnae network, we would partner with other organizations and institutions to
   support our students in this area. Particularly, given the growing educational trend and the
   significant benefits of AI in personalizing learning and boosting cognitive engagement, we will
   actively embrace AI technology across our academic departments.


4. To help teachers along the way in nurturing our students into “Proactive”, “Self-regulated” and
   “Reflective” learners, professional learning opportunities should be arranged that target at the
   needs of this stage. Pull-out programmes specifically tailored to the professional developmental
   needs of newly joined teachers should be offered.
   As for existing teachers, seeing that the team has accumulated some experiences during the past
   two years of learning and teaching, it is time to further facilitate cross-departmental
   professional exchange so that the teaching team could in itself be a professional learning
   community that practice the inter-group learning that we preach, acting as a role model for our
   students.

REFLECTION
To conclude the second year of student support work under our Major Concerns, various departments
and levels have gradually deepened, expanded or enhanced both their strategies and implementation.
Simultaneously, planning adjustments were made to follow up on last year's review. Results from the
Stakeholder Survey, APASO survey, and Major Concern Questionnaire demonstrated clear effectiveness,
with students displaying increasingly positive growth across all areas. The following points warrant
further attention for future development:
1. Continuous Program Optimization and Deepening: Established programs and activities should
    undergo ongoing evaluation to continuously optimize and deepen their impact. For instance,
    information regarding students’ performance in departmental activities should be communicated with
    class teachers for timely follow-up. By streamlining execution and sustaining high-quality initiatives,
    we can maximize overall efficacy while helping students maintain a balance in their busy academic
    lives.
2. Enhancing Alignment with Students’ Developmental Needs: As the differences in developmental
    needs and competency levels between junior and senior secondary students become increasingly
    pronounced, school-wide activities and programs require special attention. Adopting tiered designs
    and flexible arrangements are highly recommended approaches.
3. Promoting Camping Education: Camp activities effectively enrich students’ experiences and foster
    peer interaction and collaboration, offering a valuable opportunity for students to better understand
    themselves and others while sparking deep reflection. However, given students’ busy schedules, we
    should appropriately incorporate more camp experiences into school activities to nurture students’
    personal growth and talents.
4. Continuously Reinforcing Holistic Wellness: After two years of promoting this Major Concern, a
    school-wide atmosphere that prioritizes physical and mental wellbeing has been well established.
    However, continuous efforts are still required to further strengthen the following three areas:

                                                  17
   - Sustaining efforts to encourage physical exercise, helping students make sports an enduring daily
     habit.
   - Consistently promoting healthy lifestyle habits, with an ongoing focus on ensuring students get
     sufficient sleep and maintain regular routines.
   - Continually reinforcing positive mindsets, empowering students to find the bright side of daily
     events and consistently recognize failure as a vital catalyst for growth.
FEEDBACK AND FOLLOW-UP
As we enter the third year of this Major Concern, we will follow up on measures that fell short of expected
results, while continuing to deepen, broaden and elevate our currently effective initiatives.
1. Building long-term habits relies more on sustained, interactive drives than on standalone talks or
   theme weeks. More importantly, integrating timely evaluation and debriefing into activity planning
   ensures that the specific developmental needs of each grade level are effectively addressed, driving
   the long-term sustainability of the initiative. For instance, to improve student sleep habits, we can
   design level-specific activities during homeroom sessions, supplemented by periodic sleep health tips.
   Supported jointly by the Guidance Team and class teachers, this integrated approach is expected to
   deliver more impactful outcomes.

2. To further assess the effectiveness of camping activities, we could broaden their scope and scale.
   Rather than limiting them to local trips, national and international trips could be arranged, and stronger
   connections across year levels, teams, subject panels, and partner schools could be encouraged to
   create greater synergy. An example is the co-planning of camp activities across different committee,
   which balances human resources and workload while maximizing event impact and educational
   outcomes.

3. Adopting a whole-school approach is well-suited to encouraging student physical and mental well-
   being activity. Moving forward, we will mobilize a broader range of subject panels, administrative
   departments and forms to drive continuous promotion, organizing school-wide campaigns that raise
   awareness among teachers and students and help them put the concept of “Daily Exercise as a Habit”
   into practice. Another way forward to nurture students’ attitudes and positivity is to promote values
   through whole-school cross-curricular learning.

4. In addition to our existing parent talks and Parent Ambassador Group, home-school cooperation will
   be extended into daily school operations. For instance, family relationships should not only be
   addressed in parent education; relevant topics can also be incorporated into S1 and S2 Life Education
   lessons, helping students understand their parents’ perspectives and explore constructive ways to
   interact with them.
