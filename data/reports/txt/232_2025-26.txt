Major Concern 1: Elevate students’ learning efficacy to boost their motivation 「提升學習效能，激發內
在探索熱情」
Achievements
 In evaluating progress toward broadening students' learning horizons, concerted efforts were made at
 both the committee and subject levels. At the committee level, the LCEC and LTDC invited alumni and
 senior-form students to share insights into competitive fields of study, emerging industries, and effective
 learning strategies for secondary students. In addition, the LCEC, together with the Chemistry and
 Economics Departments, organised university visits to institutions such as CUHK and HKUST. These
 activities provided over 180 S3 to S5 students with valuable exposure to diverse academic environments,
 faculty-led laboratories, and tertiary learning experiences.
 At the subject level, panels actively broadened students' perspectives through competitions, enrichment
 programmes, and field studies. The Science panels established partnerships with external organisations
 and tertiary institutions to host inter-school science symposiums and provide specialised coaching for
 regional technology competitions, significantly enhancing students' competence and confidence. The
 Biology panel facilitated students' participation in the Hong Kong Biology Literacy Award, where they
 achieved top honours. The Technology panels also achieved remarkable success in a range of
 competitions, including winning the championship in the WER Robotics Competition, reaching the
 finals of the Sky Dream Inter-school Aviation Competition, and placing among the top 32 teams in the
 BattleACE Battle Robot Competition. In Visual Arts, students' design works were selected by the
 Education Bureau for public exhibition. Language panels encouraged participation in national writing
 competitions and provided specialised training for speech festivals, enabling students to develop their
 communication and language skills. Humanities panels enriched students' global and cultural awareness
 through field studies and educational visits to Zhongshan, Fukuoka, and Macau. Collectively, these
 initiatives expanded students' academic, cultural, and career horizons while fostering a spirit of
 exploration and lifelong learning.
 With regard to extending students’ horizons, the Students’ Stakeholder Survey recorded growth rates of
 0.23, 0.24, and 0.23 for Items 7, 13, and 14 respectively. Although these figures did not meet the original
 success criterion of a 0.3 increase, they nonetheless indicate steady and meaningful progress across all
 measured dimensions.
 Regarding the enhancement of students’ learning motivation, subject panels systematically diversified
 their pedagogical approaches to cater to varied learning styles and maintain active classroom
 engagement. Teachers integrated flipped learning models, tiered analytical tasks, and collaborative
 inquiry methods into daily instruction. To support high-achieving students, Mathematics, Science, and
 Language panels designed challenging tasks featuring complex problem-solving exercises and extension
 readings. In addition, departments actively piloted artificial intelligence tools, such as automated
 language writing feedback software, adaptive mathematics practice platforms, and generative AI
 research tools, to align learning processes with active student inquiry. In terms of enhancing students’
 learning motivation, the Students’ Stakeholder Survey showed growth rates of 0.31 and 0.19 for Items 8
 and 15 respectively. While only one item met the success criterion of a 0.3 increase, both results
 demonstrate positive development in fostering student learning drive.
 Efforts to promote “Reading to learn” were implemented across school, committee, and subject
 departments. The school established a designated Drop Everything And Read (DEAR) period, while the
 Reading to Learn Unit and the Library introduced a whole-school reading award scheme with structured
 criteria to foster a vibrant reading culture. At the subject level, departments compiled recommended
 booklists for leisure and academic purposes, promoted thematic titles during lessons, integrated
 curriculum-related reading materials into assignments, and distributed background reading prior to
 subject-based life-wide learning activities. Regarding the promotion of “Reading to Learn”, performance
 indicators present a mixed picture. In APASO, the Q-scores for “Reading” were 93 and 89, falling short
 of the success criterion of 100. Similarly, the Students’ Stakeholder Survey showed a growth rate of 0.24
 for Item 12, which did not meet the target of a 0.3 increase. However, it is encouraging to note that
 library borrowing records indicate a substantial 70.3% increase, suggesting a notable improvement in
 students’ actual reading habits and interest.
Reflection
 A critical review of the implementation process identifies several major facilitating factors that
 contributed to the successful execution of planned targets. The broad and inclusive framing of Major
 Concern 1 allowed every subject to establish authentic entry points aligned with its academic discipline,
 ranging from robotics competitions and field studies to artistic exhibitions. Inter-departmental
 collaborations further amplified activity impact, as seen in joint projects between Christian Ethics and
 Design and Technology on AI image generation, and Tai Po orienteering game organised by Geography,
 Mathematics and History departments. Crucially, the structural DEAR reading periods and tangible
 incentives under the whole-school reading award scheme effectively converted student motivation into
 concrete behaviour, as evidenced by the 70.3% surge in library borrowings. Furthermore, the proactive
 readiness displayed by teaching staff in exploring artificial intelligence and e-learning platforms
 provided a robust foundation for modernising classroom pedagogy.
 Conversely, several hindering factors constrained project implementation and impacted specific target
 outcomes. Uneven student autonomy led to variable submission rates in non-mandatory challenge
 exercises, particularly among lower-attaining classes. Senior secondary students demonstrated a strong
 preference for examination-oriented practice papers over general leisure reading, limiting the impact of
 broad reading drives in upper forms. The rapid adoption of AI tools highlighted emerging risks regarding
 student over-reliance and uncritical copying, emphasising the need for explicit instruction in digital
 ethics and critical thinking. Furthermore, the reliance on an overly rigid evaluation framework requiring
 a uniform 0.3 increase across all stakeholder survey items proved unrealistic for a single-year
 development cycle, creating a disparity between self-reported survey perceptions and positive physical
 behavioural indicators.
 Consolidating the experience derived from planning, implementation, and evaluation yields vital
 insights for future development. In the planning phase, evaluation benchmarks must be balanced and
 incremental rather than fixed at overly high single-year targets. During implementation, artificial
 intelligence has passed the initial exploration threshold, shifting the focus from tool awareness to robust
 pedagogical task design and academic honesty mechanisms. Finally, during evaluation, schools should
 systematically combine survey perception metrics with direct behavioral data to capture a complete and
 balanced picture of student progress.

Feedback and Follow-up

 To further assist students in achieving the Seven Learning Goals — particularly in developing self-
 directed learning skills, global perspectives, reading habits, and sound moral values — specific follow-
 up measures and plan revisions will inform the next Annual School Plan. Regarding student horizon
 expansion, Items 7, 13, and 14 in the Stakeholder Survey posted growth rates of 0.23, 0.24, and 0.23.
 Although short of the 0.3 target, these figures confirm sustained positive development.
 In light of this, the success criterion for the coming year will be adjusted to a more attainable and holistic
 target: an average growth of 0.1 across the three items. This revised benchmark better reflects
 incremental development while maintaining a focus on continuous improvement. Furthermore, in
 response to the Education Bureau’s emphasis on global perspectives, a new initiative — “Arrange
 learning activities to extend students’ global perspectives” — will be incorporated into next year’s plan.
 It is expected that interested subjects will design and implement learning experiences that broaden
 students’ international awareness and understanding, thereby preparing them to engage with an
 increasingly interconnected world.
 In terms of enhancing students’ learning motivation, the Students’ Stakeholder Survey showed growth
 rates of 0.31 and 0.19 for Items 8 and 15 respectively. While only one item met the success criterion of
 a 0.3 increase, both results demonstrate positive development. For the coming year, the success criterion
 will be revised to an average growth of 0.1 across the two items, allowing for a more balanced evaluation
 of progress. Furthermore, as various subject panels have begun exploring the integration of artificial
 intelligence (AI) into their curricula this year, the focus for the next academic year will shift towards
 practical implementation. Teachers will be encouraged to incorporate AI tools meaningfully into
 learning and teaching processes, with the aim of enhancing student engagement, personalising learning
 experiences, and strengthening students’ intrinsic motivation.
 Promotion of “Reading to Learn” presented contrasting findings. In APASO, results for "Reading"
 yielded Q-scores of 93 and 89 against a target of 100, while Students’ Stakeholder Survey Item 12 posted
 a 0.24 growth rate against a 0.3 target. Despite missing these benchmarks, library borrowing records
 rose by 70.3%, highlighting meaningful progress in student reading habits and engagement. In
 reviewing current strategies, two measures will be removed in the coming year. First, the initiative to
 “Provide students with a recommended booklist of different levels for leisure and academic purposes
 and promote the selected books during lessons” will be discontinued, as subject panels have already
 developed their respective booklists, which will be centrally uploaded to SharePoint and updated
 annually. Second, the measure to “Provide students with relevant reading materials prior to subject-
 based life-wide learning activities” will also be removed, as relevant departments have established
 sufficient resources for ongoing use. Finally, in alignment with the school’s three-year development
 plan, a new initiative — “Provide students with relevant reading materials on values education to foster
 interdisciplinary learning” — will be introduced. This measure aims to strengthen students’ reading
 engagement while promoting values education across subject boundaries, thereby enriching both their
 academic and personal development.

Achievements
 1. Foster students’ holistic well-being by promoting positive habits and encouraging collaborative
     relationships (physical well-being)
  According to the school-based questionnaire results, approximately 86% of the students and teachers
    wore sportswear on Thursdays. Teachers led exercise and mindfulness activities during morning
    devotion, while over 13 sports activities were organised during lunchtime and after school, attracting
    up to 80 participants. Furthermore, 61% of students agreed that the initiative enhanced their awareness
    of the importance of physical fitness, suggesting that it was effective in promoting health awareness
    among most respondents.
  During the “Beyond Limits” sessions, activities such as rock climbing and rowing competition were
    organised for S.4 and S.6 students respectively to enhance their physical fitness. A S.3-day camp was
    also arranged, providing opportunities for students to participate in challenging team-building
    activities. Additionally, the ‘ILC Fitness Challenge’ took place during the post-exam period, with 90%
    of the boys successfully completing the challenge. Feedback collected from the form meetings and P.E.
    department reported that students enjoyed the activities and demonstrated greater confidence,
    perseverance, and willingness to embrace challenges, reflecting positive attitudes towards a healthy
    and active lifestyle.
  To promote healthy lifestyles, videos on athletics, pickleball, fitness training, and sports theory were
    incorporated into P.E. lessons, while sports team captains were trained to lead stretching exercises
    during class, providing students with regular opportunities to practice healthy habits and engage in
    physical activity. With the teachers’ encouragement, over 50% of students were willing to share their
    healthy habits during lessons. Although the proportion of students meeting the MVPA60
    recommendation has not yet reached the EDB target of 65%, over 60% of students agreed that the
    activities fostered a growth-oriented learning environment and encouraged them to apply a growth
    mindset in their studies.
  More than 30 teachers introduced the concept of a growth mindset during morning devotion, providing
    students with regular opportunities to reflect on the importance of perseverance, embracing challenges,
    and learning from mistakes. Based on the questionnaire survey, more than 90% of students from S.1 to
    S.3 indicated that they understand the concept of a growth mindset.
  An Inter-Class Mini Game Competition and the ‘Dr. Known-Everything’ Academic Quiz were
    organised to provide senior-form students with opportunities to achieve a better study-life balance. In
    addition, the Extra-curricular Activities Committee organised an Inter-Class Slogan Design
    Competition aligned with the theme of “Growth Mindset”. The slogans created by the participating
    classes helped promote positive thinking and reinforce growth mindset values among students.
  Growth mindset elements were also integrated into subjects such as Chemistry, Putonghua, and
    Christian Education, with reflection activities conducted after tests, examinations, and newspaper-
    reading tasks to reinforce students’ learning. Evaluation results indicated positive outcomes, e.g. in
    Chemistry, 90% of students agreed that they reviewed their mistakes and sought ways to improve when
    tests or assignments were returned. In Christian Education, students gave an average rating of 4 out of
    5 in agreeing that Abraham’s example inspired them to overcome challenges and learn from setbacks.
    These findings suggest that over 70% of students demonstrated an understanding of the Growth
    Mindset concept by recognising the importance of effort, learning from mistakes, and embracing
    challenges.

  All the assigned subjects have successfully incorporated the designated virtues and values into their

    through their teachers’ lessons, indicating a generally positive impact of the lessons on students’ values
    education.

Reflection

The facilitating factors are as follows.
• Strong staff and student participation. As wearing sportswear on Thursdays provided greater
   comfort, particularly during hot weather. Both students and teachers responded positively to the
   arrangement, and it created a more relaxed learning environment and encouraged greater willingness
   to participate in physical activities. The recruitment of more young teachers contributed positively to
   the implementation. Their enthusiasm and commitment to leading exercise and physical activities
   during morning devotion and after-school sessions enhanced student engagement and supported the
   promotion of an active and healthy lifestyle.
• Diverse physical activity opportunities.           There was effective coordination among the P.E.
   Department, Discipline & Guidance Committee, Extra-curricular Activity Committee, and Sports
   Association. Through early planning and cross-departmental collaboration, a variety of activities were
   organised during lunchtime, after school, the “Beyond Limits” timeslot, and the post-examination
   period, providing students with regular opportunities to engage in physical activity.

 • Integration into the curriculum and daily routines. Health and fitness concepts were reinforced
   through P.E. lessons, sports-related videos, and regular stretching exercises led by trained sports
   captains. This helped embed healthy lifestyle messages into students’ everyday learning experiences
   rather than treating them as one-off events.
 • Student leadership opportunities. Sports team captains and Sports Association members played a

The hindering factors are as follows.
 • Possible scheduling and workload constraints. Activities under the ‘Chill-Fit Thursday’ initiative
   were conducted during lunchtime or after-school periods, when students were often occupied with
   academic commitments, assessments, tutorials, examinations, leadership duties, and other co-

   school hours due to academic commitments such as tests and homework, limiting progress towards
   the MVPA60 target.
 • Insufficient reinforcement beyond activity sessions. Health promotion messages were mainly
   delivered through specific activities rather than being consistently reinforced through daily classroom
   routines. Due to the tight schedule, few class teachers were able to conduct relaxation exercises or
   mindfulness activities during morning class or Day 3 Form Periods, limiting opportunities to help
   students develop and sustain healthy behaviours.

 1. Positive Personal Development and Life Purpose
    Our students demonstrate a strong sense of purpose and positive personal values. APASO results show

    Students demonstrate strong moral character and a strong sense of social responsibility. APASO findings

    community.

    Students show a strong sense of belonging to the school and maintain positive attitudes towards school

        2025–2026 Report on 1st Part of the “AI for Science Education” Funding Programme

1. Background and Overview
In June 2025, the school received a one-off funding allocation of HK$100,000 under the AI for Science
Education Funding Programme. The initiative aims to enhance science teachers' capacity in AI-assisted
teaching, promote pedagogical innovation, and improve student learning outcomes.
• Approval & Records: The proposal was recorded in the 2025–2026 1st Integrated Science (IS) meeting
   minutes and officially submitted to the Incorporated Management Committee (IMC) for approval in
   October 2025.
• Compliance Deliverables:
   1. Develop at least two teaching examples or sets of teaching resources using AI in Junior Secondary
      Science (S1–S3).
   2. Conduct at least one open class or classroom demonstration with AI-assisted teaching in Science (S1–
      S3) within the school.
   3. Conduct at least one experience-sharing session within the school.
2. Key Achievements and Implementation Status (As of June 2026)
The programme is progressing smoothly, and all year-one requirements have been fully met:
• Teacher Professional Development:
  o Five science teachers completed the planned training, including seminars, workshops, and short
     courses led by the Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK). The sessions covered active AI
     applications in science education and practical AI-driven teaching tools.
     Teachers involved: Ms. Cheung FC, Ms. Cheung KMK, Ms. Hung CN, Mr. Lau H, and Mr. Siu YY.
• Teaching Module Development:
  o With CUHK school-based support, two complete junior secondary science modules integrating AI
     applications were developed:
     ▪ S1 Module: Variations, developed by Mr. Lau H and Ms. Cheung KMK.
     ▪ S2 Module: Eye Defects, developed by Mr. Siu YY and Ms. Hung CN.
• Trial Lessons & Classroom Demonstrations:
  o The two developed lesson plans were tried out during the 2025–2026 second term. Teachers
     implemented these AI-integrated lessons in classrooms to assess student engagement and learning
     outcomes. These trials successfully served as the required internal classroom demonstrations.
• Evaluation & Knowledge Transfer:
    oA structured lesson evaluation meeting was conducted in June 2026. The teaching team reviewed the
     trial lessons, exchanged field observations, and identified areas for future optimization. This
     evaluation session fulfilled the requirement for an internal experience-sharing platform.
• External Professional Competition:
  o The finalized lesson plans and learning materials for S1 and S2 were compiled and submitted to the
     Education Bureau (EDB) on March 1, 2026 for the official lesson design competition.
• Infrastructure and Technology Setup:
  o The planned Mac mini M4 LLM system was successfully procured, installed, and configured. It acts
     as the infrastructure backbone to host and deploy local large language models for ongoing science
     education research and trial support.
